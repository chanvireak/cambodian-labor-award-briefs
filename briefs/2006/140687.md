# Award No. 12/06 — HSN Cambodia Co., Ltd (អេច អេស អេន ខេមបូឌា ខូ ភីធី ឌីអិលធីឌី) (Arbitration Council, 15 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: ម៉ារ សំបូរណា (employer-chosen), ទួន ស៊ីផាន់ (worker-chosen), អាំង អេងថុង (chair) · Doc ID 140687

## 1. Facts
HSN Cambodia Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេច អេស អេន ខេមបូឌា ខូ ភីធី ឌីអិលធីឌី) runs a garment factory at Bek Chan, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing 609 workers [Para 20]. On 6 June 2005 it dismissed 131 workers of Building 2 (also called Building B), saying there was no work, and closed the building for a period [Para 20]. While closed, it kept a few workers to maintain four generators [Para 21]. When it reopened and advertised for new workers about a month later, it posted the old workers' photographs in the security room so that guards would not accept their applications, and one old worker was taken on and then dismissed again two days later [Para 23][Para 24][Para 27]. Of eleven demands, four remained non-conciliated: reinstatement of the 131, permanent status for workers with over two months' service, annual leave, and protective equipment [Para 14][Para 37][Para 41].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must take back the 131 workers of Building 2 dismissed on 6 June 2005? [Para 48]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers who have worked more than two months become full-rights (permanent) workers under Article 73 of the Labour Law? [Para 72]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must grant and pay annual leave, including to casual workers who have worked more than one year? [Para 85][Para 102]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must supply hygienic protective equipment in the motor-polishing, hand-polishing and weaving sections, and whether its new penalty scale for not wearing it may be applied? [Para 104][Para 115]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេច អេស អេន (Khmer Youth Union at HSN):** the 131 dismissed workers should be reinstated; workers with over two months' service should be recognised as permanent; annual leave should be paid in lieu, including to casual workers who had served over a year; and protective equipment should be provided in the three sections [Para 2][Para 4][Para 6][Para 8].
**Employer (respondent), HSN Cambodia Co., Ltd:** there is currently no continuous work, and when there is a little it posts an announcement outside the factory so that anyone may apply like anyone else; the recently hired workers are casual workers on short written contracts, one stint at a time, so no worker has served over two months; annual leave will be paid only from the date of this conciliation; it agrees to supply equipment but wants a clear disciplinary rule for workers who do not use it [Para 3][Para 5][Para 7][Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to notify and take back the 131 dismissed workers of Building 2 as a priority whenever it recruits in that building, to recognise as permanent from the date the award takes effect every worker who worked at least 21 days a month over more than two months, to grant annual leave including to casual workers with over one and under three years' service at Khmer New Year, and to supply protective equipment under a three-stage warning scale [Para 122][Para 124][Para 125][Para 129].

On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 95 of the Labour Law, under which a dismissed worker keeps a two-year priority right to reinstatement in the same type of job in the enterprise [Para 55]. The employer must notify the worker at the last known address by registered letter with acknowledgement, or by hand against signature, and the worker must present himself within eight days [Para 57][Para 58]. The panel found the employer had done none of this, had hidden the old workers' photographs to bar their applications, and had dismissed one of them again after rehiring him [Para 62][Para 63][Para 67]. It held this a breach of Article 95 and ordered priority rehiring [Para 67][Para 68].

On Issue 2 the panel cited Articles 73 and 166: successive short fixed-term contracts, renewed without break and without any termination compensation, must be treated as continuous service, and the company never gave the workers their own copies of the contracts [Para 74][Para 77][Para 78]. Following Award 69/04 it held that casual workers may properly be converted to permanent status, and ordered recognition of workers with at least 21 days a month over more than two months' service [Para 81][Para 83][Para 124].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Articles 166, 167, 170 and 120: annual leave is earned after a full year and cannot be replaced by compensation or delayed beyond three consecutive years, and casual workers are entitled to it too [Para 85][Para 87][Para 92][Para 95]. It rejected the employer's position that leave would be paid only from the conciliation date, and ordered annual leave to be arranged for all workers, including casual workers with over one and under three years' service, at Khmer New Year [Para 96][Para 102].

On Issue 4 the panel applied Articles 229 and 230, which require clean, safe workplaces and protective equipment [Para 104][Para 106]. The employer had agreed to supply masks and gloves in the three sections but wanted a stricter disciplinary scale than Clause 12 of its internal rules [Para 110][Para 111]. Comparing the internal rules' two-stage scale with the company's new four-stage scale, the panel found the new scale more favourable to workers and permitted it [Para 117][Para 119][Para 120].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dismissed worker's two-year priority right under Article 95 is lost only if the employer notifies him at his last known address by registered or hand-delivered letter with acknowledgement and he fails to present himself within eight days; an employer that quietly re-advertises and screens out old applicants breaches Article 95 and must rehire them first [Para 57][Para 67]. Renewed short fixed-term contracts without break and without termination pay convert to permanent status under Article 73, and annual leave under Article 166 cannot be bought out or postponed [Para 77][Para 92].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the company's new four-stage warning scale for protective equipment is more favourable to workers than its registered internal rules, and so may be applied [Para 119].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award exists only in Khmer, so the case name survives as the letters "អេច អេស អេន" without an expanded Latin form. Page numbers and list numerals are interleaved with the text throughout, and a fragment of the signature block is unreadable at the end of the decision [Para 130]. The party name in the Award 69/04 citation is garbled beyond recovery [Para 81][Para 84]. The threshold day-count in Order 2 appears both as "២១" (21) and, in a stray numeral, as "២០" [Para 124]. One worker is repeatedly identified by name and employee number in the reasoning; the brief does not reproduce it [Para 27][Para 66]. Two paragraphs repeat their opening clause verbatim where the file was scanned twice [Para 115].
