# Award No. 97/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក ហ្គាមេន (Nume K Garment) (Arbitration Council, 8 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yov Soun Ty, An Non, Pen Bun Chhean) · Doc ID 140739

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក ហ្គាមេន (Nume K Garment), a Phnom Penh garment factory with 1,050 workers and a single union of 480 members, was the respondent employer [Para 20]. The workers, represented by សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក (Democratic Union of Nume K), demanded that the company pay their wages in advance for the three months of maternity leave because of shortfalls in their other expenses [Para 21][Para 3]. Both parties acknowledged that the company had been paying 50% of wages in monthly instalments during maternity leave, alongside the wages of workers still at work [Para 23]. The employer refused the demand, and conciliation failed, producing the non-conciliation report No. 1523 of 17 October 2006 [Para 22][Para 7].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 50% maternity wage in advance, at least one day before the 90-day maternity leave begins, rather than in monthly instalments during the leave? [Para 21][Para 44]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក (Democratic Union of Nume K):** The 50% wage should be paid before the leave starts: some workers live far from the factory and spend heavily travelling each month to collect it, and they lack cash for medical and other expenses [Para 26][Para 27]. They relied on Article 116 of the Labour Law, which requires wages to be paid twice a month, and on Article 115A paragraph 3, which bars payment on a rest day and requires payment one day before if the due date falls on a rest day [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ញូមេ ក ហ្គាមេន (Nume K Garment):** The hardship is not peculiar to this factory, and the company maintained it was applying the Labour Law [Para 31][Para 22]. It read Article 115A paragraph 3 as covering only public-holiday leave, and Article 116 as entitling an employer to pay wages once a month [Para 32][Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 50% of wages for the 90-day maternity leave, at least one day before the leave begins [Para 45][Para 46].
On the statutory framework, the panel cited Article 182 paragraph 1 of the Labour Law, under which every woman in an enterprise covered by Article 1 has the right to 90 days' maternity leave to give birth [Para 34]. It cited Article 183 paragraph 1, under which a woman on such leave is entitled to half her wage, including secondary benefits, and noted that earlier awards No. 49/04-Ho Hing and No. 68/4-City New had applied that rule [Para 35]. It also cited Article 115 paragraph 3, which prohibits paying wages on a day the worker is entitled to rest and requires payment one day earlier where the due date falls on such a day [Para 36][Para 37].
Applying those provisions, the panel held that wages may not be paid on a day the worker has the right to rest, and that the 90 days of maternity leave is itself a lawful rest entitlement during which the company may not pay wages [Para 38][Para 39]. It rejected the employer's narrow reading of Article 115A paragraph 3, holding that the provision is not confined to public holidays but covers any day on which the worker has the right to rest [Para 40]. It followed its earlier decision in case No. 57/06-Evergreen, which held that workers are entitled to receive wages before maternity leave under Article 115 [Para 41].
The panel answered the employer's practice argument directly: the fact that other companies do not apply the standard cannot override the statutory text, and general practice is weighed only where the law is silent [Para 42][Para 43]. It therefore ordered the employer to pay 50% of wages for the 90-day period at least one day before the leave starts [Para 44][Para 45].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must pay the 50% maternity wage for the 90-day leave at least one day before the leave begins, because maternity leave is a statutory rest period on which wages may not be paid; the practice of other employers cannot displace that rule [Para 44][Para 42].
**Obiter dicta:** Where the law is not explicit, the panel will consider equity and general practice [Para 43].
