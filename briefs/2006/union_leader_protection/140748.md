# Award No. 110/06 — ហ្គោល ដិន ក្រោន ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី (Golden Crown Cambodia Co., Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 15 December 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (employer-appointed អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី; worker-appointed អន ណន; chair គង់ ផល្លៈ) · Doc ID 140748

## 1. Facts
The employer, ហ្គោល ដិន ក្រោន ខេមបូឌា ខូអិលធីឌី, a garment factory in Dangkao district, Phnom Penh, employs 920 workers and has a single union, the Democratic Union of Garment Workers of the Golden Crown factory, registered on 2 August 2006 and affiliated to C.CAWDU [Para 17][Para 18]. The union asked the company to deduct union dues from members' wages, proposing $0.25 per member per month and later 1,000 riel per worker per month from 28 August 2006 [Para 2][Para 20][Para 22]. The company refused, saying it was too busy and invoking Article 281 of the Labour Law [Para 3][Para 27].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must deduct union dues from the wages of workers who have authorised the deduction in writing? [Para 25][Para 27]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The union argued that its statutes require every member to pay dues, that it was difficult to collect them during short breaks, and that Article 129(2) of the Labour Law together with Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 entitled it to have the employer make the deduction [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27].
**Employer (respondent):** The company refused on the ground that Article 281 of the Labour Law forbids an employer from deducting wages in order to pay union dues [Para 27][Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer, once it had each worker's written authorisation, to deduct 1,000 riel per month from the wages of union members and pay it to the union as dues [Para 41].

The panel set out Article 129(2), under which a worker may authorise deduction from wages to pay dues to a union of which he or she is a member, the authorisation to be in writing and revocable at any time [Para 28][Para 29], and Article 5 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, requiring a written request 15 days in advance [Para 30]. Against that, Article 281 prohibits employers from deducting wages to pay union dues or paying the dues on the worker's behalf [Para 33]. The panel reconciled the two provisions as its earlier awards had done — cases 03/03 Tongka, 05/03, 62/04, 94/04, 99/04, 16/05, 62/05 Jin Ta Y and 106/06 — holding that Article 281 exists to protect workers and to stop employers from bringing a union under their influence contrary to Article 280, not to bar a deduction the worker has freely requested [Para 31][Para 32][Para 34][Para 36][Para 37]. On the facts, the union was properly registered and 671 workers had signed written requests, so the deduction was voluntary and the employer's compliance was not interference [Para 38][Para 39][Para 20]. The employer was therefore obliged to deduct the dues of members who had authorised it in writing [Para 40][Para 41]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification [Para 42].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 281 of the Labour Law does not prevent an employer from deducting union dues from a worker's wages where the union is properly registered and the worker has given a written, revocable authorisation under Article 129(2); the employer then must make the deduction, because the prohibition targets employer interference with union independence and not a voluntary check-off [Para 34][Para 38][Para 40].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel distinguished a union that the employer itself created or finances from one that registered independently, observing that only the former falls within the Article 280 definition of interference [Para 36][Para 37][Para 39].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The number of members is given as 614 at the hearing and 671 in the later written requests [Para 19][Para 20]. The union's demand appears once as $0.25 per member and once as 1,000 riel per worker [Para 20][Para 22]. The operative paragraph's description of the covered workers is partly corrupted in the OCR [Para 41]. One arbitrator's name appears in two slightly different spellings in the signature block [Para 43].
