# Award No. 19/06 — Xing Hong Company (Arbitration Council, 10 April 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea as chair) · Doc ID 140690

## 1. Facts
Xing Hong Company, a garment factory on Street 369, Sangkat Prek Prar, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 300 workers, 70 to 80 percent of them women, and had operated since April 2004 without ever paying a worker's medical examination fee. [Para 4][Para 21][Para 23]
The worker party — the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Local Union of KYFTU at Xing Hong Company — lodged seven demands on 12 January 2006; two were conciliated on 14 February 2006, and at the hearing the parties agreed on the nursing room and withdrew two further demands as implemented, leaving the medical fee, the day-care centre and union dues. [Para 5][Para 14][Para 18]
The company required applicants to bring photographs, birth certificate, identity card, family book, residence letter and a medical examination certificate, then had accepted workers take the check during or after a two-to-three-month probation. [Para 22][Para 23]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel medical check fee to each worker who paid it, and bear that cost for new recruits? [Para 30]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must build a day-care centre, or instead supply three two-kilogram cans of milk formula and US$20 per month for an external baby-sitter? [Para 36]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel per month from members' wages for KYFTU dues on written requests? [Para 51]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 247 of the Labour Law obliges the employer to pay for the pre-employment check, so the 10,100 riel each worker advanced must be refunded; and because the workforce is overwhelmingly female, Article 186 requires a day-care centre, or payment for a place at an external centre. [Para 30][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** The medical check is a precondition of recruitment borne by the applicant; the company accepted the nursing room under the 24 March 2006 agreement but refused the day-care centre, and refused the dues deduction on the ground that the union could collect from members after working hours. [Para 29][Para 37][Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company must reimburse 10,100 riel to each worker who paid for the medical check within one month of the award taking effect and must pay for the check for workers who have not yet taken it, while the demands for a day-care centre, milk formula and US$20 per month were declined. [Para 56][Para 57]

On the medical fee the panel held that Article 247 of the Labour Law provides a sufficient basis to conclude the employer must cover the check, even though Article 247(a) contemplates a Prakas on examination conditions that, apart from Joint Prakas 09/94, has never been issued. [Para 31][Para 32] It followed its own line of awards from 60/04-United Art through 23/06-Max Pearl, and held that requiring the worker to pay in advance, or after starting work, does not release the employer under Article 247(c); Article 377 exposes a non-compliant employer to a fine of one hundred twenty to three hundred sixty days of base daily wage or imprisonment. [Para 33][Para 34]

On the day-care centre the panel read Article 186 to oblige an establishment with at least one hundred women to set up a nursing room and a day-care centre, or, at paragraph 2, to pay the fee for placing children at an external centre. [Para 38][Para 43] It found the company crossed that threshold, but because no worker then had a child over eighteen months old, the demand addressed a future situation rather than an existing dispute, and the panel declined it, as it had in 10/03-Jaqsintex; the alternative claim for formula and US$20 was refused for the same reason. [Para 42][Para 46][Para 48]

On union dues the panel applied Article 129 of the Labour Law and Article 5 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001, holding that deduction becomes the employer's obligation on receipt of written authorisation from each member, and followed 03/03-Tonga Garment, 16/05-New Point and 70/05-Gold Fame in ordering the company to deduct 1,000 riel each pay day. [Para 54][Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of the pre-employment medical examination under Article 247 of the Labour Law, and cannot shift it to the worker by making the check a recruitment precondition or by deferring it until after probation begins. [Para 33]
The Article 186 day-care duty arises from the number of women employed, not from the present existence of children, but a demand for a centre or for milk formula is not justiciable until a child over eighteen months actually needs a place, so the panel declines it as a future claim. [Para 42][Para 48]
Where a worker gives written authorisation under Article 129, deduction of union dues is a mandatory employer obligation, not a matter of the employer's convenience. [Para 54]

**Obiter dicta:** The observation that a non-compliant employer faces criminal exposure under Article 377, and the remark that no woman would benefit from the formula demand at that time, were not necessary to the orders made. [Para 34][Para 49]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
