# Award No. 05/06 — W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140679

## 1. Facts
W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd, at Reusey Village, Sangkat Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, had the labour physician examine newly recruited workers at the factory and then deducted the 10,100 riel fee from the workers' overtime meal allowance; later recruits had to pay for their own pre-employment examination before being hired. [Para 46][Para 50][Para 51][Para 52] The Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) and the Khmer Youth Union at W&D Cambodia Co., Ltd, registered on 30 December 2005 with about 300 members and not yet representative, put eleven demands to the company on 6 December 2005. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 43] Eight were settled in conciliation, leaving the medical fee, the disclosure of piece rates in advance and the deduction of 1,000 riel union dues. [Para 32]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel medical examination fee to each worker, whether the sum was deducted from the meal allowance or paid by the worker on recruitment? [Para 21][Para 46][Para 61]
2. (Issue 7) Whether the company must disclose the piece rate for each model three days before the workers begin work on it? [Para 22][Para 52][Para 76]
3. (Issue 11) Whether the company must deduct 1,000 riel from the wages of union members for union contributions? [Para 22][Para 85][Para 86]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company must bear the cost of the compulsory pre-employment physical examination under Article 247 of the Labour Law and Prakas 09/94, having either deducted the 10,100 riel from meal allowances or required workers to pay before recruitment; piece rates should be issued three days ahead so that wages can be checked; and the company should deduct 1,000 riel in dues for members. [Para 46][Para 47][Para 52][Para 61][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** Article 247 and Prakas 09/94 cover only periodical and special examinations, so only special checks are paid for and pre-employment checks are a condition the worker must satisfy at her own cost; a piece rate cannot be set until three to seven days after trial sewing; and it did not agree to deduct union dues. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 51][Para 53][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reimburse 10,100 riel to workers whose wage or perquisites were deducted for the examination and to workers whose contracts began from February 2003, to inform workers of each model's piece rate within three to seven days after trial sewing, and to deduct 1,000 riel in union dues on a member's request. [Para 93]

On Issue 1 the panel read Article 247(a) and 247(c) of the Labour Law with Articles 3, 5 and 7 of Prakas 09/94: examinations are compulsory before work, and Article 7 places the cost of the examinations contemplated by Article 5 on the enterprise owner. [Para 61][Para 62] It followed 02/03-Chou Sing, 21/03-Royal Cambodia, 19/04-Kbal Koh 2 and 53/04-Kung Hong, and noted the doubts raised in 64/04-Mercury, 98/04-Great Union and 106/04, most recently resolved in 16/05-New Point Trade. [Para 63][Para 64] On time limits it applied Article 120, giving three years from the date the wage fell due for deducted sums and three years from conclusion of the contract for sums the worker paid herself, so claims dating from 1998 had expired in 2001; Article 13 then made the contractual term placing the fee on the worker null. [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73] Only workers whose pay was deducted or who started from February 2003 recovered.

On Issue 7 the panel applied Article 112(b), which requires the employer to inform workers precisely of the total wage for a pay period whenever it is likely to change, and held that a change of model changes the wage, so the rate must be communicated. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 78] Article 112 sets no time, so the panel accepted the employer's three-to-seven-day period after trial sewing as reasonable, noting that in Ecent the Council had held that determination could not exceed three days and that here the company sent no expert to testify. [Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

On Issue 11 the panel set Article 281, which forbids an employer to deduct union dues, against Article 129 paragraph 2, which lets a worker authorise in writing a deduction of dues to the union she belongs to, and Article 5 of Prakas 305/01, requiring a written request fifteen days in advance; the deduction is therefore lawful, and indeed required, once the member asks. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 92][Para 93]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The cost of the compulsory pre-employment physical examination falls on the employer under Article 247 of the Labour Law and Article 7 of Prakas 09/94, so a contractual term or a payroll deduction shifting it to the worker is null under Article 13; but a claim to recover it is a claim arising from the labour contract and is time-barred three years after the deduction, or three years after the contract was concluded where the worker paid the clinic directly. [Para 62][Para 66][Para 68][Para 69][Para 73] Where wage changes with the model sewn, Article 112(b) obliges the employer to inform workers of the new piece rate, at a time that accommodates the trial sewing needed to set it. [Para 74][Para 84] And although Article 281 bars an employer from deducting union dues of its own motion, a written authorisation from a member under Article 129 paragraph 2, requested fifteen days in advance under Prakas 305/01, obliges the employer to make the deduction. [Para 87][Para 89][Para 90][Para 92]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that parties are deemed to know a law once it takes effect, grounded on Article 93 of the 1993 Constitution, is a general proposition used to explain the limitation cut-off rather than the basis of the order. [Para 72][Para 73]
