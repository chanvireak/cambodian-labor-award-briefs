# Award No. 16/06 — LA (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អិល អេ ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមេន) (Arbitration Council, 23 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: ម៉ារ សំបូរណា (employer-chosen), ស៊ីន គីម ស៊ាន (worker-chosen), ប៉ែន ប៊ុនឈា (chair) · Doc ID 140688

## 1. Facts
LA (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អិល អេ ខេមបូឌា ហ្គាមេន ភីធី អ៊ី អិលធីឌី) employs about 4,500 workers at Sleang Anhleung, Tuek Thla, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh [Para 7][Para 20]. On the evening of 16 February 2006 the company told four union officials and activists that they were dismissed; they refused the dismissal and the severance pay and returned to work, and the workers say security guards pushed them out, causing a pregnant worker to fall and be taken to hospital [Para 20][Para 21][Para 22]. About 400 workers of Building LA 2 struck in support the same day, and from 17 to 20 February nearly 4,000 workers, some 90 percent, joined [Para 23][Para 27]. Thirteen of fourteen demands were conciliated on 20 February 2006; the only outstanding demand was that the company keep the regular bonus for the strike days of 16, 17, 18 and 20 February 2006 [Para 9][Para 2]. The strikers returned on the morning of 21 February 2006 [Para 28].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain the regular bonus for February 2006 in respect of the strike days of 16, 17, 18 and 20 February 2006? [Para 2][Para 53]
2. (Issue 1, lawfulness) Whether the strike was carried out in accordance with Chapter 13 of the Labour Law, so that the strikers retain the protection of Article 333 against employer sanctions? [Para 39][Para 41]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សម្ព័ន្ធ សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (C.CAWDU) and its local union at LA:** the regular bonus for February 2006 should be kept for the strike days, and the panel should grant justice for the dismissal of the four union leaders and the pushing that felled a pregnant worker [Para 2][Para 24][Para 50].
**Employer (respondent), LA (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd:** the bonus is the company's own choice and no law requires it, and during the strike its finances fell because goods had to be shipped by air; it had notified the Ministry of Labour of the four dismissals [Para 26][Para 25].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand that the company maintain the regular bonus for February 2006 in respect of the strike of 16, 17, 18 and 20 February 2006 [Para 53].

The panel began from Article 332 of the Labour Law, under which the employment contract is suspended during a strike, so that no work is provided and no wages are paid [Para 31][Para 32]. It noted the exception in Article 334: if the employer hires replacement workers during a strike it must pay the strikers their wages for the strike period [Para 33]. It found no replacement hiring and no transfer of goods to outside factories in this case, so that exception did not apply [Para 34][Para 29].

It then tested the lawfulness of the strike. Article 324 requires at least seven days' prior notice to the employer, and Article 320(k) paragraph 4 allows a strike only after all peaceful means of settling the dispute have been exhausted [Para 35][Para 37]. The workers struck on 16 February 2006, the day the four dismissals were announced, without prior notice to the company or the labour inspector, without a union ballot, and before the conciliation and Arbitration Council procedures had run [Para 30][Para 36][Para 38]. The panel concluded the strike did not comply with the procedure [Para 39].

That finding decided the bonus. Article 333 forbids any employer sanction against strikers, but the panel held that the prohibition applies in full only where the strike itself follows the legal procedure; workers who do not comply cannot claim its protection [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42]. Because the workers had not observed Chapter 13, the panel could not extend that protection to the US$5 deduction from the bonus they complained of, and it rejected any claim for bonus or wages during the strike [Para 43][Para 44].

Finally, on the workers' request for justice over the pushing that made the pregnant worker fall — an incident the company admitted occurred — the panel applied Article 312, which limits it to the issues in the non-conciliation minutes or matters arising directly from that dispute, and held it had no jurisdiction over that incident [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51][Para 52].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A strike begun without the seven days' prior notice required by Article 324 and before peaceful settlement has been exhausted under Article 320(k) paragraph 4 is unlawful, so the Article 333 bar on sanctions against strikers does not apply and the strikers cannot claim wages or bonus for the strike days; Article 332 keeps the contract suspended so that no wage is earned even by a lawful strike [Para 39][Para 43][Para 47]. The panel will not decide a claim that falls outside the non-conciliation minutes or does not arise directly from that dispute, under Article 312 [Para 52].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that an employer which hires replacements during a strike must pay the strikers for the strike period under Article 334, an exception that did not arise here [Para 33][Para 34].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is Khmer-only, so the case name survives as the letters "អិល អេ" without an expanded Latin form, and the union's acronym is spelled C.CAWDU, CAWDU and CCAWDU in different places [Para 8][Para 15]. Stray digits intrude on dates and figures — February 2006 appears once as "២០១ ២០០៦", and the four dismissed workers are once given as "២០១៦" [Para 2][Para 25]. The chair's name is printed in two slightly different forms in the signature block [Para 55]. The list of prior awards preserves garbled English factory names, including one rendered "Wash Cncept" [Para 45]. Paragraphs are duplicated where the page was scanned twice, including the passage recording the workers' account of the pushing [Para 48]. The workforce figure appears both as 4.500 and 4500 [Para 7][Para 20]. One pregnant worker's injury is described in the facts as a fall and later as serious injury, with no further particularisation [Para 22][Para 48].
