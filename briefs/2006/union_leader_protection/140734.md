# Award No. 89/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី (YGM (Cambodia) Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 3 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី, វ៉ែន ពៅ, តាំង ទ្រី) · Doc ID 140734

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី (YGM (Cambodia) Ltd) employs about 2,000 workers and recognises at least two unions, including the claimant សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម [Para 21]. After 11 demands, four points remained unresolved [Para 12][Para 17]. They concerned the attendance bonus during three days' personal leave, transport home after overtime of two hours or more, a USD 1 payment plus meal money for such overtime, and reimbursement of health-check fees [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]. A conciliation meeting on 15 September 2006 reached agreement on points 1 to 10 and 12, leaving only point 11 (the bonus) open [Para 34][Para 59][Para 60].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must maintain the attendance bonus when a worker takes three days' leave for personal business? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must provide safe transport home when overtime reaches two hours or more? [Para 3]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay USD 1 plus meal money for overtime of two hours or more? [Para 4]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must arrange health checks for workers not yet examined and reimburse workers from whom the fee was deducted? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ ប្រចាំ រោងចក្រ វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម:** the company should keep the attendance bonus during three days' leave for personal business, provide transport home after two hours' overtime, pay USD 1 plus meal money for that overtime, and reimburse health-check fees deducted from wages [Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 5]. They relied on the 15 September 2006 conciliation minutes, which they said settled all twelve points [Para 38].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម (ខេមបូឌា) អិលធីឌី:** the company refused the four points, arguing that the collective agreement registered on 3 August 2005 governs all workers [Para 31][Para 40]. It said the conciliation signatory lacked written authority and the minutes bore no company seal [Para 51][Para 52].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the demand for the attendance bonus over three days' leave was refused [Para 144]; the company was ordered to implement points 1, 2 and 10 of the 15 September 2006 conciliation minutes, covering transport after overtime, USD 1 plus meal money, and health-check reimbursement [Para 145][Para 146][Para 147].
On the preliminary question, the panel held the conciliation minutes valid. Under Article 307 a settlement signed by the parties and endorsed by the conciliator has the force of a collective agreement [Para 75][Para 80]. The absence of the company seal did not matter, because Article 115 of the Commercial Enterprise Law gives effect to documents made in the company's name without a seal [Para 71][Para 72]. The panel also found the signatory had apparent authority, and that union leadership credentials were recognised by the Ministry under registration No. 988 [Para 58][Para 69].
On issue 1, only point 11 remained unsettled, so the panel turned to the collective agreement [Para 89]. Article 97 makes the collective agreement binding on all workers in the establishment [Para 92][Para 94], and clause 11.C allows one day's sick leave per month without bonus deduction, with deduction permitted beyond that [Para 95][Para 106]. Clause 11.C does not contradict public-order provisions of the Labour Law [Para 107], so the demand for three days failed [Para 108][Para 144].
On issues 2 and 3, the panel found no provision of the collective agreement, internal rules or Labour Law granting transport or the USD 1 meal allowance [Para 113][Para 125]. But points 1 and 2 of the settlement granted them, and a settlement may improve on the collective agreement under Articles 13 and 98.1 [Para 97][Para 98][Para 130]. Both were therefore enforceable [Para 121][Para 131].
On issue 4, Article 247(c) places health-check costs on the employer [Para 136][Para 137], and point 10 of the settlement promised reimbursement in the October 2006 payroll [Para 140][Para 143]. The panel ordered implementation [Para 147].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A conciliation settlement signed by the parties and endorsed by the conciliator is binding as a collective agreement under Article 307, and the absence of a company seal does not defeat it under Article 115 of the Commercial Enterprise Law [Para 75][Para 71]. Where a collective agreement regulates a demand, its terms govern unless the settlement grants a superior benefit permitted by Articles 13 and 98.1 [Para 94][Para 130].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's remark that a party which rejects some agreed points while staying silent on others acts inconsistently [Para 64].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name is printed in several variants, including វ៉ាយ ជី អឹម, វ៉ាយ ជិ អឹម and វ៉ាយ ជី អី ម [Para 1][Para 21]. Para 22 reverses the working hours ("ពី ម៉ោង ១៧:៣០ ចេញ ម៉ោង ១១:៣០") [Para 22]. The health-check sum appears as 100 riel in the demand, 1,100 riel in the settlement account, and 10,100 riel in the reasoning [Para 5][Para 39][Para 74]. Several dates are mistyped, including "ឆ្នាំ ២០២៦" and "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៥" [Para 18][Para 65]. The record of the 15 September meeting is duplicated and interleaved, making the agreed points hard to follow [Para 41][Para 43][Para 45][Para 48]. The signature block is partly lost to OCR [Para 149].
