# Award No. 03/06 — Flying Dragon Garment Factory (Arbitration Council, 7 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chhiv Phyrum for the employer party, Sin Kim Sean for the worker party, Nhean So Munin chair) · Doc ID 140677

## 1. Facts
Flying Dragon Garment Factory (Flying Dragon (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd), trading since 1997 from Cham Chao Street, Steng Mean Chey commune, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, runs three branches with about 1,745 workers. [Para 21][Para 22] From September 2004 the company pressed workers onto one-year fixed-duration contracts (FDCs), including people who had worked since 1997 without any written contract; the change was announced by loudspeaker and through line leaders, who told the workers only that new employment background forms were being made. [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40][Para 53] On 28 October 2005 management signed a collective bargaining agreement (CBA) with shop stewards and some union members, registered on 4 November 2005, and on 11 November 2005 notified workers that their one-year FDCs had become three-month FDCs under it. [Para 18][Para 45] The Worker Democratic Union at Flying Dragon 3, a C.CAWDU affiliate of about 601 workers, complained on 4 November 2005 that the CBA bound none of its members because its own leaders had never agreed to it. [Para 23][Para 45][Para 46]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must re-apply undetermined-duration contracts (UDCs) under the 1997 Labour Law, and, if it applies FDCs, sign one for each year and pay termination compensation correctly? [Para 4][Para 5]
2. Whether the CBA of 28 October 2005 is valid against the members of the Worker Democratic Union at Flying Dragon 3, whose representatives did not approve it? [Para 45][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the company converted long-serving workers' UDCs to FDCs without their free agreement merely by collecting thumbprints on new forms, and the CBA invoked to justify the three-month contracts was signed without the union leadership, so the UDCs must be restored. [Para 33][Para 36][Para 46][Para 47]
**Employer (respondent):** every worker signed a one-year FDC when the factory opened in 1997, and although a technical mistake meant workers were not told about renewals — which turned the FDCs into UDCs — the company would now apply the CBA of 28 October 2005 and its three-month contracts. [Para 32][Para 45]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the one-year FDCs made in 2004 were declared null and void, the three-month contracts continued from them likewise, the company was ordered to keep applying the UDCs of the union's members until those contracts are lawfully cancelled, and the CBA of 28 October 2005 was invalidated as against those members. [Para 59][Para 60]

The panel tested legality in two stages, procedure before substance. [Para 48][Para 49] On procedure it applied Article 1 of Decree No. 38 of 28 October 1988, which requires a true and free agreement without coercion, and Article 65 of the Labour Law, which leaves the form of the employment contract to the contracting parties. [Para 49][Para 50] To convert a UDC lawfully the employer must tell the workers clearly that it intends to terminate the UDC and propose the replacement FDC, and must allow at least seven days to consider it under Article 75 of the Labour Law. [Para 52][Para 53] Here the proposal came by loudspeaker and through line leaders who spoke only of new employment background forms, so the workers did not sign freely and had no clear information or time to decide; the 2004 one-year FDCs were therefore procedurally invalid and the substantive question did not arise. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56] The three-month contracts that continued from those invalid one-year contracts fell with them. [Para 57]

On the CBA the panel applied Article 96(2) of the Labour Law, which requires signature by an employer and by one or more representative trade union organisations — shop stewards being able to sign only in the transitional period when no union representative exists — and Article 6 of Prakas 305/01, under which a union with an absolute majority represents the whole workforce and otherwise only its own members. [Para 57][Para 58] No representative of the Worker Democratic Union at Flying Dragon 3 approved the text, so the panel held the CBA invalid as to its members without construing its terms. [Para 59]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that converts undetermined-duration contracts into fixed-duration contracts must obtain the workers' true and free agreement: it must state clearly that it intends to terminate the UDC, propose the new contract, and allow at least seven days for consideration under Article 75 of the Labour Law; a conversion announced by loudspeaker and through line leaders, presented as an update of employment background forms, fails that test, and any later fixed-duration contract continuing from it is void as well. [Para 52][Para 53][Para 55][Para 56][Para 57] A CBA binds a union's members only where it is signed by a representative trade union organisation as Article 96(2) requires, and a union holding an absolute majority of the enterprise's workers is the body entitled to represent them under Article 6 of Prakas 305/01, so an agreement concluded without its approval is invalid as to its members. [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's two-stage method — procedural legality first, substantive lawfulness only if procedure is sound — and its express refusal to reach the substance of the FDCs are general observations, the case having been decided on procedure alone. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 56]
