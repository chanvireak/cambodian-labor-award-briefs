# Award No. 96/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូលី មីធិត (Will Sin Garment) (Arbitration Council, 24 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, An Virak, Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 140740

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូលី មីធិត (Will Sin Garment), a factory at Prey Ta village, Chom Chao, Dangkao, employed about 1,020 workers, roughly 90% of them women [Para 27][Para 100]. The workers' union, សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន (C.CAWDU at Will Sin), filed a complaint on 21 July 2006 raising seven demands; conciliation settled four and referred three unresolved points to the Council on 16 October 2006 [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16]. The company had been paying 50% of base wage plus 50% seniority pay plus 50% of the regular attendance bonus during maternity leave [Para 28][Para 3]. Its two union leaders, the president and vice-president, were suspended twice from 25 May 2006 and dismissed on 12 June 2006 after the Labour Ministry's labour-dispute department agreed, on the ground that their attendance cards had been punched while they were at a seminar in Thailand [Para 61][Para 62][Para 39].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) How should the 50% maternity wage be calculated — on the average of total wages over the last 12 months, plus 100% seniority pay and 100% regular attendance bonus, plus annual-leave pay for the leave period? [Para 2][Para 29]
2. (Issue 2) Must the company provide a breastfeeding room and nursery, or instead pay $15 per month per worker for 24 months from her return to work? [Para 5][Para 93]
3. (Issue 3) Must the company take the two union leaders back into normal work? [Para 7][Para 192]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant) — សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន (C.CAWDU at Will Sin):** The maternity wage should be 50% of average total wages over the preceding 12 months, including base wage, overtime, seniority pay and regular attendance bonus, and should also add 100% seniority pay, 100% attendance bonus and annual-leave pay for the leave period [Para 29][Para 2]. They sought $15 per month for 24 months from the date of return to work in lieu of a nursery, and the reinstatement of the two union leaders, who said they had permission for the Thailand seminar and had not cheated the company [Para 5][Para 67].
**Employer (respondent) — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីល សិន ហ្គាមេន (ខេមបូឌា) ខូលី មីធិត (Will Sin Garment):** From 27 October 2006 it applied 50% of average total wages over the last 12 months, but excluded seniority pay and attendance bonus and did not pay annual-leave compensation [Para 30][Para 31]. It had no nursery and paid $5 per month for 18 months for children over 18 months old instead [Para 32][Para 33]. It refused reinstatement, saying the two women had committed serious misconduct and were dismissed according to procedure with ministerial approval [Para 68].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the maternity-wage recalculation, the nursery and the $15 allowance were ordered, but the seniority, attendance-bonus and annual-leave claims and the reinstatement demand were refused [Para 186][Para 187][Para 188][Para 191][Para 192].
On Issue 1, Article 182 and Article 183 of the Labour Law give a woman who has worked at least one year the right to 50% of wages plus allowances during maternity leave [Para 69]. Both parties agreed the 50% is taken from average total wages over the last 12 months, including base wage, overtime, seniority pay and attendance bonus [Para 70][Para 71]. The panel held that the law does not define "secondary benefits" clearly enough to add 100% seniority pay and 100% attendance bonus on top of the 50%, and refused those claims [Para 72][Para 74]. It also refused annual-leave pay during maternity leave, since the worker is not working and the leave is already compensated [Para 72][Para 188]. The order was to recompute the 50% as the average monthly wage over the last 12 months, counting seniority pay and attendance bonus, divided by two [Para 186].
On Issue 2, Article 186 requires any enterprise employing at least 100 women to provide a breastfeeding room and a nursery [Para 94][Para 96]. Since the company employs 1,020 workers, 90% of them women, the obligation applies [Para 100]. The panel read Article 184 (one hour of breastfeeding for one year after birth) as requiring the nursery from the date the woman returns to work, and as covering children up to 18 months, not only older children [Para 101][Para 102][Para 105]. It ordered the room and nursery within three months and, where the company cannot build one, $15 per month for children over 18 months [Para 190][Para 191].
On Issue 3, the panel noted that Article 293 requires the labour inspector's prior consent to dismiss a union leader, but Article 295 lets the employer suspend at once in a serious-misconduct case while awaiting the inspector's decision [Para 117][Para 120]. Because the labour-dispute department and the Labour Minister had already approved the dismissals on 9 June and 26 September 2006, the two women lost the extra protection attaching to their union office and the case fell to be decided as an ordinary dismissal [Para 121][Para 129][Para 133]. The panel still held it had jurisdiction, since Article 293 does not remove the Council's power to review a union leader's dismissal [Para 131][Para 132]. On the facts, the two women had notified the company of their Thailand seminar and the cards were punched by a relative without their knowledge, so they lacked any intent to defraud; at most they were negligent in entrusting their cards to a husband [Para 143][Para 145][Para 147][Para 152]. The misconduct was therefore not "serious" under Article 83, and dismissal was disproportionate to the fault under Article 27 [Para 151][Para 159][Para 161][Para 162]. Their contracts had run about six years and had become indefinite-duration, so the company could terminate only under Article 74 with valid grounds, which it lacked [Para 165][Para 167][Para 174][Para 175][Para 178][Para 179]. Reinstatement was nevertheless refused and the company was ordered to pay the statutory compensation instead: two months' notice pay under Articles 75 and 77, 15 days' wages and allowances per year of service under Article 89, damages equal to the dismissal indemnity under Article 91, and any unused annual leave under Article 167 [Para 181][Para 182][Para 183][Para 185][Para 192][Para 193].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The 50% maternity wage is 50% of average total monthly wages over the last 12 months, counting seniority pay and attendance bonus, but the law does not add a further 100% of those benefits or annual-leave pay for the leave period [Para 70][Para 72][Para 186].
An employer with at least 100 women workers must provide a breastfeeding room and nursery from the woman's return to work, and the outside-nursery allowance covers children over 18 months [Para 100][Para 102][Para 191].
Where the labour inspector has already approved a union leader's dismissal, the leader loses the added protection of union office and the dismissal is judged as an ordinary one; even so, the sanction must be proportionate to the fault under Article 27, and an indefinite-duration contract may be terminated only for valid grounds under Article 74 [Para 129][Para 133][Para 162][Para 179].
**Obiter dicta:** Reinstatement is discretionary; unlawful dismissal may be compensated in money rather than by return to work [Para 192][Para 193].
