# Award No. 81/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង អ៉ី (Arbitration Council, 11 October 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators លី តៃ សេង (employer side), អាន វីរៈ (worker side), គង់ ផល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140728

## 1. Facts
On 6 September 2006 the workers of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង អ៉ី, represented by សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ and its factory branch សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ រោងចក្រ ហុង អ៉ី, filed 16 demands; conciliation settled 15, leaving one: that the company reinstate its union president and vice-president, both dismissed on 3 July 2006 [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11][Para 20]. Both had indefinite contracts; the president had worked from 2 April 2006 at $40–60 a month and the vice-president from 14 December 2005 at $70–90 a month [Para 23][Para 25][Para 32][Para 34]. The workers said the two were elected at a 30 June 2006 ballot of more than 40 workers, the president with 32 votes and the vice-president with 6 [Para 40][Para 41]. The ballot was held outside the factory, neither the company nor the Labour Inspectorate was notified, and the union had not applied for registration [Para 42]. The company said it had no union and knew of none [Para 39]. The hearing was held on 3 October 2006 [Para 9].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Jurisdiction) Whether the dismissal of two union leaders out of 400 workers is a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law, so that the Council has jurisdiction to order their reinstatement? [Para 44][Para 46]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ and សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ រោងចក្រ ហុង អ៉ី submitted that the two had been elected union president and vice-president by more than 40 workers and should be taken back [Para 40][Para 41]. The president said he had argued with the Chinese manager on 3 July 2006 after being refused wages and bonus for two days' sick leave [Para 28][Para 29]; the vice-president said he was dismissed without valid reason [Para 35].
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ហុង អ៉ី said the factory had no union and had not been told of one [Para 39]. It dismissed the president for improper language and an arrogant attitude towards its Chinese manager [Para 27], and the vice-president because he was the president's friend and the manager disliked the president [Para 36]. It offered statutory payments: 7 days' notice pay, 15 days' annual leave and the last month's wage to the president [Para 30], and 15 days' notice pay, 15 days' sick pay, 15 days' dismissal compensation, 15 days' annual leave and the final wage to the vice-president [Para 37].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the reinstatement claim for want of jurisdiction over a collective dispute [Para 68].
The panel first asked whether it had jurisdiction at all [Para 44], noting that it ordinarily follows the Labour Inspector's and the Minister's classification of a dispute absent a clear reason to differ [Para 45]. It set out Article 302 and its three conditions: several workers against an employer, a subject concerning working conditions or professional organisation rights, and a capacity to make the enterprise run badly or disturb social peace [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]. Conditions (a) and (b) were satisfied because two workers had filed over working conditions and employment relations [Para 51][Para 52]. Condition (c) failed: two workers out of 400 were a very small number, were not key skilled staff, had no support from the rest of the workforce, and offered no evidence that the dispute could obstruct the factory or disturb social peace [Para 57][Para 58]. The panel followed its awards in cases 77/06, 20/05 and 57/06 [Para 53][Para 56][Para 59]. It also found the union-election evidence insufficient, since only an attendance list was produced and no minutes or vote tally showing the leadership result [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63]. Holding the dispute individual rather than collective, it found it should proceed under Article 301, and because Chapter 12 gives the Council power only over collective disputes, it declined to consider the claim [Para 64][Para 65][Para 67].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim by two dismissed union leaders in a factory of several hundred workers is not a collective labour dispute where their election was never notified to the employer or the Labour Inspectorate and never registered, they are not key staff, and their departure cannot obstruct the enterprise or disturb social peace; the Council then has no jurisdiction and the claim must be brought as an individual dispute under Article 301 [Para 57][Para 64][Para 65].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it normally defers to the Labour Inspector's and the Minister's view of whether a dispute is individual or collective [Para 45], and that an attendee list alone cannot prove the result of a union leadership election [Para 63].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The scan is heavily degraded. The panel's jurisdiction question is broken by stray Latin characters and the sentence at paragraph 66 is unreadable where jurisdiction is denied [Para 44][Para 66]. The reasoning quotes the earlier 77/06 passage verbatim, so "five workers" and "about 5,000 employees" survive in a case about two workers of 400 [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57]. A stray numeral follows the employee count [Para 54]; the union leaders' names lose a final letter in the party list and the reasons [Para 15][Para 23]; and the employer's name appears as "ហុង អ៉ី", "ហុង អី" and "ហុង អ៊ី" in different places [Para 1][Para 15][Para 19]. The statute reference in the jurisdiction paragraph is garbled [Para 5], and the finality clause records this award as binding, unlike the non-binding form used in the other awards of the same series [Para 69].
