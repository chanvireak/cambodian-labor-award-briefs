# Award No. 73/06 — F.I. Cambodia Fashion Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេហ្វ៉ាយ ខេមបូឌា ហ្វា ស្ហិន លីមីធីត)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Chiv Phirom, employer-appointed; Liv Sovanna, worker-appointed; Pen Bun Chhea, chair) · heard as non-binding award, 18 September 2006 · Doc ID 140722

## 1. Facts

F.I. Cambodia Fashion Co., Ltd (ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេហ្វ៉ាយ ខេមបូឌា ហ្វា ស្ហិន លីមីធីត) operates a garment factory at house 1A, Norodom Boulevard, Tonle Basak, Chamkar Morn, Phnom Penh, and employs 600 workers. [Para 1][Para 18] The claimants are the Federation of Independent Unions of Cambodia and the Independent Union of Workers of Cambodia at the F.I. factory (សហព័ន្ឋ សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ កម្ពុជា និង សហជីព ឯករាជ្យ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា នៃ រោងចក្រ អេហ្វ៉ាយ). [Para 2]

After a strike on 27-28 July 2006 the company dismissed 15 workers — 12 in the ironing section and 3 in trimming — saying they worked carelessly, slowed production and caused bottlenecks. [Para 19][Para 83] The company also stated that 10 of the 15 were still on probation, that the other 5 were full-status workers who could be dismissed on payment of statutory compensation, and that a buyer had written to complain about falling quality in ironing after the 15 were recruited. [Para 20][Para 21]

The dispute went to conciliation, which settled nothing on the single demand [Para 10][Para 14], and the Labour Dispute Department referred it by report No. 1190 dated 23 August 2006 [Para 6][Para 17]. Hearings were held on 29 August and 12 September 2006 [Para 8][Para 9][Para 12], and the award issued on 18 September 2006 [Para 1].

The company admitted recruiting 12 new workers during the strike to work nights after the dismissed 15 finished at 05:00 pm, and said it still employed those 12 because they performed well. [Para 23][Para 25] It also said the 15 had been recruited as probationers at USD 40 a month [Para 26], but the workers said they were never told their contract type and never signed a contract, only thumbprinting the top of the application form [Para 27][Para 29]. The company conceded that it filled in the probation clause itself and sometimes forgot to tell the workers about it [Para 30][Para 31], and that it never issued a warning to any of the 15 [Para 32].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. Whether the dismissal of the 15 ironing and trimming workers was lawful, and whether the company must take them back? [Para 2][Para 45]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** the 15 were ordinary workers; the company had replaced them with new recruits and used casual night labour during the strike, so the dismissals were a pretext and they should be reinstated. [Para 2][Para 22] They asked only for reinstatement and made no claim for statutory termination payments. [Para 71]

**Employer (respondent):** there was no work for the 15 and they had worked without care; 10 were probationers who could be dismissed without reason or warning, and the 5 full-status workers could be dismissed with statutory payment. [Para 3][Para 21] It relied on its evaluation of their output from recruitment to dismissal and on the buyer's complaint about ironing quality. [Para 20][Para 21]

## 4. Award and reasoning

The panel first examined how the contracts had been formed. Article 65 requires the parties' agreement, and Article 67(4) requires the employer to explain the essential terms, including the probation period, before the contract is made. [Para 47][Para 53] Here the company had the workers fill in an application form at home, filled in the probation column itself, and did not tell them what it had written. [Para 54][Para 55] The panel held that this did not create a written contract, because the workers never knew or signed its terms beyond a thumbprint on the application form. [Para 57][Para 59]

Article 67(7) requires a fixed-duration contract to be in writing and provides that an unwritten one becomes an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 58][Para 59] The panel therefore treated the 15 as holding oral, indefinite-duration contracts. [Para 57][Para 60]

On probation, Article 68 caps probation at three months for employees, two months for skilled workers and one month for ordinary workers. [Para 63][Para 64] The panel found the 15 were ordinary ironing and trimming workers, so any probation was limited to one month and had to be recorded in writing; a job application form could not serve as the contract. [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67] Because the company filled in the probation period without telling them, the panel held they had no probation at all and their contracts were indefinite-term. [Para 68][Para 69][Para 81]

Article 74 permits dismissal of an indefinite-term contract only with written notice and a valid reason relating to the worker's capacity or conduct or the needs of the enterprise. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 77] Performance shortcomings of the kind alleged are, at most, a disciplinary fault, not serious misconduct under Article 83 or Article 12 of the internal rules. [Para 82][Para 84][Para 85] A disciplinary fault becomes serious only after repeated warnings that the worker has failed to heed. [Para 88] The company issued no warning at all. [Para 102]

**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to take back all 15 workers and to pay them back wages from the date of dismissal, the reinstatement and payment to take effect from the date the award becomes enforceable. [Para 103][Para 104] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection. [Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An employer that fills in a probation clause without informing the worker, on a form the worker never signed as a contract, creates no probation and no written contract; the engagement is an indefinite-duration oral contract. [Para 55][Para 57][Para 69] Poor output is a disciplinary fault, not serious misconduct, and dismissal for it is unlawful unless the employer has warned the worker repeatedly and the worker has not improved. [Para 84][Para 88]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The OCR repeatedly corrupts the number of dismissed workers, rendering the group at one point as ten and elsewhere as fifty, and in the reasoning inserts a meaningless year, so the printed numerals cannot be relied on. [Para 21][Para 40][Para 102] It also garbles the panel's name in the reasoning section and renders the employer's name with inconsistent Khmer transliterations of "F.I." [Para 1][Para 45]
