# Award No. 93/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី (Ever Green Garment Co Ltd) (Arbitration Council, 17 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី, លីវ សុវណ្ណា, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈា) · Doc ID 140737

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី (Ever Green Garment Co Ltd) operates at National Road 5, Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employing about 850 workers [Para 1][Para 27]. The claimant is សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ រោងចក្រ អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គា មិន [Para 2]. Two demands remained unsettled after conciliation: reinstatement of Ms S, a union activist whose contract the company did not renew, and a general demand that the company stop ending fixed-duration contracts of workers without serious misconduct [Para 2][Para 9][Para 16][Para 20]. Ms S began work on 22 May 2000, signed a six-month fixed-duration contract in 2001, and had it renewed repeatedly until the company gave notice on 12 September 2006 and stopped her work on 20 September 2006 [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 33]. By then her total service was nearly six years [Para 32][Para 74].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the dismissal of Ms S was union discrimination and harassment, and whether she should be reinstated? [Para 28][Para 58]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must stop terminating the fixed-duration contracts of workers who have committed no serious misconduct under Article 83? [Para 9][Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimants), សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ រោងចក្រ អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គា មិន:** the company targeted Ms S for her union activity and must take her back [Para 2][Para 56]. On the second issue, the parties had agreed on 19 February 2004 that contracts of workers with two years' service would be kept, and on 31 August 2006 that one-year contracts would be used, with termination only for misconduct under Article 83 and the internal rules [Para 36][Para 45][Para 51][Para 52].
**Employer (respondent), ក្រុមហ៊ុន អេវើ គ្រីន ហ្គាមិន ខូអិលធីឌី:** it did not dismiss Ms S but simply did not renew her six-month fixed-duration contract, and there was no discrimination [Para 3][Para 5][Para 57][Para 71]. It agreed to one-year contracts but reserved the right to decide whether to renew at expiry [Para 53][Para 55][Para 110].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to take Ms S back and pay her salary and benefits from the date the order took effect [Para 119], but rejected the workers' demand to bar termination of fixed-duration contracts for workers without serious misconduct [Para 120].
On issue 1, the panel found no evidence that the company knew of Ms S's union membership or acted on it, so the discrimination claim failed [Para 61][Para 63][Para 65]. Article 67(2) limits fixed-duration contracts to two years, and renewals that push total service beyond two years convert the contract into an indefinite one [Para 66][Para 70][Para 72][Para 74]. Because Ms S had nearly six years' service, her contract was indefinite [Para 74]. Under Article 74 an indefinite contract may be ended only with written notice and a valid reason tied to the worker's capacity or the enterprise's needs [Para 75][Para 79]. The company gave notice but supplied no valid reason, so the dismissal was unlawful [Para 81][Para 82][Para 96][Para 99].
On issue 2, the panel held that Article 73(1) lets a fixed-duration contract expire by its terms, and that the company may choose whether to renew a one-year contract for workers whose total service stays under two years [Para 112][Para 117]. Only where renewal would push total service past two years does Article 67(2) require the protections of Articles 74 and 83 [Para 118]. It therefore refused to bar non-renewal generally [Para 120].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Repeated renewal of fixed-duration contracts beyond two years creates an indefinite-term contract under Article 67(2), and ending it requires written notice plus a valid reason under Article 74 [Para 70][Para 74][Para 75][Para 82]. A demand to prohibit non-renewal of one-year contracts that keep total service under two years fails, because Article 73(1) allows expiry by its terms [Para 112][Para 117].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that employers can wait for a short contract to expire and then dismiss a union activist lawfully but without protecting the worker, and that only Parliament can correct this [Para 151][Para 152][Para 154].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The operative order is expressly non-binding and becomes final only after eight days without objection [Para 122][Para 123]. The panel split: the employer-appointed arbitrator filed a dissenting annex, so the reasons in Paras 124-155 are his dissent, not the majority's [Para 124][Para 126]. Dates conflict: the notice is dated 12 September 2006 at Para 31 but 12 December 2006 at Para 80, and the dismissal is placed at 20 September 2006 [Para 31][Para 33][Para 80]. Arbitrator names appear in variant and partly illegible forms, and the signature block is corrupted by OCR [Para 1][Para 124][Para 155]. The company name is printed inconsistently as អេវើ គ្រីន, អេវ៉ើ គ្រីន and អេវើ គ្រិន [Para 1][Para 27].
