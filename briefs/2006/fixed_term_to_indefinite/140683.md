# Award No. 09/06 — Grand Diamond City Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 March 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ly Tayseng (employer-chosen), Ann Vireak (worker-chosen), Tang Try (chair) · Doc ID 140683

## 1. Facts
The Worker Union at Grand Diamond City demanded that Grand Diamond City Co., Ltd reinstate 23 workers it described as union leaders and founding members of the union, all dismissed between 18 November 2005 and 1 January 2006 [Para 9][Para 66]. The company refused for 18 of them, saying it was still reviewing evidence of serious misconduct, and offered termination compensation under the Labour Law to the remaining five on the ground that it had not known they were union leaders [Para 10]. Contracts had been made verbally and were of undetermined duration; on 15 January 2006 the company asked workers to sign written contracts of six months [Para 167]. Two of the 23 were reinstated before the hearing, leaving 21 claimants [Para 65]. The union was formed at an election on 15 November 2005 and registered by letter No. 861/MoLVT dated 30 December 2005 [Para 100][Para 132].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the 23 workers who were union leaders and founding members, as demanded in non-conciliation report No. 129/MoLVT? [Para 9][Para 12]
2. (Issue 1, protection test) Whether the workers satisfied the three conditions for special protection under Prakas 305, including whether the union notified the employer of the protected workers by a reliable means? [Para 172][Para 208]
3. (Issue 1, discrimination question) Whether the dismissals constituted discrimination against the union contrary to Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law? [Para 220]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Worker Union at Grand Diamond City:** the dismissed workers were leaders, candidates and founding members protected under Prakas 305; union officers tried repeatedly to hand management an envelope identifying the leaders and election participants, and the company refused it, so the company knew of their status from 13 January 2006; the dismissals were because of union membership [Para 10][Para 210][Para 219].
**Employer (respondent), Grand Diamond City Co., Ltd:** it received no notification of the election, the candidates, the founding members or the membership, did not know who was a union member, and for 18 of the workers was reviewing evidence of serious misconduct [Para 10][Para 209][Para 222].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate the workers in List No. 4 (those who voted in the 15 November 2005 union election) once the award takes effect, and the demand for reinstatement of the workers in List No. 5 and of one further worker was rejected; no compensation was ordered because none was claimed [Para 228][Para 229].

On protection, the panel applied Article 293 of the Labour Law, which bars dismissal of a worker delegate without prior authorisation of the Labour Inspector, and paragraphs 3 and 4 of Prakas 305, which extend the same protection to candidates for union leadership from 45 days before the election and to founding members and voluntary members for at least 30 days after registration [Para 168][Para 170]. Following Award 50/05 — Fortune, it required three conditions: the worker falls within Prakas 305, the dismissal falls inside the protection period, and the employer was notified of the protected identities by a reliable means [Para 172].

Lists No. 4 and No. 5 both concerned workers dismissed inside the window; the union president was dismissed three days after the election, the others during registration or within 30 days of the 30 December 2005 certificate, so the second condition was met for those who were members [Para 207]. On the third condition, the panel read Prakas 305 as prescribing no particular means, its purpose being only that the company learn of the panel of leaders [Para 211]. An envelope containing the election minutes, the leaders' names and the candidates' list was handed to management on 25 November 2005, refused and returned unopened the same afternoon, and the officials were dismissed that day; the panel held that this was a reliable attempt at notification and that the employer's failure to open the envelope could not count against the union [Para 213][Para 214][Para 215].

List No. 5 workers produced only their own testimony, with no document certifying membership or the date of joining, so they were not shown to be members during registration and were not protected [Para 202][Para 203]. The dismissal of the remaining individual, who said he joined after registration and after his dismissal, was likewise outside the protection [Para 205].

On discrimination, Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law forbid an employer to take union membership or activity into account in termination and disciplinary decisions [Para 221]. The panel found no evidence that List No. 5 workers were members when dismissed, and noted that the worker party never sent the identities of its members either to the company or to the Council, so the discrimination claim failed [Para 222][Para 223]. The panel recorded that the company dismissed workers without any valid reason and without prior notice, which would entitle them to damages and one month's wage in lieu of notice, but declined to decide that point because only reinstatement had been demanded [Para 227][Para 228].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union hands management an envelope identifying its leaders, election candidates and participants, and management refuses it or returns it unopened, the union has notified the employer by a reliable means within Prakas 305; the employer's decision not to open the envelope does not defeat the protection, and a dismissal without prior Labour Inspector authorisation requires reinstatement [Para 214][Para 216].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that the dismissed workers, having been terminated without valid reason or notice, would have been entitled to damages and one month's wage in lieu of notice had they claimed it [Para 228]; and its note that non-union workers had confessed to the misconduct under investigation [Para 167].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR duplicates several lines (the passage on the unopened envelope and notification appears twice) and splits numbered lists, so List No. 2 loses most of its item numbers and the names run together across lines [Para 215]. The employer is printed both as "Grand Diamond City Co., Ltd" and "Grand Diamond City Private Limited Company", and the province as "Banteay Meanchey", "Meanchhey" and "Menchey" [Para 41]. One manager appears under two variants of her name, one of them the fuller form in a parenthetical [Para 152]. A worker's surname is spelled two ways across the lists [Para 177][Para 98]. The demand speaks of 23 workers while the reasons speak of 21 after two reinstatements, and 18 remaining under misconduct review plus five offered compensation [Para 65][Para 10].
