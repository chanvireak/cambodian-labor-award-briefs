# Award No. 53/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ហុង ម៉ី អ៉ីម និង អេច (Arbitration Council, 28 July 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · សេង វូច ហ៊ុន, អន ណន, គង់ ផល្លៈ (president) · Doc ID 140709

## 1. Facts
The employer operated a garment factory in Phnom Penh with about 250 workers [Para 24]. Workers passed a two-month training period and a two-month probation before becoming regular workers, on probation wages of USD 40–75 per month [Para 25] [Para 26]. The employer's internal rules had been signed and filed with the Ministry of Labour but were not yet officially registered [Para 27]. In May 2006 the employer selected worker representatives with Ministry of Labour participation; Mr Y.K. and Mr S.S.L. were elected, while Ms C.M. stood but was not elected [Para 28] [Para 29] [Para 30]. On 5 July 2006 workers elected union leadership without notifying the employer; on 6 July they notified the employer of union formation, and on 7 July applied to register the union [Para 31] [Para 32] [Para 33]. On 6 July 2006 the employer orally notified Mr Y.K., Mr S.S.L., Mr Y.R. and Ms C.M. that their employment would end [Para 34]. On 7 July 2006 the workers struck after learning their representatives had been stopped from work, and the employer wrote to the labour inspector seeking to terminate the four, citing Article 82 of the Labour Law and clause 4 of the 13 May 2006 contracts [Para 36] [Para 37] [Para 38]. On 10 July 2006 the panel ordered the strike stopped and a return to work on 11 July; all workers returned except the four in dispute, whom the employer refused entry [Para 13] [Para 14] [Para 39]. The unresolved issue was referred to the Arbitration Council on 10 July 2006 [Para 12]. The hearing was held on 12 July 2006, with both parties present; further conciliation failed [Para 15] [Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr Y.K., whose contract was found to be of indefinite duration? [Para 97]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must reinstate Mr Y.R., Mr S.S.L. and Ms C.M., or whether their probationary fixed-term contracts simply expired without renewal? [Para 156]
3. (Issue 3) Whether Article 293 protection for worker representatives and union officials required labour-inspector approval for the ending of Mr Y.R.'s and Mr S.S.L.'s contracts? [Para 164]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the employer must pay wages and benefits to the workers for the period they were prevented from working before their probation ended? [Para 176]
5. Whether the dismissal of Mr Y.K. was lawful, given the absence of written notice and of proven serious fault? [Para 103]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The worker side argued that the four were excluded after the employer learned of union formation [Para 40]. It maintained that Mr Y.K. had become a regular worker after three months, that he sought to return but was refused entry, and that he had been barred from the factory premises [Para 47] [Para 53] [Para 100]. It asserted that Mr Y.R. and Mr S.S.L. were stopped during their fixed-term contracts without reason [Para 126]. The employer refused to take the four back, alleging they instigated the strike and threatened other workers not to work overtime, and that all four were probationary workers whose contracts would not be continued [Para 4] [Para 5]. It denied knowing they were union leaders or members [Para 41]. It first said it had not dismissed Mr Y.K., then stated at the hearing that it had decided to dismiss him even without fault [Para 98] [Para 102] [Para 114]. It relied on Article 82 and clause 4 of the 13 May 2006 contracts [Para 38]. It alleged incitement but admitted it could not produce evidence or witnesses for fear of harm to informants [Para 51]. It said it was dissatisfied with the performance of Mr Y.R. and Mr S.S.L. but could not give reasons when asked by the panel [Para 68] [Para 69] [Para 95] [Para 96]. For Ms C.M., it said it had given notice on 6 July, before probation expiry, and had not dismissed her [Para 179] [Para 180]. Neither side produced clear evidence on the original allegations [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted: the employer was ordered to reinstate Mr Y.K. with wages and benefits from 10 July 2006 until the award became effective; the reinstatement claims for Mr Y.R., Mr S.S.L. and Ms C.M. were dismissed; the employer was ordered to pay wages and benefits to Mr Y.R. and Mr S.S.L. from 10 to 13 July 2006 and to Ms C.M. from 10 to 22 July 2006 [Para 188] [Para 189] [Para 190] [Para 191].

F. M. Y.K., the panel found his contract of indefinite duration because it carried no end date, and that he had become a full worker after probation [Para 48] [Para 97]. The employer admitted he was no longer a probationer on 7 July 2006 and that it gave no written notice to him [Para 49] [Para 50]. Article 74 requires prior written notice and a valid reason relating to the worker's conduct or capacity or the needs of the enterprise [Para 104] [Para 106] [Para 107]. Article 75 requires seven days' notice for service of less than six months [Para 111]. The employer produced no proof of serious misconduct or operational necessity, and ultimately dismissed him even without fault [Para 113] [Para 114]. The dismissal was therefore unlawful [Para 117]. Under paragraph 34 of Prakas No. 099 of 2004, the panel ordered reinstatement with full compensation [Para 118] [Para 121].

F. M. Y.R. and Mr S.S.L., the panel held the two-month probation lawful for skilled workers under Article 68 [Para 129] [Para 132]. Article 82 does not require notice during probation, and a 1997 notice requiring seven days' notice for probationers was invalidated by the current Labour Law [Para 133] [Para 138] [Para 139]. Probation is a fixed-term contract; the employer's non-renewal after expiry was not a dismissal [Para 152] [Para 155] [Para 156]. Article 293 and Prakas No. 305 protections do not apply where a contract expires by effluxion rather than dismissal [Para 164] [Para 165] [Para 166] [Para 168]. Because the workers remained employed until 13 July 2006 and were refused entry from 10 July, they were entitled to wages and benefits for that period [Para 174] [Para 176]. The same reasoning applied to Ms C.M., whose probation ran to 22 July 2006, giving wages and benefits from 10 to 22 July 2006 but no reinstatement [Para 177] [Para 186] [Para 187].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer terminating an indefinite contract must give prior written notice and prove reasonable grounds; unsupported allegations and an admission of dismissal without fault make the termination unlawful [Para 105] [Para 106] [Para 113] [Para 114]. A probation agreement is a fixed-term contract; where it validly expires, non-renewal is not a dismissal and does not require labour-inspector approval under Article 293 [Para 155] [Para 166] [Para 168]. A worker prevented from working before the valid end of the contract remains entitled to wages and benefits for that period [Para 176] [Para 187].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel questioned whether Mr S.S.L. had sufficient qualities to be a worker representative but did not decide the point, because both parties accepted his status and the election involved the Ministry of Labour [Para 161] [Para 162].
