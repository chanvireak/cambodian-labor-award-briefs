# Award No. 49/06 — Cambodia Sport Wear M.F.L. Ltd. and Sou Pheach (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ស្ពត វែរ អិម អេហ្វ ហ្សី អេល ធីឌី និង ស៊ូ ភើថិច)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators · Doc ID 140708 · award dated 12 July 2006

## 1. Facts

Cambodia Sport Wear M.F.L. Ltd. and Sou Pheach (ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខេមបូឌា ស្ពត វែរ អិម អេហ្វ ហ្សី អេល ធីឌី និង ស៊ូ ភើថិច) operated a garment factory on National Road No. 2, Chak Angre Krom, Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employing about 5,250 workers [Paras 1, 11]. The worker party was the National Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ជាតិ កម្មករ កម្ពុជា), represented by its president and officials [Para 2]. The panel was Kaow Thach, chosen by the employer; An Nan, chosen by the workers; and Ang Eng Thong, president chosen by the two arbitrators [Para 1].

On 25 May 2006 the federation notified a strike at the factory [Para 49]. On 1 June 2006 the company suspended nine union leaders and worker representatives [Para 54], and workers struck from 1 to 8 June 2006 [Para 52]. On 7 June 2006 the company dismissed 23 workers [Para 55]. On 8 June 2006 the Phnom Penh Municipal Court issued an order requiring all workers to return to work temporarily pending judgment [Para 57]. On 27 June 2006 the Labour Disputes Department issued letters No. 899 and No. 900 refusing the company permission to dismiss the 23 workers and to suspend the nine representatives, on the ground that both measures were contrary to the Labour Law [Paras 58–59]. The non-conciliation report No. 860 K.B.V/A.K./V.K. was dated 21 June 2006 [Para 9], and the hearing was held on 30 June 2006 [Para 11]. By the hearing all striking workers had been allowed back except the nine representatives and 23 workers [Para 70].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must take back the nine union leaders and worker representatives and the 23 workers it suspended or dismissed? [Para 2]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must comply with the Arbitration Council's award in case 30/06-Sport Wear? [Para 4]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must comply with the Phnom Penh Municipal Court order of 8 June 2006? [Para 6]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The workers demanded that the company take back the nine union leaders and worker representatives and the 23 other workers [Para 2]. They also demanded that the company comply with the Council's award in case 30/06 and with the Phnom Penh Municipal Court order [Paras 4, 6]. They denied inciting the strike [Para 62].

**Employer (respondent):** The company refused to take the workers back, claiming they had caused it substantial loss because production was disrupted by the strike they joined and incited [Para 72]. It suspended the nine representatives for allegedly inciting workers with loudspeakers, which it treated as serious misconduct [Para 86], and dismissed the 23 for improper conduct during the strike [Para 74]. It said it had already complied with the Council's award [Para 5] and that it suspended the representatives before the court issued its order [Para 7].

## 4. Award and reasoning

On issue 1, the panel applied Article 333 of the Labour Law, which prohibits any sanction against a worker for participating in a strike [Para 75], and held that participation in a lawful strike cannot be treated as misconduct [Para 75]. It noted that even in an unlawful strike every disciplinary measure must be proportionate to the fault under Article 27 [Para 76], and that an indefinite suspension is not a proportionate sanction for workers who joined a strike [Para 77]. It found the strike lawful: the parties had exhausted conciliation and arbitration, and notice was given seven days in advance under Article 324 [Paras 79–82]. It found no violence or destruction; the employer itself admitted at the hearing that the strike was non-violent and caused no damage [Paras 63, 88–89], and the photographs showed only one of the 23 workers walking behind others, insufficient to prove misconduct under Article 330 [Paras 90–92]. The Labour Disputes Department had already refused both the dismissals and the suspensions as unlawful [Paras 93–94]. The panel therefore ordered reinstatement of the nine representatives and the 23 workers with all benefits from the date of suspension or dismissal until reinstatement [Paras 95, 108].

On issue 2, the panel found that its award in case 30/06 was non-binding, that the company had appealed it on 23 May 2006 [Para 48], and that case 45/06 had been opened and then closed by award 45/06 [Paras 66–68, 97]. Applying the principle of res judicata, it refused to decide the same dispute again [Paras 98–101, 109].

On issue 3, the panel held that it is an out-of-court labour dispute body and has no power to enforce court judgments; Article 312 of the Labour Law and Article 40 of Prakas No. 99/04 limit it to interpreting and applying legal provisions and collective agreements [Paras 103–107]. It therefore refused the demand to enforce the Municipal Court order [Paras 106–107, 110].

**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and benefits were ordered for the nine representatives and 23 workers; the demands to enforce award 30/06 and the court order were refused.

## 5. Ratio decidendi

An employer may not discipline a worker for taking part in a lawful strike, and even where a strike is unlawful any sanction must be proportionate to the fault; indefinite suspension is disproportionate [Paras 75–77]. A strike is lawful once conciliation and arbitration are exhausted and seven days' notice is given under Article 324 [Paras 79–82]. The Council will not re-decide a dispute already concluded by a prior award, and it has no jurisdiction to enforce court judgments [Paras 98–101, 105–107].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified.
