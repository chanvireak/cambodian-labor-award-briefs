# Award No. 43/06 — Vin Cam Corp (Arbitration Council, 23 June 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា for the employer party, អន ណន for the worker party, ញាន សុមុន្នីន្ទ chair) · Doc ID 140702

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន, a garment factory at Building 62R, Tuol Sangke, Russey Keo, with about 400 workers, hired Mr P as a cutter on 3 February 2006 on a two-month probation contract; when the probation ended the company let him continue but signed no new written contract, so his engagement became one of indefinite duration. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 30][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38] He cut pattern No. 0146 wrongly, ruining cloth for nearly one thousand swimsuit sets, some of it repairable at a smaller size. [Para 39][Para 40] The company decided on 11 May 2006 to dismiss him, telling him orally, and the written notice was never properly made out or served on him; when he came back from leave on 18 May 2006 the guards refused him entry. [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44] He had been elected president of សហជីព លើកស្ទួយ សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ រោងចក្រ វីន ខេម, an affiliate of សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព លើកស្ទួយ សិទ្ធិ កម្មករ, at an election of 4 May 2006; the union was not yet registered with the Ministry. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 32][Para 59]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether Mr P enjoys the protection given to a union leader or founder during the election and registration period, so that his dismissal required the consent of the labour inspector? [Para 2][Para 47]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Mr P was elected union president on 4 May 2006, the union notified the company by letter left with the factory guard on 8 May 2006, and he must be taken back and work normally. [Para 2][Para 33]
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន denies receiving any notification letter, says there was a change of guard that day and no witness was produced, and dismissed Mr P for serious fault under its internal rules because his cutting error cost it a great deal. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 45][Para 72]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel converted the dismissal into a first written warning for a medium-grade fault, recorded in his personal file, and ordered the company to take Mr P back and pay him wages from 18 May 2006 until his return, payable with the July 2006 wages. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]

On the protection question the panel set out Article 12, which forbids using union membership or activity as a ground for a hiring, disciplinary or termination decision, and Article 293, which allows the dismissal of a staff delegate or a candidate only with the labour inspector's consent, failing which the dismissal is void and the worker must be reinstated. [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50] Prakas No. 305 SK.B.Y. of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to candidates in a union leadership election for 45 days before and 45 days after the ballot, and to founders and voluntary members, on condition that the union notifies the employer of the protected names by any reliable means, following awards 02/04 (Hotel Cambodiana), 50/05 and 64/05. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 56][Para 58] The panel found Mr P was a candidate and elected president within 45 days of the election, satisfying the first two conditions, but that the third failed: the only evidence was delivery to a guard on 8 May 2006, which the company denied, and with no supporting witness the notification was not proved. [Para 61][Para 62][Para 63]

On proportionality the panel noted that Article 74 governs termination of an indefinite contract, and Article 27 requires the penalty to match the fault. [Para 64][Para 67] Article 11 of the internal rules of 22 February 2006 lists the faults treated as serious and warranting dismissal, and negligent damage to company property is not among them; the rules classify negligent damage as a medium fault, punished first by a written warning entered in the personal file, then by suspension without pay of no more than seven days, and only at the third occurrence by dismissal. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71] His careless cutting was a medium fault, so dismissal exceeded the scale and was disproportionate; the panel held the company should have allowed him in on 18 May 2006 and could at most issue a first written warning. [Para 74][Para 75][Para 76]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Article 293 protection of a union leader or candidate depends on the union having notified the employer of the protected names by reliable means; where that notification is not proved, a dismissal without the labour inspector's consent is not void on that ground. The dismissal may still be measured against Article 27 and the registered internal rules: where those rules treat negligent damage to property as a medium fault carrying a graduated response, a first occurrence cannot be punished by termination, and the worker must be reinstated with wages from the date he presented himself for work. [Para 58][Para 63][Para 70][Para 76][Para 78]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's reference to award 18/04, that an employer may not impose a penalty for which its internal rules provide no basis, is a general remark on disciplinary power not needed for the order, since the rules here did provide a basis. [Para 68]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The union-side document list includes a fingerprint sheet submitted to the secretariat on 22 June 2006, one day before the award, which the panel expressly says it did not consider [Para 25].
- The employer's name is printed at least four ways (វីន ខេម ខប ភើរេសិន, វីន ខេម, វីន ខូច, វីន ខេ), and the factory is placed in Toul Kork at [Para 30] while the parties' address is Russey Keo [Para 1].
- The Prakas date is garbled as "ថ្ងៃទី ២២ ខែ វិច្ឆិកា ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០០១" at [Para 51], and the Prakas and case numbers in the reasoning are corrupted in places [Para 58].
- The internal-rules date is given as 17 March 2006 in the evidence list [Para 15] but 22 February 2006 in the reasoning [Para 69].
- The operative paragraph at [Para 77] and the reasoning at [Para 75] are partly unreadable, with clauses interleaved; the orders were reconstructed from [Para 74] and [Para 76].
- The second unresolved issue, on 50 per cent wages when there is no work, was withdrawn by the federation at the close of the second hearing and was not decided [Para 3][Para 13][Para 14].
