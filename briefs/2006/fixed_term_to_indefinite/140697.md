# Award No. 32/06 — Thop Vorn Garment Cambodia M&V Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 22 May 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា for the employer party, លីវ សុវណ្ណា for the worker party, តាំង ទ្រី chair) · Doc ID 140697

## 1. Facts
ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន ហ្គាមែន ខេមបូឌា អិម អេវ ជឺ ខូអិលធីឌី, a garment factory on National Road 5 in Sangkat Svay Pak with more than 730 workers, told the Labour Inspectorate it would move to a site some three kilometres away on 25 May 2006. [Para 1][Para 17][Para 19] The plant union សហជីព សាមគ្គីភាព កម្មករ កាត់ដេរ នៃ រោងចក្រ ថប វ៉ន, backed by the federation សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព សាមគ្គី ឯករាជ្យ និង ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ, demanded that the company clear "seniority pay" (ប្រាក់អតីតភាពការងារ) for 94 workers before the move, which at the hearing it defined as the dismissal indemnity of Article 89 of the 1997 Labour Law [Para 2][Para 20]. Both parties agreed that the 83 termination requests collected by the union were never sent to the employer, and that no worker had been dismissed. [Para 25][Para 33] The second demand, on transport to the new site, was settled by agreement during the hearings and was not decided. [Para 13][Para 14]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay the Article 89 dismissal indemnity to the 83 workers before it relocates the factory, when no employment contract has been terminated? [Para 19][Para 27][Para 31]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the relocation is a change of a term of the employment contract that will cause them loss, so the company should terminate their contracts, pay the dismissal indemnity now, and let their seniority start afresh at the new site; if the panel refuses, they will keep working for the same company at the new site. [Para 21][Para 29][Para 40]
**Employer (respondent):** ក្រុមហ៊ុន ថប វ័ន ហ្គាមែន ខេមបូឌា អិម អេវ ជឺ ខូអិលធីឌី has dismissed nobody, has received no termination request, and has arranged transport to the new site as agreed. [Para 22][Para 42][Para 43]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel ordered that the union's and workers' demand for seniority pay (dismissal indemnity) for the 83 workers before the factory move not be considered. [Para 56][Para 57]

The panel first fixed the character of the claim: the parties agreed the contracts were of indefinite duration and that "seniority pay" meant the Article 89 indemnity, so the question was whether any termination had occurred. [Para 20][Para 23][Para 27] Article 89 gives an indemnity where the employer unilaterally terminates the contract, and the panel found as a fact that the employer had dismissed no one; Article 89 could therefore not apply. [Para 32][Para 33][Para 34] Article 90 extends the indemnity to a worker who terminates because of the employer's wrongful conduct, but that too presupposes an actual termination. [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37]

The panel then held it had no power to grant the remedy sought: under Article 65 a labour contract is the contract of its parties, so only a party to it may terminate it, and the union's request for the panel itself to sever the contracts of 83 workers was outside its competence. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46] It followed earlier awards in case 25/06 (SHP), 48/05 (Meathan), 70/05 (Gold Fame) and 76/05 (Global Footwear) on that limit. [Para 47] Because no termination had yet occurred, the compensation claimed was a right contingent on a future event, and the panel recalled its practice of not deciding rights disputes that have not yet arisen, citing cases 10/03 (Zaksintec) and 19/06 (Singhong). [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 55] Only 83 of the 94 workers named in the conciliation demand had in fact applied, so the panel confined itself to those 83. [Para 26][Para 41] Its order was therefore to decline the claim rather than to rule on the merits. [Para 56][Para 57]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for the Article 89 dismissal indemnity cannot be granted before any contract has been terminated: the indemnity arises only on termination by the employer, or on termination by the worker for the employer's wrongful act under Article 90, and because only a party to a labour contract may terminate it under Article 65, a panel cannot itself sever the contracts to trigger payment. A claim for money that depends on a termination not yet occurring is a rights dispute over a future event that the panel will not decide. [Para 33][Para 36][Para 46][Para 54][Para 57]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's observation that a three-kilometre relocation could be treated as a breach of a contract term capable of causing loss to some workers through travel was not relied on, since no termination followed from it. [Para 39][Para 40]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The number of claimants is printed inconsistently: 94 in the conciliation demand [Para 2], 83 in the panel's findings and order [Para 26][Para 57], and a garbled "983" at [Para 50]; the annexed list of 83 names is heavily corrupted and many entries no longer pair a name with an employee number.
- The panel's numbering is corrupted: the single decided issue is labelled "Issue 16" at [Para 19] although the award records only two demands [Para 2][Para 3].
- The arbitrator chosen by the workers is printed លីវ សុវណ្ណា in the panel composition but លវ សុវណ្ណា in the signature block [Para 1][Para 59].
- The employer's name and the factory name appear in several spellings (ថប វ៉ន, ថប វន្ត, ថប វិន) across the award [Para 1][Para 17][Para 18].
- The list of documents received from the employer is cut off mid-entry at [Para 14], so one exhibit cannot be identified.
- The operative paragraph attributes the decision to the "ក្រុមប្រឹក្សា អាជ្ញាធរ កណ្តាល" and describes the order as both a refusal to consider and a rejection of the claim [Para 57].
