# Award No. 28/06 — GDM Enterprise Co., Ltd. (GDM) (Arbitration Council, 24 April 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, An Nan, Kong Phallack as chair) · Doc ID 140694

## 1. Facts
The employer is GDM Enterprise Co., Ltd. (GDM), at Chaom Chao Street, Sangkat Chaom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, represented by its director, an administrator, an assistant administrator and a GMAC representative. [Para 1][Para 3][Para 7]
The worker party is the Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), which refused to select an arbitrator, so the Secretariat selected one by lot under Prakas 99/04; An Nan was drawn. [Para 7][Para 14][Para 15][Para 17]
The collective dispute reached the Council with non-conciliation report No. 510 KKBVIVK dated 5 April 2006, at a time when the workers of GDM were on strike. [Para 12][Para 13]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the panel may continue to settle the collective labour dispute of 28/06-GDM when the worker party has remained on strike after two interim orders to return to work? [Para 20][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, worker party of KYFTU):** The source records no argument by the worker party on the procedural question; its only recorded conduct is the refusal to select an arbitrator and the continuation of the strike after both interim orders. [Para 14][Para 22]
**Employer (respondent, GDM Enterprise Co., Ltd.):** No position of the employer is recorded; the employer appears only through its representatives and the report that its workers were on strike. [Para 3][Para 13]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel ordered "Discontinue the arbitral proceeding of 28/06-GDM". [Para 27]

The panel rested on Article 20 of Prakas No. 99 on the Arbitration Council, which requires the parties, during the arbitration process, to abstain from strikes, lockouts as defined in Article 318 of the Labour Law, and any other action likely to aggravate the situation, and to attend all meetings to which the panel calls them. [Para 23][Para 24]
The panel read that article, as it had in earlier interim orders, to mean that the arbitration process does not continue while the worker party is on strike, and it recalled its own precedents: the Notification of 29 January 2004 in 04/04-MSI, stating that the Council cannot settle or issue an award where the parties strike during proceedings; Interim Order 2 of 22 April 2004 in 18/04-Hotels, reserving the right to terminate proceedings on breach of the order; and point 4 of Interim Order 2 of 24 December 2005 in 77/05-Franco Knitting Garment. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]
The procedural history underpinned the outcome: an interim order of 5 April 2006 ordering the workers back to work, a pre-hearing on 7 April warning that proceedings require the strike to end, a second return-to-work order on 10 April whose clause 4 warned that proceedings would not continue during a strike, and a second pre-hearing on 11 April repeating the warning; the workers nevertheless continued to strike. [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20][Para 22]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Under Article 20 of Prakas 99/04 a strike during arbitration is a bar to the panel's work: while the worker party remains on strike after being ordered back, the panel discontinues the proceeding rather than issuing an award, and the dispute may be taken up again once the strike ends. [Para 23][Para 24][Para 26]
The bar operates on the panel's own procedure and does not depend on any request by the employer, so a refusal to select an arbitrator does not prevent the proceeding from starting but the continuance of a strike does prevent it from ending in an award. [Para 15][Para 27]

**Obiter dicta:** The recital of the 04/04-MSI, 18/04-Hotels and 77/05-Franco Knitting Garment precedents is illustrative of the panel's consistent practice rather than an independent ground of decision. [Para 25][Para 26][Para 27]
