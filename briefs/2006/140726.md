# Award No. 79/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ូ ស៊ូ ស៊ី អេន អេស (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Voo Su Si ENS) (Arbitration Council, 6 October 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators ម៉ារ សំបូរ ណា (employer side), អន ណន (worker side), គង់ ផល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140726

## 1. Facts
On 11 September 2006 about 1,200 workers of ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ូ ស៊ូ ស៊ី អេន អេស (ខេមបូឌា) ខូអិលធីឌី (Voo Su Si ENS), led by the សហជីព សេរី កម្មករ នៃ ព្រះរាជាណាចក្រ កម្ពុជា and the factory's own free union, struck over eight demands [Para 12]. Conciliation on 13 September 2006 settled five of the eight; the three unresolved points came to the Council on 15 September 2006 [Para 13][Para 14]. At the hearing on 19 September 2006 one further point, the third, was settled, leaving issues 1 and 2 [Para 18][Para 19]. The two unresolved demands were that the company take back its union president and deputy, suspended since 9 September 2006, and that it dismiss its general manager [Para 2][Para 29][Para 36]. The employer had asked the Labour Inspector to authorise dismissal of the two union leaders and was awaiting that decision [Para 30][Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the Council has jurisdiction to order the company to reinstate the union president and deputy while the Labour Inspector has not yet decided the company's request to dismiss them? [Para 45]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the Council should order the company to dismiss its general manager for using inappropriate language towards workers? [Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The unions demanded that the company take back the union president and deputy and treat them as ordinary employees [Para 24]. They alleged that the general manager used inappropriate language, made leave requests difficult, including special leave for a parent's funeral, and incited misunderstanding between the union and the workforce [Para 37][Para 41][Para 43].
**Employer (respondent):** The company refused to take the two back because they had committed numerous faults in the factory, including unauthorised absence, disrespect to superiors, improper conduct, incitement of other workers to strike and threats [Para 25][Para 30]. It denied that the general manager had insulted or abused anyone [Para 40][Para 71].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the reinstatement demand and refused the demand to dismiss the general manager [Para 76][Para 77].
On issue 1 the panel asked whether it had jurisdiction while the matter lay within the Labour Inspector's competence [Para 45]. Article 293 of the Labour Law requires the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation before a staff delegate or union candidate is dismissed, and the Inspector had not yet ruled on the company's request [Para 45][Para 34]. The panel held the dispute therefore still fell under the Inspector's authority, to be decided under Article 293, and that the Council could not consider it until that decision was final [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 56]. It followed its award in case 91/04 [Para 53]. On issue 2 the panel applied Article 65 of the Labour Law on the employment contract and Article 22 of Kret 38 of 28 October 1988, under which a contract binds only its parties and only they may end it [Para 58][Para 61][Para 64]. Hiring and dismissing workers is the employer's exclusive contractual right, and the Labour Law does not require dismissal even where a worker commits serious misconduct under Article 83 [Para 65][Para 66]. The panel noted one exception, drawn from award 76/05, where an employee endangers other workers' health and safety [Para 70], but found no evidence that the general manager had struck, insulted or humiliated anyone [Para 71][Para 44]. It therefore held the demand neither correct nor lawful [Para 72][Para 73].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Council will not order an employer to dismiss a worker, because termination is the employer's contractual prerogative and the Labour Law merely permits rather than requires it; and where the worker is a union leader or staff delegate, the Council declines jurisdiction until the Labour Inspector has finally decided the request for authorisation under Article 293 [Para 65][Para 72][Para 56].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it may exceptionally order removal or transfer where the employee's presence endangers other workers' health and safety [Para 70].
