# Award No. 06/06 — M & V International 1 (Arbitration Council, 16 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana for the employer party, An Nan for the worker party, Ang Eng Thong chair) · Doc ID 140678

## 1. Facts
M & V International 1, a company on National Road 2, Chak Angrei Kraum, Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, introduced a policy requiring every worker to take all accrued annual leave between 1 January and 31 March each year — the months in which it closes its accounting and finance system and has too little work. [Para 20][Para 27][Para 48] The company gave 18 days of annual leave in the first and second years and 19 days from the third year onward; the workers, through the Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF) and the Cambodian Industrial Union at the company, demanded leave scheduled by seniority under Article 167 of the Labour Law and one further day for workers with more than three years' service. [Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 31] The dispute reached the panel on two non-conciliation issues after a complaint of 29 December 2005 and conciliation on 5 January 2006. [Para 10][Para 11][Para 12]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company's policy requiring all accrued annual leave to be taken between 1 January and 31 March each year is lawful under Article 167 of the Labour Law, and whether leave must instead be scheduled by seniority? [Para 6][Para 20][Para 51]
2. (Issue 2) Whether Article 166 paragraph 4 of the Labour Law entitles a worker to one additional day of annual leave for every three years of service, so that 19 days accrue in the 4th to 6th years and 20 days from the 7th year? [Para 29][Para 52][Para 55]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a worker acquires the right to annual leave only after one year of service, so a policy forcing leave between January and March is unlawful, harms special leave, and denies workers the chance to take pay instead of leave in busy months like June and July; seniority should add a day at three years and again thereafter. [Para 21][Para 23][Para 25][Para 30]
**Employer (respondent):** the January–March window suits the accounting close and the low season and protects the production line from disruption in June and July; the company already adds a day from the third year, and the Labour Law does not clearly require a further day at six or seven years. [Para 27][Para 28][Para 31][Para 55]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the union's challenge to the January–March leave policy was rejected, but the company was ordered to add one working day of annual leave for every three years of service, giving 19 days in the 4th, 5th and 6th years and 20 days in the 7th, 8th and 9th years and each later three-year block. [Para 85][Para 86]

On the first issue the panel read Article 170 of the Labour Law as placing annual leave at the Khmer New Year in principle unless the parties agree otherwise, and found no such agreement here. [Para 33][Para 34] It held that Article 167 paragraph 1, which acquires the right to leave only after one year of service, does not forbid an employer from granting leave sooner, so a January–March policy is not contrary to the Labour Law. [Para 37][Para 39] Article 2 of the Labour Law, defining the enterprise, was read as giving the employer the right to supervise and direct its workforce, subject to lawfulness and reasonableness; the panel accepted the accounting and low-season justification, following award 28/04-Raffles Grand Hotel. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 51] On special leave it applied Clause 2 of Prakas 267/01 of 11 October 2001, under which special leave may be deducted from annual leave or made up by working. [Para 41][Para 42] On payment in lieu it held that although Article 167 requires compensation for unused accrued leave when a contract ends, Article 166 forbids any agreement by which a worker renounces or waives the right to annual leave, so the union's "pay instead of leave" argument failed. [Para 44][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]

On the second issue the panel found the words "at the rate of one day per three years of service" in Article 166 paragraph 4 unclear and construed them by legal principle, equity and purpose. [Para 53][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58] Seniority, as used in Articles 75, 89 and 95, gives progressively greater rights — longer notice, larger dismissal indemnity, last-in-first-out in lay-offs — and those rights are automatic and cannot be reduced. [Para 60][Para 63][Para 67][Para 72][Para 75][Para 76] The panel therefore adopted a cumulative reading, consistent with awards 51/04-Sam Han, 62/04-E Cent, 68/05-Gold Lid and 75/05-Fortune. [Para 85]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 166 paragraph 4 of the Labour Law adds one working day of annual leave at the completion of each three-year block of service, so that the entitlement rises cumulatively — 19 days in years four to six, 20 in years seven to nine — and that seniority-based entitlement is an automatic right that neither the employer's will nor any agreement may reduce. [Para 54][Para 75][Para 76][Para 85] An employer may schedule accrued annual leave within a fixed window such as January to March under its power to direct the enterprise in Article 2 of the Labour Law, provided the policy is lawful and reasonable; Article 167 paragraph 1 fixes when the right accrues, not when leave may be taken, and so does not invalidate a window that allows leave before a year's service. [Para 37][Para 39][Para 48][Para 49]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's survey of Articles 75, 89 and 95 and its observations on equity and on the law's purpose in motivating long service are support for the interpretive method rather than holdings on those articles. [Para 60][Para 81][Para 84]
