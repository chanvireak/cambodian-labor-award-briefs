# Award No. 30/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខាំបូឌា ស្ពត វែរ អិម អែហ្វ ហ្សឺ អែល ធី ឌី (Cambodia Sport Wear IMF SGL TD) (Arbitration Council, 18 May 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៉ីង សុទ្ធី / Ing Sothy, ទូន ស៊ី ផាន់ / Toun Syphan, ប៉ែន ប៊ុន ឈាន / Pen Bunchhea as chair) · Doc ID 140696

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ខាំបូឌា ស្ពត វែរ អិម អែហ្វ ហ្សឺ អែល ធី ឌី, a garment factory at No. 1533 National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Krom, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, founded in 1997 and employing about 5,250 workers. [Para 1][Para 21]
The worker party is សហជីព ជាតិ នៃ ឧស្សាហកម្ម វាយនភ័ណ្ឌ កាត់ដេរ កម្ពុជា (National Union of the Textile and Garment Industry of Cambodia) at the company, represented by federation officers and worker delegates. [Para 1][Para 3]
Roughly 400 workers work in the finishing section, inspecting garments for defects and sending them back for correction; six demands were lodged on 3 March 2006, five were conciliated on 27 March 2006, and only the piece-rate demand reached the panel. [Para 11][Para 12][Para 21][Para 22]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the company must pay the piece rate each time it requires finishing-section workers to re-inspect and correct defective products, and whether its new piece-rate calculation must guarantee at least the minimum wage? [Para 44][Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, ភាគី កម្មករ និយោជិត):** The company should return to the old practice of paying a piece rate every time a defective product must be corrected; failing that, it should pay a piece rate to the roughly 400 finishing workers whenever it orders re-correction, as it did before the November 2005 restructuring. [Para 27][Para 28]
**Employer (respondent, ភាគី និយោជក):** The company is entitled to set and change piece rates; it now counts re-correction only once because workers could collude to earn more, and it tops every wage up to the 45 dollar minimum when the piece rate falls short. [Para 32][Para 36]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay a piece rate every time it requires workers to correct products again and to guarantee that workers receive at least the minimum wage fixed by law. [Para 72]

The panel accepted that the employer has the right to fix and revise piece rates, citing 03/05-Fai Ying Dragon. [Para 45] That right is bounded by Article 108 of the Labour Law, which requires a piece rate to let a worker of average skill working normal hours earn at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and by Notification No. 017 of the Ministry, which requires top-up to 45 dollars a month where output falls below that. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 50]
Weighing the evidence, the panel found the company's methodology unsupported: the lawyer gave no specific evidence of how the new rate was set, no worker was chosen for a trial, all workers were tested for one week under the company's own assessment, and the company simply waited for objections before adopting the rate. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63] It found that about 90 percent of workers could not reach the 45 dollar minimum under the new rate, that the 03/05 rate was too low, and that the top-up did not cure the breach because Article 108 governs the rate itself. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 59][Para 69]
The panel therefore held the rate inconsistent with Article 108, while confirming the employer's prerogative to change it, provided the rate complies with that article. [Para 69][Para 70]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may set and revise piece rates, but a rate that does not let a worker of average skill earn at least the statutory minimum wage in normal hours is unlawful, and a monthly top-up to the minimum does not cure the defect in the rate itself. [Para 46][Para 69]
Where an employer requires work to be redone, that rework must be paid at the piece rate; an employer cannot count a correction only once in order to depress earnings. [Para 71][Para 72]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's suggestions that the company run a trial period to calibrate the rate, and that it allow all workers of differing skill to test the rate beforehand, were guidance rather than operative orders. [Para 66][Para 68]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The findings conflict on the share of workers unable to reach the minimum: one passage says 55 percent and three others say 90 percent; the brief uses 90 percent, the figure repeated in the reasoning and the conclusion. [Para 35][Para 56][Para 59][Para 69] The worker party's name as printed includes a reference to a different company ("ប្រចាំ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ស្តុតារា"), which does not match the employer named in the case, and the employer's name is spelt several ways, including a "ស្គុត វែរ" form in the ministry report. [Para 1][Para 19][Para 21] The text is heavily damaged by stray OCR fragments ("AIF(", "AN", "MF", "/97") and loses whole words in the reasoning. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 44][Para 65] The arbitrator chosen by the employer is printed one way in the panel list and another in the signature block, and the chair's signature block carries an extra name not found in the panel list. [Para 1][Para 73] The judgment's own case number and the sections of the ministry correspondence are partly unreadable. [Para 7][Para 20]
