# Award No. 92/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន រ៉ូ ស៊ីង ហ្គាមិន លីមីតធីត (Arbitration Council, 08 November 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · អ៉ីង សុទ្លី / អន ណន / គង់ ផល្លៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140736

## 1. Facts
The employer is a Phnom Penh garment company at Toul Sangke with about 4,400 workers. [Para 21] It has one union, which had only just filed for registration on 3 October 2006. [Para 22] [Para 23] A collective complaint was received by the Labour Dispute Department on 7 September 2006. [Para 10] Conciliation settled nine of eleven demands; the two unresolved points were sent to the Council with a non-settlement report dated 10 October 2006, received 11 October. [Para 11] [Para 12] [Para 8] The hearing was held at the Phnom Penh Centre on 20 October 2006; both parties attended, further conciliation failed, and the panel considered party documents but no outside witnesses or experts. [Para 9] [Para 14] [Para 15] [Para 16] The first claim was reimbursement of health examination fees of 10,100 riel per worker; the second was holiday wages of USD 1.73 for piece-rate sewing workers who did not work on national holidays. [Para 2] [Para 4] [Para 45]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must reimburse workers for health examination fees of 10,100 riel, and if so which claimants and what period qualify? [Para 24] [Para 76] [Para 84]
2. (Issue 2) Whether piece-rate sewing workers must receive wages of USD 1.73 for national holidays when they do not work? [Para 45] [Para 102]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The union argued the examination fee was an employer obligation under Prakas No. 09 and Article 47 of the Labour Law, and that "worker" and "employee" mean the same company personnel. [Para 24] [Para 30] It noted most workers paid 10,100 riel themselves after the entry test, while a smaller number had the sum deducted from wages at the factory. [Para 32] [Para 33] It supplied no claimant list despite promising one for 27 October 2006. [Para 31] The employer refused, saying the fee was a hiring condition under Article 1 of its internal regulations, that Prakas No. 09 covers only "employees" not "workers", and that Article 247 of the Labour Law matches its internal rule. [Para 25] [Para 26] [Para 28] It distinguished unskilled manual workers from skilled office employees and produced a list of personnel who declined reimbursement. [Para 29] [Para 43] On holiday pay, the employer paid salaried staff but not piece-rate sewing workers, saying it already paid double the base salary plus output when they work; the internal regulations contain no holiday policy for piece-rate sewers. [Para 46] [Para 47] [Para 52] It accepted the annual ministry notice applies to workers generally. [Para 48] About 100 workers were in piece-rate sections, and four ironing workers at the hearing had taken holiday leave. [Para 49] [Para 51]

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel had jurisdiction under Chapter 12, Part II, Articles 309 to 317 of the 1997 Labour Law and Prakas No. 099. [Para 6] On Issue 1, it read Articles 3 and 5 to mean the Labour Law does not divide "worker" from "employee": "កម្មករនិយោជិត" (workers) and "និយោជិត" (employees) are persons earning wages alike, the former term covering both manual and non-manual work. [Para 54] [Para 56] [Para 58] [Para 61] [Para 62] Although Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 uses "employee" in paragraph 7, and prior awards diverge on whether that Prakas, made under the superseded 1992 law, remains in force, Article 247 of the 1997 Labour Law supplies an adequate independent basis requiring the employer to pay examination costs before admitting workers. [Para 67] [Para 70] [Para 71] [Para 73] As examinations occurred from 1998 through 2006, some paid personally and some deducted, Article 120's three-year limitation from the date of payment applies, leaving only workers examined on 7 September 2003 entitled. [Para 77] [Para 78] [Para 82] [Para 84] On Issue 2, Article 163 entitles daily and piece-rate workers to holiday compensation equal to lost wages, even when they do not work the holiday. [Para 86] [Para 88] [Para 89] Notice No. 017 of 2000 sets a USD 45 monthly minimum, so a piece worker earning above that keeps the surplus, and one below must be topped up; the minimum average daily wage is USD 1.73. [Para 90] [Para 92] [Para 93] [Para 94] Under Article 312's equity power the panel held piece-rate workers must receive at least USD 1.73, the same given to salaried staff. [Para 97] [Para 100] [Para 101]
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The employer was ordered to reimburse the examination fee to workers examined from 7 September 2003 onward, within 15 days of the award, and to pay USD 1.73 to piece-rate workers for national holidays not worked. [Para 103] [Para 104] The award is binding and final immediately upon notification. [Para 105]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Labour Law protections apply to all wage earners regardless of whether they are labelled "worker" or "employee". [Para 58] [Para 61] An employer must bear health examination costs under Article 247 before employment begins, but reimbursement claims are limited to three years from payment under Article 120. [Para 73] [Para 78] Piece-rate workers are entitled to at least the statutory minimum daily wage for national holidays even when they do not work. [Para 89] [Para 102] This panel's reasoning is not binding precedent.
**Obiter dicta:** The panel left open whether Prakas No. 09 retains legal force, since Article 247 supplied an independent basis. [Para 70] [Para 71]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The health-fee amount appears as 10,100 riel in the claim and reasoning [Para 2] [Para 76] but as 100 riel in the issue statement and operative order [Para 24] [Para 103]. The union cites Article 47 while the panel and employer cite Article 247 for the same obligation. [Para 24] [Para 28] Prakas 09 paragraph 7 is described as using only "employee" [Para 67] and elsewhere as requiring payment for "workers/employees". [Para 69] OCR losses include broken brackets around the employer arbitrator's name, duplicated phrases, and garbled words at paragraphs 53, 66, 68 and 106. [Para 53] [Para 66] [Para 68] [Para 106]
