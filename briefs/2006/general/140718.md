# Award No. 67/06 — Nouv Khob (Arbitration Council, 23 August 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphann, worker-appointed; Kong Phalha, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140718

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុនូវ ខប (Nouv Khob), runs a factory on National Road 51 at Cheung Ek village, Lumhach commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, and hired 962 workers; it began operating on 19 April 2006 and reached full activity on 13 May 2006. [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]

On 11 August 2006 the workforce struck over nine demands without giving prior notice to the company or the Kandal provincial labour office. [Para 8] Conciliation on 14 August 2006 settled eight of the nine points; the single unresolved point reached the panel on 16 August 2006. [Para 9]

That point was the demand that the company remove two section heads, អ៊ុក សំអុល (Uk Samol) of packing and ស្រ៊ ស៊ូងីម (Sro Soungeam) of ironing, whom the workers accused of oppressing staff and using abusive language. [Para 2][Para 16] The company refused, saying its director had only just learned of the matter and needed time to investigate. [Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must dismiss the two section heads, អ៊ុក សំអុល and ស្រ៊ ស៊ូងីម, from the factory? [Para 2][Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** A worker witness testified that on 5 July 2006, ill with diarrhoea, she asked the packing head for leave at 15:30 and was refused, so she worked until 18:00 and was put on a drip until 22:00. [Para 20][Para 21] The workers alleged both heads used abusive words such as calling subordinates blind and telling them to eat excrement. [Para 25] Thirty-eight workers thumbprinted a statement that they left the factory at 11:00 on 17 August 2006 because of heavy flooding, not in defiance of the panel's return-to-work order. [Para 12]

**Employer (respondent):** The packing head admitted using inappropriate words but said the workers, especially one worker whose name is redacted, did not respect him as their section head. [Para 26] He denied refusing leave and said he only asked the worker to obtain a certificate from the company doctor, as company rules required, which she never did. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 24] He had completed evaluations and forwarded them to senior management but did not himself decide wage increases. [Para 28][Para 29] The ironing head admitted some abusive words but denied others. [Para 31] The director asked the workers to show understanding and agreed to issue warnings to both if the allegations proved true. [Para 18][Para 19]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused the demand that the employer remove the two section heads from the factory. [Para 49] The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the secretariat. [Para 50]

The panel followed its earlier Case 73/04 (Yen Yu In), which held that workers have no right to order or demand that an employer dismiss any employee unless they can prove the person is dangerous and cannot be retained in the company or factory, and that retaining such a person could create job insecurity. [Para 34] It listed its own Awards 04/03 (Lida), 14/03 (Jushing), 17/03 and 18/03, 06/04, 15/04, 16/04, 32/04, 34/04 and 52/04 as consistent. [Para 35]

On the evidence, the workers produced no concrete proof that the two heads were dangerous or would create insecurity at work; their case rested only on dissatisfaction with the supervisors' language. [Para 36] Article 65 of the Labour Law 1997 provides that an employment contract creates the employment relationship between worker and employer, and such contracts fall under civil-law rules, so Kret-Chbab 38 on contracts also covers them. [Para 37][Para 39] Article 22 of Kret-Chbab 38 of 1988 provides that a contract is the law of the parties, may be changed only by mutual consent and binds only those parties. [Para 40][Para 41][Para 42] It followed that only the parties to the employment contract may terminate it, so hiring and dismissing a particular worker is the exclusive right of the employer and that worker. [Para 43][Para 44]

Citing Cases 73/04 and 87/04 (Noble), the panel added that even where a worker has committed the serious misconduct described in Article 83, the Labour Law does not require the employer to dismiss; it merely gives the employer the right to do so. [Para 45][Para 46] The demand therefore had no legal basis and was rejected. [Para 47][Para 48]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Workers cannot compel an employer to dismiss a supervisor or fellow employee; the demand succeeds only where concrete evidence shows the person is dangerous and cannot be kept without creating job insecurity, and even proven serious misconduct under Article 83 leaves dismissal to the employer's discretion rather than making it obligatory. [Para 34][Para 45][Para 47]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that both heads did use inappropriate language and that the leave refusal was made out on the worker's testimony, but treated these as insufficient to order removal; it also noted the director's offer to issue warnings if the allegations were true. [Para 19][Para 21][Para 26][Para 31]
