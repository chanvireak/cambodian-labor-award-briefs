# Award No. 135/15 — E. Garmen Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 7 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Yu Soun, appointed by the employer; Tun Si Phan, appointed by the workers; Pen Bun Chhea, president) · Doc ID 139214

## 1. Facts
E. Garmen Co., Ltd is a garment factory at Village 3, Svay Rolum, Sa'ang, Kandal, employing about 3,000 workers on unlimited-duration contracts. [Para 1][Para 43] Two union federations — Khmer Youth Voice and Labour for Cambodia — brought nine collective claims after conciliation at the Kandal provincial labour department settled only two; a third (childcare) was settled at the hearing, leaving six for decision. [Para 19][Para 24][Para 25] All six remaining claims were dismissed.

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay severance for unlimited-duration contracts according to each worker's length of service? [Para 49]
2. Whether, on a day when the output bonus is not earned, the employer must pay the ordinary base wage, and on a day when it is earned, pay by output actually sewn? [Para 62]
3. Whether the employer must maintain 100% of the base wage when it has no work and workers go home? [Para 82]
4. Whether the employer must raise the base wage by 5% (1–2 years' seniority), 6% (over 4 years) and 7% (over 6 years)? [Para 88][Para 150]
5. Whether the employer must pay a regular attendance bonus of USD 15 per month? [Para 94]
6. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per worker per day? [Para 101]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Long service justifies immediate severance; the bonus is clawed back across the week so effort on good days is lost; shortfalls in work are the employer's fault, and past suspensions paid only 50%. [Para 51][Para 66][Para 68]
**Employer (respondent):** Order prices and seasonality caused the idle periods, production has normalised, and the 50% rate rests on prior notice and individual signed agreements; it pays the USD 10 attendance bonus required by Notification 230 and otherwise applies the law. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 84][Para 170]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all six remaining claims rejected; the award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notification absent objection to the Minister. [Para 187][Para 193]
On issues 1, 2 and 4 the panel applied *res judicata* ("អាជ្ញា អស់ ជំនុំ"): arbitration procedure contains no rule on the point, but where the same issue between the same parties was fully decided, the panel may decline to decide again. [Para 108][Para 109] It held all three conditions met against Award No. 039/14 — same parties, same issue, decision on the merits — and so refused to reconsider severance, the output-bonus recalculation, and the 100% wage for idle days. [Para 117][Para 118][Para 121][Para 149]
On issues 5 and 6 (seniority increments and the USD 15 bonus) the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice creating the claimed entitlement, characterising them as interest disputes (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 150][Para 152][Para 172][Para 174] Because an award on an interest dispute substitutes for a collective agreement for one year, only a most-representative union may bring one; the Khmer Pride Workers Union, not the two claimants, held that status, so the claims were inadmissible. [Para 158][Para 165][Para 167]
Issue 7 failed the same way; the attendance bonus and lunch allowance claims likewise exceeded what the law or Notification 230 required. [Para 171][Para 180][Para 185]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim already litigated between the same parties on the same issue and decided on the merits will not be reopened, and an interest dispute may be entertained only where the claimant union holds most-representative status in the enterprise, since such an award displaces the collective agreement for a year. [Para 112][Para 160]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that it retains discretion whether to reconsider a decided issue, res judicata not being mandatory in arbitration, and that barring repeat claims serves to prevent contradictory outcomes and secure finality. [Para 110][Para 113]
