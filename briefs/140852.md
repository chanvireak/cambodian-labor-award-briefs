# Award No. 040/21 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជុន ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខេស មើរ ថេកស្តាយ ខូ អិលធីឌី (Arbitration Council, 18 January 2022)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (អ៊ុក រី; អន ណន; រុន សារ៉ាយ presiding) · Doc ID 140852

## 1. Facts
The employer is ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជុន ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខេស មើរ ថេកស្តាយ ខូ អិលធីឌី, a garment and textile factory on National Road 21 at Koh Thom, Kandal, employing 500 workers [Para 1][Para 58]. The claimants are the worker representatives of that factory, acting with the union សហជីព ប្រជាធិបតេយ្យ កម្មករ និយោជិត នៃ ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជុន ស៊ូ (ខេមបូឌា) ខេស មើរ ថេកស្តាយ ខូ អិលធីឌី, registered on 26 August 2021 [Para 1][Para 59]. Of six demands, one was an individual dispute and four were settled in conciliation on 6 December 2021, leaving a single collective issue [Para 13][Para 14]. A further demand, that nine union founders and activists be reinstated with back wages, was raised only at the hearing and did not appear in the non-conciliation report [Para 73][Para 105]. The factory works from February to September each year and has no orders from October to year-end, so fixed-duration contracts are allowed to expire [Para 65][Para 66].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must stop terminating the employment contracts of the union's founders? [Para 62][Para 89]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** After the employer learned of the union's creation, ten leaders and activists became targets of harassment and were ultimately dismissed, nine of them founders or activists, which is a picture of union discrimination [Para 70]; a founder may be dismissed only for serious misconduct [Para 62]; the terminations came after the Ministry of Labour conciliation and the nine were already named in the original complaint [Para 81][Para 83].
**Employer (respondent):** The factory uses fixed-duration contracts, so non-renewal on expiry is not discrimination against founders or activists [Para 86]; roughly 200 workers reach contract expiry and about 60 to 70 per cent of staff are let go at each year-end, then recalled when work returns [Para 64][Para 69]; the additional demand was not in the conciliation report and cannot be considered [Para 85].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the demand that the employer stop terminating the contracts of the union's founders was rejected [Para 123], and the panel declined to consider the additional demand for reinstatement and back wages [Para 124].

The panel applied Article 62 of the Law on Trade Unions of 17 May 2016, which forbids an employer from discriminating on grounds of union office-holding or union activity, including in the non-renewal of successive fixed-duration contracts [Para 90]. It held, consistently with earlier awards, that the worker side bears the burden of proving that discrimination [Para 91][Para 93].

On the evidence, the workers filed eleven separate handwritten notes, five from one worker and six from another, one of each set bearing a duplicated date [Para 97]. Under Rule 25 of Prakas No. 099 SKBY of 21 April 2004 the panel weighs the admissibility and weight of evidence freely [Para 96], and it found the notes described meetings and notices about contract termination rather than harassment, so they could not establish that the employer persecuted union founders [Para 98][Para 99]. With no other evidence of union discrimination, and with terminations matching the seasonal expiry of fixed-duration contracts, the panel found no link between the non-renewals and union activity [Para 101][Para 103].

On the additional demand, Article 312 of the Labour Law and Rule 33 of Prakas No. 099 confine the panel to issues recorded in the non-conciliation minutes and to matters arising after those minutes as their direct consequence [Para 108][Para 109][Para 110]. Three of the nine were terminated on 30 November 2021, before the 6 December 2021 conciliation, and a fourth only after the 23 December 2021 hearing, so none was a direct consequence of the recorded dispute [Para 114][Para 117][Para 120]. Five others had no recorded entry or exit dates at all [Para 119].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A claim of anti-union discrimination in the non-renewal of a fixed-duration contract fails where the worker side produces only self-made notes that do not show persecution and the employer shows a consistent seasonal pattern of expiry and recall; the burden stays with the claimant [Para 91][Para 99][Para 103]. A panel will not reach a demand absent from the non-conciliation minutes unless it arose afterwards as a direct consequence of the recorded dispute [Para 110][Para 120].

**Obiter dicta:** Handwritten worker notes bearing duplicated dates carry little weight even when numerous [Para 97].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is cited as No. 881 K.B/AK/VK in the procedural section and as No. 811 in the referral and factual sections [Para 9][Para 14][Para 59]. The panel's constitution date reads "17 December 2019 2021", and the extension of the award deadline is printed as "6 January 20 2022" [Para 15][Para 23]. The award is dated 18 January 2022, yet the reasons state that a worker's contract ended on 12 January 2022 and that the employer had not yet terminated it by the hearing [Para 116][Para 117]. The employer's name is spelled inconsistently, including variants with ខេស មើល and ខូច អិលធីឌី [Para 25][Para 31]. Individual names of the nine workers are reproduced unevenly across the award, with two never named and one spelled two ways [Para 61][Para 78].
