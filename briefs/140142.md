# Award No. 114/12 — Fortune Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 12 July 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, employer-appointed; An Non, worker-appointed; Pen Bunchhea, president) · Doc ID 140142

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment Co., Ltd is a garment exporter in Kandal province employing about 1,400 workers; the CCAWDU union at the factory, with 784 members, was the claimant [Para 41][Para 42]. Its complaint of 29 February 2012 raised seven claims — wages during short work suspensions, attendance-bonus deductions, a day-care centre (ទារកដ្ឋាន), a $5 bonus increase, a 2,000 riel lunch allowance and a $10 monthly lodging and travel allowance; one claim settled in conciliation on 24 May 2012 and six were referred to the panel [Para 18][Para 19][Para 20]. The hearing was held on 21 June 2012 with both sides present [Para 17][Para 22]. The parties had signed a memorandum of understanding of 28 September 2010 making awards on rights disputes binding between them [Para 24][Para 159].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the wage shortfall for periods when it suspended work without labour-inspector authorisation, at 60 per cent for three years back from 2009? [Para 45][Para 92]
2. Whether the employer must repay attendance bonuses it deducted when workers took rest days? [Para 53]
3. Whether the employer must build a day-care centre of adequate standard? [Para 58][Para 118]
4. Whether the monthly attendance bonus should be raised by $5, to $12? [Para 64]
5. Whether the employer must contribute 2,000 riel a day towards the midday meal? [Para 71]
6. Whether the employer must pay $10 a month for lodging and travel? [Para 78]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** when work was suspended without authorisation the employer paid only the 40 per cent base wage plus seniority and attendance components, and must pay the full 100 per cent [Para 48]. Deductions from the attendance bonus for rest days breached award 94/07, and the existing day-care centre lacks carers, toys and ventilation [Para 59]. Wages are low and prices, rent, utilities and transport have risen, which justifies the extra $5, the lunch money and the $10 allowance [Para 66][Para 73].

**Employer (respondent):** suspensions lasted only one or two days because materials such as collars and labels had not arrived, too short a time to notify the labour inspector [Para 46][Para 47]. The company already has a day-care centre which no worker has asked to use, and since 3 May 2006 has paid a $10 monthly subsidy in its place [Para 60][Para 61]. It pays a $7 attendance bonus under the ministerial Prakas and cannot afford more [Para 69]. No law requires midday meal or lodging payments, and it will comply if one is enacted [Para 75][Para 81].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all six claims refused; no sum was ordered [Para 156].
The panel classified the claims before deciding them: issues 1 and 2 turn on rights under the Labour Law and Prakas 41/11, issue 3 on Article 186 of the Labour Law, so those are rights disputes; issues 4 to 6 rest on no law, collective agreement or agreement and are therefore interest disputes [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 91].
On issue 1 the panel found the identical claim had already been decided in case 38/10, where an award of 4 June 2010 ordered a 60 per cent top-up, and that the employer had lawfully objected to it [Para 93][Para 101]. Article 40 of Prakas 099 permits objection within eight days and provides that an objected award cannot be enforced; the panel held it has no jurisdiction over objections or enforcement of its own awards, so the claim was refused [Para 93][Para 98][Para 107].
Issue 2 was decided the same way: the claim in substance asked for enforcement of award 94/07, which the employer had objected to, so the panel had no jurisdiction [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117].
On issue 3 the panel applied res judicata: the same parties had litigated the same day-care demand in case 24/06, decided on the merits on 11 April 2006, and all three conditions were satisfied, so the claim was refused [Para 119][Para 123][Para 126][Para 135].
Issues 4 to 6 were refused on standing. Article 43 of Prakas 099 makes an award on an interest dispute substitute for the collective agreement for one year and bind all workers, removing non-members' right to strike over the same interest demands; only a union that is most representative in the enterprise may therefore bring such a claim [Para 139][Para 140][Para 141]. CCAWDU's most-representative status had expired on 8 January 2012, so it lacked the legal character to represent the workers on interest disputes and those claims were refused [Para 143][Para 144][Para 146][Para 151][Para 155].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that in substance asks the panel to enforce an earlier award to which a party has lawfully objected is not a fresh rights dispute: Article 40 of Prakas 099 leaves both the objection and enforcement to the courts, and the panel has no jurisdiction over either [Para 98][Para 116]. Equally, where the same parties bring the same demand that was already decided on the merits, res judicata lets the panel decline it, since arbitration procedure contains no provision requiring a re-hearing [Para 119][Para 121]. Finally, only a union that is most representative in the enterprise at the time may bring an interest dispute, because an interest award binds all workers and displaces the collective agreement [Para 141][Para 145].

**Obiter dicta:** although the day-care claim was already barred, the panel observed that a centre in any enterprise with 100 or more women workers eases their working life and should be adequately sized, hygienic and consistent with health and safety standards [Para 133][Para 134].

Conciliation is described as leaving 01 of 07 claims unsettled at one point and as referring 06 unsettled claims at another [Para 19][Para 20]. The employer's Khmer name is OCR'd in at least three different spellings across the file, so the English rendering above follows the award's own trade name rather than any single line [Para 52][Para 105].
