# Award No. 057/18 — Meng Da Footwear In Das Steel (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 24 January 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Sothy (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kong Phallak (president) · Doc ID 138960

## 1. Facts
Meng Da Footwear In Das Steel (Cambodia) employs 4,500 workers and recognises 15 unions, none of which holds most-representative status. [Para 45][Para 48] Workers filed 13 demands on working conditions in a letter of 12 November 2018, received by the Labour Disputes Department on 20 November 2018. [Para 21][Para 37] Conciliation on 21 December 2018 failed entirely, and non-conciliation report No. 1188 ក.ប/អក/វក of 27 December 2018 reached the Secretariat the same day. [Para 22][Para 18] The panel was constituted on 28 December 2018 and heard the case on 8 January 2019, when the parties settled nine demands (Nos 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 12 and 13), leaving Nos 1, 2, 4 and 5. [Para 24][Para 27][Para 28] The parties agreed to extend the award date from 21 to 24 January 2019. [Para 31]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the regular attendance bonus already paid? [Para 49]
2. Whether the employer must add 5 USD to the travel and accommodation allowance already paid? [Para 56]
3. Whether the employer must grant 90 days of maternity leave as working days, excluding public holidays and Sundays? [Para 62]
4. Whether the employer must allow one hour per day for breastfeeding until the child reaches 18 months? [Para 69]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The $10 attendance bonus no longer covers living costs and should rise with the minimum wage as at other firms paying $15–20, such as FWKK; the $7 travel and lodging allowance is short of $15 bus fares and $25–40 rent, while other firms pay up to $12. [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54][Para 57][Para 58][Para 60] Article 182 says 90 days, not three months, so it must mean working days, and one hour of breastfeeding for only 12 months is too short. [Para 63][Para 65][Para 72][Para 73]

**Employer (respondent):** It follows the ministerial prakas and cannot pay more; it already pays $10 to ordinary workers and $15 to team leaders, and $7 travel and lodging. [Para 55][Para 61][Para 92] Article 182 does not say the 90 days are working days, and it already grants 90 calendar days. [Para 67][Para 68] It already allows one hour per day for 12 months, which is what Article 184 requires. [Para 74]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all four remaining claims were refused; the order is non-binding (មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final eight days after notification absent written objection to the Minister. [Para 151][Para 152][Para 154][Para 155]

On issues 1 and 2 the panel found the employer already meets point 2 of Ministry Notification No. 230 ក.ប/ស.ជ.ណ of 25 July 2012, which requires at least $10 per month for full attendance, and point 1 of the same notification, which sets $7 travel and lodging; no law, collective agreement, internal rule, contract or past practice obliges more. [Para 93][Para 95][Para 118][Para 120][Para 121] Each demand therefore sought a benefit above the legal floor and was an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍). [Para 99][Para 122]

Because an interest award becomes the collective agreement for one year and strips all workers of the right to strike on that subject, the panel may decide such a claim only where the claimant is the most-representative union or unions meeting the Article 54 thresholds of the 2016 Law on Trade Unions — over 30 per cent membership, or majority support — with Article 72 providing the bargaining-council route where none qualifies. [Para 103][Para 109][Para 111][Para 112] Following Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Great Union, 02/11-Pou Yuen, 66/11-Inhansung, 118/16-E-Cheng and 203/16-Inkyung Vun Dang, it declined jurisdiction over both. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115][Para 116]

On issue 3 the panel held Article 182 fixes 90 days without saying working or calendar days, no prakas defines it, and the purpose — recovery and care of the newborn — supports counting 90 calendar days, as Awards 23/08-IW 1, 25/08-SD, 152/08-Wilson and 041/18-BE Carry held; the employer complied. [Para 132][Para 133][Para 135][Para 138][Para 140]

On issue 4 the panel read Article 184 as one hour per day during working hours for one year from childbirth, i.e. until the child is 12 months, following Award 335/14-Cambodian Sunrise Garment and Case 31/11-Queen's Melter; 18 months has no legal basis. [Para 142][Para 147][Para 148][Para 149]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand for a benefit exceeding the minimum fixed by ministerial notification is an interest dispute, and the panel will not decide it unless the claimant is the most-representative union under Article 54 of the Law on Trade Unions, because an interest award replaces the collective agreement for a year and removes the right to strike on that issue. [Para 111][Para 112] The one hour of paid breastfeeding time under Article 184 runs for one year, that is until the child is 12 months. [Para 147]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observation that where a statute does not specify working or calendar days, a period of 15 days or less is read as working days and a longer one as calendar days. [Para 137]

[Para 21][Para 23][Para 38] The company's registration number is given twice in irreconcilable forms — "MOC-៩០៧៣៤៥២៤" and "3000១៧៧១៨៩" (old list Inv. ១៣៥៧ E/២០០៨) — with both dated 30 September 2015. [Para 23][Para 109] The worker representatives' names appear in full in the source but are rendered here as initials per brief convention. [Para 46]
