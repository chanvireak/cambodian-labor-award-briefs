# Award No. 050/18 — V.C. (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 February 2019)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Hem Hor Narith (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Kuy Neam (chair) · Doc ID 138955

## 1. Facts
V.C. (Cambodia) Ltd sells medicines, hygiene products and medical equipment from Boeung Keng Kang I, Chamkar Mon, Phnom Penh, and employed about 100 staff. [Para 94][Para 95] Eight sales and promotion staff, all founders or elected leaders of the V.C. Democratic Workers' Union, were dismissed — Ms S Lakkhena on 23 October 2018 by email, and seven others on 30 October 2018 without prior notice. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 260] The union had held its leadership election on 5 August 2018, notified the employer on 7 August 2018 and applied for registration on 21 August 2018. [Para 128][Para 131][Para 132] Three points failed conciliation on 7 December 2018 and the file reached the panel on 13 December 2018. [Para 14][Para 15]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the elected union leaders and the named workers — Ms S Lakkhena among them — and pay wages from the date of dismissal to the date of return, ending union discrimination? [Para 99][Para 215]
2. Whether the employer must pay the outstanding wages and allowances owed to the workers? [Para 137]
3. Whether the employer must give union-member workers working conditions and the USD 20 wage increase equal to those of non-member workers in similar roles with comparable seniority? [Para 178][Para 313]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** All eight were founders or candidates in the union election and so enjoyed special protection against dismissal; the employer dismissed them without the labour inspector's permission as required, without any misconduct on their part, and in breach of Article 83 of the Labour Law. [Para 102][Para 103][Para 105] They had never signed written contracts, and the employer's new demand that members scan fingerprints four times a day was itself discrimination under Articles 62 and 63 of the Law on Trade Unions. [Para 109][Para 124][Para 196]

**Employer (respondent):** The eight were dismissed for serious misconduct under Article 83 of the Labour Law and the internal rules: scanning in once instead of four times a day, leaving early, disclosing company information on social media, using company time for personal business, diverting invoices, refusing to sign the new written contracts, and forming a union without notifying management. [Para 112][Para 116][Para 123] It denied any anti-union motive and said it could not grant the remaining wage increases because they depend on annual performance evaluation. [Para 173][Para 180][Para 193]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted. The panel ordered the employer to take back all eight dismissed workers in their original positions and pay back wages from the date of dismissal until the date of return, and to pay the USD 20 wage increase, incentive and commission to the seven named workers per the table annexed to the award, totalling USD 13,874.72; it also ordered payment of the USD 20 increase to Ms S Lakkhena, Ms D Naret and Ms N Thida and continuation of that increase for all ten. [Para 335][Para 336][Para 337][Para 27]

On issue 1, the panel first held that because the eight had worked without written fixed-duration contracts, Article 67(7) of the Labour Law converted their engagement into an indefinite-duration contract. [Para 219][Para 230][Para 233] It then applied the three conditions in Article 67 of the Law on Trade Unions — protected status, dismissal within the protected window, and notice to the employer and the Ministry — and found all three met, since the dismissals fell between the 21 August 2018 registration application and the 4 December 2018 certificate. [Para 247][Para 261][Para 267][Para 269] As the employer never sought the labour inspector's permission required by Articles 43, 45 and 67, the dismissals were unlawful, and Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 empowered the panel to order reinstatement and immediate payment. [Para 274][Para 276][Para 277][Para 280]

On issue 2, the panel invoked Article 118 of the Labour Law, which puts the burden of proving wage payment on the employer, and found the employer produced no attendance or evaluation records for earlier years and could not say what evaluation result earned the USD 20 increase. [Para 282][Para 303][Para 305][Para 307] It therefore preferred the workers' evidence on the increase, incentive and commission, but declined to consider the residual wages and 13th-month pay because neither side argued them at either hearing. [Para 309][Para 141][Para 281]

On issue 3, the panel treated the claim for the same seven workers as already decided under issue 2 and refused to award it twice; for the three workers not covered by issue 2 it ordered the USD 20 increase and, for all ten, that the increase continue. [Para 332][Para 333][Para 334]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a union is in the registration process, Articles 43, 45 and 67 of the Law on Trade Unions protect its founders and election candidates, and a dismissal carried out in that window without the labour inspector's prior permission is unlawful and will be remedied by reinstatement plus back wages to the date of return. [Para 261][Para 274][Para 276][Para 280] On wage claims the employer carries the burden under Article 118 of the Labour Law, and an employer that cannot produce attendance and evaluation records supporting its own pay criteria will lose on that point. [Para 282][Para 303][Para 309]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the copy-to-Ministry requirement in Article 67 is not an extra obligation where the workers have already notified the employer by any reliable means, since its purpose is to facilitate — not obstruct — the constitutional right to form a union. [Para 249][Para 250] It also noted that no most-representative union existed at the company and recognised five of the six named workers as representatives under Article 1 of Prakas No. 362. [Para 98][Para 213]

[Para 74][Para 259] The first hearing is printed as 10 January 2019 while the postponement paragraph also fixes 10 January 2019 and the summons bears 17 December 2018. [Para 11][Para 19][Para 87] The Prakas No. 220 appointment date of the panel is garbled as "23 [month lost] 2018". [Para 7] The company name is printed variously as វីស៊ី, វី អ៊ី, វីអ៊ុ and វី ម៉ី, and worker names as តាំង សក់ មួយ / បួយ / បូយ / ប៊ូយ. [Para 94][Para 213][Para 253] Worker counts shift between seven and eight, and the annex table prints several totals as "206552062" and "13,874.72" without a currency label. [Para 281][Para 339] A sentence in the issue list is truncated to "ភាគី និយោជក អវត្តមាន នៅ ថ្ងៃ 1". [Para 6]
