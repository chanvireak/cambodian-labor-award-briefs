# Award No. 150/13 — Te Yong (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 August 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Sin Kim Sean for the workers, chair Kong Phalha) · Doc ID 139834

## 1. Facts
Te Yong (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, a garment factory at Bori Kammakar village, Baek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal, employing about 700 workers, faced 19 demands raised through the Federation of Union of Labour for Cambodia (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ការងារ ដើម្បីកម្ពុជា) on behalf of 14 workers at the factory, who had transferred their rights to it. [Para 51] [Para 52] [Para 53] [Para 54] The factory-level union had not yet received its registration certificate from the Ministry of Labour. [Para 55] Conciliation on 08 July 2013 left all 19 points unsettled; at the hearing the parties agreed 15 of them and the workers withdrew points 15 and 17, leaving points 2 and 18. [Para 27] [Para 32] [Para 33] [Para 34]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Panel's issue 2) Whether the employer must add a further 2,000 riel of meal allowance to the 2,000 riel it already pays for overtime worked from 4:00 to 6:00 pm, making 4,000 riel? [Para 3] [Para 56]
2. (Panel's issue 18) Whether the employer must pay a production bonus to the sections that do not receive one — checking, cutting table, packing, warehouse, carton and hygiene — as it does to the sewing section? [Para 19] [Para 60]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the meal allowance for the 4:00 to 6:00 pm overtime should be doubled because prices have risen; and that workers in the non-sewing sections want the same bonus that sewing workers receive, no other reason being advanced. [Para 58] [Para 62] [Para 63]

**Employer (respondent):** that it already pays 2,000 riel for that overtime, has never paid a further 2,000, has no agreement or internal rule providing one, and is applying the law correctly; and that a bonus for the other sections is unnecessary, the bonus having always been confined to sewing. [Para 57] [Para 59] [Para 61] [Para 64]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel declined to consider both remaining demands; the 15 points the parties agreed at the hearing stand as agreed, and the award is non-binding. [Para 85] [Para 86] [Para 87]

On issue 2, the parties agreed that the employer pays 2,000 riel for overtime from 4:00 to 6:00 pm and that no agreement covered a further 2,000 riel on top of it, nor did the internal rules provide for one. [Para 56] [Para 57] The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, past practice or internal rule obliging the employer to pay the additional sum, and so characterised the demand as an interests dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 67] [Para 68] It then applied Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001, under which the right to conclude a collective agreement, and so to bring an interests claim, belongs to the most representative union, with Article 43 of Prakas 099 providing that an interests award replaces the collective agreement for one year. [Para 69] [Para 70] [Para 71] [Para 72] Following Awards 81/04-Evergreen, 98/04-Fret Union and the refusal in Award 169/11-Fortune TV, and because only 14 workers were claimants and the factory union was unregistered, the panel held the claimant lacked the legal character to bring an interests dispute and declined to consider it. [Para 74] [Para 75] [Para 77] [Para 78] [Para 79]

On issue 18, the parties agreed that the bonus is paid only to the sewing section and has never been paid to the other sections. [Para 60] [Para 61] The panel found no provision of the Labour Law, collective agreement, agreement, past practice or internal rule requiring the employer to extend the bonus to checking, cutting, packing, warehouse, carton and hygiene, so this too was an interests dispute, and it adopted its reasoning on issue 2 and declined to consider the demand. [Para 82] [Para 83] [Para 84] [Para 86]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where the parties agree on the allowance actually paid and no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice requires more, a demand for an increase is an interests dispute, and an interests dispute may be brought only by the most representative union in the enterprise — a federation acting for a small group of workers, where the factory union is unregistered, cannot obtain a ruling on it. The same applies to a demand to extend a bonus paid to one section to sections that have never received it. [Para 67] [Para 78] [Para 82]

**Obiter dicta:** none identified — the panel confined itself to standing and characterisation and did not reach the merits of either demand. [Para 79] [Para 86]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award is dated 14 August 2013 on its face, but the reasons record an agreement to postpone issuance from 05 August 2013 to 16 August 2013, a third date. [Para 1] [Para 35] The panel is said to have been constituted on 16 July "2019 2013", and the employer's authority letter is dated 01 August 2013 for a hearing that day. [Para 29] [Para 36] Demand 14 is partly garbled, reading "កុំ ទំលាក់ ថា ក ហ្គេត ក្នុង មួយ ម៉ោង -១០០ ទៅ ១៥០", and the panel's reasoning at issue 2 repeats the same clause twice. [Para 15] [Para 67] The chair's name is legible in the header but replaced by noise in the signature block ("%(@$97"), and the employer and worker arbitrators' names survive without their signatures. [Para 1] [Para 88] Individual workers and union officials are reduced to initials under the briefing convention.
