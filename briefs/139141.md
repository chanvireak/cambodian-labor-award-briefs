# Award No. 033/15 — Rilay Abel South Industrial (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 26 February 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, chosen by the employer; An Non, chosen by the workers; Tang Tri, chair) · Doc ID 139141

## 1. Facts
The enterprise (រីឡាយ អេប៊ល សូស អ៊ិន ដាស្ទ្រៀល (ខេមបូឌា)) cuts and sews garments at Kakab, Por Sen Chey District, Phnom Penh, with about 2,200 workers. [Para 45][Para 46] A local union of the Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) had held its leadership election on 02 January 2015 and notified the company, but had not yet applied to the Ministry of Labour for registration. [Para 47][Para 48] Its letter of delegation from the workers carried no list of names or thumbprints, and the ordered list was never filed by the deadline. [Para 49][Para 50] Only three workers attended the hearing, and the panel confined itself to them. [Para 51][Para 52] The disputed acts are the employer's refusals of five benefit claims pressed through conciliation, of which five remained non-conciliated when the case reached the Secretariat on 29 January 2015. [Para 14][Para 19][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a meal allowance for overtime worked from 16:00 to 18:00 of 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 53][Para 85]
2. Whether the employer must let every pregnant worker take one full day per month for an antenatal check while keeping her wage and other benefits unchanged? [Para 56][Para 103]
3. Whether the employer must keep full wages, the 2,000 riel midday meal allowance and one carton of milk for a worker who occasionally arrives late? [Para 62][Para 112]
4. Whether the employer must raise the regular-attendance bonus from USD 12 to USD 15 per month? [Para 73][Para 146]
5. Whether the employer must cut code-card swiping from four times to twice a day? [Para 77][Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Other companies pay a better overtime meal allowance; equal treatment requires that pregnant workers living near and far both get a full day, since most of them wait hours at the clinic; late arrival is caused by traffic or a broken motorcycle, not choice, so pay and benefits should be untouched; and four swipes a day cost time when the card fails, so two would suffice. [Para 54][Para 59][Para 60][Para 64][Para 65][Para 79]
**Employer (respondent):** It already pays 2,100 riel a day for overtime meals plus a 2,000 riel lunch allowance and a carton of milk, all above the Prakas minimum; it grants half a day to pregnant workers living in Phnom Penh and a full day to those living outside it because the city has many nearby hospitals; it deducts wages only for the minutes actually not worked, applying the same rule to administrative staff and supervisors; and its attendance bonus of USD 12 exceeds the legal floor. [Para 55][Para 61][Para 66][Para 67][Para 76]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all five claims were refused, three of them without any ruling on the merits because the claimants lacked standing in an interest dispute. [Para 176][Para 177][Para 180][Para 182]
On issue 1 the panel found the employer already gives 2,100 riel a day for overtime meals, which is more favourable than point 2 of Ministry of Labour Notification No. 041/11 of 07 March 2011 requiring 2,000 riel a day plus one free meal, and that no law, collective agreement, internal rule or past practice fixes an hourly rate of 2,000 riel. [Para 85][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88] It classified the claim as an interest dispute (វិវាទអំពីផលប្រយោជន៍) and then held that under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305 of 22 November 2001 only a most-representative union, or several unions together with more than half the workforce, may bring such a dispute, following Awards 81/04 Evergreen, 98/04 Great Union, 169/11 Fortune TV, 02/11 Pou Yuen, 66/11 Inhansung and 278/13 Meng Da Footwear. [Para 89][Para 90][Para 93][Para 95][Para 98][Para 99] The three claimants were individuals, not a most-representative union, so it refused to consider issues 1, 2 and 4. [Para 100][Para 101][Para 102][Para 108][Para 111][Para 152][Para 154]
On issue 3 the panel reasoned on the merits instead. Under Article 664 of the Civil Code a worker owes labour and the employer owes wages in return, and Article 102 defines wages as the price of work done; time spent not working because of lateness therefore earns nothing, and paying by actual time worked is consistent with both provisions. It followed Awards 59/10 Takhmao, 208/12 Ocean Garment and 308/14. [Para 113][Para 115][Para 119][Para 123][Para 127][Para 128] For the meal allowance and milk it applied Article 2 of the Labour Law, which places the enterprise under the employer's management and direction, and found that conditioning these benefits on arriving before 7:04 and leaving at 16:00 is a lawful and reasonable use of that right intended to encourage punctuality and is more generous than the law requires. [Para 132][Para 141][Para 143][Para 144][Para 145]
On issue 5 the panel again applied Article 2 and the earlier Awards 62/06, 108/06, 33/07, 119/09 and 127/13, holding that an employer needs to know when work starts and ends for attendance, payroll and production records, that two swipes a day could not achieve those purposes, that the workers produced no evidence of real hardship from four swipes, and that the employer had shown each swipe takes about five minutes and provides a fallback form when the card fails. [Para 159][Para 161][Para 163][Para 170][Para 172][Para 174][Para 175]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An arbitration panel will decide an interest dispute only where the claimant is the most-representative union in the enterprise, or several unions whose combined membership exceeds half the workers; individual workers, or a union that is neither registered nor most representative, lack standing, and their benefit claims are refused without any ruling on the merits. Where a claim is decided on the merits, an employer's managerial authority under Article 2 sustains conditions attached to discretionary benefits and to attendance monitoring — wages paid only for time actually worked, meal and milk benefits tied to punctuality, four code-card swipes a day — provided the arrangement is lawful, reasonable and no worse than the statutory floor. [Para 98][Para 102][Para 127][Para 143][Para 172][Para 174]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that the employer's overtime meal payment of 2,100 riel a day and its USD 12 attendance bonus both exceed the statutory minimum, and that a code-card process should not unreasonably encroach on workers' personal time, did not alter the dismissals, which rested on standing or on Article 2. [Para 87][Para 148][Para 167]

[Para 1][Para 14][Para 19][Para 45] The panel gives no reasons for its award date beyond an extension from 20 February to 26 February 2015. [Para 16][Para 26] The conciliation is described as covering eight issues with five unresolved, yet only five were referred and the award disposes of five. [Para 18][Para 19] The order on issue 3(b) prints the meal allowance as "២០ រៀល" (20 riel) where the claim and reasoning say 2,000 riel. [Para 112][Para 179] Individual and union names are carried in the body but are redacted here; the signature block loses the chair's printed name, which the header supplies.
