# Award No. 10/08 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ជី អេច ជី (Arbitration Council, ថ្ងៃទី ១៨ ខែ កុម្ភៈ ឆ្នាំ ២០០៨)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · three-member panel: មាំរ សំបូរ ណា (employer-appointed), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker-appointed), chair សុខ ម៉ាធឿង · Doc ID 140484

## 1. Facts
The Khmer Youth Federation union filed a collective complaint against the employer on 14 December 2007 seeking improved working conditions; the Labour Dispute Department received it on 18 December 2007 [Para 10]. Conciliation failed on two of five claims, and the non-conciliation report No. 080 was sent to the Arbitration Council secretariat on 16 January 2008 [Para 11]. The panel heard the parties on 30 January 2008; both appeared, further conciliation failed, and the award considers only the two unresolved points [Para 12]. The parties agreed the factory had over 900 workers and the union about 300 members [Para 28]. The 62 claimants were occasional ("floating") workers on short contracts of less than 21 days, repeatedly ended and renewed after one or two days [Para 29]. The employer said the contracts were often 15 or 20 days, ended before national holidays, with severance paid at each end [Para 31]. The union said the workers were paid half-monthly and kept working daily under new contracts and new cards [Para 33]. The panel ordered wage lists for the workers, which the employer submitted [Para 34]. The employer agreed to a USD 5 incentive for good work but not the claimed general attendance bonus [Para 37]. The union said the workers had worked continuously through full months [Para 42]. The panel reviewed employer documents, union documents including worker thumbprints, ministry records, and payroll tables for July–December 2007, with no outside witnesses or experts [Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether occasional workers who worked more than 21 days should be converted to regular workers with the same benefits as regular workers [Para 2]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the USD 5 monthly regular-work bonus to occasional workers who had not received it [Para 4]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The employer argued it could not convert workers on contracts of over 21 days into regular workers, but said it provided other benefits like those of regular workers and agreed to provide detailed wage lists [Para 3]. It refused the USD 5 monthly bonus as demanded [Para 5], describing any USD 5 payment as a good-work incentive only, not a general entitlement [Para 37]. It maintained the contracts lasted under 21 days with one or two day gaps for renewal tied to production cycles [Para 41]. The union argued the workers should be converted to regular status with regular-worker benefits [Para 35], that the USD 5 bonus had never been paid to the 62 occasional workers [Para 39], and that the workers had worked without any day off through full months [Para 42]. It relied on the workers' half-monthly pay and immediate renewal by new contract and card as evidence of continuous work [Para 33].

## 4. Award and reasoning
The panel held that a worker who works regularly and full-time cannot be treated as occasional [Para 49]. Article 99 divides workers into regular and occasional workers [Para 44], and Article 166 deems a worker who averages 21 days of work per month in an irregular occupation to have worked continuously [Para 50]. The panel applied its earlier decision in Award No. 26/04 that a worker averaging more than 21 days over two consecutive months is a regular worker [Para 51]. Article 10 gives occasional workers the same rights and duties as regular workers absent a distinct agreement [Para 53]. The employer was told to align its policy with Articles 9, 10 and 166 [Para 54]. Minimum wage rules under Prakas 17/00, including allowances and overtime, apply to occasional workers [Para 55]. On issue 2, the USD 5 bonus is a wage supplement within Article 120's three-year limitation period [Para 69], so the claim was timely [Para 70]. Notice 017 of 18 July 2000 provides that workers who work the required days in a month receive at least USD 5 per month [Para 75], and Notice 745 preserved those benefits [Para 73]. The wage lists were complex and incomplete: only 48 of the 62 claimants appeared by name, so the panel could not determine days worked [Para 63] and declined to use them as a basis of decision [Para 78]. The panel therefore ordered joint verification by the employer and the union of each claimant's days worked [Para 64]. The award is non-binding and becomes final eight days after notice absent written objection [Para 83].
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must reclassify as regular any of the 62 claimants who averaged at least 21 days per month over two consecutive months and extend regular-worker benefits to all casual workers from the award's effectiveness [Para 80]; pay the USD 5 monthly bonus immediately to claimants who worked regularly in any month after the award's effectiveness [Para 81]; and verify attendance with the factory union leadership to implement both orders [Para 82].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker who averages 21 days of work per month for two consecutive months is a regular worker, whatever the contract label [Para 62]. Occasional workers enjoy the same rights as regular workers under Article 10 absent a distinct agreement [Para 53]. A regular-attendance bonus is a wage supplement, so Article 120's three-year limitation applies [Para 67]. Where wage records are too unclear to identify eligible workers, the panel may order joint verification rather than decide individual entitlements [Para 63]. This is an arbitration award, not binding precedent.
**Obiter dicta:** Classification still matters because occasional workers lack seniority-based benefits, such as an extra day of annual leave per three years of service [Para 60].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The claimant count conflicts: 62 claimants [Para 29] against 462 in the payroll order and operative paragraph [Para 34]. A garbled passage states USD 50 where USD 5 appears elsewhere [Para 99]. The appended pages after the signatures contain OCR noise, unrelated dates and English fragments [Para 90]. The worker-arbitrator's objection is appended but not clearly separated from the award text [Para 96].
