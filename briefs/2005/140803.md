# Award No. 79/05 — Evergreen Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 2 February 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, employer-appointed; Tuon Siphan, worker-appointed; Koy Neam, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140803

## 1. Facts
Evergreen Apparel Co., Ltd of Khan Rusey Keo, Phnom Penh, faced a collective dispute brought by the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) over money withheld for employment books. The Labour Dispute Resolution Department conciliated on 16 December 2005 without settlement, and non-conciliation report No. 797 AABVIAKIVK reached the Council on 26 December 2005. [Para 5][Para 7][Para 14][Para 15][Para 18] The employer organised the workers' employment books in bulk, first in September 2005 and again in December 2005, and in July 2005 deducted US$2.50 from each worker for that purpose without consulting them or obtaining their approval. [Para 31] The official fee for an employment book is 2,500 riel (about US$0.625) under Notification 013/97 and Joint Prakas 147/01. [Para 25][Para 38]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse workers the amount it charged them for employment books above the official fee set by Notification 013/97 and Joint Prakas 147/01? [Para 11][Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The employer overcharged them for the employment books it obtained on their behalf, and the excess should be paid back. [Para 11][Para 16]
**Employer (respondent):** The excess went to Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training officials for services connected with the applications; had workers applied themselves they would have spent more than US$3.00 on two round trips, the application form, submission, medical checks and lost working time, besides interrupting production, so the deduction saved both sides. [Para 12][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay back to the workers the amount charged in excess of the official fee fixed by Notification No. 013/97 and Joint Prakas No. 147/01 for making an employment book, within at least three weeks after the award comes into effect. [Para 44]

The panel began from Article 32 of the Labour Law, under which the obligation to hold an employment book rests solely on the worker and an employer may not employ a worker who lacks one, and found no provision requiring an employer to obtain the book on the worker's behalf. [Para 31][Para 32] Notification 013/97 and Joint Prakas 147/01 require the employer only to "facilitate" the application, a word the instruments leave undefined, and practice ranges from granting time off to inviting issuing officials to the factory. [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35] Article 36 makes issuance subject to a fee remitted to the national budget, which the Joint Prakas fixes at 2,500 riel, comprising 1,500 riel for the application form and 1,000 riel for stamp duty. [Para 37][Para 38][Para 39] While the worker bears that fee, no provision permits a charge above it, so a unilateral decision to require a worker to pay more does not comply with the Labour Law; the panel followed Awards 21/03-Loyal Cambodia, 05/04-Thai Dou and 106/04-Suit Way. [Para 40][Para 41] The employer was repeatedly given the chance to produce a receipt showing it had paid more than the official fee and failed to do so by the date set of 26 January 2006, and the panel drew an adverse inference from that failure, as in Awards 10/03 Jacqsintex Garment, 25/03-Advanced Industries, 22/04-Raffles Le Royal and 91/04-Honey Wear. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43] Its justification of saved transport and lost time did not displace the statutory fee ceiling, because the employer had told the workers of the charge but never sought their agreement to it. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer that obtains employment books in bulk for its workers may recover only the official fee fixed by Notification 013/97 and Joint Prakas 147/01, and any unilateral deduction above that fee is unlawful and must be repaid, whether or not the excess was passed to issuing officials; an employer asserting that it paid more must prove it with a receipt or suffer an adverse inference. [Para 40][Para 42]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's account of the two acceptable facilitation practices — time off during working hours, or bringing issuing officials to the factory — and its observation that the latter expedites the process and saves time for both sides. [Para 35][Para 36]
