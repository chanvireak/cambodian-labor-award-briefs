# Award No. 23/05 — Jung Min (Cambodia) Apparel Co., Ltd v. Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF) (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuochhun, Ven Pov, Koy Neam) · Doc ID 140771

## 1. Facts
Jung Min (Cambodia) Apparel Co., Ltd employs approximately 1,600 workers, of whom 600 to 700 are called "floating workers" by the company [Para 12]. Most work in the sewing, ironing and trimming sections [Para 13]. The company's peak season runs from October to March, and it hires these workers on fixed-duration contracts (FDCs) of 3 to 6 months, working 8 hours a day and 26 days a month, then re-hires many of them under another 3-month FDC [Para 13][Para 14]. They are paid US$35 to US$45 per month with an overtime rate of 100% to 150%, but receive neither annual leave nor severance pay at the end of their contracts [Para 14][Para 15]. On 14 March 2005 the workers complained to the Daungkor district Labour Inspector over 12 issues, 11 of which the parties settled, leaving the demand that workers with three or more months of regular service be recognised as regular workers [Para 2][Para 8][Para 10]. Non-conciliation report No. 491/MoLVT dated 1 April 2005 reached the Secretariat on 4 April 2005 [Para 3]. The employer filed its evidence two days after the 25 April 2005 deadline, gave no valid reason, and the panel excluded it [Para 21][Para 22][Para 23][Para 24].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must accept as regular workers all workers who have worked regularly for three consecutive months or more, in particular the 600 to 700 workers the company calls "floating workers"? [Para 2][Para 12]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Cambodian Industrial Union Federation (CIUF)):** All workers who have worked regularly for three consecutive months or more should be accepted as regular workers [Para 2]. At the hearing the union demanded that floating workers receive the same wages and benefits as permanent workers [Para 15].
**Employer (Jung Min (Cambodia) Apparel Co., Ltd):** It classified the 600 to 700 workers as "floating workers" hired only for the October-to-March peak season on 3- to 6-month fixed-duration contracts, paid US$35 to US$45 per month with overtime at 100% to 150% but without annual leave or severance pay [Para 13][Para 14][Para 15]. It submitted its documents on 27 April 2005, explaining only that it was confused about the deadline [Para 18][Para 19].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to provide the workers who signed fixed-duration contracts and whom it called floating workers with the same wages and other benefits as regular workers under fixed-duration contracts, in accordance with the Labour Law [Para 40].
On evidence, the panel relied on Articles 18 and 24 of Prakas 99/04 for its power to require documents and on Article 25 for its freedom to determine admissibility and weight [Para 19][Para 20]. Having given the employer several chances to justify the two-day delay and receiving only confusion about the date, it declined to consider the documents filed on 27 April 2005 [Para 23][Para 24].
On the merits, the panel held that the Labour Law does not recognise the status of "floating worker" and that Article 9 sorts all workers into casual and regular [Para 27][Para 28]. It reviewed its own line of authority, which reads Article 166(3) (an average of 21 days a month for jobs not performed regularly through the year) with Article 68 (probation of no more than three months, two for specialised workers) to treat garment workers as regular after two months, and has held that a worker averaging at least 21 days over two consecutive months is regular whatever the employer calls him [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31][Para 32][Para 33]. It recorded doubt whether that 21-days/2-months rule was apt on these facts, noting Guideline #1 lets it depart from its jurisprudence where the facts differ [Para 34][Para 35]. It avoided the question by holding that even if the workers are casual, Article 10 gives casual workers the same rules, obligations and rights as regular workers except for separately stipulated clauses, and Articles 10 and 104 require minimum working conditions and wages for all workers [Para 36]. Since each worker signed a fixed-duration contract, they are entitled to all the rights of such contracts, including minimum conditions during the term and severance pay at termination under Article 73 [Para 37][Para 38]. The panel found the employer in violation of Articles 10, 73, 104 and 139, the parties having conceded that some received no severance, some less than minimum wage, and some less than the 150% overtime rate [Para 38][Para 39].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The label an employer gives a worker does not determine the worker's legal status or entitlements: a worker on a fixed-duration contract is entitled to the minimum conditions of that contract and to severance pay on its expiry, and even a casual worker enjoys the same rights as a regular worker under Article 10 of the Labour Law [Para 36][Para 38]. Evidence filed after a deadline set by the panel will be excluded where the party, given repeated opportunities, offers no valid reason for the delay [Para 23][Para 24].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel doubted whether its earlier 21-days-over-two-months rule for converting casual workers into regular workers suited this case, and noted that Guideline #1 permits departure from prior jurisprudence on different facts, but it decided the case without resolving that question [Para 34][Para 35].
