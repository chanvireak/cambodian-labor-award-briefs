# Award No. 81/05 — Supreme Garment Company (Arbitration Council, 19 January 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (KAO Thach, employer-appointed; TUON Siphann, worker-appointed; PEN Bunchhea, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140804

## 1. Facts
Supreme Garment Company of Doeum Mean, Kandal Province, faced a collective dispute brought by three unions — Khmer Union of Supreme Garment, Solidarity Union of Supreme Garment and Labour Union of Supreme Garment. [Para 1][Para 3][Para 11][Para 15] On 21 December 2005 the Kandal labour office conciliated 22 issues, settling 17 and leaving five, which reached the Council on 27 December 2005 under report No. 006/05 K.B.V/KN dated 26 December 2005; the parties agreed at the hearing that the workforce was about 1,100 rather than the 1,000 stated in the official report. [Para 30][Para 31][Para 40] The five unsettled demands concerned sick leave and medical certificates, annual leave, reimbursement of pre-recruitment medical checks of 10,100 riel, the pay date, and piece rates. [Para 22][Para 24][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must grant paid sick leave and cover medication for workers certified by any hospital doctor, including those who recover at home rather than in hospital? [Para 22][Para 41]
2. (Issue 2) Whether annual leave must be 19 days in the third, fourth and fifth years and 20 days from the sixth year, rather than the company's 19, 18, 18, 19 pattern? [Para 24][Para 47]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must reimburse the 10,100 riel medical check fee and the wage lost on the day of the check? [Para 26][Para 50]
4. (Issue 4) Whether wages must be paid between the fifth and seventh day of the following month? [Para 27][Para 50]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must disclose each piece rate before assigning the work, and refrain from reducing a rate once workers, knowing it, sew faster? [Para 28][Para 136][Para 144]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Clause 4(g) of the Internal Work Rules requires wage and medication wherever a certified sick worker stays, since workers who cannot afford a hospital room buy medicine and recover at home; annual leave should reach 19 days from the third year and 20 from the sixth; the company should repay the 10,100 riel check fee and the 25 per cent of wage withheld on the check day; wages should be paid by the seventh because rent and utility bills fall due at the start of the month; and rates should be fixed with the group leaders and a union representative and never cut when a section works fast. [Para 42][Para 58][Para 67][Para 86]

**Employer (respondent):** It paid sick workers only where a public hospital certified hospitalisation, because certificates obtained at home are unreliable; it granted 19 days in the third year, 18 in the fourth and fifth and 19 in the sixth as its reading of Article 166; it bore no recruitment medical costs because those checks preceded employment; it could not pay before the seventh or eighth day because bank transfers and withdrawal formalities took time; and it never reduced rates, only moved workers between sections, with rates guaranteed above the minimum wage. [Para 41][Para 45][Para 49][Para 54][Para 29]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to recognise a medical certificate from any hospital authorised by the competent authority and abide by Clause 4 of its Internal Work Rules; to reimburse 10,100 riel to each worker who paid for a medical check, restricted to those with not more than five years' service and payable within 15 days of the award taking effect; to grant 19 days of annual leave in the fourth, fifth and sixth years and 20 in the seventh, eighth and ninth, rising one working day every three years; and to observe the oral agreement paying wages between the fifth and tenth day; the demand that piece rates never be adjusted was rejected, and the company was instead ordered to run a trial sewing and notify rates in advance. [Para 158][Para 159][Para 160][Para 163][Para 164]

On issue 1 the panel held paragraph 2 of Clause 4(g), which confined certificates to public hospitals, inconsistent with Article 71 paragraph 3, which requires only a qualified doctor; Article 13 being a matter of public order, an employer may give more but not less than the law. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81] Treatment does not require hospitalisation, so home recovery with a doctor's certificate is covered, and the company must meet the medical fees and maintain the wage. [Para 76][Para 82]

On issue 2 the panel found Article 166(4) ambiguous between the parties' readings and interpreted it through the seniority concept in Articles 73, 75, 89 and 95, where rights once gained at a stage of service cannot be reduced, and through equity and the law's purpose; it followed Awards 51/04-Samhan, 62/04-Ecent and 68/05-Gold Lida. [Para 87][Para 90][Para 93][Para 101][Para 109][Para 111]

On issue 3 the panel applied Article 247(c), which obliges the employer to cover physical examination costs, and Article 377, which penalises breach of Article 247, so the obligation covers checks made before recruitment; Decree 38 Article 25 limits claims to five years, hence the restriction to workers with not more than five years' service. [Para 112][Para 117][Para 122][Para 123] It followed Awards 97/04-Great Union, 106/04-Suit Wear and 60/04-United Art. [Para 114][Para 121]

On issue 4 the panel noted that Article 116 requires payment at least once a month without fixing a date, and Article 2 leaves the date to management, but found an oral agreement between the parties for payment between the fifth and tenth day, which binds them both. [Para 128][Para 131][Para 133][Para 135]

On issue 5 the panel held Article 112 requires the employer to inform workers of wage terms before assignment, and Article 2 permits adjusting wages within the minimum-wage floor, so a ban on adjustment could not be granted; but Article 108 obliges a piece-rate employer to set rates so that a worker of mediocre ability earns the minimum wage, which requires a trial sewing of at least eight and at most sixteen hours with three sewers of different speeds. [Para 140][Para 150][Para 151][Para 153][Para 157] A Chinese sewer's trial was held inconsistent with Article 108. [Para 157]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An internal rule that restricts a sick worker's medical certificate to a public hospital is void to the extent it falls below Article 71(3), which requires only a qualified doctor, and the employer must then honour its own more generous rule on wage and medication. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 82] A piece-rate employer must pre-determine rates by a trial sewing of three workers of differing speed so that a worker of mediocre ability reaches the minimum wage, and must notify the rate before assigning the work. [Para 151][Para 140]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that a doctor's certificate is often thought unreliable because the doctor follows what the worker says, and that paying wages on the eighth or ninth day does not offend the parties' agreement so long as payment is not later than the tenth. [Para 45][Para 134]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Clause 4(g) is quoted in three places with differing wording, and the reasoning twice refers to "Article 4(g) of the Company Regulation" as though it were a separate instrument. [Para 63][Para 74][Para 75] Article 166(4) is quoted once as "at the rate of one year per three years of service" and elsewhere as one day per three years. [Para 83][Para 87] The employer's practice is described as payment between the seventh and tenth day in the reasoning, while the order enforces an agreement between the fifth and tenth. [Para 125][Para 163] Article 25 of Decree 38 is dated 28 October 1988 in one citation and 28 October 2005 in another. [Para 122][Para 125] The comparative table of annual-leave positions is scrambled by OCR and the figures used here come from the narrative. [Para 87][Para 111] The signature block carries no names at all, though the panel is named on the face of the award. [Para 167]
