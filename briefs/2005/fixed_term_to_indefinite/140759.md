# Award No. 07/05 — Cambodia Beverage Company Ltd. (Coca Cola) (Arbitration Council, 18 March 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140759

## 1. Facts
Cambodia Beverage Company Ltd. (Coca Cola) ("CBC") bottles at #287, National Road 5, Russey Keo, Phnom Penh and employs 197 workers [Para 57]. On 24 January 2005 the labour inspectorate received a complaint about working conditions; conciliation ran from 26 January to 4 February 2005, settled two of six issues, and referred four to the Council on non-conciliation report No. 226 of 15 February 2005 [Para 57][Para 58][Para 31][Para 32]. The four unresolved demands were reinstatement of two workers transferred and dismissed in the production and sales sections, compliance with the agreement of 23 September 2004 on floating workers, and wages for the strike of 21–22–23 September 2004 [Para 21][Para 24][Para 28][Para 29]. Separately, the company dismissed 36 floating workers in early January 2005 — 20 in sales and 16 in production — all of whom had worked more than a year [Para 102]. The worker party is the Cambodian Industrial Food Union Federation ("CIFUF") together with the Worker Development Union of CBC (Coca Cola) ("WDUCBC") [Para 2]. Both parties agreed at the hearing that the award would be non-binding [Para 57].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the production worker suspended in January 2005 and dismissed in March 2005, rather than hold him on suspension? [Para 21][Para 28]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reinstate the sales worker dismissed on 8 November 2004 for misappropriating a case of soda? [Para 24][Para 26]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must comply with the 23 September 2004 agreement by converting the floating workers into regular workers under the Labour Law? [Para 28][Para 118]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay wages for 21–22–23 September 2004, when the workers struck, under Point 15 of that agreement? [Para 29][Para 123]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Cambodian Industrial Food Union Federation ("CIFUF") and the Worker Development Union of CBC (Coca Cola) ("WDUCBC") demanded the return of both dismissed workers, conversion of the floating workers to regular status as the agreement promised, and wages for the three strike days, arguing that the deductions amounted to retaliation [Para 21][Para 24][Para 28][Para 29][Para 228]. **Employer (respondent):** Cambodia Beverage Company Ltd. ("CBC") refused both reinstatements on the ground of serious misconduct, said the floating-worker issue was already settled and that it had paid those workers as regular staff, and refused strike wages because the strike was unlawful [Para 23][Para 26][Para 30][Para 111].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the reinstatement demands and the strike-wage demand were rejected, but the company was ordered to compensate the floating workers equally for layoff compensation on demand, to redo the calculation for any worker who was underpaid, and to cooperate with union representatives to resolve the matter from the date of the award [Para 234][Para 235][Para 236][Para 237][Para 238].
On jurisdiction, the panel held that the labour inspector and the Minister decide whether a dispute is individual or collective, and that it follows that decision absent clear reasons to depart from it, as in Case 41/04 Mikasa, Case 02/04 Cambodiana and Case 10/03 Jacqsintex [Para 128][Para 129]. It found all three conditions of Article 302 met and so upheld the ministerial finding that the two dismissals were collective disputes [Para 130][Para 141].
On issue 1, Article 295 allows instant suspension pending the labour inspector's decision in a case of serious misconduct, and Article 83(B)(2) counts refusal to comply with the employment contract and sabotage as serious misconduct [Para 148][Para 151][Para 152]. The panel found the worker had refused a task set out in his terms of reference, that his refusal shut the production line for many hours, and that this was comparable to sabotage, while the union-discrimination allegation was unsupported [Para 158][Para 160][Para 166][Para 172][Para 174]. It therefore rejected reinstatement [Para 176].
On issue 2, the worker admitted taking a case of soda meant for a customer [Para 184]. Article 26 requires the employer to act within seven days of learning of the misconduct; the company learned on 7 November 2004 and dismissed on 8 November 2004, and the conduct fell under Article 83(B)(1) [Para 182][Para 184][Para 185][Para 186]. Reinstatement was rejected [Para 186].
On issue 3, Article 9 defines casual work and Article 166(3) treats a worker averaging 21 days a month as continuous; earlier awards convert floating workers who work 21 days over two consecutive months [Para 187][Para 188][Para 189]. The company itself treated all 36 as regular staff on undetermined-duration contracts, paying notice and dismissal indemnity [Para 105][Para 191]. The panel found no evidence of operational requirements, that benefits were omitted from the notice and indemnity calculation, and that the indemnity was miscalculated under Article 168, so the workers were entitled to damages under Article 91 [Para 198][Para 199][Para 200][Para 203][Para 204]. It recommended that the worker who sought reinstatement be given a chance to return [Para 207].
On issue 4, Article 332 suspends the contract during a strike and withholds wages, and the strike lacked the seven working days' notice required by Article 324 [Para 210][Para 221]. Point 15 of the agreement bars retaliation, not deduction of wages, and under Article 23 of Decree-Law No. 38 an unclear contract term is read against the party who drafted it, here the company, so the deduction was proper [Para 226][Para 232][Para 233].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Strikers who strike without the statutory seven working days' notice are not entitled to wages for the strike days, and a clause forbidding retaliation does not forbid the lawful withholding of wages during an unlawful strike; but where an employer has treated floating workers as regular staff under undetermined-duration contracts, they are entitled to a termination calculation made under the Labour Law, and any calculation that omits benefits or understates the dismissal indemnity must be redone [Para 221][Para 226][Para 199][Para 236].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's suggestion that the employer should consider letting the dismissed worker who sought reinstatement return was a recommendation, not an order [Para 207].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is repeatedly split mid-sentence and duplicated, producing passages where the same clause appears twice in a row or where a finding and its denial run together [Para 79][Para 101]. One worker is headed "Mrs." in the evidence list and "Mr." throughout the findings [Para 41][Para 148]. His surname is spelled two ways within the award, and the same inconsistency affects the second worker [Para 24][Para 45][Para 93]. The decisive dates are abbreviated or garbled in the hearing record ("16 March 05", "21 On 27 January 2005") [Para 73][Para 78]. The whole operative section is compressed into a single numbered line, so the individual orders are hard to separate [Para 234]. Both parties agreed the award would be non-binding, yet the closing clause states that it binds after 8 days unless opposed [Para 57][Para 242].
