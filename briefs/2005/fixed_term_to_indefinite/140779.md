# Award No. 34/05-Jacqsintex — Jacqsintex Industrial Cambodia Co., Ltd and C.CAWDU (Arbitration Council, 27 June 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Ann Vireak, chair Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140779

## 1. Facts
Jacqsintex Industrial Cambodia Co., Ltd ("JIC"), at Moal village, Sangkat Dangkau, Khan Dangkau, Phnom Penh, employs 385 workers. [Para 4] [Para 40] On 20 May 2005 the district labour inspectors received a call from workers demanding improvement of five working conditions; conciliation on 31 May 2005 settled four, and the single remaining issue, reinstatement of three union leaders, came to the panel on 3 June 2005, which heard it on 7 and 15 June 2005. [Para 16] [Para 17] All three had worked on successive six-month fixed-duration contracts and were told their contracts would not be renewed, each receiving notice between 15 and 18 days before expiry. [Para 46] [Para 57] [Para 64] A local union election was held on 9 May 2005 at the headquarters of the Dama Thpaktay Federation Union, at which Mr P became secretary, Mr L adviser and Mr K vice-president, and neither they nor the union notified the company in advance of the election, the candidates or the result. [Para 67] [Para 68]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company decided not to renew the three contracts because the workers were union leaders? [Para 69] [Para 74]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the non-renewal of the three contracts at their expiry date was lawful, given Article 67 paragraph 2 and the notice period in Article 73? [Para 84] [Para 97]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the protection of union leaders under Article 293 of the Labour Law requires Labour Inspector authorisation before a fixed-duration contract is allowed to expire? [Para 98]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the demand for reinstatement is in substance a request to be rehired, which the employer is entitled to refuse? [Para 103] [Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that the company discriminates against unionism, that it always terminates employees who are union leaders, that the three committed no fault, that it hired temporary workers including former employees after their departure, and that as union leaders they were protected and must be reinstated. [Para 69] [Para 72] [Para 76] The union also argued that past practice showed the company forcing activists to resign to reduce membership. [Para 81]
**Employer (respondent):** that each worker held a fixed-duration contract giving the company the right to terminate at expiry, that there was not enough work, that it did not know of the election because no union document had been notified or submitted, and that it first learned of it during the Ministry's conciliation. [Para 14] [Para 69] [Para 70]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' claim that the company reinstate the three, finding no evidence of union discrimination and no requirement of Labour Inspector authorisation for a contract allowed to run its term. [Para 106]
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 12 of the Labour Law, which forbids using union participation to decide whether to hire, discipline or terminate a worker, but found the union's assertions of a pattern of terminating leaders unsupported and the company's denial unshaken. [Para 75] [Para 76] [Para 83] It held the employee responsible for notifying the employer of a union election, and found no immediate notification and no evidence that the company knew of the election when it gave notice on 10 and 30 May 2005. [Para 78]
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 67 paragraph 2, under which a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years in total, and followed 10/03-Jacqsintex Industrial of 23 July 2003, holding that a contract whose original and renewed terms together exceed two years becomes of undetermined duration. [Para 84] [Para 85] [Para 86] All three had worked less than two years in total, so their contracts remained of fixed duration. [Para 87] Under Article 73 a fixed-duration contract terminates at its stated date, and notice is required for contracts over six months; the panel found the notice given was the legally required notice, so termination at expiry was lawful. [Para 88] [Para 90] [Para 96] [Para 97]
On Issue 3 the panel held that Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 7 of Prakas 313 protect the union president, vice-president and secretary against dismissal, but that dismissal means ending a contract still in operation. [Para 98] [Para 101] Allowing a contract to expire at its agreed date is not dismissal, and the union itself accepted the contracts had expired, so Article 293 did not apply. [Para 102]
On Issue 4 the panel treated the reinstatement demand as a request to renew and continue work, that is a question of rehiring, which Article 2 of the Labour Law leaves to the employer; absent evidence that refusal was based on union activity, the demand failed. [Para 103] [Para 105] [Para 106]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Because Article 293 protects union leaders against dismissal, and dismissal means ending a contract still running, it does not require Labour Inspector authorisation before a fixed-duration contract is allowed to expire at its agreed date. [Para 101] [Para 102] Non-renewal at expiry is lawful where the contract remained of fixed duration under Article 67 paragraph 2 and the notice required by Article 73 was given; a contract whose original and renewed terms together exceed two years converts to undetermined duration and cannot then be ended by expiry. [Para 86] [Para 90] [Para 97] It is for the workers and their union to notify the employer of an election, its candidates and result; without that notification and without evidence of anti-union motive, refusing to renew and to rehire does not breach Article 12, and rehiring is the employer's prerogative under Article 2. [Para 78] [Para 105]

**Obiter dicta:** that the company rehired temporary workers, many of them former employees, after the terminations, a fact noted but given no legal weight. [Para 72] [Para 73]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer appears as Jacqsintex Industrial Cambodia Co., Ltd on the face of the award and as Jacqsintex Garment Industrial Cambodia Co. Ltd in the facts, while its village is printed "Moal", "Muol" and "Mul" in successive paragraphs. [Para 4] [Para 39] [Para 40] The commercial registration certificate is dated 29 May 11998, and one hearing is dated "15 June 205". [Para 25] [Para 19] The notice dates do not reconcile: the vice-president is said to have been told on 30 May 2005 of a contract expiring 14 June 2005, and the adviser's expiry is put at 14 June 2005 with notice on 30 May although his own contracts ran to 26 May 2005. [Para 57] [Para 64] [Para 96] The union vice-president's name appears in four spellings, one of them attaching a different given name, and one passage names him as secretary rather than vice-president; initials are used here. [Para 66] [Para 40] The non-conciliation report is cited as No. 779 of 2 June 2005 but said to have been submitted on 3 June 2005. [Para 16] [Para 38] OCR runs words together throughout ("themployer", "withe", "athearing"). [Para 74] [Para 99]
