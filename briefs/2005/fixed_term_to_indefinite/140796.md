# Award No. 64/05-Chian Hwey — Chian Hwey Shoes Co., Ltd and Cambodian Union Federation (CUF) (Arbitration Council, 30 November 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea chair) · Doc ID 140796

## 1. Facts
Chian Hwey Shoes Co., Ltd, on Veng Sreng road, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employs 1,215 workers [Para 14]. Four workers — referred to here as Mr Y, Mr R, Mr T and Mr N — had worked for the company for more than two years on unwritten contracts of undetermined duration, and three of them were under 25 [Para 15][Para 26]. The employer dismissed all four on 30 September 2005, notifying the Department of Labour Dispute Resolution, for three acts: riding motorbikes inside the factory, eating peanuts at work stations and staying in the rest room during working hours [Para 22][Para 24]. Of six disputes, two were settled at conciliation and three more at the hearings, leaving only the dismissal [Para 8][Para 9][Para 12].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the four dismissed union leaders were entitled to the protection afforded to worker delegates, and whether their dismissal was legal? [Para 36]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), CUF and CUF of Chian Hwey Factory:** the four were elected as factory union leaders at a meeting on 18 September 2005 and notified to the director on 21 September 2005, so their dismissal was unlawful discrimination against the union [Para 16][Para 17].
**Employer (respondent), Chian Hwey Shoes Co., Ltd:** the company never received the notification letter, and dismissed the four for misconduct — motorbikes in the compound, peanuts at work stations and rest-room loitering — because they caused trouble and ignored instructions [Para 2][Para 18][Para 22].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — order Chian Hwey Company Shoes Co, Ltd. to reinstate Mr Y, Mr R, Mr T and Mr N and pay all wages starting from the issuance date of this award, 30 November 2005, and not from the dismissal [Para 58].
On protection, Article 3(3) of Prakas 305/01 gives a leadership candidate the same protection as a worker delegate from 45 days before the election to 45 days after it, and Article 4(1)-(2) extends that protection to founding members and joiners from the filing of the registration application, for 30 days after registration, and thereafter to three union leaders under Articles 282 and 293 of the Labour Law [Para 37][Para 39][Para 41]. Because the union must inform the employer of the candidacy by any reliable means, and the panel found the election date contradictory — the minute said morning, the notification letter said 8:00 p.m. — and no reliable evidence that the employer was told, the panel declined to apply delegate protection and turned to the dismissal itself [Para 19][Para 48][Para 49][Para 51].
On the dismissal, Article 74 requires a valid reason relating to aptitude or behaviour, and Article 74 read with Article 27 requires proportionality [Para 54]. Under Article 10(a) of the company's Internal Work Rules, driving a motorbike and leaving duties are minor misconduct, calling for a verbal warning, then a written warning, then dismissal, and Article 10(c.1) requires heavy loss for serious misconduct, which the employer did not prove [Para 55][Para 57]. The employer admitted it neither followed the Internal Work Rules nor sought Labour Inspector authorisation [Para 25][Para 57]. Article 34(a) of Prakas 99/04 empowered the panel to order reinstatement [Para 58].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Delegate protection for union leaders depends on the employer being informed of the candidacy by reliable means; where that is not proved, the dismissal is still judged on ordinary law, and dismissal for minor misconduct listed in the Internal Work Rules without the prescribed verbal and written warnings, and without proof of heavy loss, is unlawful and requires reinstatement [Para 49][Para 57][Para 58].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Article 3 of Prakas 305/01 requires the union to inform the employer but does not itself withdraw protection when the employer is uninformed [Para 47].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The fourth worker's surname is printed "Noun Sarann" in the findings and "Nuon Sarann" elsewhere [Para 22][Para 58]; the employer is "Chian Hwey Shoes Co., Ltd" at the head but "Chian Hwey Company Shoes Co, Ltd." in the order [Para 1][Para 58]. Two hearing dates, 27 October and 8 November 2005, are printed in the same block, and the conciliation report is dated both 21 September and 24 October 2005 [Para 6][Para 10]. The order pays wages only from the date the award issues, although the dismissal was on 30 September 2005, with no explanation of the gap [Para 22][Para 58]. The claimant union's election minute and registration form are recorded as unsigned [Para 13]. OCR drops "th" throughout ("themloyer", "athe", "withemployer"). Individual workers appear here only as initials.
