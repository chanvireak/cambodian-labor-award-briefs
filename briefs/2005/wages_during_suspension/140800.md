# Award No. 72/05-North Gaiety — North Gaiety Apparel Co, Ltd and National Industrial Federation Trade Union of Cambodia (NIFTUC) (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, An Nan, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140800

## 1. Facts
North Gaiety Apparel Co, Ltd, at National Road 2, Toul Roka Village, Sangkat Chak Angreh Krom, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh, employs 1,103 workers and began business in 1999 [Para 1][Para 23][Para 24]. Facing a lack of orders, the company asked the Ministry of Labour to authorise a two-month suspension of all labour contracts from 1 December 2005 to 31 January 2006 [Para 4][Para 25]. It began the suspension on 1 December 2005 before any reply, and the Ministry's authorisation letter issued only on 13 December 2005 [Para 26][Para 31][Para 32]. In earlier years the company had paid 50 percent of wages and 50 percent of the attendance bonus during lay-offs, the bonus under a written agreement of 16 August 2004 [Para 27][Para 28]. The panel also addressed the union's demand for an assurance that the factory would not close [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the two-month labour contract suspension from 1 December 2005 was lawful, and whether the workers are entitled to 50 percent of wages and the attendance bonus during it as on earlier lay-offs? [Para 2][Para 33][Para 37]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), NIFTUC and NIFTUC of North Gaiety:** the company must honour the 16 August 2005 agreement on 50 percent payment for the lay-off period, and US$5 for accommodation is not enough [Para 2][Para 26][Para 34].
**Employer (respondent), North Gaiety Apparel Co, Ltd:** a financial crisis with no orders forced the suspension; the company requested the two-month suspension from the Labour Inspection Department and pays only the US$5 accommodation allowance that the Labour Law requires [Para 3][Para 4][Para 34].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the correct amount calculated for 1 to 12 December 2005 and US$8.269 for the suspension period from 13 December 2005 to 31 January 2006, and declined to consider the demand for an assurance that the factory would not close [Para 67][Para 68]. The award becomes binding 8 days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition [Para 69].
Article 71(11) of the Labour Law permits suspension of enterprise operations for serious economic difficulty, for no more than two months, under the control of the Labour Inspector [Para 37]. Following 22/05 Ocean, the panel held that a de jure suspension requires both a request and a written authorisation issued after inspection, and that silence by the Inspector is refusal [Para 41][Para 47][Para 48]. The request was made by letters of 11 November and 1 December 2005 but authorisation came only on 13 December 2005, so the period 1 to 12 December 2005 was unlawful and full wages were due — for a worker on the US$45 minimum wage, (US$45 / 26) x 12 = US$20.77, with higher earners calculated under Article 103 [Para 43][Para 45][Para 50][Para 53].
For the authorised period, Article 72(1) suspends only the main obligations to work and to pay wages, while obligations such as accommodation continue [Para 54][Para 55]. Article 13 permits an employer to grant benefits superior to the law, and the panel found the US$5 monthly accommodation allowance appropriate for the lawful period [Para 57][Para 58][Para 59].
On the second demand, the panel held the decision whether to close a business belongs to the employer under Article 71 and is outside its jurisdiction, following 28/04 Grand Hotel [Para 62][Para 64][Para 66].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A labour contract suspension is lawful only from the date the Labour Inspector issues written authorisation, not from the date the employer applies, so wages are owed in full for the interval before authorisation; once authorisation issues, Article 72(1) relieves the employer of wage payment and only a voluntarily granted allowance such as accommodation survives [Para 48][Para 54][Para 58].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel remarked that it was sympathetic to the employer because the Ministry did not reply in time, and that a decision to close a factory is an employer prerogative under Article 71 rather than an arbitrable demand [Para 44][Para 66].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The case number is printed "72105-North Gaiety", the slash lost [Para 1]. The company's address appears as building 633 in the party block and building 636 in the findings [Para 1][Para 23]. The suspension request letters are variously dated 11 November, 14 November and 1 December 2005 [Para 25][Para 31][Para 38]. The award orders US$8.269 for the authorised period although its own formula of US$5 divided by 26 days over 17 days yields about US$3.27, so the figure cannot be reconciled with the method stated [Para 61][Para 62][Para 67]. The unlawful period is described as ending on 12 December in one place and "up to 13 December" in another, and the 12-day count is inconsistent with that [Para 49][Para 50]. OCR drops "th" and loses the slash in dates throughout. Union officers, worker representatives and the company director appear here only by role.
