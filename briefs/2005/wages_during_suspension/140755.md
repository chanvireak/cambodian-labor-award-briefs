# Award No. 02/05 — Bu Min Cambodian Garment (Arbitration Council, 27 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vuoch Hun, Tuon Siphann, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140755

## 1. Facts
Bu Min Cambodian Garment employs 600 workers at National Road 2, Chak Angre Krom commune, Mean Chey district, Phnom Penh [Para 1][Para 20]. On 3 January 2005 the workers complained by telephone to the Mean Chey labour inspectors, raising five matters; conciliation settled four and left one unresolved, which the Ministry sent to the Council on non-conciliation report No. 025 dated 6 January 2005 [Para 20][Para 21][Para 11]. The unresolved demand was that the company dismiss its General Manager and three group leaders — the Group 5 leader, the Group 8 leader and the QC team leader (the workers are identified here by role, not by name) [Para 9][Para 22]. The workers alleged that the General Manager failed to announce a planned suspension of 200 staff and a dismissal, withheld leave and took bribes, and that the three leaders insulted subordinates, refused leave and passed on confidential information [Para 28][Para 30][Para 32][Para 34]. Both parties agreed at the hearing that the award would be non-binding [Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must dismiss the General Manager and the three group leaders because of the misconduct the workers allege against them? [Para 9][Para 22]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Cambodian Labor Union Federation (CLUF) and Worker Union of Bu Min (WUBM) argued that the four managers were dangerous to the workforce — that the General Manager concealed the planned suspension of 200 workers and a dismissal, refused leave, and took bribes on recruitment, and that the group leaders insulted workers and leaked confidential information — so the company should dismiss all four [Para 28][Para 30][Para 34][Para 35].
**Employer (respondent):** Bu Min Cambodian Garment, through its Director and the four managers whose dismissal was sought, denied every allegation and refused the demand [Para 10][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the employees' claim that the company dismiss the General Manager and the three group leaders [Para 70].
The panel held that workers have no right to order or demand that an employer dismiss anyone, manager, supervisor or worker, and followed a long line of its own awards to that effect [Para 57][Para 58]. It grounded that rule in Article 65 of the 1997 Labour Law, under which a labour contract establishes the working relationship between worker and employer, and in Article 22 of Decree-Law No. 38 on Contracts, under which a contract is law between the parties and may be amended only by agreement, so that hiring and dismissal are the employer's alone [Para 59][Para 61][Para 64]. It added that even where a worker commits serious misconduct of the kind described in Article 83, the Labour Law gives the employer the right to dismiss but does not require it [Para 65].
On the facts, the panel found no evidence that the four managers had caused danger to any worker [Para 40][Para 55]. The General Manager was only the company's General Manager and could not decide suspensions, dismissals or leave on her own, those matters having to pass through the Director and other officers [Para 45]; the corruption allegation was a bare verbal assertion [Para 47]. The suspension of 200 workers was planned only: the company notified the Ministry on 22 December 2004 because it had no buyers, found buyers on 31 December 2004, and cancelled the plan the same day [Para 48][Para 49][Para 50]. The dismissal of a worker on 24 December 2004 was the company's own act, for serious misconduct, after warning letters the worker had thumbprinted [Para 51]. As for the three leaders, the union produced no written warnings or witnesses, only dissatisfaction with their management style [Para 52][Para 53][Para 54][Para 68].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union or group of workers cannot compel an employer to dismiss a manager, supervisor or fellow worker: dismissal lies within the employer's contractual prerogative, and even proven serious misconduct under Article 83 gives the employer a right to dismiss rather than a duty to do so [Para 65][Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of the company's leave procedure — that a group leader and supervisor screen requests and the final say rests with a manager — was recorded as background and played no part in the order [Para 37].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The application to suspend 200 workers is dated "22 December 2005" in the findings but 22 December 2004 everywhere else [Para 36][Para 43][Para 48]. The dismissed worker's date of dismissal appears as 22 December 2004 and as 24 December 2004 [Para 43][Para 51]. The natures of the General Manager are printed inconsistently as "Mr." and "Miss." across the findings [Para 45][Para 68]. Both parties agreed the award would be non-binding, yet the standard clause still states it binds after 8 days unless opposed [Para 20][Para 73]. OCR drops "th" throughout ("themployer", "athe"), and the panel's names are split across four numbered lines, one of which attaches the worker-chosen arbitrator's label to the wrong name [Para 12][Para 13].
