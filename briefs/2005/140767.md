# Award No. 19/05 — In Kyung Company v. Cambodian Union Federation (CUF) (Arbitration Council, 19 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, An Nan, Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 140767

## 1. Facts
In Kyung Company operates at Chamcar Ouv Lek, Sangkat Kakab, Dangkor district, Phnom Penh, and employs 620 workers [Para 24]. Ms S began work there on 28 August 2004 on a basic wage of US$60 per month, which included a US$5 monthly attendance bonus [Para 31]. She was pregnant and then miscarried, took a week's leave in early November 2004, and was then asked by the employer to take a further month off at full wage of US$65 per month [Para 33][Para 34]. She returned in mid-December 2004 and signed a resignation letter on 14 December 2004, but the employer reinstated her and the other resigning workers on 15 December 2004 [Para 35][Para 36]. The company then terminated her; she had become union secretary in December 2004 and her husband, the union's factory representative, was also dismissed [Para 41]. Of five working conditions demanded by the workers, four were conciliated and one remained unresolved [Para 25].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the termination of Ms S was lawful, given that she was a union secretary holding the protection the law gives to union leaders at the time of dismissal? [Para 46][Para 47]
2. Whether Ms S is entitled to reinstatement in the same position and to payment of wages and other benefits from February 2005 onward? [Para 56][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Cambodian Union Federation (CUF)):** The dismissal was linked to Ms S's involvement with the union; she had become union secretary in December 2004 and the company had also dismissed her husband, the union's factory representative; the company had paid no salary since February 2005 [Para 37][Para 39][Para 41].
**Employer (In Kyung Company):** Ms S resigned, so there was no dismissal to answer for [Para 28]. The employer accepted her back at work but denied that the termination was due to her union involvement and said it did not know when she was elected secretary [Para 41][Para 42]. It still wanted to terminate her and pay termination compensation, without explaining why [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel accepted the workers' claim for Ms S's reinstatement to the same position and ordered the employer to pay her wages and other benefits in the amount of at least US$65 per month starting from February 2005 onward [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].
The panel framed the decisive question as whether Ms S was a union secretary, a position protected by law, at the time of her dismissal [Para 46][Para 47]. It found that she was, and that union leaders are entitled to protection under clause 4 of Prakas 305/01 [Para 48][Para 49]. An employer wishing to terminate such a leader must respect Article 293 of the Labour Law and obtain the approval of the Labour Inspector before dismissing, citing the provision printed as Article 29(1) [Para 50]. The employer did not follow those procedures and produced no evidence that it had contacted the labour inspectors [Para 51]. The panel therefore held that the termination could not lawfully be made and that she should also receive a payment reflecting her statutory entitlements [Para 52]. It noted that the parties had negotiated at the hearing and should still look for an agreement on this point, but that failing agreement the employer must reinstate her in the same position and pay her wages and benefits, the dismissal being unlawful and made without the Labour Inspector's approval [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55][Para 56].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker holds office as a union leader, the employer cannot lawfully terminate her without first obtaining the approval of the Labour Inspector as required by Article 293 of the Labour Law; a dismissal made without that approval is unlawful, and the remedy is reinstatement in the same position together with wages and other benefits running from the date of the unlawful act [Para 50][Para 52][Para 56].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel encouraged the parties to reach their own agreement on the outstanding point, and recorded that both sides had agreed at the hearing that the award would be non-binding [Para 23][Para 54].
