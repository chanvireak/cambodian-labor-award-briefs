# Award No. 62/05-ZhinTai — Zhin Tai Co. Ltd. and Khmer Youth Union at Zhin Tai (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Liv Sovanna, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140794

## 1. Facts
Zhin Tai Co. Ltd., at Domnak village, Phnom Penh Thmei commune, Resey Keo, Phnom Penh, was established in December 2004 and employs approximately 500 workers [Para 20]. The Khmer Youth Union at Zhin Tai (សហជីពយុវជនខ្មែរនៅហាងជីនតាយ) was registered in June 2005, and its representatives had been collecting dues directly from members [Para 23]. In July 2005 the union asked the company to withhold 1,000 riel per member from wages, supporting the request with a list of 221 members who had each applied in writing [Para 21]. The company refused, and six of the union's seven demands were settled at conciliation on 29 September 2005, leaving the deduction demand alone [Para 13].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must deduct 1,000 riel in Khmer Youth Union dues from the wages of each member who has authorized the deduction in writing? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Union at Zhin Tai:** deduction is authorized by Article 129 of the Labour Law and by Prakas 305/01, and direct collection is unworkable because the short rest breaks make it difficult to gather dues [Para 24][Para 27].
**Employer (respondent), Zhin Tai Co. Ltd.:** the Labour Law does not require an employer to deduct union dues from wages, deduction confuses workers about why their pay is lower, and the company's buyers Levi Strauss and AMC Lin Fing disapprove of such deductions [Para 22][Para 21]. The employer could not identify which articles of the Labour Law it relied on [Para 23].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct union dues of 1,000 riel from the wages of each worker who is a member of the Khmer Youth Union and who has authorized the employer in writing to do so [Para 34]. The award is binding immediately on receipt [Para 35].
Article 129 paragraph 2 of the 1997 Labour Law provides that a worker may authorize deductions from wages for dues to the trade union to which he belongs, that the authorization must be in writing, and that it may be revoked at any time [Para 27][Para 28]. Article 5 of Prakas No. 305/01 adds that a member may request in writing, at least 15 days in advance, that dues be withheld in accordance with Article 129 [Para 29]. Article 112 obliges the employer to inform workers precisely of the items making up their wage each pay period, which answers the employer's fear of confusion at the pay window [Para 30].
The panel held that on the law and the Prakas the employer is required to deduct when a written request is made, following its earlier awards in 03/03 Tonga Garment, 05/03 Top One, 94/04 Enternity Apparel, 99/04 A.I.A and 16/05 New Point World Trade Cambodia [Para 30]. It addressed Article 281, which forbids employers to deduct union dues and to pay them for workers: that article targets employer interference in union affairs, as defined by Article 280, not a deduction made at the worker's own written request [Para 31][Para 32].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must withhold union dues from a member's wage where the member has authorized the deduction in writing at least 15 days in advance, and Article 281 of the Labour Law does not excuse refusal, because that article prohibits employer interference with unions rather than worker-authorized deduction [Para 31][Para 29].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's account of Article 280 — interference includes measures tending to create employer-dominated worker organizations, support for a workers' organization, disciplinary measures and dismissal — was explanatory of Article 281 and not itself applied [Para 33].
