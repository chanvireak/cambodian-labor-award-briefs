# Award No. 71/05-Phnom Penh Garment City — Phnom Penh City Garment Co., Ltd and Labour Free Union of Lever Khmer (LFULK) (Arbitration Council, 6 December 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (You Suonty, Ven Pov, Kong Phallack chair) · Doc ID 140799

## 1. Facts
Phnom Penh City Garment Co., Ltd, on National Road No. 2, Sangkat Chak Angre Kraum, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, employs approximately 700 female and 153 male workers [Para 1][Para 21]. Between 30 and 40 of the women gave birth in the year from January 2005 [Para 22]. The company has neither a nursing room nor a day-care centre; instead it allows each mother a half-hour break morning and afternoon, from 10:30 a.m. and 12:30 p.m., and the women usually go home to feed their children [Para 22][Para 23]. It says its premises are too small and shared with the Gold Lida factory [Para 23].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must erect a day-care centre and nursing room, or instead provide milk or cash so that mothers can breast-feed? [Para 9][Para 10]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), LFULK and Khmer Labour Free Union at Phnom Penh City Garment Company:** the company must build a day-care centre and nursing room as the Labour Law requires, and if it cannot, must at least provide milk or cash to mothers who breast-feed [Para 9][Para 10].
**Employer (respondent), Phnom Penh City Garment Co., Ltd:** the premises are too small — there is not even accommodation for the Chief of the Cutting Section — and the company cannot provide milk or cash either [Para 11].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the company to build a day-care centre and nursing room under Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training supervision, or to pay the cost of caring for children over 18 months old against receipts from mothers who send them to outside day care, after the award takes effect [Para 37]. The award becomes binding 8 days after notification unless a party lodges written opposition [Para 38].
Article 186 of the Labour Law requires an enterprise with at least 100 women workers to set up a nursing room and day-care centre in or near the establishment, and where that is impossible for children over 18 months, to place them in a day-care centre at the employer's charge [Para 26][Para 27]. Article 187 leaves conditions to a Prakas of the labour ministry [Para 28]. The panel read Article 186 as offering two options — build, or pay cash — following 83/04 June Textile Company [Para 29].
On the alternative demand, the panel refused to order milk or milk money: earlier awards 63/04 Shinewell and 86/04 City New held that formula or payment in lieu of a nursing room is not permitted, because the law aims to encourage direct breast-feeding [Para 31][Para 32]. Article 184 gives mothers one hour a day during working hours for this purpose [Para 33]. Feeding children is a parental duty under Article 47 of the Constitution and Article 115 of the Family Law, which cannot be shifted to the employer [Para 34][Para 36][Para 37]. The panel followed 53/04 Kong Hong, 94/04 Eternity Apparel and 107/04 Jacqsintex on the employer's obligation [Para 37].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Article 186 of the Labour Law obliges an enterprise with 100 or more women workers either to build a nursing room and day-care centre or to pay for outside day care of children over 18 months against receipts, but it does not permit the employer to discharge the duty by supplying milk or cash for milk, because feeding a child is a parental obligation the employer cannot assume [Para 29][Para 36].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the law's preference for direct breast-feeding is what makes formula an inadequate substitute, though no order turned on that preference alone [Para 32].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer is named "Phnom Penh City Garment Co., Ltd" in the party block, while the case title and findings call it "Phnom Penh Garment City" [Para 1][Para 21]. The non-conciliation report's date is scrambled ("dated , 8 November 2005" with fragments repeated) [Para 12]. The panel states that Article 184 requires one hour per day for breast-feeding, whereas the findings describe two half-hour breaks [Para 33][Para 22]. The reasons speak of "two options" but are set out as build-or-pay with no third alternative [Para 29].
