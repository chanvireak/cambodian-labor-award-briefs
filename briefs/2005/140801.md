# Award No. 76/05 — Global Footwear Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 January 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Ann Vireak, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140801

## 1. Facts
Global Footwear Co., Ltd of Ang Snuol faced a collective dispute brought by the Federation of Democratic Thoamear Union and the Democratic Thoamear Union of the company, a union registered on 30 August 2005 with 501 members out of 1,390 workers but no representative status. [Para 1][Para 49] Of thirteen demands conciliated on 15 December 2005, five were settled and eight remained, and two more — transport after overtime and public-hospital medical certificates — were settled at the hearing. [Para 24][Para 25][Para 29] The dispute's centre was Mr B, a warehouse worker since 28 March 2002 on an average wage of US$60 per month, elected a worker delegate and union president on 31 July 2005, whom the company suspended without pay on 3 December 2005 and asked the Labour Inspection Office to let it terminate. [Para 52][Para 54]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate Mr B, worker delegate and union president, whom it suspended without pay and sought authority to dismiss? [Para 50]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay a 1,000 riel meal allowance for overtime from 6:00 p.m. to 8:00 p.m.? [Para 78]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay holiday work at triple, rather than double, the normal rate? [Para 85]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay US$30 per month towards children's meals and external day-care instead of building an on-site centre? [Para 86]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must dismiss its administrator at the workers' demand? [Para 88]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the company must maintain 100 per cent of wage and fringe benefits where a worker is absent on sick leave certified by a public hospital but stays outside it? [Para 95]
7. (Issue 7) Whether the company must pay US$8 of seniority benefit at six years and US$11 at seven years? [Para 97]
8. (Issue 8) Whether the company must hold a re-election, or a by-election, of worker delegates to fill vacant seats? [Para 100][Para 105]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The 1,000 riel meal allowance was justified by rising commodity prices and the workers' voluntary contribution to a prosperity that had produced two further factories; holiday work is overtime and should attract triple pay as at Camdel and Chuo Shing; US$30 was needed for child meals and outside care; the administrator was difficult, had threatened workers and ignored the Labour Law; and full wage plus all bonuses should continue through certified sick leave. [Para 79][Para 85][Para 89][Para 95]
**Employer (respondent):** It respected freedom of association but would continue the suspension for misconduct under its Internal Work Rules; it could not fund the meal allowance although it acknowledged rising prices, paid holiday work under Prakas 10/99, offered US$5 per month for external day-care, and would comply with the Labour Law on seniority; the administrator only executed company decisions; dismissal was pending ministerial approval and would attract only the last month's wage and unused annual leave. [Para 12][Para 80][Para 87][Para 78]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to reinstate Mr B and pay his wage from 18 December 2005 to the date the award takes effect, to pay US$15 per month for external child care until it builds its own centre, and to pay 100 per cent of wage in the first month of certified sick leave, 60 per cent in the second and third and zero with employment preserved from the fourth to the sixth, plus a pro-rata attendance bonus; the 1,000 riel allowance, the dismissal demand and the re-election demand were rejected, and the triple holiday pay and US$8/US$11 seniority claims were not considered. [Para 233][Para 234]

On issue 1 the panel tested the dismissal against Article 83(B)(2), (3) and (4) and found the refusal of a 2 December 2005 assignment reasonable, since Mr B was counting buttons within his own section rather than idling. [Para 107][Para 111][Para 112] Distributing ceremonial paper after permission was refused was misconduct, but a first infraction deserving a warning; walking the buildings during the two hours per week allowed to delegates had always been tolerated. [Para 118][Para 125] The company held only draft Internal Work Rules, and Article 24 requires them to be visaed by the Labour Inspector before taking effect, so neither the rules nor a contract lacking a job description could ground dismissal. [Para 131][Para 132][Para 135] Under Article 27 a sanction must be proportional, and the actual suspension from 3 December 2005 to 20 January 2006 exceeded what equity allowed. [Para 137][Para 139]

On issues 2 and 3, Section 4 of Notice 017/00 entitles a voluntary overtime worker to 1,000 riel per day or one free meal without fixing a duration, and Article 4 of Prakas 10/99 gives 200 per cent of normal wage for holiday work, so both demands went beyond the law and became interest disputes. [Para 140][Para 144][Para 156][Para 157] Because the union lacked most representative status under Article 277 it had no capacity to bring an interest dispute, and Clause 43 of Prakas 99/04 would turn any award into a collective agreement binding all workers for a year. [Para 148][Para 151][Para 152] Issue 7 failed on the same ground, Notice 017 capping seniority benefit at US$5 per month. [Para 221][Para 222]

On issue 4, Articles 184 to 186 leave an employer two options, and a company unable to build a centre must pay the external charge, set at the US$15 per month established in Award 68/04-City New. [Para 166][Para 174][Para 176] On issue 5, Article 65 and Article 22 of Law-Decree No. 38 make hiring and firing the employer's own right, Article 83 confers a power and not a duty, and the panel could order removal only where a worker endangers others. [Para 182][Para 183][Para 187] On issue 6, Articles 71(3) and 72(1) suspend the contract without guaranteeing wage, so the panel followed Awards 24/03-Thab Garment and 26/03-Sportswear on the declining scale, with attendance bonus deducted at US$0.19 per day absent. [Para 193][Para 203][Para 219] On issue 8, Article 312 confined the panel to the non-conciliation report and Article 298 left election fairness to the courts, but the complaint was really a shortage of delegates, curable by seating the highest-polling waiting-list candidates for the term ending 8 January 2007. [Para 225][Para 226][Para 230]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A worker delegate may be dismissed for misconduct only as a serious infraction defined by valid Internal Work Rules visaed by the Labour Inspector under Article 24, and any sanction must be proportional to the misconduct under Article 27, so the suspension was unlawful and reinstatement with back pay followed. [Para 132][Para 136][Para 137][Para 139] A demand that exceeds a statutory minimum — a longer meal allowance, triple holiday pay, seniority above the Notice 017 scale — is an interest dispute, which a union without most representative status under Article 277 has no capacity to bring. [Para 144][Para 148][Para 157]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's suggestions that the company establish and publicise a complaint-handling procedure and include paid sick leave in future Internal Work Rules; and that the two weekly hours allowed to delegates be used without disrupting the production line. [Para 189][Para 206][Para 123]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated 20 December 2005 in the jurisdiction section and 7 December 2005 in the procedural history, and the hearing is given as 5 December 2006 in an award dated 20 January 2006. [Para 24][Para 26][Para 27] The employer appears variously as Global Footwear Co., Ltd and Global Footwear Inter. Corp., and the sister firm Sky Star Footwear Cambodia Co., Ltd. is named separately. [Para 1][Para 35] The administrator's name is printed in at least three different spellings and is reduced here to his role. [Para 19][Para 88] The worker's surname is spelled two ways, and the order pays his wage from 18 December 2005 while the reasoning treats the suspension as running from 3 December 2005; both dates are reported as printed. [Para 54][Para 139][Para 233] The OCR fuses words throughout ("themployer"), and one passage on delegate communication is duplicated verbatim. [Para 123][Para 140]
