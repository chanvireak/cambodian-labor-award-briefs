# Award No. 11/05 — South Bay Company v Khmer Youth Free Trade Union (KYFTU) and CCAWDUnion of South Bay Factory (Arbitration Council, 1 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Ly Tayseng (employer party), Mr Huon Chundy (worker party), Mr Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140761

## 1. Facts
South Bay Company, at Building E, Vathanak Garden street, Chaum Chao quarter, Dangkau district, Phnom Penh, employs 620 workers [Para 22]. Workers raised 13 demands; eight were conciliated before the referral and two more (issues 2 and 5) at the hearing, leaving three for the award [Para 22][Para 23]. Women who have given birth make up 5% of the 620 workers [Para 24]. The company had arranged a nursing room and day-care centre in Building F of Golden Crown company, under an agreement dated 8 December 2004 [Para 26][Para 27]. About 60 workers paid US$6 each for medical checkups; the company paid 1,000 Riel of the 15,000 Riel fee and deducted the remaining 14,000 Riel from wages in January 2005 [Para 42]. Working hours run 7:30 to 11:30 and 12:30 to 16:30, and the company had moved the gate opening from 12:00 to 12:10 for the small gate and 12:15 for the large gate [Para 44][Para 45].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must allow women who have given birth one hour per day, half an hour in the morning and half an hour in the afternoon, to leave and breastfeed their babies? [Para 14][Para 25]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the company must reimburse health check fees of US$3.50 (14,000 Riel) to each worker under Article 247 of the Labour Law? [Para 15][Para 68]
3. (Issue 4) Whether the company must open the factory gate for workers to enter the compound at 12:00 noon? [Para 15][Para 77]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Free Trade Union (KYFTU) and CCAWDUnion of South Bay Factory:** the company has no nursing room or day-care centre on site, so mothers must go home to breastfeed, and about 40 percent of workers come from the provinces while there is neither shade nor shelter outside the gate [Para 25][Para 47][Para 48]. The workers asked the company to reimburse the deducted 14,000 Riel under Article 247 [Para 44].
**Employer (respondent), South Bay Company:** it offered the nursing room in Building F but cannot afford babysitters and cannot easily reprogramme the card-punching system for staggered exits [Para 33][Para 40][Para 41]. Its internal work rules do not require it to pay medical check fees [Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel accepted the demand for one hour of breastfeeding per day, ordered thirty minutes in the morning from 9:30 to 10:00 and thirty minutes in the afternoon from 14:30 to 15:00, ordered the company to provide staff to care for the babies, ordered reimbursement of 14,000 Riel or US$3.50 per worker, and rejected the demand to open the gate at 12:00 noon [Para 85][Para 86].
On Issue 1, the panel found the parties had not agreed on a time, so Article 184 of the Labor Law 1997 applied, entitling a mother to one hour per day for one year from delivery, divisible into two thirty-minute periods, fixed at the midpoint of each shift where there is no agreement [Para 55][Para 56][Para 59][Para 60]. It set the breaks at 9:30 to 10:00 and 14:30 to 15:00 [Para 61]. Article 186 requires enterprises employing at least one hundred women to set up a nursing room and day-care centre, and the panel found the Building F arrangement satisfied that obligation because the factory employs more than one hundred women [Para 62][Para 64][Para 65]. Article 187 requires a Prakas on such centres, and pending it the panel held babysitters necessary, ordering the employer to place them in the nursing room the company had arranged [Para 65][Para 66][Para 67].
On Issue 3, the panel found the deduction was made regardless of the group leader's agreement [Para 69]. It followed its earlier awards on medical check costs and held Article 274(C) and Article 247 sufficient legal ground to place the cost on the employer, including for newly recruited workers, even without the new Prakas [Para 71][Para 72][Para 73]. It noted Article 377 makes a breach of Article 247 punishable by a fine of 120 to 360 days of base daily wage and imprisonment of one to five years, or one of the two [Para 74][Para 75]. It ordered reimbursement of 14,000 Riel per worker [Para 76].
On Issue 4, the panel held that determining working hours and opening the gate fall within each company's jurisdiction under Article 138 [Para 78][Para 79]. It relied on Case 28/04 and Article 2, which gives the employer the right to manage and lead human resources, to hold the employer not obligated to open the gate outside working hours [Para 80][Para 81]. The lunch break is not working time [Para 82]. It also noted that Award 24/04 of 21 May 2004 had ordered a canteen and the company had already provided one [Para 84].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer must bear the cost of workers' medical checkups under Articles 247 and 274(C) of the Labor Law even before the Prakas contemplated by Article 247 is issued [Para 72][Para 73]. Where a mother and the employer cannot agree on the timing of breastfeeding breaks, Article 184 fixes them at the midpoint of each shift, and an enterprise with at least one hundred women must staff its nursing room with babysitters [Para 60][Para 67]. The employer determines working hours and when the gate opens under Article 138 [Para 79].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that, viewed as an interests dispute, the gate demand would impose security, sanitation and safety costs on the company for workers inside the compound outside working hours, and equity did not require that burden [Para 83].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is heavily corrupted, rendering "the employer" as "thempoyer"/"thempoyer" and "the employee" as "thempoyee", "with the" as "withe", "to" as "o" and "and" as "anday". The union's name is printed "CCAwDUnion of Suoth Bay factory" in the evidence list [Para 20] but "CCAwDUnion of South Bay Factory" in the style of cause [Para 1], and "KFYTU" appears once for "KYFTU" [Para 23]. The company address is Building A in the header [Para 5] but Building E in the case summary [Para 22], and the distance discussion refers to "Building E and Building A" where Building F was plainly meant [Para 34]. The district is spelled "Dangkao" in the header [Para 5] and "Dangkau" in the summary [Para 22].
