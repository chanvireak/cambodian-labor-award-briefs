# Award No. 12/05 — P & E Company (Cambodia) PTE, LTD v Worker's Union at P & E (Arbitration Council, 1 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Ing Sothy (employer party), Mr Liv Sovanna (worker party), Mr Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140764

## 1. Facts
P & E Company (Cambodia) PTE, LTD is at #3, Russian Blvd, Tektlar commune, Reseykao district, Phnom Penh and employs 220 workers in printing and knitting [Para 13]. A female printing-section worker began in June 2004 on three months' probation and then signed a six-month fixed-duration contract running from 17 September 2004 to 17 March 2005 [Para 22]. On 1 March 2005, sixteen days before the end date, the company notified her that the contract would not be renewed because staff were redundant [Para 14][Para 22]. The union asked for renewal on the ground that many workers supported her; on 2 March 2005, 87 printing workers went on strike [Para 17][Para 23][Para 24]. Of 13 issues, 11 were conciliated and the two remaining ones were referred to the Council on 10 March 2005 [Para 18][Para 19]. The employer issued notices on 2 and 3 March 2005 ordering a return to work, and the workers returned on 4 March 2005 after a strike of one and a half days [Para 34][Para 37][Para 70].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate the female printing-section worker whose fixed-duration contract expired on 17 March 2005 [Para 5][Para 81]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages and the attendance bonus for the period of the strike [Para 5][Para 72]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Worker's Union at P & E:** the dismissal was discrimination, because the worker knew the Labor Law and regularly helped other workers resolve problems, and the union considered that this was why she was let go [Para 26][Para 27]. The strike was called after the company failed to inform the union in time [Para 17]. The workers claimed wages and the attendance bonus for the strike period because the company moved goods to Den Printing with two vehicles, number plates D 1218 and D 9736, to have the work done elsewhere, contrary to Article 334 of the Labor Law [Para 35][Para 37].
**Employer (respondent), P & E Company (Cambodia) PTE, LTD:** the fixed-duration contract ended because work was short and the company meant to reduce staff, and only two workers held fixed-duration contracts, both of whom were dismissed the same way [Para 30][Para 31]. It promised to pay all amounts owed under the law [Para 33][Para 34]. It said the decision to move goods to Den Printing was the owner's, since P & E is only a subcontractor of Yi Hong (Cambodia) Garment Industry and a purchase order was due [Para 37][Para 38].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected both the demand to reinstate the worker and the demand for wages and the attendance bonus for the strike [Para 81][Para 82].
As a preliminary point, the panel held that it has no jurisdiction over individual labour disputes under Chapter 12, Section 2, but that the labour inspectors and the Minister decide the characterisation, and the panel follows that decision absent clear objection [Para 40]. It agreed that this was a collective dispute satisfying the three conditions of Article 302: it arose between an employer and a group of workers, concerned the exercise of recognised rights and relations between the parties, and could jeopardise the enterprise [Para 40][Para 41][Para 43][Para 44][Para 48][Para 50].
On Issue 1, the panel asked whether notice was given lawfully and whether the dismissal was discriminatory [Para 55][Para 56]. Article 73(1) provides that a fixed-duration contract terminates at its stated end date, and Article 73(5) requires ten days' notice where the contract exceeds six months [Para 58]. The worker had signed a three-month probationary contract and then a six-month contract ending 17 March 2005, and the notice signed on 1 March 2005 gave 16 days [Para 59][Para 60]. The panel found the notice complied with the Labour Law [Para 61]. On discrimination, Article 12 forbids termination based on union membership or activity, and Articles 279, 282 and 293 protect worker delegates, members and leaders [Para 63][Para 64]. The panel found no discrimination because the worker was neither a union leader nor a member, only two workers held fixed-duration contracts and both ended, and the union conceded that some sections were temporarily suspended for lack of work [Para 66][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69].
On Issue 2, the panel found the employer responsible for moving goods to Den Printing despite its subcontractor argument, since the transfer could not have happened without its agreement [Para 73]. It noted that moving goods out can be equivalent to recruiting replacements under Article 334, which requires strikers to be paid, as held in 04/03-Lida Garment [Para 74][Para 75]. But Article 332 suspends the labour contract during a strike so salary is not paid [Para 73][Para 74]. Under Article 324 strikers must give at least seven calendar days' notice, and these workers gave none [Para 76]. Article 320(4) allows the right to strike only after all peaceful methods are exhausted, and the workers struck before the arbitration process [Para 77][Para 78]. Because the strike was procedurally unlawful, the wage claim failed and deducting the attendance bonus was not a sanction prohibited by Article 333 [Para 78][Para 81].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A fixed-duration contract of more than six months ends lawfully where ten days' notice is given under Article 73(5), and selecting one of a small number of fixed-duration workers for non-renewal is not discrimination under Article 12 where the worker holds no union office and the contract simply expired [Para 58][Para 61][Para 66]. Strikers who give none of the seven days' notice required by Article 324, and who strike before exhausting peaceful settlement under Article 320(4), forfeit wages for the strike period and cannot invoke Article 334, so withholding the attendance bonus is not a sanction barred by Article 333 [Para 76][Para 78][Para 81].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed generally that moving goods out of a factory can make a strike ineffective and undermine its purpose of pressuring the production line [Para 75].
