# Award No. 22/05 — Ocean Garment v. Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union and Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140772

## 1. Facts
Ocean Garment is in Praytea village, Chamchao, Dangke, Phnom Penh, and employs approximately 1,167 workers [Para 19]. On 11 March 2005 the Dangke district Labour Inspector received a complaint from the workers asking that working conditions be applied in accordance with the Labour Law [Para 20]. Conciliation on 23 March 2005 left two points unresolved, and these reached the Arbitration Council on 1 April 2005 under non-conciliation report number 482 [Para 13][Para 21][Para 22]. Issue 1, the local union's demand that the company deduct 1,000 riel from wages for union dues under Prakas 305 dated 22 November 2001, was settled by agreement signed on 20 April 2005, so the panel decided only Issue 2 [Para 12][Para 23][Para 24]. On Issue 2 the workers sought 100 per cent of their daily wage when the company has no work, occasionally or for long periods [Para 12][Para 25]. In the past, when work ran out, the company told workers to take leave without pay, without notifying the Labour Inspector, and it acknowledged suspending contracts in the ironing and packing sections occasionally without notice and without pay [Para 27][Para 33]. A conciliation report of 10 July 2004 recorded an agreement to pay 50 per cent wages in occasional work shortages, but both sides asked the panel to disregard it [Para 27][Para 29][Para 30].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay 100 per cent of wages to workers when it has no work for them, occasionally or for long periods, instead of suspending their contracts without pay? [Para 12][Para 25]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union and Free Trade Union of Workers of the Kingdom of Cambodia):** The company must pay 100 per cent of the daily wage when it has no work, occasionally or over a long period, and must apply the Labour Law if the shortage lasts; the claim is not for past damages but for future suspensions [Para 12][Para 26]. The union first invoked the 10 July 2004 agreement on 50 per cent wages but later asked the panel to apply the Labour Law alone [Para 27][Para 28].
**Employer (Ocean Garment):** It would apply the Labour Law procedure, although it had not done so before, and would pay 100 per cent wages if the law so required [Para 26]. It refused the 50 per cent agreement, saying it did not know or understand Khmer and that the arrangement covered only the strike day of 11 March 2004 [Para 28].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to stop suspending labour contracts in a manner that does not follow the procedure in Article 71 of the Labour Law, and to pay 100 per cent of wages to the workers during any suspension that does not accord with Article 71(11) [Para 39][Para 40].
The panel set out Article 71(11): a contract may be suspended where the enterprise faces a serious economic or material difficulty, or any particularly unusual difficulty, causing suspension of operations; the suspension may not exceed two months and must be under the control of the Labour Inspector, which in practice means notified to and approved by him [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]. It found the company had told workers to take leave without pay in the past and had suspended contracts in the ironing and packing sections occasionally without notifying the Labour Inspector or paying wages, so it had not applied Article 71(11) [Para 33]. It held the company responsible for paying 100 per cent wages to workers suspended occasionally or for long periods [Para 34], citing its earlier awards 21/03-Loyal Cambodia, 46/04-M and A Cambodia, 01/04-New Point (II) and 60/04-United Art for the proposition that suspending contracts without pay is unlawful under Article 71(11) [Para 35][Para 36]. Because the union claimed no past damages, the payment obligation runs only from the date of the award, and workers lawfully suspended under the Labour Law receive nothing for the suspension period [Para 37][Para 38]. Both parties having rejected the 50 per cent agreement and asked the panel to decide on the Labour Law alone, the award rests solely on statute [Para 29][Para 34].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may suspend labour contracts only within Article 71(11) of the Labour Law — for serious economic or material difficulty or other particularly unusual difficulty, for no more than two months, and under the control of the Labour Inspector; a suspension made without that notification and control is unlawful and obliges the employer to pay full wages for the suspension period [Para 30][Para 31][Para 34][Para 36].

**Obiter dicta:** Where the suspension is lawful under the Labour Law, the workers receive no wage during the suspension period [Para 38]. Because the union sought no damages for past suspensions, the full-wage obligation applies only from the date of the award [Para 37].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR text is heavily corrupted, with "the" fused into following words ("themployer", "thearing", "with respecto") and whole clauses repeated [Para 16][Para 20][Para 31]. The worker headcount is 1,167 in the case summary and "approximately 1,667" in the findings, and 1,000 riel of union dues is written with a space inside the number [Para 12][Para 19][Para 24]. Article 71 is cited as "Article 71(11)" and once as "article 71(11)" in a passage that also misprints "as established by" — the subparagraph numbering cannot be verified from the text [Para 30][Para 33]. The 10 July 2004 agreement is dated 10 July 2004 in one place and described as covering "11 March 2004" in another, while the complaint in this file arose on 11 March 2005 [Para 27][Para 28]. The employer representatives are listed with full names in the party particulars but the panel's findings refer only to "the company" [Para 4]. The decision section is truncated into the signature block, with the closing punctuation stranded as isolated marks [Para 44].
