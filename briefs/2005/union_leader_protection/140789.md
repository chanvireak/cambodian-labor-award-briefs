# Award No. 50/05 — Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory Co, Ltd (Arbitration Council, 28 September 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Ouk Ry, Ann Vireak, Koy Neam (Chair) · Doc ID 140789

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory Co, Ltd, at Prek Kse Village, Roka Kpos Commune, Sa-ang District, Kandal Province, employed about 3,500 workers in 2003, 3,200 in 2004 and 2,700 at the time of the award [Para 24][Para 27]. The Coalition of Cambodian Apparel W.D.U in Fortune Garment (C.CAWDU) sought the reinstatement of two dismissed workers, Mr N and Mr B, both active union members in the Apparel Section whom the company dismissed on 9 July 2005 [Para 25][Para 34]. The union had elected a new leadership group on 29 May 2005 without notifying the company, and registered it with the Ministry of Labour and Vocational Training on 2 June 2005, receiving the receipt on 18 June 2005 [Para 25]. Employment in the Apparel Section fell from 60 workers in 2000 to 45 in 2005, and the company recruited no replacements after the dismissals [Para 34].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the two dismissed workers must be reinstated, because they were protected union members or candidates for union leadership, or because the company discriminated against them for their union activity? [Para 12][Para 35]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** C.CAWDU of Fortune Garment argued that Mr N and Mr B were dismissed because they were active union members, that many activists had been dismissed before, and that the union had tried repeatedly to notify the company of its leadership and members but the company refused to accept the letters [Para 35][Para 36][Para 65]. Mr N said he collected members' donations monthly, after working hours, and had not told the company of his membership [Para 41][Para 42].
**Employer (respondent):** Fortune Garment and Woolen Knitting Factory Co, Ltd said the two were dismissed in July 2005 for personal reasons, that the company does not discriminate and did not know they were union members or activists, and that it never received any notification of the names to be protected [Para 38][Para 64]. It declined to comment on the falling headcount when questioned at the second hearing [Para 31][Para 35].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for reinstatement of Mr N and Mr B [Para 78][Para 80].
The panel first tested shop-steward protection. Article 293 of the Labour Law allows dismissal of a shop steward or candidate only with authorisation from the Labour Inspector, and the third paragraph of Article 3 of Prakas 303 extends that protection to any worker who runs for a leadership position in his union, for 45 days before and, if not elected, 45 days after the election, provided the employer is duly informed of the candidacy by any reliable means [Para 47][Para 48][Para 50]. Article 4 of Prakas 305/01 protects founding and joining members for 30 days after registration, again on condition that the union communicates the names to the employer by any reliable means and copies the Ministry [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54]. The panel distilled three requirements: the worker must fall within the Prakas, the dismissal must fall inside the protection period, and the names must have been communicated reliably [Para 59]. The first two were met, since both were members at registration and were dismissed within 30 days of it [Para 61][Para 63]. The third was not: the panel found no proof of any reliable communication, and said the union should at least have produced a notification letter signed by its management and acknowledged, or refused, by the employer's staff [Para 66][Para 67]. Absent that, the two were not entitled to the protection given to worker delegates [Para 68].
On discrimination, the panel applied Article 12, which bars an employer from terminating a contract on account of union membership or activity, and Article 279, which forbids taking union affiliation into account in decisions [Para 69][Para 71]. The workers had to prove both that the employer knew of their membership and that the dismissal was caused by it, and produced no evidence of either [Para 71][Para 72]. The panel noted that Mr N never told the company of his membership, that Mr B did not attend the second hearing and so abandoned his chance to prove knowledge, and that the declining headcount with no replacements did not by itself indicate discrimination [Para 42][Para 46][Para 77].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The dismissal protection of Prakas 303 and Prakas 305/01 is conditional: a union member or leadership candidate is protected only where the union has communicated the names of those to be protected to the employer by a reliable means, and a bare assertion that letters were offered and refused does not discharge that burden [Para 55][Para 66]. A claim of anti-union dismissal under Articles 12 and 279 fails unless the worker proves both that the employer knew of the union membership or activity and that the dismissal was motivated by it [Para 71][Para 72].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel recorded the Apparel Section's shrinkage from 60 to 45 workers and the absence of replacement recruitment without treating either as evidence of discrimination [Para 34][Para 77].

The workforce in 2004 is printed as "200 workers" in the facts and as 3,200 in the reasoning [Para 24][Para 27]. The requirements in the reasoning are numbered "(i)", "(iii)", "(iii)", repeating the third numeral [Para 59]. Words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "withemployer"), and Article 279's text is scrambled by OCR into a partly unreadable repetition [Para 69]. The two dismissed workers and the union officers are named in full on the face of the award and are reduced to initials here [Para 3].
