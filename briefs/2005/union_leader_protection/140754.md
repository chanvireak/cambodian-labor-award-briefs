# Award No. 03/05 — Flying Dragon Com., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 February 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, Sin Kim Sean, Run Saray) · Doc ID 140754

## 1. Facts
Flying Dragon Com., Ltd — described in the case summary as the Cambodia Flying Dragon Garment Company — runs a garment factory at Phum Trea, Stoeung Mean Chey, Khan Mean Chey, Phnom Penh and employs 1,850 workers [Para 1][Para 12]. Conciliation of a 14-point collective dispute settled 8 issues and left 6 unresolved, which reached the Council on a non-conciliation report [Para 14][Para 5]. The six demands were a 2,000 riel meal allowance for each worker doing 4 hours 30 minutes of overtime a day; payment of wages and attendance bonus by the 10th of the following month; wages and attendance bonus for the strike of 24–28 December 2004; conversion of casual workers with two months' service into permanent workers; a change of start time from 6:00 a.m. to 7:00 a.m.; and restoration of the pre-existing piece rate [Para 15][Para 16]. The workers struck from 24 to 28 December 2004, the company issued a disciplinary notice on 18 December 2004, and it adjusted its piece rates shortly before the hearing [Para 28][Para 59][Para 51].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel meal allowance to workers who work 4 hours 30 minutes of overtime per day? [Para 15]
2. (Issue 2) Whether wages and attendance bonus must be paid no later than the 10th of the following month? [Para 15]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer must pay wages and attendance bonus for the strike of 24–28 December 2004? [Para 16]
4. (Issue 4) Whether casual workers with two months or more of service must be converted to permanent workers? [Para 16]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the start time at Flying Dragon II must be moved from 6:00 a.m. to 7:00 a.m.? [Para 16]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the employer must increase the piece rate back to the rate applied before the change? [Para 16]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** C.CAWDU to Flying Dragon III argued that 2,000 riels was owed for the daily overtime, that a January 2004 agreement fixed payment by the 10th, that the strike was provoked by the company hiding the piece-rate record, that casual staff worked exactly like regular staff, that Flying Dragon II workers wanted a 7:00 a.m. start, and that the new rates cut incomes — one local union president's monthly pay fell from USD $160 to USD $80 [Para 17][Para 24][Para 28][Para 35][Para 40][Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** Flying Dragon Com., Ltd answered that overtime was voluntary and thumbprinted, that a March 2004 safety agreement allowed payment between the 8th and 13th, that the strike was illegal for want of notice, that no casual worker had two months' continuous service, that the Flying Dragon III union had no authority over Flying Dragon II, and that the rate change increased the share of workers reaching the minimum wage [Para 18][Para 24][Para 30][Para 37][Para 41][Para 59].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pay 1,000 riel a day as a meal allowance, or provide a free meal, to workers who volunteer for overtime, and to reset the piece rate within one month so that an average worker in normal hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage; the payment-date, strike-pay and casual-conversion claims were denied and the start-time claim was declined [Para 114][Para 116][Para 117].

On the meal allowance, Prakas 80 MOSALVY of 1 March 1999 and point 4 of Notification No. 17 MASALVY of 18 July 2000 entitle a volunteer overtime worker to 1,000 riels a day or a free meal, and Article 13 of the Labour Law voids agreements that depart from those rules; the panel therefore ordered 1,000 riels regardless of the length of overtime, not the 2,000 demanded [Para 66][Para 68].

On the payment date, Article 116 requires payment at least once a month but fixes no particular day, and the workers could not produce the January 2004 agreement they relied on, so the March 2004 agreement providing payment between the 8th and 13th bound the parties [Para 69][Para 72][Para 73].

On strike pay, Article 337 gives the courts power over strike legality, but the panel followed its earlier awards in Lida 04/03, Wash Concept 08/04 and Lucky Zone 15/04 and examined legality because wages for the strike period were claimed [Para 74][Para 73]. Applying Articles 324, 332 and 336, it found the strike unlawful — no seven-day notice and peaceful settlement not exhausted — so no wage or attendance bonus was due [Para 78][Para 79][Para 82].

On casual workers, Articles 9, 10 and 166(3) of the Labour Law and the Sport Wear award No. 26/04 treat a worker averaging more than 21 days a month for over two months as regular; but the union named no individual worker and tendered no evidence, so the panel found the practice did not occur at the factory and denied the demand [Para 83][Para 86][Para 90].

On the start time, there was no evidence that Flying Dragon II workers had authorised the Flying Dragon III union, and 227 workers had petitioned to keep the 6:00 a.m. start, so the panel declined to consider the claim [Para 90][Para 46][Para 47].

On the piece rate, Article 2 gives the employer the right to manage and set rates, and Article 108 requires a rate that lets a worker of average skill in normal hours earn the minimum wage; after the change only about 45% of Line A reached the USD $45.00 minimum, so the rate was not compliant and had to be reset within one month [Para 93][Para 94][Para 96][Para 117].

On warnings, the 18 December 2004 notice departed from Article 10 of the Internal Work Rules; because it modified those rules the employer had to follow Article 30 — consult the workers' representatives, obtain Labour Inspector approval and notify the workers — and apply a verbal warning first, then three written warnings, before considering termination [Para 110][Para 111][Para 113][Para 117].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may set and change piece rates as part of its management authority, but the rate must be calibrated so that a worker of average skill working normal hours — without overtime, public holidays or Sundays — earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage, and subsidising the shortfall afterwards does not cure a rate that leaves most workers below that floor [Para 105][Para 104]. A worker who volunteers for overtime is entitled to the statutory 1,000 riel meal allowance regardless of how many overtime hours are worked [Para 68].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's comparison with Chusing 14/03 — that the payroll there showed no minimum-wage shortfall and no overtime, holidays or Sundays — explains the distinction rather than deciding anything [Para 98][Para 99].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The employer carries two names: "Flying Dragon Com., Ltd" in the header and "Cambodia Flying Dragon Garment Company" in the case summary [Para 1][Para 12]. The wage-payment agreement is dated three different ways — 23 March 2004, 29 March 2004 and 24 March 2004 [Para 24][Para 71][Para 73]. The jurisdiction recital puts conciliation on 11 June 2004, while the complaint was filed on 24 December 2004 and the non-conciliation report is dated 5 January 2005 [Para 5][Para 13]. OCR systematically drops "th" ("themployer", "athe"), and the third-warning step is printed as "the 34 written warning" in the order [Para 117]. A local union president is named in full at the point where her wage fall is described; the name is deliberately not reproduced here [Para 53].
