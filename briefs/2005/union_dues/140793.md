# Award No. 60/05 — Evergreen Apparel (Cambodia) Co. Ltd (Arbitration Council, 14 November 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: You Suonty, Ann Vireak, Tan Try (Chair) · Doc ID 140793

## 1. Facts
Evergreen Apparel (Cambodia) Co. Ltd, at Chong Yang Industrial Park, Chong Thnol Village, Sangkat Tirk Thlar, Khan Russey Keo, Phnom Penh, employs 1,161 workers [Para 2][Para 11]. Its director had previously run Long Glory Factory, where the KYFTU local was registered on 19 March 2004 and had its members' monthly dues deducted from wages [Para 11]. Evergreen was registered on 22 February 2005 and began operating in January 2005, taking on most of the Long Glory workforce [Para 12][Para 13]. The Khmer Youth Federation Trade Union (KYFTU) of Evergreen Apparel was registered by Ministry approval letter No. 1135 of 22 August 2005 and requested deduction of members' dues, which the company refused because the application named the company incorrectly [Para 10][Para 16].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must deduct the monthly union membership fee of 1,000 riels from the wages of members of KYFTU of Evergreen Apparel, and on what documentary conditions? [Para 10][Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the KYFTU and its Evergreen Apparel local argued that the union had sent its members' CVs and a request for deduction, that Long Glory had deducted the same fee, and that the company had in fact deducted the January 2005 fee during the transfer to Evergreen [Para 11][Para 18].
**Employer (respondent):** Evergreen Apparel (Cambodia) Co. Ltd said it would deduct dues once the union was formally registered and members petitioned for it, but rejected the application because the company name was wrong and later because the form lacked ID numbers, section and team, and because a corrected application had been altered by gluing paper over the old name [Para 3][Para 16][Para 18].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to deduct the membership fee for the KYFTU of Evergreen Apparel factory on receiving members' requests and the application form, and ordered the union to supply the list of authorising members and their authorisation letters, with the form giving names, ID number, function, team, section and signature or fingerprint [Para 43][Para 44].
The panel held that Article 129(2) of the Labour Law allows a worker to authorise deduction of wages for dues to the union to which he belongs, that the authorisation must be in writing, and that it may be revoked at any time [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. Article 5 of Prakas No. 305 requires a written request at least 15 days in advance and obliges the employer to comply properly [Para 29]. It found no provision specifying what information an application must contain [Para 33]. On the January deduction the panel rejected the union's claim: the payroll showed no such deduction and the union produced no evidence that it received the January fee [Para 19][Para 20]. It did find the application had been altered by a piece of paper bearing the name "Evergreen Apparel", and held that the misunderstanding arose from the instruction of the company's staff, while an application for deduction should not be scratched or glued over [Para 21][Para 30][Para 31]. The purpose of the requirement, it said, is to protect workers against deductions made against their will [Para 32].
Following 62/04-Y Sin, the panel held that the union must submit appropriate documents — a name list with signatures or fingerprints plus documents showing the workers authorised the contribution — and that on receipt the employer must deduct and remit monthly [Para 39][Para 40]. It also required the union to notify any change in authorisation in writing [Para 41]. The new form produced at the hearing, with names, section, team, ID numbers, signatures or fingerprints and the union representative's signature, was sufficient for an accountant to operate and had already been accepted by the employer [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. The panel added that the Labour Law does not oblige the union to hand over its whole membership list, including workers who did not authorise deduction; the employer may ask only for the list of those who did [Para 37][Para 38].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a worker gives written authorisation under Article 129(2) and Prakas No. 305 Article 5, the employer is obliged to withhold the union dues from that worker's wage and remit them to the union monthly, and it may not refuse for want of information the law does not require [Para 29][Para 40]. The employer's legitimate interest is confined to verifying authorisation, so it may require a list of the authorising workers with signatures or fingerprints but not the union's entire membership list [Para 37][Para 39].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the alteration by gluing paper over the company name arose from the company staff's own instruction and that the underlying purpose of the formalities is to protect workers from unauthorised deductions [Para 30][Para 32].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award gives two different addresses for the employer, one at Chong Yang Industrial Park in the party list and one on Russian Federation Boulevard in the facts, both in Khan Russey Keo [Para 2][Para 11]. The employer's name is split across two numbered lines and the punctuation is lost ("Co." / "Ltd") [Para 1][Para 2]. The union is printed as "KYFTU" and "KYTFU" in different documents within the same evidence list [Para 10]. A date is printed as "18 June 2005" for the conciliation meeting while the conciliation minute is dated 20 September 2005 and the report 17 October 2005 [Para 4][Para 11]. Words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "deducthe", "receiving"), and the evidence list is cut off mid-word [Para 10].
