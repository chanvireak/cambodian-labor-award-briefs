# Award No. 05/05 — G.H.G (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 1 February 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, An Nan, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140756

## 1. Facts
G.H.G (Cambodia) operates at Resey village, Sangkat Steng Meanchey, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh with 344 workers [Para 24]. On 11 January 2005 striking workers telephoned the labour inspectors; conciliation settled 4 of the 9 issues raised and left 5 for the Council on non-conciliation report No. 045/MoLVT dated 12 January 2005 [Para 24][Para 25][Para 13]. The five demands were reimbursement of a 16,000 riel medical examination fee, reimbursement of a 12,000 riel employment card fee, the same benefits for casual workers as for permanent staff, a day care centre with a nursing room and babysitters (or 3 cans of milk a month and $20 for a babysitter), and reinstatement of three suspended union leaders [Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 8][Para 9]. The workers are the Union of Worker in G.H.G (Cambodia), represented by officials of the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) [Para 2][Para 3].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reimburse each worker the 16,000 riel fee deducted for the medical examination? [Para 3]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse each worker the 12,000 riel fee deducted for the employment card? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Union of Worker in G.H.G (Cambodia) demanded repayment of the 16,000 riel medical fee and the 12,000 riel card fee, equal benefits for casual staff, childcare facilities, and reinstatement of three union leaders who had done nothing wrong [Para 3][Para 5][Para 6][Para 7][Para 9]. **Employer (respondent):** G.H.G (Cambodia) refused the medical refund, agreed to the card refund, rejected casual-worker parity on the ground that it already complies with the law, refused the childcare claim because its premises are narrow and it has traded only one year, and refused reinstatement because two of the three were too young to hold union office and the third had incited workers to stop work and refuse overtime [Para 4][Para 6][Para 7][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to compensate each worker 16,000 riels for the medical examination deducted and 4,060 riels for the employment card deduction and other expenditures [Para 74].
On the medical fee, the panel held that Article 247(C) of the 1997 Labour Law obliges the employer to bear the cost of medical checks, and followed its earlier awards including #02/03 Chou Sing, #21/03 Loyal Cambodia, #19/04 Kba Koh II, #53/04 Kong Hong, #60/04 United Art and #63/04 Shine Well [Para 49]. It noted the dissent in #60/04 over whether Joint Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 survives the 1997 Labour Law, but held that Article 247 alone is a sufficient basis and that Article 377 exposes a breach to a fine and imprisonment [Para 51][Para 54][Para 55]. The order covers only workers from whose wages the fee was actually deducted [Para 56].
On the card fee, the panel held that Article 32 requires every worker to hold an employment card, that the worker must register and pay for it personally, and that the lawful cost is 2,500 riels under Notice No. 13/MoSALVY/97 [Para 58][Para 59][Para 61][Para 62]. Had the workers registered themselves they would have spent 7,940 riels, made up of the 2,500 riel fee, about 2,000 riels of transport and 3,440 riels in lost half-day wages [Para 68][Para 69]. The company deducted 12,000 riels, so the overcharge was 4,060 riels, ordered refunded under the power in Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 to award fair compensation [Para 69][Para 71][Para 74]. The casual-worker, childcare and reinstatement demands had already been conciliated or settled on 17 January 2005 and were not decided [Para 42][Para 44].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not pass on to the workforce a medical examination cost that Article 247 of the Labour Law places on the employer; and where the employer arranges employment-card registration at the factory it may recover only the lawful card fee together with the transport and lost-wage costs the worker has thereby saved, and must refund any deduction above that measure [Para 56][Para 69].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that it need not resolve whether Joint Prakas No. 09 of 19 January 1994 remains effective, because Article 247 suffices, was not necessary to the order [Para 54][Para 55].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing dates are printed as 14 January 2004 and 18 January 2004 although the award is dated 1 February 2005 [Para 17]. The employer's address block is badly corrupted, with long runs of bracket and comma fragments replacing the street address and telephone details [Para 3]. The medical fee appears as "16,00 riels" in one passage and 16,000 riels elsewhere [Para 49][Para 56]. Both parties agreed to a non-binding award, yet the standard clause still states that it binds after 8 days unless opposed [Para 24][Para 75]. The three suspended workers' names are printed inconsistently across the award, with one name appearing in two different orders in adjacent lines [Para 9][Para 10][Para 42].
