# Award No. 75/05 — Fortune Garment and Woollen Knitting Factory (Arbitration Council, 27 December 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Samborana, employer-appointed; Ann Vireak, worker-appointed; Kong Phallack, chair) · non-binding award · Doc ID 140802

## 1. Facts
Fortune Garment and Woollen Knitting Factory of Sa-ang District, Kandal, faced a collective dispute with C.CAWDU of Fortune Garment. On 28 November 2005 the Kandal labour office conciliated 14 issues; ten were resolved and four were not, and report No. 003/05 reached the Council on 08 December 2005. [Para 4][Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] None of the four was conciliated at the hearing of 13 December 2005, and on 15 December 2005 the union withdrew issues one, two and four, leaving only the annual-leave demand to be arbitrated. [Para 14][Para 15][Para 16] The company gives 19 days of paid leave in the third year, 18 days in the fourth and fifth, and 19 days in the sixth, on its reading of Article 166. [Para 17]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 3) Whether the company must grant 19 days of paid annual leave in the third, fourth and fifth years and 20 days from the sixth year onwards, as the workers' reading of Article 166(4) requires? [Para 7][Para 19]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 166(4) adds one day of paid leave for each three years of service, so the entitlement is cumulative: 19 days in the third, fourth and fifth years and 20 days from the sixth year on. [Para 20][Para 24]
**Employer (respondent):** The company already applied Article 166 by granting one additional day in the third year (19 days), 18 days in the fourth and fifth, and 19 days in the sixth, which it treated as full compliance. [Para 18][Para 23]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the company was ordered to provide paid annual leave by seniority, adding one working day for every three years of service, meaning 19 days in the fourth, fifth and sixth years and 20 days in the seventh, eighth and ninth, with the same increase every three years thereafter. [Para 56]

The panel found the dispute arose purely from divergent readings of Article 166(4), whose words "at the rate of one day per three year of service" are not clear enough to settle the question. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 25] Reading the provision by its purpose and its relation to other articles, the panel defined seniority through Articles 73, 75, 89 and 95, where notice periods, dismissal compensation and mass-layoff priority all grow with length of service. [Para 26][Para 29][Para 30][Para 36][Para 40] From Article 75 it drew the principle that a right once acquired at a stage of service is not reduced when the worker passes into a later stage, and it applied that to leave. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 39] Seniority was defined as successive service that adds rights automatically, not as a productivity bonus or a gift of the employer, and those rights cannot be cut by agreement, circumstance or the employer's will unless the change favours the worker. [Para 41][Para 44][Para 45] Equity reinforced the reading, since longer service brings greater familiarity, speed and honesty, benefiting the employer too, so leave should rise and never fall while seniority grows. [Para 47][Para 49][Para 50][Para 54] The panel followed its earlier Awards 51/04-Sam Han, 62/04-E Cent and 68/05-Gold Lida. [Para 55]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where Article 166(4) is ambiguous, one additional working day of paid annual leave accrues for every three years of continuous service and accumulates, so that leave entitlement may rise with seniority but may never be reduced; an employer's contrary practice, even one applied consistently, does not satisfy the article. [Para 35][Para 45][Para 54]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's observations that a worker who moves to a new workplace loses the accumulated leave advantage, and that long-serving workers reward the employer through experience, speed of work, honesty and mutual understanding. [Para 52][Para 54]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The parties' positions are printed in a garbled comparative chart in which year labels and day counts are jumbled, so the figures used here are taken from the narrative findings and the order. [Para 25][Para 55] The facts state that the company already gives 19 days in the third year, while the chart and the order treat the first three years as 18 days; both figures are reported as printed, and the order gives 19 days only from the fourth year, less than the workers had demanded from the third. [Para 17][Para 25][Para 56] The non-conciliation report reached the Council on 08 December 2005 but is described as dated 7 December 2005. [Para 9][Para 11] The award cites its own Award No. 20/05 as dated 21 April 2004, a year earlier than the case number implies. [Para 16] One worker's name appears in the issue list and the party list, and is reduced here to a role; the union's address line carries corrupted Khmer characters ("Addresនៈ"). [Para 4][Para 7] The OCR fuses words throughout ("thecompany", "annualeave", "remaing").
