# Award No. 18/05 — AIA Company Co., Ltd v. KYFTU and Local Union of KYFTU at AIA factory (Arbitration Council, 26 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Ven Pov, Pen Bunchhea) · Doc ID 140768

## 1. Facts
AIA Company Co., Ltd is at Street 265, Sangkat Tek lak 3, Khan Toul Kork, Phnom Penh, and employs 320 workers [Para 27]. On 11 March 2005 the Labour Inspection Office received a complaint from the workers' representative, the employees then being on strike, demanding improved working conditions [Para 28]. Conciliation on 15 March 2005 settled six of the 12 issues, and the non-conciliation report number 309/MoLVT/AK dated 21 March 2005 reached the Arbitration Council Secretariat on 23 March 2005 [Para 12][Para 29]. At the first hearing the parties withdrew four issues as unnecessary and withdrew issues 2, 3 and 6 as already resolved in award 78/04 dated 22 September 2004 [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]. The live facts concerned a thumbprint sheet dated 4 August 2004: the local union president and vice president were accused of collecting false thumbprints from 21 workers to demand improved conditions; the 21 workers denied the prints, the police found the prints were not theirs, the company suspended the two officers on 26 August 2004 and asked the Labour Inspection Department to dismiss them, and the Inspector authorised termination by letter number 2705 dated 4 November 2004 [Para 39][Para 59][Para 63][Para 65][Para 67][Para 69][Para 71].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must reinstate Ms K and Mr S, the local union president and vice president of KYFTU at AIA company, whom the Labour Inspection Department had authorised the company to dismiss? [Para 3][Para 4]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the company must dismiss Mr Y, an administrator, on the workers' demand that he conspired with the owner to take profits from the workers, forced withdrawal of complaints and exploited Article 83 of the Labour Law? [Para 6][Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (KYFTU and Local Union of KYFTU at AIA factory):** The company should reinstate the union president and vice president, since the Labour Inspection Department's authorisation to dismiss did not oblige the company to act on it [Para 3][Para 4]. The administrator Mr Y should be dismissed because he conspired with the owner over profits, pressed workers to withdraw a complaint the company had not complied with, and exploited Article 83 [Para 7].
**Employer (AIA Company Co., Ltd):** The company could not reinstate the two officers because it was following the Labour Law, the Labour Inspector having authorised their dismissal for serious misconduct [Para 4][Para 71]. It did not agree with the demand to dismiss its administrator [Para 8].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the company reinstate Ms K and Mr S, and rejected the demand that the company dismiss administrator Mr Y [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149].
On issue 1 the panel read Article 83(B-2) of the 1997 Labour Law as listing fraudulent acts, including presentation of false documentation, among serious offences, and held that all fraudulent acts committed at signing or during performance are serious misconduct justifying termination [Para 83]. It found Mr S had prepared the thumbprint sheet himself and admitted giving the paper to a worker to obtain her thumbprint, while 21 workers refused to acknowledge the sheet and the police technical office found the prints were not those of the workers who complained [Para 88][Para 91][Para 93][Para 95]. It therefore held the dismissal of Mr S correct under Article 83(B-2) [Para 98][Para 100]. For Ms K, the panel found her sworn denial inconsistent with the testimony of Mr S and the witness, unsupported by any evidence, and contradicted by the police record showing she carried the sheet to two workers for their thumbprints [Para 113][Para 116][Para 118][Para 121]. It also weighed her statement at the hearing that, if reinstated, she would push the company, and concluded she had no real intention to return to work and remained responsible for activities under her supervision, including arranging false documents [Para 129][Para 131][Para 132]. The reinstatement demand was denied [Para 133][Para 134].
On issue 5 the panel held that it has no power to order an employer to terminate a worker on the demand of other workers, citing awards 04/03-Lida, 14/03-Chou Sing, 17/03 & 18/03-Hu Hing, 06/04-Chou Sing, 15/04-Lucky Zone, 16/04-Yada Printing, 32/04-E-cent and 34/04-Full Value [Para 136]. It noted an exception where employees endanger the health and safety of others, but found Mr Y had never persecuted or scolded any worker [Para 136][Para 137]. Reading Article 65 of the Labour Law with Article 22 of state decree number 38, it held that only the parties to the contract may terminate it, so hiring and dismissal is the employer's sole right [Para 139][Para 143][Para 144]. Even for serious misconduct, Article 83 gives the employer the right to terminate, not a duty to do so [Para 145].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The Arbitration Council will not order an employer to dismiss a worker at the demand of fellow workers: hiring and dismissal is the right of the employer as a party to the labour contract, and Article 83 of the Labour Law confers on the employer a right, not an obligation, to terminate for serious misconduct [Para 144][Para 145]. Producing or arranging false documentation in support of a complaint is fraudulent conduct within Article 83(B-2) and justifies termination, and no notice is required where the reason is serious misconduct [Para 80][Para 83][Para 98].

**Obiter dicta:** The Council could order dismissal or transfer where employees cause trouble to the health and safety of other workers, an exception not engaged here [Para 136]. The parties agreed to take the issues already decided in award 78/04 to court instead [Para 35].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR text is badly degraded: "the" is fused into following words throughout ("themployer", "Athearing", "withe", "humbprints"), and the names of the two union officers are split across sentence boundaries so that the surname runs into the next line's first name [Para 84][Para 85]. Surname variants appear for the same two people across the file — "Ses Sophy", "Ses Sohpy", "Ses Spohy" and "Ses Samphy", and "Sambath Sreytouch", "Sabath Sreytouch" and "Sambth Sreytouch" [Para 15][Para 89][Para 100][Para 121]. The earlier award is cited as "17/03 & 18/03-Hu Hing" in the reasons but "17/03 & 18/04-Hui Ying" a few paragraphs later [Para 136][Para 145]. The employer's federation is printed both "KYFTU" and "KyFTU" [Para 58]. Both parties agreed at the hearing to a non-binding award [Para 27], yet the closing boilerplate states the award becomes binding eight days after notification [Para 150], and the source does not reconcile the two. The "six issues" sentence in the jurisdiction section is itself garbled, merging the non-conciliation report number into the sentence [Para 12].
