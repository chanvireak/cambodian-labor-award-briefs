# Award No. 46/05 — Ocean Garment (Arbitration Council, 22 August 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Kao Thach (employer-chosen), Ven Pov (worker-chosen), Pen Bunchhea (chair) · Doc ID 140784

## 1. Facts
Ocean Garment operates in Prey Tea village, Sangkat Choum Chao, Khan Dang Kor, Phnom Penh, and employs approximately 1,636 workers [Para 15][Para 16]. On 4 August 2005 the Khan Dan Kor labour inspectorate received a complaint demanding that the company apply working conditions under the Labour Law; conciliation failed and non-conciliation report No. 145 KKBVIVK dated 8 August 2005 reached the Secretariat on 9 August 2005 [Para 17][Para 15][Para 20]. The dispute concerned the suspension of four sections — cutting, sewing, warehouse and mechanics — on 5 and 6 July 2005, affecting about 1,400 workers because of a shortage of raw materials [Para 25][Para 33]. The company had suspended work twice before, from 7 to 16 June and from 20 June to 1 July 2005, with what the union accepted as proper notice [Para 27]. On 1 July 2005 the company requested permission to suspend; at a meeting on 4 July 2005 the labour inspector accepted that materials were short but said the notification could not issue until the morning of 5 July 2005 [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]. The company then assigned four administrative staff to tell workers verbally at the gates, missing roughly 50 or 60 who had already left [Para 46]. At the hearing the workers confined their demand to wages for 5 and 6 July 2005, dropping the bonus [Para 21][Para 22].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay wages to the approximately 1,400 workers for the suspension of 5 and 6 July 2005 because notice of the suspension was not given in advance? [Para 12][Para 26]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, Khmer Youth Free Trade Union (KYFTU)):** most workers learned of the suspension only when they arrived at 8:30 a.m. on 5 July 2005, the verbal communication having been given at 4:30 p.m. when office hours had already ended at 4:00 p.m.; the company also let some 380 workers in the finishing section keep working, so the suspension was irregular and the company must bear the wages [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28][Para 29][Para 39][Para 47].
**Employer (Ocean Garment):** the suspension responded to a genuine shortage of raw materials, the request was filed on 1 July 2005, the inspectorate met on 4 July and accepted the reasons, and the delay in issuing the notification lay with the Ministry, not the company [Para 33][Para 39][Para 40][Para 45][Para 46].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the company provide wages during the suspension of 5 and 6 July 2005 [Para 55].
The panel framed the question under Article 71(11) of the 1997 Labour Law, which allows suspension of an enterprise's operation for not more than two months where it faces serious economic or material difficulty or any particularly unusual difficulty, and it noted that a suspended contract may be terminated only after the reasons are remedied and prior notice given [Para 40][Para 41]. It found the four sections, employing 1,400 workers in total, were suspended on 5 and 6 July 2005 and turned to whether Article 71(11) was followed [Para 41][Para 42]. The request had been filed with the Director of the Labour Inspection Department on 1 July 2005, and on 4 July 2005 the inspector and labour security officials met worker and employer representatives, agreed with the request and recognised the material difficulty [Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]. Because the meeting ran to 5:30 p.m., the official could not issue the notification until the morning of 5 July 2005, and the company posted the approval letter No. 917 KKBVIAK as soon as it arrived [Para 46][Para 53][Para 55]. On the competing accounts of how many workers were warned verbally, the workers could produce no document to prove who had left or stayed, and the panel found the short delay attributable to the Ministry [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]. It held the suspension procedurally proper, observing that the 1997 Labour Law fixes no period of prior notice, so the duty is to post the approval letter immediately or as soon as possible after receipt [Para 52][Para 54]. Refusal to pay wages for the two days was therefore proper and reasonable [Para 55].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A labour contract suspension under Article 71(11) of the Labour Law is procedurally proper where the employer has requested the Labour Inspectorate's permission, the inspectorate has verified the material difficulty and issued an approval letter, and the employer posts that letter immediately on receipt; the Labour Law prescribes no minimum period of prior notice to the workers, and a delay caused by the inspectorate itself does not transfer the wage burden to the employer [Para 51][Para 52][Para 54].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remark that the company's earlier suspensions in June 2005 carried proper notice serves only as background and did not enter the holding [Para 27].
