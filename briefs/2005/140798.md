# Award No. 68/05-Gold Lida — Gold Lida Garment Co, Ltd and Coalition of Cambodian Apparel WDU (C.CAWDU) (Arbitration Council, 28 November 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Sin Kim Sean, Sok Mathoeung chair) · Doc ID 140798

## 1. Facts
Gold Lida Garment Co, Ltd occupies building 650, National Road 2, Sangkat Chak Angreh Leur, Khan Meanchey, Phnom Penh; it was founded in 1995 as "United Eternity" and later renamed [Para 11][Para 12]. The C.CAWDU union at the factory was registered on 14 May 2001 [Para 13]. The employer reports about 600 workers, of whom around 500 have more than three years' service, 50 have more than six years, and the rest between 4 and 11 months [Para 13]. Of nine demands, seven were settled in conciliation and one of the two remaining was settled at the hearing, leaving the additional annual leave for long-serving workers [Para 7][Para 8][Para 9].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether workers with three or six years' service are entitled to additional paid annual leave beyond 18 days, and how that increment accrues in later years? [Para 4][Para 20]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), C.CAWDU and C.CAWDU of Gold Lida Garment:** one extra day of paid leave must be added for each three years of service and must continue in later years, so 19 days in the 4th to 6th years and 20 days from the 7th year [Para 18][Para 19].
**Employer (respondent), Gold Lida Garment Co, Ltd:** the company already complies by giving one extra day in the 4th year and again in the 7th year only, producing 19 days in those years, and argues that the Labour Law is unclear on the point [Para 4][Para 16][Para 17].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered Gold Lida company to provide 19 days of paid annual leave in the 4th, 5th and 6th years of service and 20 days in the 7th, 8th and 9th years for workers with seniority of three or six years respectively [Para 48]. The award is binding immediately because both parties chose a binding award on 11 November 2005 [Para 48].
The dispute turned on Article 166(4) of the Labour Law, which increases paid leave "at the rate of one day per three years of service", a text the panel found unclear as to whether the increment repeats [Para 15][Para 19][Para 45]. Invoking Article 312 jurisdiction and the principle that unclear law is read by its purpose, the panel defined seniority through Articles 73 and 75 (notice periods rising with service), Article 89 (dismissal compensation rising with service) and Article 95 (last-in-first-out in mass layoffs), each of which increases rights progressively and never reduces them [Para 22][Para 26][Para 31][Para 35].
From that the panel concluded that seniority is continuous service which automatically adds rights, cannot be reduced by the employer's will or agreement, and ends only when the worker leaves [Para 35][Para 39][Para 40]. Equity reinforced the reading, since longer service correlates with better performance and greater productivity for the employer [Para 41][Para 43][Para 44]. The increment therefore accumulates: one extra day after three years and a second after six [Para 47].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The annual leave increment in Article 166(4) of the Labour Law accrues with seniority and is not spent once granted, so a worker with three years' service keeps the extra day in every later year and gains a further day at six years, because seniority rights under the Labour Law increase progressively and cannot be reduced [Para 45][Para 47].

**Obiter dicta:** none identified; the panel's survey of Articles 73, 75, 89 and 95 was used to construe Article 166(4) and not decided as separate claims [Para 26][Para 31].
