# Award No. 106/04 — Suit Way and Larisaka Fashion Factory Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 10 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ly Tayseng, Liv Sovanna, chair Sok Mathoeung) · Doc ID 139360

## 1. Facts
Suit Way and Larisaka Fashion Factory Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment factory in Tuol Sangke employing 690 workers and hosting three unions, was confronted with 13 demands by the Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) [Para 19][Para 20]. Labour inspectors attempted conciliation on 23 November 2004 and the case reached the Council on 6 December 2004; eleven demands were settled and only two remained — reimbursement of health check fees and of employment-book charges [Para 21][Para 22][Para 10]. The panel heard the parties on 16, 23 and 27 December 2004, with Ly Tayseng, Liv Sovanna and chair Sok Mathoeung sitting [Para 15][Para 18].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must reimburse the 10,100 riel health check fee each worker paid before commencing employment? [Para 10][Para 24]
2. Whether the employer must refund the money it collected for employment books above the 2,500 riel fixed by law? [Para 10][Para 28]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Labour Law places pre-employment medical checks at the employer's cost, yet each worker paid 10,100 riel personally; the company collected 12,000 riel per employment book while the law requires only 2,500 riel, and the excess should come back [Para 25][Para 27].
**Employer (respondent):** the law does not state expressly that the employer must pay for a pre-employment medical check, and payment was company policy; the book money had already been handed to the Ministry, workers signed agreeing to 12,000 riel, and the company offered to help them petition the Ministry for the excess [Para 26][Para 28][Para 29].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the company was ordered to pay back 10,100 riel per worker for medical checks and 4,100 riel per worker for employment-book overcharges, within one month, but only to the 29 KYFTU members listed in Annex 1 [Para 53][Para 54][Para 55].

Preliminarily, the panel held that KYFTU lacked most representative status under Article 6 of Prakas 305/01, so it could represent only its own members, and only those whose membership applications and wage-deduction forms predated 6 December 2004, when the dispute was referred; the award was therefore confined to the listed 29 members [Para 32][Para 34][Para 35].

On issue 1, Article 247(c) of the 1997 Labour Law was found an adequate legal basis on its own for requiring the employer to bear medical check costs even though no new Prakas had been issued, the panel also invoking Joint Prakas 09/94 and Article 7 thereof while acknowledging its own contrasting opinions on whether that Prakas survived the 1997 Law; award 60/04 (United Art) was cited for the dissent, and 02/03 Chou Sing, 21/03 Loyal Cambodia, 19/04 Kbal Koh 2 and 53/04 Kong Hong for the earlier practice [Para 37][Para 38][Para 41]. Workers paying their own fees did not discharge the employer's obligation, so the full 10,100 riel was ordered reimbursed [Para 42][Para 43].

On issue 2, Article 32 of the Labour Law makes the employment card the worker's own responsibility, and a Ministry letter of 29 May 1997 fixes its price at 2,500 riel (1,500 riel application form plus 1,000 riel stamp); deductions beyond that were improper [Para 43][Para 45]. The panel nevertheless refused to order repayment of the whole 9,500 riel excess: because the company had collected and processed the money, workers saved roughly half a day, valued at US$0.86, and about 2,000 riel in travel, or some 5,400 riel in total, so repayment of the full overcharge would leave the employer losing while workers gained [Para 49][Para 50][Para 51]. Using its power over civil damages under Article 34 of Prakas 99/04, the panel set the refund at 4,100 riel (12,000 riel minus 2,500 riel for the book and 5,400 riel) per worker [Para 52].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer bears the cost of pre-employment medical checks under Article 247(c) even absent a new Prakas, and a worker's own payment does not shift that cost; separately, where an employer collects employment-book money, it may retain only the statutory 2,500 riel, but the refund of the excess is reduced by the value of the practical saving the collection service gave the workers, so the remedy is restitution of the net overcharge rather than of the whole sum collected [Para 41][Para 45][Para 52].

**Obiter dicta:** that the workers' true debtor for money actually handed to Ministry officials was the receiving official, and that a union without most representative status speaks only for members enrolled before the referral date [Para 48][Para 35].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The award states both that eleven issues were conciliated and that the union withdrew three so that eight were settled, and the arithmetic in the two passages cannot both be right [Para 9][Para 22]. KYFTU's registration is dated 29 September 2004 in the findings but its certificate is dated 29 December 2004 in the evidence list [Para 30][Para 18]. Annex 1 is heavily corrupted: the columns are jumbled, several entries carry no name or no identification number, and the list stops at 29 although the panel speaks of 29 members, so the membership roll cannot be verified from the text [Para 63]. "Men Tin" in the party list appears as "Men Tyn" in the annex [Para 6][Para 63]. The decision makes the award effective immediately on receipt while the closing paragraph recites the ordinary eight-day rule, which the parties' agreement to a binding award explains but does not reconcile [Para 55][Para 59].
