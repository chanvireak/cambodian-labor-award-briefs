# Award No. 49/05 — Ocean Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 September 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Kao Thach, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bunchhea (Chair) · Doc ID 140787

## 1. Facts
Ocean Garment Co., Ltd, a factory at Prey Trea Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employed 1,636 workers when the Free Trade Union of Workers of Kingdom of Cambodia (FTUWKC) brought a collective dispute against it [Para 6][Para 1]. On 17 August 2005 the workers struck to demand improved working conditions, and conciliation by the Labour Inspector settled four of the eight demands, leaving four to the panel [Para 7][Para 8]. The disputed acts were the suspension of about 1,400 workers in the Cutting, Sewing, Warehouse and Mechanical sections on 5–6 July 2005 and the strike that ran from 17 to 23 August 2005 [Para 20][Para 39]. Two of the four demands — 50 percent wages during a suspension covered by a Ministry authorisation, and 100 percent wages where the employer has no work for up to seven days — went beyond the Labour Law and the panel declined to decide them [Para 44][Para 70].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay wages for the 5–6 July 2005 suspension of about 1,400 workers, where most workers learned of the suspension only on the morning it took effect? [Para 22]
2. (Issue 3) Whether the workers are entitled to their wages and attendance bonuses for the strike period of 17–23 August 2005? [Para 71]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** FTUWKC argued that the suspension was unlawful because most workers were told only on 5 July 2005, after they had already come to work, and that letting 127 workers in the Finishing section keep working showed the suspension was irregular [Para 22][Para 29]. It sought full wages and attendance bonuses for the strike period [Para 71].
**Employer (respondent):** Ocean Garment Co., Ltd said it requested approval on 1 July 2005 and again on 4 July 2005, was told by the Labour Inspection official that formal notification could not issue until the next morning, and gave verbal notice at the gates; the short delay was the Ministry's responsibility [Para 24][Para 26][Para 31].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider the 50 percent and seven-day demands, rejected the demand for wages for the 5–6 July 2005 suspension, and rejected the demand for wages and attendance bonuses for the 17–23 August 2005 strike [Para 82].
On Issue 2 the panel held the suspension lawful: Article 71(11) of the 1997 Labour Law permits suspension for serious economic or material difficulty for not more than two months under Labour Inspector control [Para 39]. The Department of Labour Inspection approved it by letter No. 917 dated 4 July 2005, so the delay from 5:30 p.m. to 8:30 a.m. was a technical problem of the Ministry rather than a breach by the company [Para 61]. Article 72(1) means a suspension removes both the duty to work and the duty to pay wages [Para 42][Para 43]. The panel added that the authorisation letter obliged the company to inform all workers, and that it met this by posting the letter at 8:00 a.m. on receipt [Para 63][Para 65].
On the strike limb, Article 324 permits a strike only after all peaceful methods of settlement have been tried, and this strike broke out without prior notification [Para 72][Para 73]. Prior awards hold that attendance bonuses are payable only where the strike followed legal procedure [Para 76]. Article 332 provides that a strike suspends the labour contract, so wages are not paid during a strike even where it is lawful [Para 77][Para 80]. Because the strike here was illegal, neither wages nor bonuses were due [Para 81].
The declined demands were disposed of on standing and on the nature of the claim: Article 43 of Prakas 099 dated 21 April 2004 makes an award on an interest dispute a collective bargaining agreement binding all workers for one year, and FTUWKC lacked most representative status in the factory, so it could not bring an interest dispute at all [Para 47][Para 45][Para 49]. Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented on Issue 2, arguing on equity under Article 312(2) that employer and workers should share the damage caused by the late notification [Para 92][Para 94].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union without most representative status in the enterprise cannot bring an interest dispute to the Arbitration Council, because an award on such a dispute would become a collective bargaining agreement binding every worker in the factory [Para 47][Para 49]. A suspension approved by the Labour Inspector under Article 71(11) is lawful despite a one-night delay in notifying workers caused by the Ministry, the employer discharging its duty by posting the authorisation as soon as it arrives [Para 61][Para 65]. Wages are not payable during any strike, lawful or unlawful, and attendance bonuses are payable only where the strike followed legal procedure [Para 80][Para 76].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that, to maintain good workplace relations, an employer should still notify employees of a suspension in advance [Para 66].
