# Award No. 109/04 — Great Fortune Garment (Arbitration Council, 4 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Tuon Siphann, chair Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 139361

## 1. Facts
Great Fortune Garment, a knitting factory at Prek Khse village, Sa'ang district, Kandal, employing 2,700 workers, was struck after the Kandal labour inspectors failed on 14 December 2004 to conciliate three demands about piece rates for "PN" lots and leave during work shortages [Para 22][Para 24]. The Council ordered a return to work on 15 December and brokered an agreement to that effect on 16 December, but the stoppage resumed and further conciliation on 23 and 28 December failed [Para 26][Para 27][Para 32]. The hearing was held on 23 December 2004 before Kao Thach, Tuon Siphann and chair Kong Phallack [Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the knitting workers in the old building are entitled to a lot price of US$4.30 for PN 113? [Para 7]
2. Whether the knitting workers in the new building are entitled to an increase in the lot price of all styles by US$0.50? [Para 7]
3. Whether the workers are entitled to paid leave when the company has no work for them? [Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** a PN 133 lot takes two or three days to sew, so the employer's final offer of US$3.80 is too low against their demand of US$4.30, and PN 121 at US$4.20 is harder than PN 320 at the same price; workers who cannot reach the target are dismissed after three months, and when no work is available they should be paid [Para 41][Para 52][Para 46][Para 53].
**Employer (respondent):** the proposed prices are proper, and any worker earning under US$45 a month is topped up to US$45 as the law requires, so no legal duty is breached; there was enough work, but workers refused it and some sabotaged machines [Para 46][Para 53][Para 54].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all three claims, and instead ordered the company and worker representatives to form a bargaining committee to discuss resetting the prices for PN 133 and PN 121 and any other interests, negotiating within six months of the award taking effect at no less than one working hour per week [Para 78][Para 79].

On issues 1 and 2, the panel first classified them: because the employer already topped up sub-US$45 earnings, its conduct breached neither Article 104 of the Labour Law nor Notification 017/00, and the claims sought benefits above the legal floor, making them interests-based rather than rights-based [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. It separately held that the customary practice of the employer proposing and workers bargaining did not ensure compliance with Article 108, which requires piece rates allowing a worker of average ability working normally to earn at least the guaranteed minimum wage for the same hours, and that the employer must set prices accordingly even though a violation was not established [Para 63][Para 64].

The decisive obstacle was representational: the union in the case, C.CAWDUOFG, held neither representative nor most representative status, which under Article 277 of the Labour Law and Article 6 of Notification 305/01 requires more than half the factory's workers to register with the Ministry, and both parties conceded no union had that status [Para 66][Para 67][Para 55]. Since an award on an interests issue would, under Article 43 of Prakas 99/04, replace a collective bargaining agreement and bind all workers for a year, the panel held the union had no right to demand such an award, following 04/03 Lida, 06/04 Chou Sing, 24/03 Top One, 61/04 Best Honor, 62/04 Yi Sin, 31/03 Hong Wa, 60/04 United Art and 99/04 AIA [Para 65][Para 68][Para 69].

On issue 3, the Labour Law gives no right to paid leave where work is unavailable, and the right to strike is unpaid; the company produced evidence that jobs were offered and refused, so the claim exceeded the law and, being interests-based, failed for the same lack of representative status [Para 72][Para 73][Para 74][Para 75].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that does not hold representative or most representative status cannot obtain an award on an interests-based claim, because such an award would substitute for a collective bargaining agreement and bind every worker in the enterprise for one year; the panel's response is to refuse the claim and remit the matter to a mandatory bargaining process rather than to rule on the merits [Para 65][Para 68][Para 79]. Rates set by custom rather than by reference to Article 108 do not satisfy that article, and the employer must ensure its piece rates let a worker of average ability earn at least the guaranteed minimum wage [Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** the suggestion that the parties try a pilot sewing group drawn from workers of differing ability to fix lot prices, with a trial not exceeding three days [Para 70][Para 71].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The first issue is framed as a demand for US$4.30 on "PN 113", but the findings and reasons discuss PN 133 throughout, and the decision refers to PN 133; the paragraph numbers show the "113" as a typographical loss [Para 7][Para 40][Para 78]. The second issue is described in the non-conciliation report as a US$0.50 increase on all styles, while the reasons treat it as PN 121 moving from US$4.20 to US$4.70 [Para 7][Para 58]. Line 21 records that both parties agreed at the hearing that the award would not be binding, while the closing paragraphs recite the ordinary eight-day binding rule [Para 21][Para 82]. The union's name appears as "C.CAWDUOFG", "C.CAWDU C.CAWDUnion of Fortune Garment" and "C.CAWDUOGF" [Para 2][Para 66]. The worker representative is "Sien Sros" in the party list and "Sien Sras" in the findings, and "lem Lim" is probably "Iem Lim" [Para 6][Para 49]. Considerable OCR loss runs through the text ("thearing", "bargaing", "torder").
