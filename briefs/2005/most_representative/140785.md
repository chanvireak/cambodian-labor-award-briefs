# Award No. 45/05-B&N — B&N Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (Arbitration Council, 25 August 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Kao Tach, Tuon Siphann, Pen Bunchhea · Doc ID 140785

## 1. Facts
The dispute arose at B&N Garment (Cambodia) Co., Ltd., a Phnom Penh factory employing about 500 workers, nearly all female [Para 17] [Para 74]. A complaint listing 15 working-condition demands was made to the Labour Inspection Department on 13 July 2005; conciliation settled nine issues, and the remaining six were sent to the Arbitration Council on 4 August 2005 [Para 18] [Para 19] [Para 20]. The employer paid annual leave only to workers with over one year of service, in four groups by start date, with payment in the month following the relevant quarter [Para 22] [Para 25]. It said budget-transfer difficulties from abroad prevented payment before K. N. Year or P. B. [Para 26]. During no-work periods the company mandated one or two days' leave, deducting annual leave and paying 30% of wages where leave remained [Para 35] [Para 36]. No worker held an employment book, as recruitment never required one [Para 37] [Para 41]. Female workers were never allowed breastfeeding breaks and used their lunch break, sometimes returning late [Para 44] [Para 46]. The union had about 333 members and had not applied for most representative status [Para 40].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must grant annual leave or payment in lieu to workers with less than one year of service, and whether payment or leave must fall before K. N. Year or P. B.? [Para 11] [Para 60]
2. (Issue 2) Whether workers are entitled to 50% of daily wages when the company has no work for them? [Para 11] [Para 64]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay for the workers' employment books? [Para 11]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the company must enlarge the infirmary with hot water, build a nursing room, and allow two hours of daily breastfeeding for 18 months? [Para 12] [Para 13]
5. (Issue 5) Whether the company must pay wages to workers absent without permission on the same day other workers receive their wages? [Para 11]
6. (Issue 6) Whether the US$5 attendance bonus must be preserved for workers taking special leave or absent one day per month? [Para 11] [Para 13]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The employer asserted compliance with the Labour Law by paying annual leave only to workers with over one year of service in quarterly groups, and said advance festival payment was impossible because funds had to be transferred from abroad [Para 22] [Para 26]. It argued workers under one year had no statutory entitlement [Para 27]. On suspension, it said about five workers lacked work and were required to take leave, with annual leave deducted and 30% paid where available [Para 35] [Para 36]. It admitted not requiring employment books, but said workers must bear the cost and wages would be deducted for time spent obtaining them [Para 41] [Para 43]. It admitted refusing breastfeeding breaks and lacking a day-care centre, but offered a US$5 monthly child allowance and some breastfeeding time until 18 months [Para 48] [Para 50]. It allowed one leave day per month without a medical certificate while maintaining the attendance bonus, but wished to repeal that agreement as invalid [Para 55] [Para 56].

Workers demanded payment in lieu of leave before the festivals for family celebrations, and annual leave accruing from the start date [Para 28] [Para 30]. They claimed no wages for lawful suspension, but 50% wages where suspension was unlawful because the lack of work was the company's problem [Para 32] [Para 34]. They said the company's recruitment failure made it responsible for employment-book costs, needed for seniority proof [Para 38] [Para 39]. They sought two daily breastfeeding hours, a day-care centre, and hot water in the nursing room [Para 47] [Para 75]. They sought preservation of the agreed attendance-bonus practice for single-day absences [Para 54].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** Partly granted. The panel declined to consider annual leave for workers with under one year of service and refused payment in lieu of leave; ordered the company to arrange annual leave during K. N. Year and/or P. B.; ordered it to stop suspensions non-compliant with Article 71 and pay 100% of wages during suspensions unless approved by the Labour Inspector under Article 71(11); rejected the employment-book demand; ordered one hour of daily breastfeeding during working hours for 18 months from delivery; ordered a nursing room or day-care costs as agreed; and ordered a proportional attendance bonus for special leave, with the full bonus where overtime substitutes for the leave [Para 158] [Para 159] [Para 160] [Para 161].

On Issue 1, Article 167 gives paid leave only after one year of service, so the under-one-year demand exceeded the law and was an interest dispute [Para 84] [Para 86]. Because FTUWKC lacked most representative status, it had no legal standing to bring interest disputes [Para 88] [Para 89]. Payment in lieu of leave is contrary to Article 167(3), which voids agreements compensating or waiving paid leave [Para 103] [Para 105]. Article 170 requires annual leave at K. N. Year absent agreement, with notice to the Labour Inspector for other arrangements; the employer may limit leave to 15 days at a time but may not unilaterally fix leave timing [Para 95] [Para 100]. On Issue 2, annual leave could not be unilaterally substituted for no-work periods absent agreement, contrary to Article 170 [Para 116] [Para 119]. Legal suspension under Article 71(11) requires serious economic difficulty, a two-month maximum, and Labour Inspector control; without it, 100% of wages are due, not the 50% requested [Para 121] [Para 128] [Para 131]. On Issue 3, each worker must pay for their own employment book under Article 32 [Para 133] [Para 134]. On Issue 4, the two-hour demand exceeded Article 184's one hour and was an interest dispute, but the panel still ordered the statutory one hour daily [Para 137] [Para 141]. Article 186 requires a nursing room and day-care centre for enterprises with 100 or more women, or employer-paid external day-care [Para 142]. On Issue 6, Notice 017/00 of MoSALVY was unclear, so equity required a bonus proportional to days worked, as total forfeiture for authorised special leave would be unfair [Para 144] [Para 156] [Para 157].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union without most representative status cannot bring an interest dispute seeking benefits beyond statutory minimums, because an award settling an interest dispute takes the place of a CBA and would bind other workers [Para 90] [Para 92]. Annual leave vests only after one year of service and cannot be commuted into payment; its timing requires agreement unless taken at K. N. Year [Para 84] [Para 105]. A no-work suspension outside Article 71(11) and Labour Inspector control requires full wages, and an award cannot grant less than the law guarantees [Para 128] [Para 131] [Para 133]. Statutory maternity entitlements may be ordered even where a broader demand is a non-justiciable interest dispute [Para 141].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel cited prior awards on interest disputes, employment books, nursing rooms and suspension jurisprudence [Para 91] [Para 129] [Para 135] [Para 143].
