# Award No. 42/05 — Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 3 August 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3: Ing Sothy (employer-chosen), Liv Sovanna (worker-chosen), Run Saray (chair) · Doc ID 140782

## 1. Facts
Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd operates on Street 210, Prek Samraung, Takmao commune, Takmao district, Kandal province, and employs 4,000 workers, 350 of them members of the Coalition of Cambodian Apparel Workers Democratic Union (C.CAWDU) [Para 1][Para 14]. Workers punch in and out through seven card reading machines with their identity cards, and the company assesses attendance from each machine's report; a worker recorded as arriving five minutes or more late in a month does not receive the attendance bonus [Para 26][Para 28][Para 30]. The attendance bonus of US$5 per month is provided by Notification 017/MoSALVY dated 18 July 2000, which entitles workers who come to work regularly on the regular working days of the month [Para 41][Para 57]. A complaint went to the labour inspector on 7 June 2005 and conciliation opened on 8 July 2005 over 12 issues, 10 of which were settled, leaving two for the panel [Para 14][Para 15]. Non-conciliation report number 005/05 reached the Secretariat on 13 July 2005 and the hearing was held on 18 July 2005 [Para 14][Para 16]. On the evening of 12 April 2005 the company announced that day-shift workers need not attend from 14 to 16 April 2005 for Khmer New Year while night-shift workers stopped from 13 April, and on 13 April the day-shift workers realised the night shift had been released a day earlier and stayed away [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]. At the hearing the workers reformulated the first demand, asking for a full US$5 bonus where the machines misread a worker once a month [Para 19]. The company then cut their April attendance bonus [Para 56].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel can decide the workers' demand that the company cut their attendance bonus by only US$2.50, later put at US$5, where the card reading machines fail to record their attendance once a month [Para 10][Para 19]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the day-shift workers who did not attend work on 13 April 2005 are entitled to the attendance bonus for April 2005? [Para 55][Para 56]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant, C.CAWDU):** the card reading machines do not always work properly and sometimes record a wrong arrival time even when a worker punches in on time, so the company should cut the bonus by only US$2.50, or in the version put at the hearing provide the full US$5, where the machine misreads a worker once a month; on Issue 2 the day shift stayed away because the night shift was released a day earlier, an unequal treatment [Para 10][Para 19][Para 30][Para 35].
**Employer (Yung Wah Industrial (Cambodia) Co., Ltd):** the machines work properly and make no mistakes, and workers who do pay attention do not lose the bonus; the different treatment of the shifts followed Prakas 333/05 making 14, 15 and 16 April 2005 the public holiday, and the night shift starting at 6:30 p.m. on 13 April would have ended at 5:00 a.m. on 14 April [Para 30][Para 36][Para 37].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel dismissed the workers' demand on the first issue of non-conciliation report 005/05 as clarified at the hearing of 18 July 2005, and rejected the demand for the attendance bonus in April 2005 [Para 59].
On Issue 1 the panel first classified the demand: a rights dispute concerns legal rights arising from a contract, a collective bargaining agreement or provisions of law, while an interests dispute concerns a future benefit not resting on such rights [Para 40][Para 41]. The demand was based on Notification 017/00 but sought no prior bonuses already cut, only relief in the future, so it was a rights dispute about a future occurrence [Para 41][Para 48]. The panel held that it cannot decide a rights dispute which may occur in the future, because it can never know what scenarios will arise or which parties and provisions will be involved, and because deciding a rights dispute needs specific evidence of actual events compared against the law [Para 49][Para 50]. It noted the workers' two alternative routes: to claim bonuses already cut, with evidence that a machine fault caused the cut, or to seek an additional comparison system as an interests demand, which would generally fail for a union without most-representative status, citing 31/03-Wah [Para 51][Para 53][Para 54]. Conciliation on an extra comparison system was attempted at the hearing and the employer refused, the workers maintaining their demand [Para 42][Para 43][Para 45]. On Issue 2 the panel applied the attendance bonus provision in Notification 017/MoSALVY dated 18 July 2000, and found 13 April 2005 to be a working day under Notification 333/MoLVT dated 11 March 2005 [Para 57]. Day-shift workers who absented themselves without authorisation were therefore not entitled to the benefit regardless of their motives [Para 58]. The panel added that the employer should in future explain to its workers why it treats the shifts differently [Para 59]. The award is non-binding and binds eight days after notification absent written opposition [Para 59].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that an employer refrain from cutting a bonus on a contingency that has not yet happened is a rights dispute about the future, which the panel will not decide: the workers must instead claim a bonus already cut and prove by evidence of actual events that a faulty card reading machine caused it [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51]. Where an employee is absent without authorisation on a day that the applicable labour notification treats as a working day, the monthly attendance bonus is forfeited whatever the motive [Para 57][Para 58].

**Obiter dicta:** the suggestion that the employer should explain to its workers why it releases the two shifts differently for Khmer New Year is prospective advice, not an order, and did not affect either dismissal [Para 59].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Duplication is heavy: whole sentences and headings appear twice in one paragraph, for example the company's name and workforce sentence and the statement that the employer did not agree [Para 14][Para 37][Para 45]. One sentence is garbled into nonsense, recording that "the workers still maintained their machines" where their demand was evidently meant, and the employers' refusal is printed twice [Para 44][Para 45]. OCR drops the "t" of "the" throughout ("thearing", "withe", "attendance bonus whose attendance") and renders "Training" as "Traing" [Para 14][Para 30]. The instrument establishing the holiday is printed as Prakas 333/05 in the exhibit list and as Notification 333/MoLVT dated 11 March 2005 in the reasons, and the attendance bonus instrument appears both as Notification 017/MoSALVY and as Notification 017/00 [Para 20][Para 41][Para 57]. The header breaks the union representatives' names and titles across lines so that surnames and offices run together, and one employer representative's name appears in two spellings [Para 21][Para 26].
