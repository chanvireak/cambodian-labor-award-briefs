# Award No. 53/05 — Finegis (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 1 October 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Ang Eng Thong (Chair) · Doc ID 140790

## 1. Facts
Finegis (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd, at Prey Tea Village, Sangkat Chom Chao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employed 1,285 workers, with undetermined-duration contracts in the Old building and three-month fixed-duration contracts in the New building [Para 18][Para 26]. On 26 August 2005 about 500 workers struck over 14 working conditions; conciliation settled 10 and forwarded four to the panel [Para 9][Para 44]. Separately, on 10 June 2005 the company failed to pay wages on the usual pay day because funds had not arrived from Korea, and from 11 June 2005 workers refused to work until they were paid; the company sent them home, the Old building closed, and on 15 June 2005 workers were paid but had wages for 11, 13 and 14 June 2005 and the US$5 attendance bonus deducted [Para 32][Para 37].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must convert the fixed-duration contracts of three months into undetermined-duration contracts? [Para 1]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the workers are entitled to the US$5 attendance bonus for June 2005 and to wages for 11 to 15 June 2005, which the company deducted after the stoppage? [Para 3]
3. (Issue 3) Whether the company must pay an additional 300 riel per hour whenever overtime exceeds two hours a day? [Para 6]
4. (Issue 4) Whether the workers are entitled to the US$5 attendance bonus for August 2005 and to wages for the strike days of 26, 27 and 29 August 2005? [Para 8]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Federal Union of Solidarity (FUS) and Federal Union of Solidarity of Finegis Factory argued that fixed-duration contracts let the employer dismiss a unionist at will, that the June deductions punished workers who had simply refused to work without pay, that the 300 riel top-up was owed because the law permits only two hours of overtime, and that the August strike was lawful because notice had been given a month in advance [Para 28][Para 41][Para 45].
**Employer (respondent):** Finegis (Cambodia) Garment Co., Ltd said the fixed-duration contracts were used to evaluate attitude and performance, not to dismiss workers, that the June stoppage was an unlawful strike so Article 332 applied, and that it could not afford the 300 riel increase [Para 30][Para 8][Para 7].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the demand for undetermined-duration contracts, rejected wages and the attendance bonus for 11, 13 and 14 June 2005, declined to consider the 300 riel demand, and rejected the attendance bonus for August 2005 and wages for the 26, 27 and 29 August 2005 strike [Para 98].
On Issue 1 the panel held that Article 65 places the labour contract under ordinary law, and Article 1 of Decree No. 38 on Contract Law makes a contract an agreement freely entered into, so no third party, the panel included, can force a contract type on an unwilling employer [Para 53][Para 54][Para 59]. The three-month fixed-duration contract was not contrary to any law and did not discriminate [Para 61]. The panel did note, as a compliance problem, that the company had neither a worker delegate nor Internal Work Rules [Para 62].
On Issue 2 the panel asked whether the workers were on strike from 11 to 15 June 2005 and held that they were: the stoppage was concerted and aimed at payment of wages already due [Para 66][Para 73]. It ran from 11 to 14 June 2005, excluding Sunday 12 June [Para 74]. The strike was unlawful because Article 323 procedures and the prior notice required by Article 324 were not followed [Para 74][Para 80]. Under Article 332 a strike suspends the labour contract and the salary is not paid, even for a lawful strike, while an attendance bonus is payable only in proportion where the strike is lawful [Para 75][Para 77][Para 79]. The panel followed 04/03-Lyda Garment and 12/04-P&E [Para 80].
On Issue 3 the panel classified the demand: a rights dispute concerns a right in a contract, collective agreement or legal provision, an interest dispute concerns future benefits [Para 81]. Article 140 on extended hours concerns make-up time after mass interruptions such as bad weather or holidays, not voluntary overtime, so the premise that only two hours of overtime are lawful was wrong [Para 84][Para 85]. Notification 017 of 18 July 2000 entitles a volunteer overtime worker to a 1,000 riel meal allowance or a free meal regardless of hours [Para 87]. The demand therefore went beyond the law and was an interest dispute, which a union without most representative status cannot bring, following 31/03-Hong Wah and 42/05-Yong Wah [Para 88][Para 90].
On Issue 4 the panel applied Articles 332 and 334: wages are not paid during a strike, and Article 334 does not assist because no replacement workers were recruited [Para 90]. Article 324 requires at least seven working days' prior notice, and the claim that a notice letter accompanied the request of 25 July 2005 was unfounded [Para 92][Para 94]. Article 333 bars punishment of strikers but protects only a lawful strike, so the bonus deduction was not a disciplinary act [Para 95][Para 96]. Arbitrator Liv Sovanna dissented on Issue 2, treating the closure of the Old building from the afternoon of 11 June to 14 June 2005 as a lockout under Article 318, unnotified as Article 324 requires, which under Article 335 obliges the employer to pay two-and-a-half days' wages [Para 102][Para 109][Para 110].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The panel cannot order an employer to replace fixed-duration contracts with undetermined-duration contracts, because Article 65 and Decree No. 38 leave the form of contract to the parties' agreement and prohibit a third party from imposing it [Para 55][Para 57]. A union lacking most representative status may not bring an interest dispute, so a demand for a benefit beyond what the law and Notification 017 provide is declined rather than decided [Para 88][Para 90]. Wages are never payable for strike days under Article 332, and the proportional attendance bonus is payable only where the strike complied with the notice requirements of Article 324 [Para 77][Para 79][Para 92].

**Obiter dicta:** an enterprise without a worker delegate and Internal Work Rules fails to comply with the Labour Law and will struggle to resolve problems at enterprise level [Para 62][Para 63]. The panel also listed the legal limits on fixed-duration contracts — two-month probation cap, two-year renewal ceiling, automatic conversion on expiry — without applying them [Para 64][Para 65].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Issue 4's demand is printed as strike days "26, 27 and 29 June August 2005", mixing months, and the decision repeats the form without resolving it [Para 90][Para 98]. The dissenting annex is captioned "Annex to Award 53105" although the award is 53/05 [Para 98]. Prior awards are cited inconsistently as "04/03-Lyda Garment", "04/03 - Lynda Garment" and "12/05 = P&E" versus "12/04-P&E" [Para 80][Para 97]. Personal names are split onto separate numbered lines throughout, so the party and witness lists are fragmented [Para 13][Para 15]. Words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "withemployer") [Para 19].
