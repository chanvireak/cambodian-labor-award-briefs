# Award No. 16/05 — New Point World Trade Cambodia Company v Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Union at New Point World Trade Company (Arbitration Council, 8 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Ouk Ry (employer party), Mr Liv Sovanna (worker party), Mr Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 140766

## 1. Facts
New Point Trade Cambodia Company is at #7, National Road Number 5, Resey Keo commune, Resey Keo district, Phnom Penh and employs approximately 700 workers [Para 23]. Two unions operate there: the Union of Workers affiliated with the Cambodian Labour Union Federation, which holds most representative status, and KYFTU, which had no registration certificate [Para 23][Para 24]. The labour inspectorate received a complaint on 14 February 2005 and conciliated on 6 and 7 March 2005 without settling the 12 demands [Para 24][Para 25][Para 26]. The case reached the Council on 21 March 2005 and seven of the 12 issues were settled at the hearing [Para 28][Para 29]. KYFTU was established on 24 December 2004 and applied for registration on 30 December 2004 [Para 56]. Its vice-president, Ms O.D., was elected on 24 December 2004, was suspended for lack of work from December to January 2005, and received three warnings on 21 October 2004, 2 March 2005 and 7 March 2005 [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35][Para 36][Para 42][Para 44]. The company dismissed her on 7 March 2005 under internal work rule clause 2 point 7 and clause 10 without informing the labour inspector [Para 49]. It paid US$274 in severance, notice compensation, damages and annual leave, of which she accepted only the US$53.58 final month's wage [Para 55][Para 56].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must compensate medical check costs of 10,100 riel per worker [Para 6][Para 30]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must reimburse the amount charged for employment cards above the required fee [Para 6][Para 30]?
3. (Issue 9) Whether the company must pay full wages to Ms O.D. for the period of leave it authorised [Para 8][Para 32]?
4. (Issue 11) Whether the company must deduct a 1,000 riel union contribution from the wages of workers who joined KYFTU [Para 8][Para 56]?
5. (Issue 12) Whether the company must reinstate Ms O.D. as vice-president of KYFTU at New Point [Para 8][Para 32]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Khmer Youth Federation of Trade Union (KYFTU) and Khmer Youth Union at New Point World Trade Company:** the company should pay 10,100 riel per worker for medical checks and refund the excess charged for employment cards [Para 6]. It should pay Ms O.D. full wages for the leave it authorised, deduct 1,000 riel per month for KYFTU dues, and reinstate her [Para 8]. She alleged that the real reason for dismissal was her refusal to resign from KYFTU, though she produced no evidence [Para 54]. KYFTU's representative argued that the Council had refused a comparable agreement in 60/04-United Art [Para 72].
**Employer (respondent), New Point World Trade Cambodia Company:** the CLUF union with most representative status signed an agreement on 6 April 2005 settling medical checks at 5,000 riel and the employment card at US$3, and the company had already paid [Para 30][Para 31][Para 32]. It dismissed Ms O.D. for misconduct after three warnings, denied the union reason, and followed its internal work rules [Para 49][Para 55]. It refused the KYFTU deduction because that union lacked a registration certificate and workers might object to the loss of wages [Para 58][Para 59].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the medical check and employment card demands, ordered the company to reinstate Ms O.D. in her previous position when the award takes effect and to reimburse her wages and other benefits from the dismissal date until reinstatement, and ordered the deduction of 1,000 riel per month from each worker who consents in writing and belongs to KYFTU [Para 98][Para 99][Para 101][Para 102].
On Issues 1 and 2, the panel noted Article 247(c) of the Labour Law, which requires employers to bear the cost of medical examinations, and followed prior awards applying it [Para 60]. Article 32 requires every worker to hold an employment card, and the panel had held that the worker pays for it only up to the official fee, with any excess reimbursed [Para 61][Para 62]. It then examined the 6 April 2005 agreement between the employer and CLUF and found it did not violate Article 13, which makes labour law rules of public order [Para 63][Para 64]. The agreement settled past disputes only and did not bind the parties for the future [Para 65][Para 66]. Since it gave 5,000 riel to all employees without requiring proof of deduction and even where the three-year limitation period under Article 120 had expired, the panel found its terms consistent with public order and rejected the demands [Para 69][Para 70][Para 74].
On Issues 9 and 12, the panel cited Article 293, which allows dismissal of a shop steward or candidate only with labour inspector authorisation, and clause 4(2) of Prakas 305/2001 extending protection to three union leaders [Para 75]. The company's own letter of 12 January 2005 refusing to acknowledge the new union showed it knew the committee was being registered and that its leaders were protected [Para 82][Para 83]. The law requires the employer to obtain the inspector's approval, and the company neither informed nor sought it [Para 84][Para 85]. The dismissal was therefore contrary to Article 293, and the company's argument that ordinary workers may be dismissed under the internal rules did not apply to a union vice-president [Para 87][Para 88][Para 89].
On Issue 11, the panel cited Article 129, which permits wage deductions for union dues where the worker authorises them, and clause 5(5) of Prakas 305/01, which requires a written request 15 days in advance [Para 90][Para 92][Para 94]. It found the company understood the law because it deducted 1,000 riel for CLUF [Para 94]. Under Article 268(2), a union is deemed registered if the Ministry does not reply within two months of the application, so KYFTU became legal two months after 30 December 2004 [Para 96]. The company must therefore make the deduction [Para 97][Para 102].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer cannot dismiss a union leader without prior authorisation from the labour inspector under Article 293, and the employer's knowledge that the union committee is being registered, shown by its own correspondence with the Ministry, makes the procedural requirement binding [Para 75][Para 83][Para 85]. A union becomes legally registered two months after applying where the Ministry does not reply, under Article 268(2), so the employer must withhold the dues of its members on written request under Article 129 and Prakas 305/01 clause 5(5) [Para 92][Para 96]. An agreement settling past medical check and employment card costs does not offend Article 13 and bars the individual claims it covers [Para 64][Para 66][Para 74].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The OCR is severely corrupted, repeating whole clauses twice in the same line and merging the individual's name into preceding sentences throughout [Para 37][Para 38][Para 44][Para 48]. The employer's arbitrator is printed "Ou Ry" in the panel list [Para 10] and "Ouk Ry" at the signature [Para 103]. The company is called "New Point World Trade Cambodia" in the style of cause [Para 1], "New Point Trade Cambodia" in the case summary [Para 23] and "New Point World Trade" in the address [Para 4]. The district appears as "Resey Keo" and the commune as "Methapheap Villege" [Para 6]. The agreement relied on is dated 6 April 2005, after the 25 March 2005 hearing [Para 12][Para 31]. The conciliated issue list omits a comma so that "five six" reads as one number [Para 29], and prior awards are cited as "27103-Standard Garment" and "Mecury" for 27/03 and Mercury [Para 90][Para 60].
