# Award No. 09/05 — Kin Tai Co. Ltd v C.CAWDU Federation and Union (Arbitration Council, 22 March 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Kao Thach (employer party), Mr Ven Pov (worker party), Mr Pen Bunchhear (chair) · Doc ID 140760

## 1. Facts
Kin Tai Cambodia Garment Co., Ltd., located on National Road No. 2 in Meanchey district, Phnom Penh, employed approximately 800 workers, down from about 1,000 [Para 18][Para 24]. The company had already terminated the contracts of approximately 350 workers in the cutting, packing and laundry sections and paid them compensation under Articles 75, 89 and 91 of the Labor Law [Para 25]. It did not terminate the roughly 800 workers in the sewing section and paid them nothing [Para 25]. The sewing workers therefore demanded that the company terminate their contracts as well and pay them the same compensation [Para 26][Para 56]. The union brought the dispute after conciliation settled one of three points and referred the other two to the Arbitration Council [Para 20][Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel should order the company to terminate the employment contracts of the sewing-section workers and pay them termination compensation? [Para 7][Para 34]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the company must pay compensation under Articles 75, 89 and 91 of the Labor Law to workers who resign from work individually? [Para 9][Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), C.CAWDU Federation and Union:** the company's selective termination denied fairness and equality to all workers, and the company pressured employees to perform two or three different tasks until they were forced to quit [Para 25]. The workers wanted the company to terminate the sewing section's contracts and pay the same compensation as the other sections [Para 26].
**Employer (respondent), Kin Tai Co. Ltd:** the demands had no legal basis, because the company terminated the 350 workers for a clear economic reason and itself decided the termination, following the Labor Law on compensation [Para 27]. It still needed the sewing workers and had no policy or intention to terminate them [Para 28].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the company terminate them, declined to consider the demand for compensation under Articles 75, 89 and 91 for any employee who resigns individually, and ordered the employer and employees to bargain to settle the second issue [Para 57].
On Issue 1, the panel held that Article 65 of the Labor Law establishes the employment relation, and although no written contract was produced, both parties confirmed the contracts were of unspecified duration [Para 29]. Article 74 lets either party terminate such a contract at will, subject to prior written notice [Para 31]. The employee therefore has sufficient rights to cancel by written notice, but cannot force the employer to cancel, since hiring, rehiring and terminating are the employer's rights [Para 32][Para 33]. The demand to force cancellation was held contrary to the Labor Law [Para 34]. The panel added that terminating the three sections was the employer's lawful right where work was unavailable, and continuing to employ the sewing workers did not violate the law unless the company forced a worker who wished to resign to stay, or pushed workers by negative behaviour to resign under Article 90 [Para 35][Para 36][Para 37].
On Issue 2, the panel held that Article 75 requires prior notice within the legal time limit, Article 89 requires termination compensation in addition to notice compensation absent serious misconduct, and Article 91 requires a party who intentionally terminates to pay damages [Para 38][Para 39]. The employer had not intentionally terminated, still provided work, and had not forced resignation under Article 90 [Para 39]. The panel found the demand above the law, because resignation is the employee's right but does not attract benefits under Articles 75, 89 and 91, which apply only where the employer alone terminates [Para 39][Para 40][Para 41]. No sufficient evidence or legitimate reasons were offered for the resigning workers' claim [Para 43]. The panel also found that the union lacked most representative status under Article 277, citing prior awards, and that a union without that status cannot bring an interests dispute [Para 46][Para 50][Para 53]. Under Article 34(E) of Prakas 99 the panel may order the parties to bargain, and it did so on the second issue [Para 54][Para 57].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
On an unspecified-duration contract an employee may resign at will by written notice under Article 74, but cannot compel the employer to terminate the contract, and termination compensation under Articles 75, 89 and 91 is payable only where the employer terminates, not on voluntary resignation [Para 41][Para 34]. A union that has not obtained most representative status under Article 277 cannot bring an interests dispute to the Arbitration Council [Para 53].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the parties did not really understand each other, and that workers wondered whether the general manager had acted inappropriately because they never saw the foreign manager [Para 55].
