# Award No. 27/05 — Jung Min Cambodia Apparel Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 30 May 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Mr. O Ry, Mr. An Nan, Mr. K Phallack · Doc ID 140776

## 1. Facts
Jung Min Cambodia Apparel Co., Ltd is a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 1,500 workers, with the CIUF as worker party [Para 13] [Para 1]. On 27 April 2005 the Department of Labour Inspector received a workers' request to change 14 working conditions [Para 14]. Conciliation followed through the Dangkao district Labour Inspection Office, with the last hearing on 29 April 2005; 13 of the 14 issues were settled [Para 15]. The one unresolved issue was forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 4 May 2005 with non-conciliation report No. 614, and a hearing was held on 6 May 2005 [Para 16] [Para 8]. The panel found that when lack of work lasted one or two days and affected only 200 workers, the company paid 50% wages, but where more than 200 workers were affected it applied annual leave instead [Para 18] [Para 19]. The 50% practice was not a written agreement, and the company did not notify the Labour Inspector of the labour-contract suspensions [Para 20]. The union confirmed it did not claim for the past [Para 21].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the panel had jurisdiction over the workers' claim for 50% wage payment for future no-work periods [Para 21] [Para 22]?
2. (Issue 2) Whether past labour-contract suspensions were valid where the company did not notify the Labour Inspector [Para 33] [Para 38]?
3. (Issue 3) Whether the employer could deduct annual leave during periods of no work [Para 42] [Para 51]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
The worker party demanded that the company provide 50% of wages when it had no work for a short period or failed to follow Labour Law procedures [Para 5]. At the hearing the union representative framed the claim as 50% payment for future suspension periods, and did not claim back pay or produce evidence of a specific violation [Para 21] [Para 28]. The union also lacked most-representative status at the factory and was not the only union there [Para 21]. The employer argued that when there was no short-term work for regular workers it took their annual leave instead [Para 6], and said it did not notify the Labour Inspector because the request procedure took longer than the no-work period [Para 37].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** no jurisdiction — the panel declined to consider the workers' claim for 50% wage payment for future no-work periods, and no monetary order was made [Para 52].

The panel held it lacked jurisdiction over the future claim because it resolves existing labour disputes, not disputes that have not yet occurred [Para 22]. Following its approach in 10/03-Jacqsintex, workers must present factual evidence of an existing dispute and an actual violation before a rights-based claim can be decided [Para 25] [Para 31]. Here no actual Labour Law violation had occurred and no evidence was presented [Para 30]. Even if treated as interest-based, the panel would refuse the claim because the union lacked most-representative status [Para 32].

On past suspensions, the panel cited Article 71(11) of the Labour Law, under which suspension for serious economic, material or unusual difficulty may not exceed two months and must be under Labour Inspector control [Para 33] [Para 34]. The Labour Law does not allow annual leave to be taken instead [Para 35]. Because the company did not notify the Labour Inspector, the past suspensions were invalid [Para 38]. Invalidly suspended workers would be entitled to wages and benefits as though their contracts had not been suspended, but because the union confirmed it was not claiming the past, the employer was not ordered to reimburse [Para 39] [Para 41].

On annual leave, the panel cited Article 170 of the Labour Law 1997, under which annual leave is given at K. N. Year unless the parties agree otherwise, with notice to the Labour Inspector [Para 42] [Para 44]. Absent agreement, annual leave can be used only for K. N. Year [Para 45]. The employer may retain leave above 15 days and reject first-year leave under Article 167(1), but may not use annual leave instead of a no-work suspension [Para 46] [Para 51]. Both parties have equal rights to negotiate the timing of leave outside K. N. Year [Para 49] [Para 50].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A rights dispute requires an accusation of an actual existing violation supported by evidence; a prospective wage claim is outside the panel's jurisdiction [Para 31] [Para 22]. A union lacking most-representative status cannot bring an interest-based claim before the panel [Para 32]. Suspension under Article 71(11) is valid only where the statutory grounds exist and the Labour Inspector controls the suspension [Para 34]. Annual leave cannot be substituted for a no-work suspension absent an agreement between employer and workers, notified in advance to the Labour Inspector [Para 44] [Para 51].

**Obiter dicta:** Workers subject to invalid suspension would ordinarily be entitled to wages and benefits as though their contracts had not been suspended, but recovery was unavailable here because the union abandoned past claims [Para 39] [Para 41].

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The chair appears as "K ong Phallack" in the panel composition and "Kong Phallack" in the signatures [Para 11] [Para 54].
