# Award No. 24/05 — Wear Well Co., Ltd v. Free Trade Union of Cambodia of Wear Well Factory (Arbitration Council, 9 May 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Tuon Siphann, Ang Eng Thong) · Doc ID 140773

## 1. Facts
Wear Well Co., Ltd is at Trapang Tlang, Sangkat Chomchao, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, and employs approximately 1,580 workers [Para 23]. On 1 April 2005 the Khan Dangkor Labour Inspection Department took a telephone complaint from workers demanding improved conditions on five points [Para 23]. Conciliation on 4 April 2005 settled none of the five, and the non-conciliation report number 304 L.V.T dated 5 April 2005 reached the Secretariat on 6 April 2005 [Para 17][Para 25][Para 26]. Three hearings were held on 8, 19 and 28 April 2005 [Para 20][Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. Three of the five issues were conciliated in a separate agreement, leaving two for the award [Para 30][Para 31]. The company fixes working hours at eight a day with no overtime, pays a basic wage of US$54 a month plus statutory bonuses, and runs an incentive bonus for workers who sew more than 70 per cent of a daily production target, with advice, retraining and warnings for those below 100 per cent [Para 31][Para 32][Para 33][Para 34][Para 35]. Wages are paid under a table set for 2005, and the worker delegates signed an agreement on the schedule on 15 March 2005; a 6 January 2005 meeting record between the director and union leaders had set payment on the 7th with three days' notice if later [Para 37][Para 39][Para 40][Para 41].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the company must not set a production target beyond the workers' capacity, the workers seeking a bonus by piecework and no warnings for missing the target? [Para 9][Para 10][Para 37]
2. (Issue 4) Whether the company must pay wages regularly on the 7th of the following month, as the workers demand, or may follow its 2005 payment schedule? [Para 12][Para 13][Para 39]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (Free Trade Union of Cambodia of Wear Well Factory):** The daily production target exceeds the workers' capacity; the company should not warn workers who miss it and should pay the bonus by completed piecework [Para 37]. Wages should be paid on the 7th of each month, because late payment makes workers who borrow pay more interest and leaves some unable to pay rent and daily food [Para 39].
**Employer (Wear Well Co., Ltd):** The production target is set according to the actual situation and is not beyond capacity [Para 10]. The incentive policy pays a bonus from 70 to 100 per cent of target and its warnings are not intended to lead to dismissal, since skilled workers are hard to find [Para 34][Para 35][Para 36]. Wages are paid between the 5th and 10th of the following month under a schedule agreed with the worker delegates, because payroll is difficult with so many workers and cash cannot safely be kept at the factory [Para 13][Para 38][Para 39].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel declined to consider the demand that the company not set production targets beyond the workers' capacity, and ordered the parties to implement the agreement dated 15 March 2005 [Para 70].
On the production target, the panel began from Article 2 of the Labour Law, which it read as giving the employer the right and authority to manage and lead human resources if done according to law [Para 42][Para 43][Para 44]. It found the Labour Law neither requires nor prohibits incentive bonuses, and Article 13 permits an employer by unilateral decision to grant benefits superior to those in the law, so the incentive policy is lawful and confers better benefits than the statutory minimum [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47]. It classified a complaint against that policy as an interests dispute, and held that for interests disputes the Council has always examined whether the union bringing the case has most representative status [Para 47][Para 48]. Quoting Article 43 of Prakas 99/04, it noted that an award settling an interests dispute takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for one year and would remove other workers' right to strike on interests, which could be unfair to them [Para 48][Para 49]. It found the Free Trade Union of Cambodia at Wear Well lacked recognition as most representative under Article 277 and so could not conclude a collective bargaining agreement for all workers, that right belonging to a union with a majority of members meeting the Article 277 criteria [Para 50][Para 51][Para 53][Para 54]. It therefore declined to consider the demand, citing 04/03-Lida, 06/04-Chousing, 24/03-Top One, 61/04-Best Honor, 62/04-Ecent and 31/03-Hung Wah [Para 55][Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].
On wages, the panel followed 03/05-Flying Dragon: Article 116 requires payment at least once a month but fixes no date, so the date is a management decision under Article 2 [Para 59][Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. It found the 2005 schedule satisfied the monthly requirement and matched the 6 January 2005 agreement, since the 15 March 2005 agreement with the worker delegates provided for later dates with three days' notice and a member of the employee party had signed it as delegate [Para 63][Para 64][Para 67][Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]. It held the parties bound by the 6 January 2005 agreement to pay on the 7th, with three days' notice where payment runs later, and accepted the 15 March 2005 schedule as consistent with it through December 2005 [Para 64][Para 65][Para 66][Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union without most representative status under Article 277 of the Labour Law cannot bring an interests dispute to the Arbitration Council, because an award on interests takes the place of a collective bargaining agreement for a year and would bind all workers and remove their right to strike on interests [Para 48][Para 50][Para 51][Para 55]. The Labour Law fixes no particular payday, only payment at least once a month under Article 116, so the employer may set the date, and a payday schedule agreed with the worker delegates binds the parties [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63][Para 64].

**Obiter dicta:** An incentive bonus is a benefit superior to the statutory minimum and is lawful under Article 13, and a warning for missing a production target need not lead to dismissal [Para 45][Para 46][Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The text is badly OCR-damaged: "the" is fused into following words throughout ("themployer", "thearing", "possed", "signed"), and several passages are printed twice consecutively [Para 43][Para 45][Para 48][Para 55]. The union's acronym is rendered "FTUC" in the reasoning where the party name in the header is the Free Trade Union of Cambodia, and "FTUWKC" appears in the meeting record, so the three cannot be reconciled from the text [Para 1][Para 41][Para 50]. The Prakas on the Arbitration Council is cited as "99704" in the jurisdiction paragraph [Para 16]. The non-conciliation report number "304 L.V.T" is identical to the number used in another award in this collection, so the identifier is unreliable [Para 17]. The payday table is reproduced twice with different starting months, and the "7th" is printed as "7the" and "7\"h" in places [Para 37][Para 63][Para 67]. The employer's address appears both as "Phum Trapoengthleoung" and "Trapang Tlang" [Para 1][Para 23]. The award states it is non-binding by agreement of the parties, yet the closing paragraph recites the eight-day binding rule [Para 22][Para 73].
