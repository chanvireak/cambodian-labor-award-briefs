# Award No. 41/05 — Violet Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 27 July 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Mr H. H. Naryth, Mr L. Sovanna, Chair Mr P. Bunchhea · Doc ID 140783

## 1. Facts
Violet Apparel (Cambodia) Co., Ltd is a Phnom Penh garment factory employing 2,300 workers [Para 37]. The worker party was the K. Y. Federation Trade Union (KYFTU), with K. Y. Union (KYU) representatives at the enterprise [Para 11]. A complaint to the Khan Russey Keo Labour Inspection Office on 14 June 2005 led to conciliation; five of sixteen issues were settled and eleven were forwarded to the Arbitration Council on 7 July 2005 [Para 38] [Para 39]. After three hearings (13, 19 and 25 July 2005) and a factory visit on 27 July 2005, the parties agreed on issues 1, 4, 5 and 9, the worker party withdrew issue 3, and issues 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11 remained for decision [Para 39]. Evidence included the company's registered I. W. Rules, contracts, conciliation minutes and a list of 295 workers claiming seniority and annual bonuses and medical check fees [Para 43].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 6) Whether workers on permitted sick leave should retain the US$5 monthly attendance bonus, subject to deductions of 5,000 riel for one day's absence, 10,000 riel for two days, 15,000 riel for three days and loss of the whole bonus for four days? [Para 25] [Para 46]
2. (Issue 7) Whether the company must reimburse medical check fees of 10,100 riel per worker and send workers not yet checked at the company's expense? [Para 26]
3. (Issue 8) Whether the company must maintain the same piece rate for the same shirt style? [Para 27] [Para 89]
4. (Issue 10) Whether the company must reimburse seniority bonus of US$13.78 per worker after terminating the workers' undetermined-duration contracts in 2004 with US$3 severance? [Para 30] [Para 62] [Para 63]
5. (Issue 11) Whether the company must dismiss the Administration Department assistant for allegedly appropriating wages, detaining workers and interfering with union affairs? [Para 32] [Para 33]

## 3. Positions of the parties
The workers argued that permitted sick leave should not destroy the attendance bonus and proposed the graduated deduction scale, without specifying the reason for absence [Para 46] [Para 47]. On medical checks, they sought reimbursement of 10,100 riel per person and company-funded checks for those not yet examined; the company said it had never paid such fees, thinking this not its burden, but would comply if the Labour Law required it [Para 26] [Para 50] [Para 51]. On lot prices, workers alleged daily price changes and lower prices resumed for the same shirt style, leaving them unsure of prices and below the US$45 minimum wage; the company said prices varied by colour, size, label and purchase order, and market competition, and that it topped up wages below the minimum [Para 53] [Para 54] [Para 55] [Para 58] [Para 60]. On seniority, workers said the US$3 termination payment was accepted because they were misled; the company said each worker accepted voluntarily in a valid contract [Para 63] [Para 64]. The union alleged misconduct by the assistant but produced insufficient evidence; the company denied the allegations and said it could not dismiss him without misconduct [Para 69] [Para 70].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the attendance-bonus demand was rejected; the employer was ordered to reimburse medical check fees to workers listed in Annex 1 within seven days; lot prices were to be set so normal-hour workers earn at least the legal minimum wage, with disciplinary measures restricted; seniority bonus was ordered in addition to the US$3 already paid, within one month; the dismissal claim was rejected [Para 112] [Para 113] [Para 114] [Para 115] [Para 116] [Para 117].

On Issue 6, the panel distinguished award 15/05-Wing Tai of 6 April 2005, where equity allowed proportional payment because the notification was unclear and the employer's rules did not address the bonus [Para 73] [Para 75] [Para 76]. Here, Article 5(3) of the registered I. W. Rules expressly conditioned the US$5 bonus on regular attendance, no sick leave and no lateness even of three minutes, so equity was unavailable; the proposed 5,000 riel deductions had no legal basis, but the workers' demand was rejected [Para 71] [Para 77] [Para 78] [Para 80]. On Issue 7, Article 247(c) of the 1997 Labour Law and Article 7 of Joint Prakas 09/94 obliged the employer to pay medical check fees, and workers' advance payment did not exempt the employer; the panel ordered reimbursement to each worker who had paid their own fees, whose names are listed in Annex 1 [Para 81] [Para 82] [Para 85] [Para 88]. On Issue 8, the employer retained the right to set and modify lot prices under Article 2, but Article 108 and Notification 017/00 required prices enabling workers of mediocre ability, working eight hours daily or 48 hours weekly, to earn at least US$45 per month; warnings without clear evidence of laziness were impermissible [Para 92] [Para 93] [Para 94] [Para 96]. On Issue 10, the 2004 terminations were intended to erase seniority while workers continued working; contracts providing less than the law were null under Article 13, and the three-year wage-claim period under Article 120 had not expired, so the employer must pay the seniority bonus in addition to the US$3 [Para 64] [Para 99] [Para 100] [Para 101]. On Issue 11, only the parties to a labour contract may cancel it, so the panel had no jurisdiction over the assistant's employment contract [Para 104] [Para 106] [Para 107].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A registered internal rule expressly governing an attendance bonus displaces the equity-based approach used where the law, notification and rules are silent [Para 77] [Para 79]. Employers may set piece rates as a management right, but rates must allow normal-hour workers of average ability to earn the guaranteed minimum wage, and discipline cannot be imposed for output shortfalls without proof of laziness or misuse of working time [Para 92] [Para 94] [Para 96]. A contract termination that strips accrued seniority while employment continues, paying less than the legal benefit, is null to that extent under Article 13 of the Labour Law [Para 64] [Para 100].

**Obiter dicta:** workers alleging misconduct causing damage may file a separate damages complaint [Para 110]. The K. Y. Union of Violet Apparel lacks most-representative status, so under Article 6 of Prakas 305/01 this award applies only to workers listed in Annex 1 [Para 110] [Para 111].
