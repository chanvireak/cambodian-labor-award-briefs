# Award No. 15/05 — Wing Tai 2 v Dhama Thparktay Federation Union (DTFU) and Union of DTFU at Wing Tai 2 (Arbitration Council, 6 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Arbitrators: Mr Ouk Ry (employer party), Mr An Nan (worker party), Mr Ang Eng Thong (chair) · Doc ID 140765

## 1. Facts
Wing Tai 2 is located at building 1782, National Road No. 5, Toulsangkao commune, Russeykeo district, Phnom Penh, and employs 1,350 workers [Para 17][Para 18]. Labour inspectors received a complaint on 14 February 2005 and conciliated on 16 and 25 February 2005 without settling the workers' five demands [Para 21]. The case reached the Council on 15 March 2005, and at the hearing of 24 March 2005 the parties settled issues 2, 3, 4 and 5, leaving only issue 1 [Para 23][Para 24]. In the past the company paid wages for sick leave under its internal work rules but withheld the US$5 monthly attendance bonus unless the leave related to a work accident or work-related illness [Para 24]. The internal work rules say nothing about the attendance bonus during sick leave [Para 29].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must pay the US$5 attendance bonus to workers who take sick leave certified by a doctor's certificate, other than where the illness is not work-related [Para 9][Para 25]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), Dhama Thparktay Federation Union (DTFU) and Union of DTFU at Wing Tai 2:** the company should pay the US$5 attendance bonus to workers on sick leave where the leave is clearly certified by a doctor, except where the illness is not a work-related illness or accident [Para 9][Para 25]. The unpaid bonus was one of five demands covering annual leave, medicine for sick workers, transfer of a supervisor and seniority [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11].
**Employer (respondent), Wing Tai 2:** its internal work rules provide wages for sick leave but not the attendance bonus, which it withholds whenever the sick leave does not relate to a work accident or work-related illness [Para 24].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to provide the attendance bonus to workers who take sick leave with proper authorisation, in proportion to the number of workdays on which they come to work [Para 37].
The panel framed the question as whether a worker on authorised sick leave should be treated as having come to work regularly in that month [Para 26]. It followed its earlier awards, 62/04-Ecent and 63/04-Shine Well, which found that Notice 017 of MoSALVY dated 18 July 2000, requiring a bonus of at least US$5.00 per month for workers who come regularly, says nothing about sick leave [Para 25][Para 27][Para 28]. No provision of the Labour Law addresses the point either, and the internal work rules are silent [Para 29].
The panel described the attendance bonus as an incentive for regular attendance in a full month without leave or unjustified rest [Para 30]. It held that the law does not intend to discipline workers who take leave with proper authorisation [Para 31]. Losing the entire US$5 because of authorised sick leave is unfair, since the absence results from illness rather than misconduct, and by authorising the leave the employer recognises that the absence is not misconduct [Para 32][Para 33]. Conversely, requiring the full bonus would be unfair to an employer that loses its benefit while the worker is absent [Para 34]. Applying equity, the panel allowed the employer to deduct the bonus in proportion to the number of authorised leave days in the month [Para 34][Para 35]. Where the factory works 26 days a month, the panel computed the daily proportion at US$0.19 of the US$5 bonus [Para 37].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employer may not forfeit the whole monthly attendance bonus for authorised sick leave, because absence on properly authorised leave is not misconduct, but it may reduce the bonus in proportion to the days of authorised leave, at the daily rate the bonus bears to the month's working days [Para 33][Para 34][Para 35].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that Notice 017 of MoSALVY fixes only a minimum of US$5.00 per month and does not itself resolve the treatment of sick leave [Para 25][Para 28].
