# Award No. 48/05 — Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp (Arbitration Council, 8 September 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Kao Thach, Ann Vireak, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140788

## 1. Facts
Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp operates on National Road 7, Kompong Cham District, Kompong Cham Province, and employs 1,636 workers, 87 of whom were involved in this dispute [Para 14][Para 15]. The Free Trade Union of Worker of Manhattan Factory raised seven demands; conciliation settled three, a hearing on 24 August 2005 settled issues 3 and 4, and two remained for decision [Para 6][Para 8]. The first was the deduction of a US$5 attendance bonus from any worker taking a one-day leave, including certified sick leave [Para 15]. The second was the night-shift rate: the company paid 150 percent of the regular wage under an agreement of 14 February 2003, while the workers claimed 200 percent from that date [Para 19][Para 36].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the employer may deduct the whole US$5 attendance bonus from a worker who takes a one-day sick leave supported by a proper medical certificate? [Para 17]
2. (Issue 2) Whether night-shift work from 10:00 p.m. to 5:00 a.m. must be paid at 200 percent of the regular wage, and whether the shortfall since 14 February 2003 must be reimbursed? [Para 36][Para 52]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the Free Trade Union of Worker of Manhattan Factory argued that a worker on certified sick leave loses the US$5 bonus even though the leave is lawful and not the worker's fault, and that the 200 percent night rate is fixed by law and cannot be bought down by agreement [Para 19][Para 45]. It sought reimbursement of the difference from 14 February 2003 [Para 52].
**Employer (respondent):** Manhattan Textile and Garment Corp relied on Notification 017/00, treating the US$5 as an incentive for regular attendance and deducting it for any leave other than annual leave [Para 17][Para 18]. On night work it relied on the 14 February 2003 agreement with the representative union CFITU, on the 200 percent rate being higher than elsewhere, and on 24-hour operation [Para 42][Para 43].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer may deduct the US$5 attendance bonus in proportion to the days of authorised sick leave, and must pay night-shift workers 200 percent of the regular wage from 14 February 2003, reimbursing the arrears within one month of the award taking effect [Para 55].
On Issue 1 the panel found no article of the Labour Law dealing with attendance bonuses, and no clause in the company's Internal Work Rules either [Para 21][Para 23]. Notification No. 017 dated 18 July 2000 requires a bonus of at least US$5 for a worker who works the full number of days in a month but does not define regular work [Para 22]. National holidays and special leave are lawful leave permitted by the employer, so workers on such leave keep part of the bonus [Para 26][Para 27]. The bonus is an incentive, not a punishment for sickness, so a total loss for certified sick leave would be unfair to the worker while full payment would be unfair to the employer [Para 28][Para 29][Para 30]. The panel followed 26/04-Sportwear, 63/04-Shine Well and 15/05-Wing Tai II, and declined to follow 41/05-Violet Apparel, noting that Internal Work Rules cannot override the law [Para 31][Para 33]. The deduction is therefore proportional, on the formula (US$5 / 26 days) × days of leave [Para 34][Para 36].
On Issue 2 the panel applied Article 14, which defines night as at least eleven consecutive hours including 22:00 to 05:00, and Article 139 on overtime premia [Para 37][Para 39]. Circular 024/99 entitles night-shift workers between 22:00 and 05:00 to double the regular wage, and the company's own Internal Work Rules, Article 4, say the same [Para 40][Para 42]. The 150 percent rate in the 14 February 2003 agreement is below the statutory 200 percent and confers no equal benefit on the workers [Para 44][Para 45]. Following issue 12 of Award 62/04-Ecent, Articles 13 and 119 render null and void any convention that contravenes the Labour Law, and Article 120 protects the wage claim [Para 46][Para 47][Para 48]. The panel held the agreement null and void and ordered the statutory rate [Para 49][Para 52]. On limitation, Article 120(1) sets three years from the date the wage fell due, and the claim from 14 February 2003 was within it [Para 53][Para 54].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A collective agreement that fixes a night-shift rate below the 200 percent required by the Labour Law and Circular 024/99 is null and void under Articles 13 and 119 and cannot be pleaded against workers claiming the statutory rate [Para 47][Para 49]. An attendance bonus is an incentive for regular attendance, not a penalty for lawful leave, so where a worker takes sick leave certified by a doctor and authorised by the employer the bonus is deducted only in proportion to the days of leave [Para 28][Para 34].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that Internal Work Rules cannot be used to interpret an order or law already interpreted by the Arbitration Council [Para 33]. It also noted the employer's arguments that 200 percent exceeds rates abroad and that the factory runs 24 hours nonstop [Para 42][Para 43].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The attendance bonus is printed both as "US$5" and as "USS5", and words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "withemployer") [Para 15][Para 29]. The reasoning at one point states the workers may claim night-shift wages for three years from 2001 to 2004, while the order and the limitation analysis run only from 14 February 2003 [Para 49][Para 55]. Article 144 and Prakas No. 80/99 are cited without their text [Para 49]. The panel composition in the header is printed out of order, with the arbitrator labels detached from the names [Para 1]. The two individuals whom the workers demanded be removed are named on the face of the award; they are not named here [Para 1].
