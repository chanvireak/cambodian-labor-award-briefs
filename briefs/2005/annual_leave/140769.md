# Award No. 21/05 — Sinomax International Cambodia v. CCAWDU and CCAWDUnion of Sinomax International Cambodia (Arbitration Council, 22 April 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kao Thach, Huon Chundy, Kong Phallack) · Doc ID 140769

## 1. Facts
Sinomax International Cambodia sits on the newly built road, Tuol Sangke quarter, Russey Keo district, Phnom Penh, and employs 321 workers [Para 1][Para 15]. It was formerly Kbal Koh II and changed its name on 1 November 2004, replacing both its management and its administration [Para 20][Para 48]. Under the old name all workers could take annual leave in times of urgent matters; after the change the company stopped allowing it, arguing that some workers used the time to hang around without working and that this hurt production [Para 21][Para 49][Para 50]. On 10 March 2005 the district Labour Inspection Office received a telephone complaint demanding improved conditions on five points, conciliation on 18 March 2005 settled three, and the two unresolved issues went to the Arbitration Council on 29 March 2005 under non-conciliation report No. 455 dated 25 March 2005 [Para 10][Para 16][Para 17]. At the hearing the parties conciliated issue 2 by separate agreement, leaving only the annual-leave issue for adjudication [Para 18][Para 19]. The company had not drafted or registered Internal Work Rules [Para 46].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must allow workers to use annual leave in times of urgent matters, as it did before the change of name, or instead apply the Labour Law as the company contends? [Para 6][Para 7]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (CCAWDU and CCAWDUnion of Sinomax International Cambodia):** Workers should be free to use annual leave whenever they wish, in particular in times of urgent matters, as was the practice before the company changed its name [Para 6][Para 20][Para 22]. The union conceded that a few workers abused leave but asked the company to resume the former practice [Para 22]. The workers accepted one day of leave per month but refused the loss of the five-dollar attendance bonus, offering instead a proportional deduction of five dollars divided by 26 working days, about 0.192 dollars [Para 24][Para 25].
**Employer (Sinomax International Cambodia):** Annual leave would be implemented in accordance with the Labour Law [Para 7]. The company never banned exchanging annual leave for special leave, but no other kind of leave would be allowed, and remaining annual leave would have to be used up at one time [Para 22][Para 23]. It said leave time was being used to hang around without working and wanted to know why employees sought leave [Para 21][Para 42]. It offered one day of leave per month against deduction of the monthly attendance bonus, and left the bonus question to the Arbitration Council to decide by law [Para 24][Para 26].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the company to keep carrying out the old agreement, allowing workers to take leave on various occasions provided it is scheduled at appropriate times that do not affect production, until it negotiates a new agreement with the workers or arranges and registers new Internal Work Rules stating precisely the use of annual leave [Para 59].
Reading Article 170 of the Labour Law, the panel held that annual leave is given in principle at Khmer New Year unless the employer and worker agree otherwise, and the employer must notify the Labour Inspector of such an agreement [Para 29][Para 30]. It found the article does not prohibit leave at other times, so leave at other times depends on agreement, with both sides having equal rights to bargain and the employer obliged to give notice [Para 33][Para 37][Para 38]. It held the employer has no absolute right to fix leave time but may impose limits such as advance notice, and may cap leave at 15 days at a time and refuse leave in the first year of service under Article 167(1) [Para 34][Para 35][Para 40][Para 41]. On purpose, it found Articles 166 to 170 do not restrict leave by the reason for it, though the employer may bar workers on leave from the factory compound [Para 43]. On the change of name, the panel applied Article 87(1) of the Labour Law, under which labour contracts in effect at a change in the legal status of the employer remain binding on the new employer, and extended that logic to Internal Work Rules [Para 51][Para 52]. Since the company had no registered new rules and did not argue otherwise, the prior agreement on urgent-matter leave continued to bind it [Para 46][Para 53]. It also held the requirement to use all annual leave at once unreasonable and contrary to Article 170(1) and Article 167(4), which contemplate leave taken in more than one period [Para 55]. On the bonus, Article 168 requires an allowance at least equal to average wage, bonuses and benefits, so taking leave does not relieve the employer of the attendance bonus [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A change in the legal status of the employer does not extinguish existing labour agreements or internal rules: under Article 87(1) of the Labour Law the old terms continue to bind the new employer until new contracts, agreements or registered Internal Work Rules replace them [Para 51][Para 52][Para 53]. Annual leave beyond the Khmer New Year period is a matter for bargaining between the parties, not a unilateral employer prerogative, and leave may be taken in parts rather than all at once [Para 37][Para 55].

**Obiter dicta:** The employer may prohibit workers on leave from entering the factory compound, and may require advance notice of leave to limit disruption to production [Para 40][Para 43]. Special leave under Article 171 and Prakas 76/98 and Prakas 267/01 is confined to events directly affecting the worker's immediate family, such as the worker's own marriage, a wife giving birth, a child's marriage, or the death or illness of a spouse, child or parent [Para 50][Para 51].
