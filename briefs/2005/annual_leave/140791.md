# Award No. 52/05 — Huy Fu (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 20 September 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · Panel: Seng Vuoch Hun, An Nan, Kong Phallack (Chair) · Doc ID 140791

## 1. Facts
Huy Fu (Cambodia) Co., Ltd, at Chomrok village, Sangkat Trapeang Krasaang, Khan Dangkor, Phnom Penh, employs approximately 600 workers, about 300 of them on piece rates and there are about 100 who cannot sew to the required standard [Para 23][Para 26]. Piece-rate sewing has been in effect since 2000, and most piece-rate workers earn US$20 to US$40 a month, calculated by garment lot and style prices [Para 26][Para 28]. The Cambodian Labour Union Federation (CLUF) and the local union at the Huy Fu Company raised nine demands on 27 July 2005; conciliation resolved seven and forwarded two to the panel [Para 17][Para 19]. The two remaining demands were payment of at least the minimum wage to piece-rate workers and retention of the US$5 attendance bonus where the company allows up to seven days of leave [Para 13][Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether piece-rate workers must be paid at least the minimum wage, with arrears for those paid less in July and August 2005? [Para 24]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the US$5 attendance bonus must be retained where the company authorises workers to take leave of up to seven days in a month? [Para 38]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** CLUF and the local union argued that piece-rate pay that yields less than the minimum wage breaches Notification 017/00, that the company failed to provide enough work which itself violates Article 83 of the Labour Law, and that in this case the workers did not ask for leave but were asked by the company to take it [Para 35][Para 40][Para 63]. The union promised a list of under-paid workers by 12 September 2005 [Para 37].
**Employer (respondent):** Huy Fu (Cambodia) Co., Ltd said it paid no top-up to reach US$45 for regular and US$40 for probationary workers because the workers were lazy, that the bonus is an incentive under Notification 017/00, and that it deducted the bonus only for leave beyond two days a month as previously agreed [Para 29][Para 63][Para 64]. It never produced its payroll despite promising to do so [Para 38].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay at least the minimum wage, US$45 for regular workers and US$40 for probationary workers, to all piece-rate workers paid below it, including arrears for July and August 2005, and to pay the attendance bonus in proportion to the days actually worked by workers authorised to take up to seven days of leave in those months [Para 74].
On Issue 1 the panel built the rule from Article 104, which fixes a guaranteed minimum wage, Article 105, which voids any agreement to pay less, and Article 108, which requires piece rates to let a worker of average ability working normally earn at least the minimum wage in the same time [Para 44][Para 51]. Notification 017/00 sets US$45 a month for regular garment workers and US$40 for probationary workers, and expressly requires the employer to top up piece workers whose output yields less [Para 47][Para 50]. Article 137 caps hours at eight a day and 48 a week, and the panel read the piece rate against that normal working time [Para 44][Para 52]. There is no provision permitting payment below the minimum wage, whether by piece rate or salary [Para 53]. About half the workers earned US$45 or more while the rest earned US$20 to US$40 [Para 54][Para 55]. The company's laziness explanation failed twice: it produced no evidence of laziness, and Article 108 makes no reference to it, so laziness must be met with disciplinary action under the Internal Work Rules, not with underpayment [Para 30][Para 57][Para 58]. The argument that workers agreed to piece rates at hiring also failed, since Article 105 voids any such agreement below the minimum [Para 60].
On Issue 2 the panel found no article of the Labour Law providing for an attendance bonus, and relied on Article 3 of Notification 017/00 together with its own earlier cases, 63/04-Shine Well, 62/04-Ecent, 15/05-Wing Tai 2 and 48/05-Manhattan [Para 65][Para 66][Para 71]. The bonus motivates regular attendance and does not punish authorised absence, but requiring full payment for absence would unfairly burden the employer [Para 69][Para 70]. The panel held that paid annual leave does not cut the bonus, while unpaid authorised leave reduces it in proportion, and applied the formula (US$5 / 26 days) × 2 = US$0.385 [Para 73]. Neither party produced the Internal Work Rules, the payroll or the leave records, so the panel ruled on the principles and ordered proportional payment for July and August 2005 [Para 33][Para 43][Para 74].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A piece rate that lets a worker of average ability working normal hours earn less than the guaranteed minimum wage is unlawful, and the employer must top the wage up to US$45 for regular and US$40 for probationary garment workers, the worker's agreement to the piece rate being void under Article 105 [Para 52][Para 60]. Poor work is answered by discipline under the Internal Work Rules, never by paying below the minimum wage [Para 57][Para 58]. An attendance bonus is an incentive for regular attendance and is reduced only in proportion to days of authorised unpaid leave, so it is not forfeited by leave the employer itself authorised [Para 70][Para 73].

**Obiter dicta:** the panel noted that the employer's failure to supply enough work may violate Article 83, without deciding the point [Para 40]. It also observed that about 100 piece-rate workers cannot sew to standard, most of them probationary [Para 26].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The non-conciliation report is dated "31 September 2005", a date that does not exist, while the same passage says the issues were sent on 31 August 2005 and the reasons repeat 31 August [Para 17][Para 19]. The award writes the minimum wage both as "US$45" and as "$45" [Para 62]. Article 3 of Notification 017/00 is quoted only in truncated form, breaking off mid-sentence [Para 66]. Personal names throughout the party and representative lists are split across separate numbered lines, and the representative list contains a stray "4. ]" fragment [Para 7]. Words are merged throughout ("themployer", "Traing", "indusry", "receivocuments"), and the dissenting-opinion and signature blocks are corrupted into stray characters [Para 75].
