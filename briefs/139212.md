# Award No. 134/15 — Sun Gre Sport (Cambodia) (Arbitration Council, 9 July 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Ing Suthy (employer-appointed, dissenting), An Non (worker-appointed), Run Saray (president) · Doc ID 139212

## 1. Facts

Sun Gre Sport (Cambodia) Co., Ltd. (សាន់ ហ្គ្រេ ស្ព័ត (ខេមបូឌា)) makes gloves in Kien Svay district, Kandal, with about 200 workers; it had begun operating only in March 2015. [Para 43][Para 44][Para 55] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព ស្មារតី កម្មករ ខ្មែរ) organised a local union on 16 March 2015, which had not yet been registered at the Ministry of Labour when the award issued, and took transfer of rights from 109 workers. [Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] Of thirteen original demands, six were settled in conciliation and one more at the hearing, leaving six for decision — items 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 7. [Para 22][Para 27][Para 28]

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Item 1) Whether the company must pay a midday meal allowance of 4,000 riel per day, handed out once a week? [Para 50]
2. (Item 2) Whether the company must raise the overtime meal allowance by 1,000 riel to 2,000 riel per hour? [Para 58]
3. (Item 3) Whether the company must raise the regular attendance bonus to US$15 a month and may deduct it pro rata for days of leave taken for personal business? [Para 64]
4. (Item 4) Whether the company must raise the transport and accommodation allowance to US$10 a month and must not pro-rate it against days of leave? [Para 71]
5. (Item 5) Whether the company must allow a pregnant worker one day a month for a health check while keeping her wages and benefits? [Para 80]
6. (Item 7) Whether the company may deduct wages when the electricity supply fails? [Para 87]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** Market prices and fares have risen, other factories pay more, and the attendance bonus is earned over 26 working days, so deducting the whole US$10 for a single day of leave is wrong; the protest over the electricity cuts was met with pay deductions the workers never agreed to. [Para 52][Para 67][Para 92][Para 224]

**Employer (respondent):** The factory opened only in March 2015 and cannot afford the increases; it already pays the statutory US$10 attendance bonus and US$7 transport allowance, and it deducted wages during power cuts after the workers thumbprinted their agreement to go home. [Para 55][Para 123][Para 187][Para 223]

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** partly granted — the meat of the wage demands was refused for want of jurisdiction, but the employer was ordered to pro-rate the regular attendance bonus against days of authorised leave (not deduct it whole), to keep the transport and accommodation allowance at full rate when a worker takes leave, and to pay 100 per cent of wages and benefits whenever it suspends work during a power cut without following the Labour Law. [Para 243][Para 245][Para 247]

The panel began with classification. Applying article 96 of the Labour Law and article 9 of Prakas No. 305 SKBY of 22 November 2001, it held that a claim found nowhere in the law, a collective agreement, the internal regulations or past practice is a dispute of interest, and that only the most representative union in an enterprise may bring such a dispute to arbitration; anything above the statutory floor is interest, anything the law already guarantees is rights. [Para 96][Para 97][Para 101][Para 103] Because the claimant union was not the most representative — it was not even registered — the panel declined to consider items 1, 2, 3(a), 4(a) and 5 at all, while item 7 was retained as a rights dispute. [Para 108][Para 109][Para 119][Para 218]

On item 3, the attendance bonus, the panel found that Notice No. 230 K.B/S.CH.N of 25 July 2012 makes the US$10 monthly bonus mandatory for workers who attend every working day without absence, and that leave authorised by the employer suspends the contract under articles 71 and 72, so no wage is owed for those days. [Para 140][Para 167][Para 170] The bonus is nonetheless a form of wage under article 103, and it is earned over 26 working days; deducting all of it for one authorised day of leave was therefore disproportionate, and the employer may deduct only the fraction corresponding to the days taken. [Para 164][Para 166][Para 172][Para 173]

On item 4, the panel read Notice No. 230 as fixing the transport and accommodation allowance at US$7 a month with no condition of full monthly attendance, so the employer had no basis for pro-rating it; the allowance must be paid in full. [Para 196][Para 209][Para 213][Para 215]

On item 7, it held that a power cut that stops work is a suspension of the contract under article 71(11), which requires the Labour Inspector's prior authorisation and a limit of two months; the company suspended work twice for half a day each time without notice or authorisation, so it must pay wages and benefits in full for those periods. [Para 225][Para 228][Para 234][Para 236] Arbitrator Ing Suthy dissented on item 3, arguing that a "bonus paid as a reward" under article 103 is earned whole once its conditions are met and cannot be apportioned unless the announcement says so. [Para 259][Para 263][Para 264]

## 5. Ratio decidendi

A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise has no standing to bring a dispute of interest — a demand for benefits above what the law, a collective agreement, the internal rules or past practice already provide — and the panel will decline to consider it, leaving only rights disputes arising under the Labour Law for decision. [Para 103][Para 106][Para 109] A statutory monthly attendance bonus, being a form of wage earned across the month's working days, may be reduced only in proportion to days of authorised absence, never forfeited in full. [Para 166][Para 172]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel's discussion of whether "absence" in Notice No. 230 covers authorised as well as unauthorised absence, which it said no competent body had yet clarified and which it declined to resolve, did not drive the outcome. [Para 143][Para 144][Para 148]

## 6. Source defects disclosed

The award is dated 9 July 2015 in the header and at the foot, and paragraph 31 records the parties' agreement to move the date from 30 June 2015 to 9 July 2015. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 265] The number of unsettled items is unstable: thirteen were conciliated with six settled, seven were sent on, one more settled at the hearing, and six were decided, while paragraph 22 leaves the unsettled figure as unreadable OCR. [Para 22][Para 23][Para 27][Para 28] The claimant group is given as 109 workers in one passage and 99 in another, the transfer letter containing 13 duplicated names. [Para 47][Para 49] The company's name appears as សាន់ ហ្គ្រេ ស្ព័ត, សាន់ ហ្វ្រេស្ព័ត and សាន់ ហ្ព្រេ ស្ព័ត, with no English form printed. [Para 18][Para 23][Para 1] The figure in the operative paragraph on meal allowance is garbled ("២៥ ៤០០០ រៀល"). [Para 239] Sentences are split across page breaks at paragraphs 236–238, and several prior award numbers in the panel's lists are OCR-mangled. [Para 237][Para 136]
