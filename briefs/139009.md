# Award No. 067/16 — Cambo Yon Xing Garment Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2016)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mr Yu Soun Ty, appointed by the employer; Mr Liv Sovanna, appointed by the workers; Mr Ang Eng Tong, chair) · Doc ID 139009

## 1. Facts
Cambo Yon Xing Garment Co., Ltd, which had changed its name from Bes Vorn in June 2014, cut and sewed garments under registration Co.1301 E/2014 of 24 April 2014 and employed about 393 workers before it closed on 07 February 2016. [Para 53] [Para 54] [Para 78] The union at the factory had been registered since 12 July 2012, and on 05 February 2016 131 workers signed a letter transferring their right to be represented to the president of the Cambodian Union Federation. [Para 55] [Para 58] [Para 59] After the closure the workers claimed their last month's wages, outstanding annual leave, notice compensation, dismissal compensation and moral damages (សោហ៊ុយជម្ងឺចិត្ត). [Para 60] The panel ordered the union to file a specific list of claimants, which came to 78 named workers. [Para 110] [Para 111]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the 75 claimant workers their notice compensation, dismissal compensation, moral damages, outstanding annual leave and final month's wages following the closure of the factory? [Para 153] [Para 152]
(The panel's issue 2 — whether the workers must be taken back if the company reopened — was withdrawn by the workers at the hearing, so the panel considered issue 1 only. [Para 4] [Para 17] [Para 18])

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** that after two years' service their fixed-duration contracts had become indefinite-term contracts under Article 67(2) and Article 73(5), so closure entitled them to the indefinite-term severance package; that the company gave notice on 06 February 2016 for a closure on 07 February 2016, with no written notice and no time allowed to look for new work; and that the payroll slips show entry dates from 2011, not 2014 as the employer's table asserted. [Para 64] [Para 66] [Para 74] [Para 95] [Para 112]
**Employer (respondent):** that it could not meet the claims because the company had closed for want of orders — its roof was old and could not be repaired, so it failed the buyers' safety inspections — and that it had already paid the final month's wages, 5% end-of-contract bonus and outstanding annual leave, leaving only a residual group unpaid. [Para 71] [Para 78] [Para 91]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer must pay each of 75 workers notice compensation under Article 75 calculated on actual seniority less the 5 days' notice already given, dismissal compensation under Article 89 on actual seniority, and moral damages under Article 91 on actual seniority; no order was made on final wages and annual leave, which were no longer disputed. [Para 166] [Para 178] [Para 186] [Para 193] [Para 194] [Para 195]
On contract type the panel applied Article 87: a change in the employer's legal status — including a change of company name — leaves existing employment contracts standing between the new employer and the old workforce, and the new employer must recognise seniority from the worker's first day at Bes Vorn. [Para 114] [Para 121] [Para 122] [Para 123] Reading Article 67(2) with Article 73(5), it held that successive fixed-duration contracts whose total exceeds two years convert into an indefinite-term contract, and that the two-year limit is measured on the cumulative total, not on each renewal. [Para 131] [Para 135] [Para 140] [Para 145] [Para 146] [Para 147]
On the evidence the panel rejected the employer's table of 78 workers, which showed every entry date in 2014: the contract and payroll slip of one worker showed she was hired before 2014, and the workers' own table matched the payroll slips and the record. [Para 125] [Para 126] [Para 127] [Para 130] Three workers — the panel named them — had cumulative service still under two years and stayed on fixed-duration contracts; the remaining 75 had more than two years and therefore indefinite-term contracts. [Para 148] [Para 149] [Para 152]
On notice, the panel found the closure was announced on 02 February 2016 for 07 February 2016, giving only five days, well short of the minimum in Article 75 (one month for two to five years' service, rising with seniority), so Article 77 compensation was owed, less the five days actually given. [Para 155] [Para 156] [Para 157] [Para 158] [Para 162] [Para 165] [Para 166]
On moral damages, the panel held that the aged roof was the employer's own burden and responsibility, that the employer produced no evidence it could not repair it, and that a closure caused by the employer's own failure to meet the buyers' safety conditions is not a valid ground for terminating contracts or for escaping moral damages. [Para 181] [Para 182] [Para 183] [Para 184] [Para 185]
Finally, the panel held the 5% end-of-contract bonus under Article 73(6) is payable only where a fixed-duration contract ends, so no such bonus is owed to indefinite-term workers, and the employer may deduct the 5% it had already paid to the 75. [Para 189] [Para 190] [Para 191] [Para 192]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a factory is sold or renamed, the incoming employer inherits the workforce's full seniority, and successive fixed-duration contracts totalling more than two years become an indefinite-term contract; on closure such workers are owed notice compensation under Article 75, dismissal compensation under Article 89 and moral damages under Article 91, while the 5% end-of-contract bonus under Article 73(6) belongs only to genuinely fixed-duration contracts and may be set off against sums already paid. An employer cannot rely on its own failure to maintain the premises as a lawful reason for closure. [Para 122] [Para 145] [Para 184] [Para 191]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that Cambodian labour law shows a deliberate preference for indefinite-term contracts because job security benefits workers and long service rewards employer loyalty, and that ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, though non-binding, is useful context for reading Article 67. [Para 138] [Para 141] [Para 143]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
OCR damage is extensive: the evidence schedules are fragmentary, and passages of Latin text ("Cambo Yon Xing", "The Vicky", "Shij") are embedded in the Khmer. [Para 76] [Para 127] [Para 188] The company appears as Cambo Yon Xing Garment, Cambodia Yon Xing, Bes Vorn and "Cambo Yon Sing". [Para 53] [Para 55] [Para 113] The closure is dated 02 February 2016 in the announcement, 04 February 2016 in the declaration deciding closure, 06 February 2016 in the workers' account of when they were told, and 07 February 2016 as the closing date; the panel adopts 02 February 2016 and five days' notice without reconciling these. [Para 26] [Para 66] [Para 74] [Para 162] [Para 165] Two evidently unrelated documents — a Cambodia Airport Management Services conciliation report of 09 March 2016 — are listed among the Ministry's papers in this garment case. [Para 49] [Para 50] The 78-worker table is described in one place as 78 workers who had received final wages and annual leave only, and elsewhere as those who had received nothing. [Para 88] [Para 110] Individual workers are reduced to initials per convention, except the three fixed-duration workers the panel names to exclude them from the order. [Para 148] [Para 152]
