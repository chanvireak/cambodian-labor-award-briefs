# Award No. 215/12 — Lian Xin (Cambodia) Shoe Workshop (Arbitration Council, 22 November 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · panel of 3 (Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Tang Tri presiding) · Doc ID 140209

## 1. Facts
Lian Xin is a shoe-making workshop at Bek Chan, Ang Snuol, Kandal, employing about 25 workers and taking subcontracted work from other factories. Its union held leadership elections on 23 September 2012 and gave the employer written notice on 24 September; registration was acknowledged on 4 October. Seventeen workers transferred their rights to the union federation to bring the claim. Of eight demands, five (Issues 4–8) were agreed at the hearing, the water-cooler demand (Issue 3) was withdrawn, and Issues 1 and 2 were merged into one, leaving the panel to decide the dismissal of three union leaders on 28 September 2012. [Para 1][Para 20][Para 21][Para 37][Para 40]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issues 1 and 2, merged) Whether the employer must take the three dismissed union leaders back into normal work and pay them wages from dismissal to return? [Para 21][Para 42]
2. (Issues 1 and 2, first limb) Whether the dismissals amounted to discrimination against the union? [Para 91]
3. (Issues 1 and 2, second limb) Whether the dismissals complied with the procedure required by the Labour Law? [Para 91]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the three had been dismissed because they stood for union office, having had no problems while members of the previous union; they had refused to sign new three-month fixed-duration contracts only because such contracts make dismissal easy at each expiry, and refusal of an unlawful contract variation is no misconduct. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 66]
**Employer (respondent):** the dismissals had nothing to do with union activity; the three had been told at hiring that probation ran three months; they refused to thumbprint the new contracts when called in August and again on 26 September; and they had long ignored management instructions, dropping leave forms in front of supervisors when refused. [Para 59][Para 60][Para 64][Para 98]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to take the three workers back into work and to pay them wages from the date of dismissal until the date of their return; no sum was fixed in the award. [Para 119][Para 120]
Contract status: the panel first classified the contracts, since the whole claim turned on it. Under Art 68 of the 1997 Labour Law probation may not exceed three months for ordinary workers, two months for skilled workers and one month for unskilled; the three worked in the cutting section, which requires identifiable skill, so they were skilled workers and their probation was capped at two months. Because they served more than three months on probation and the employer never put a fixed-duration contract in writing, Art 67(7) applied and their contracts became indefinite. [Para 69][Para 72][Para 75][Para 79]
Variation: an indefinite contract can only be changed by agreement, and Art 336 of the Civil Code requires offer and acceptance; the workers' refusal to convert to three-month terms was therefore lawful and the employer's imposed variation had no legal basis. [Para 85][Para 89][Para 90]
Discrimination: the panel found none, reasoning that an employer intending to victimise union members would not have invited them to sign replacement contracts in August and September; the operative reasons were the refusal to sign and the disregard of instructions. [Para 97][Para 99]
Procedure: Art 293 requires the Labour Inspector's approval before a staff delegate or candidate is dismissed, and Art 3 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to union election candidates for 45 days before and after the poll, provided the union notified the employer. All three conditions were met — candidacy, dismissal on 28 September, five days after the election, and notice received on 24 September — so the three were protected. The employer never sought the Inspector's authorisation, and the dismissal was unlawful, following Award 91/11 and earlier practice. [Para 103][Para 108][Para 111][Para 115][Para 117]
Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on the remedy, accepting that the dismissal was substantively grounded in Art 74 but that, Art 293 protection existing and the Inspector never asked, the employer should pay wages until the Inspector rules rather than reinstate. [Para 124][Para 125]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Probation served beyond the statutory maximum for the worker's skill category cannot be probation, and where no fixed-duration contract is put in writing the relationship is an indefinite contract; an employer that dismisses a union election candidate within 45 days of the poll without the Labour Inspector's prior approval acts unlawfully, whatever the substantive grounds, and must reinstate with back pay. [Para 75][Para 79][Para 115]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that an employer's willingness to offer replacement contracts is itself evidence against an inference of anti-union motive. [Para 97]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The three dismissed workers are named in full and with employee numbers throughout; they are identified here by role and initial only. The numbers conflict — one worker is given as No. 029 in the demand list and No. 039 in the reasons [Para 43], and one name is spelled two ways across the document. Paragraph 20 garbles the count of unresolved issues ("both 4 08 points"), and the factory is called Lian Xin (Cambodia) Co., Ltd in the document list but Lian Xin workshop in the reasons. No amount is printed for the back pay.
