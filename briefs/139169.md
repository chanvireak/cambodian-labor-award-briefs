# Award No. 074/15 — Syu An Fang Gamin Factory (Arbitration Council, 28 April 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, An Vira for the worker party, Run Saray chair) · Doc ID 139169

## 1. Facts
Syu An Fang Gamin Factory, a garment manufacturer in Samraong district, Takeo province, employing about 580 workers, was the subject of a collective dispute brought by the Cambodian Labour Confederation federation on behalf of three named workers. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 39] A factory union was elected on 12 February 2015 and 104 workers applied for membership, but the union had not yet been registered at the Ministry of Labour. [Para 36][Para 37] Conciliation at the Takeo labour department on 11 March 2015 addressed 13 demands, of which six were settled and seven were not; a seventh, the demand for a nursery and breastfeeding room, was settled at the hearing of 6 April 2015, leaving six issues for the panel. [Para 15][Para 20][Para 21]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay a milk allowance of USD 10 per month to women workers with children up to 18 months old? [Para 42]
2. Whether the employer must pay a lunch allowance of 4,000 riel per worker per day? [Para 45]
3. Whether the employer must add USD 5 per month to the USD 7 transport and accommodation allowance already paid, making USD 12 in total? [Para 49][Para 97]
4. Whether the employer must pay a welfare allowance of USD 10 per worker per month? [Para 53]
5. Whether the employer must deduct the regular attendance bonus pro rata to the days of absence lawfully authorised by the employer, rather than forfeiting it in full? [Para 58][Para 113]
6. Whether the employer must supply two sets of work clothing per worker per year free of charge? [Para 66][Para 155]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** other Phnom Penh factories — Beary, Glad Pie Garment and others — already pay the USD 10 milk allowance even where a nursery exists, and pay lunch money of 1,000 or 2,000 riel, transport of USD 10–12 and welfare of USD 8–10; workers are poor and prices are rising, and a lawful absence authorised by the employer is not an absence at all. [Para 43][Para 46][Para 51][Para 56][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** the company is only three to four months old and not yet profitable, so it cannot fund these benefits; it already pays USD 7 transport and accommodation in compliance with Ministry Notification No. 230, has agreed to build the nursery and breastfeeding room by early June 2015, and deducts the whole attendance bonus because the practice has always been to do so. [Para 44][Para 48][Para 52][Para 64][Para 68]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the claims in issues 1 to 4 were refused consideration; the employer was ordered to deduct the regular attendance bonus of USD 10 per month only in proportion to the days of lawfully authorised absence, and to supply two sets of work clothing per worker per year at no charge. [Para 164][Para 165][Para 166][Para 167][Para 168][Para 169]

On issue 1 the panel found no provision of the Labour Law, no collective agreement, no internal rule and no past practice creating a duty to pay the USD 10 milk allowance, and held that the claim was consequently "better than the law" — an interest dispute rather than a rights dispute. [Para 73][Para 74][Para 75][Para 76] Issues 2, 3 and 4 were disposed of in the same way: on issue 2 no text or practice obliged the employer to pay 4,000 riel per day; on issue 3 the employer already met Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 by paying USD 7, so the demand for a further USD 5 was again an interest claim; and on issue 4 no instrument obliged a welfare allowance. [Para 91][Para 92][Para 97][Para 99][Para 100][Para 107][Para 108] Each of these was then refused on the threshold point: under Article 96 of the Labour Law and Article 9 of Prakas No. 305, only the most representative union may bring an interest dispute, and the panel followed its earlier awards holding that a union without that status cannot do so — here the claimants were three workers, the union was unregistered, and its 104 membership applications fell far short of a majority of some 580 workers. [Para 77][Para 80][Para 86][Para 87][Para 88][Para 89][Para 90]

On issue 5 the panel held that the regular attendance bonus is a payment made as a reward under Article 103 and therefore a form of wage. [Para 144][Para 145][Para 146] An absence authorised by the employer suspends the employment contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1), so no wage is earned for those days; but the panel found that the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days absent, since forfeiting the whole USD 10 would unjustly enrich the employer while a flat 2.5-dollar-per-day deduction would encourage absenteeism and disrupt production. [Para 147][Para 148][Para 149][Para 153][Para 65] Arbitrator Ing Sothy dissented on this point, reasoning that a bonus is by definition conditional and is not wages, so it cannot be apportioned. [Para 173][Para 185][Para 187]

On issue 6 the panel applied Article 6 of Prakas No. 307 of 14 December 2007, which requires the employer to supply at least two sets of work clothing free of charge each year, noted that the employer had never done so, and followed four earlier awards to the same effect. [Para 155][Para 157][Para 158][Para 161][Para 162]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A demand that exceeds what the Labour Law, a collective agreement, internal rules or established practice secures is an interest dispute, and the panel will decline to consider it unless the union bringing it is the most representative in the enterprise — here read as a membership above half the workforce; a union that is unregistered and instructs a handful of workers has no standing to litigate such a demand. [Para 75][Para 86][Para 89] Where an absence is lawfully authorised by the employer, the contract is suspended and the attendance bonus, being a component of wages under Article 103, may be reduced only in proportion to the days absent, not forfeited in full. [Para 146][Para 153][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the Labour Advisory Committee has never clarified whether "absent" in Notification No. 230 means absence with or without permission, that it could not itself supply that meaning, and that each attendance-bonus claim must therefore be assessed on its own facts; it also noted that the employer's commitment to build a nursery and breastfeeding room by early June 2015 disposed of the settled issue 3. [Para 123][Para 127][Para 70]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The header carries a stray "2017-" before the case number, but the award date is printed as 28 April 2015 there, in the signature block and at the foot of the text; 2015 is used throughout. [Para 1][Para 188]
- The panel constitution date reads "24 March 2021 2015", two years printed together. [Para 17]
- The dispositive order on issue 1 prints the milk allowance as "410 dollars" where the claim and reasoning use USD 10; the order on issue 4 prints the welfare allowance as "USD 100" where the claim is USD 10. Both are internally contradicted within the same sentences. [Para 164][Para 167]
- The Labour Advisory Committee statement quoted at [Para 119] says "USD 10 per year month", a year/month contradiction also noted at [Para 120].
- The employer's name is OCR'd inconsistently across the document ("Syu An Fang Gamin", "Ssu An Fang Gamin", "Sbu An Fang Gamin"), and the case name is rendered as "Syu An Fang Gamin Factory" here as the most frequent form. [Para 34][Para 36][Para 11]
- Arbitrator names appear only in Khmer; the Latin spellings above are transliterations, since the award prints no romanised form. [Para 171]
- Cited prior awards have their party names reduced to OCR fragments (e.g. "81/04", "202/14", "206/12 Khmer ... Gamin"), so their identities cannot be verified from this text. [Para 87][Para 159]
