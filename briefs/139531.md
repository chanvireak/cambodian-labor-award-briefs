# Award No. 019/20 — Ken Sport Shoe (Arbitration Council, 21 April 2020)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer party, Liv Sovanna for the worker party, Pen Bun Chhean chair) · Doc ID 139531

## 1. Facts
Ken Sport Shoe, a shoe factory in Kampong Chhnang province employing about 11,470 workers, faced a collective dispute over 11 working conditions raised by four factory unions and their federations, following conciliation that failed entirely on 13 March 2020. [Para 21][Para 22][Para 53] At hearing the third demand was withdrawn and seven were settled by agreement, leaving three: the overtime meal allowance, annual leave, and card-swipe machines at the main gate. [Para 29][Para 30][Para 31] Two of the unions withdrew from the meal-allowance claim, so it was decided only for the Cambodian Workers' Rights Union and the Cambodia Development Union. [Para 98][Para 99]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must pay the overtime meal allowance weekly, every Saturday, rather than fortnightly as it changed to on 25 January 2020? [Para 2][Para 56]
2. Whether the employer must let workers use their annual leave as the law requires, and pay compensation for leave not taken in past years? [Para 68]
6. Whether the employer must add one new card-swipe machine and repair one old machine at the main entrance to prevent congestion at shift change? [Para 84]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** workers live hand to mouth, so weekly payment of the meal allowance lets them meet daily needs; they have no time to withdraw the money from an ATM, and small residual amounts cannot be withdrawn at all; and the payslip does not itemise the overtime meal allowance, so it cannot be read. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62] Team leaders rarely grant annual leave, so days accumulate and the employer then cuts them back to six days for the following year; and workers take 20 to 30 minutes to leave the factory because there are too few swipe machines. [Para 74][Para 76][Para 91]
**Employer (respondent):** paying in cash weekly would waste time and it is hard to obtain 2,000 riel notes for more than ten thousand workers; payment by Wing account is safe, fast and available at any hour, and small residues stay in the account. [Para 63][Para 65] It also says adding a machine at the main gate would draw all workers there and worsen congestion and safety. [Para 93]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay the overtime meal allowance weekly every Saturday and to facilitate annual leave in accordance with its internal rules, and dismissed the claim for additional swipe machines. [Para 160][Para 161][Para 162]

On the meal allowance the panel applied Point 2 of Notice No. 041/11 of 7 March 2011, which entitles a worker who voluntarily works overtime at the employer's request to 2,000 riel a day or a free meal, without limiting that by the number of hours worked. [Para 100][Para 101] The Notice fixes no payment cycle, but the panel reasoned that the meal sustains further overtime, and that payment must therefore come at an interval that lets the worker buy food when working overtime. [Para 103][Para 105] It weighed the employer's administrative difficulty against the workers' need and, having found the need real, held that a weekly cycle was required. [Para 113][Para 114][Para 115]

On annual leave the panel applied Articles 166 and 170 of the Labour Law and Article 1.10.3 of the factory's internal rules, which require 18 days a year, at least 12 of which must be used, with the balance carried over for no more than three years. [Para 116][Para 127] It held that annual leave is not an absolute right and requires the employer's prior authorisation, save during Khmer New Year, and that the employer may refuse on production grounds. [Para 122][Para 123] Because team leaders were restricting leave so that it had to be taken in December, the panel found the employer's practice inconsistent with its own rules and ordered it to facilitate leave. [Para 137][Para 138] But since the workers had not exhausted the internal procedure by appealing a team leader's refusal to administration, the claim as pleaded was dismissed. [Para 140][Para 141][Para 143]

On the swipe machines the panel treated the number and placement of machines as a management right under Article 2 of the Labour Law, exercised lawfully and reasonably. [Para 144][Para 145] It compared the employer's swipe records for the last 15 workers leaving on 30 March 2020 with the workers' 25-minute video, and found that all workers, including a pregnant worker, had left by about 4:10 pm, so the 20 to 30 minute wait was not made out. [Para 153][Para 155][Para 156][Para 157] The burden lay on the party asserting the claim, and it was not discharged. [Para 149][Para 151]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a statutory benefit payable in kind or in cash is meant to be consumed during the working day, the employer must pay it at an interval that allows the worker to buy what it is meant to buy: Notice No. 041/11 fixes no cycle for the overtime meal allowance, but a fortnightly or monthly cycle that leaves the worker without money at the overtime meal defeats the purpose of the benefit. [Para 103][Para 105][Para 115] Annual leave is not an absolute right: the worker must apply and obtain the employer's prior authorisation, and a claim that leave has been denied fails where the worker has not exhausted the internal procedure of appealing to administration. [Para 122][Para 140][Para 141]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that payment by Wing account keeps small residual amounts safe and that the employer showed willingness to review buildings short of machines are observations on the employer's arrangements, not reasons for the orders made. [Para 65][Para 94]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- Every year in the award is printed only as "20", the last two digits having been lost throughout, so the header reads "21 April 20" [Para 1] and the hearings "24 March 20" and "27 March 20" [Para 19][Para 20]; the year 2020 is inferred from the Prakas and the Wing reference.
- The panel is said to be appointed under Prakas No. 181 dated 23 May 2019 for term 17 [Para 15], which is consistent with a 2020 sitting but is the only dated appointment in the file.
- The ratio of machines to workers is given as one machine per 500 workers [Para 87], as one per 50 workers [Para 146] and, in the same passage, as buildings of about 1,108 workers; the total workforce is 11,470 [Para 53].
- The time needed to leave the factory is stated as 20 to 30 minutes by the workers [Para 91], as about 10 minutes by the employer [Para 96], and as about 5 minutes earlier in the same passage [Para 96].
- The award number appears only as 019/20 in the header [Para 1]; the closing order carries no number [Para 160], and the signature block names the panel as "Ing Sothy, Liv Sovanna, Pen Bun Chhean" in a different order from the face of the award [Para 164].
