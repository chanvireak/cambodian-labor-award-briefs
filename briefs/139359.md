# Award No. 107/04 — Jacqsintex Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 6 January 2005)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (KOL Vathana, TUON Siphann, chair KOY Neam) · Doc ID 139359

## 1. Facts
Jacqsintex Co., Ltd, a Phnom Penh garment enterprise employing 820 workers, faced a collective complaint its union filed with the Dangkor labour inspectors on 3 November 2004 over seven working conditions; three were conciliated and four remained for the panel [Para 16][Para 17][Para 18]. The unresolved demands were piece-rate pay for the cutting section's group 13, a maternity payment in lieu of a day-care centre, reimbursement of a 10,100 riel medical check fee, and notice and leave compensation for four dismissed workers [Para 10]. The hearing was held on 24 December 2004 before KOL Vathana, TUON Siphann and chair KOY Neam [Para 12][Para 15].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether group 13 of the cutting section is entitled to wages calculated as a percentage of production like other sections, or alternatively to a US$3 monthly allowance for overtime beyond ten nights? [Para 10][Para 19]
2. Whether the employer must pay US$10 to women who have just given birth instead of establishing a day-care centre? [Para 10][Para 20]
3. Whether the employer must reimburse each worker the 10,100 riel medical check fee? [Para 10][Para 21]
4. Whether four probationary workers dismissed in 2004 are entitled to seven days' notice compensation, 5 percent severance and unused annual leave? [Para 23]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** percentage pay would exceed the fixed US$45 a month, and the overtime bonus paid to every other group since 1998 was withheld only from group 13; the employer must follow the Labour Law and reimburse the full 10,100 riel; the four workers were owed notice, severance and leave under Articles 75 and 166 [Para 28][Para 22][Para 23].
**Employer (respondent):** it may lawfully choose monthly payment, has paid the US$45 the law requires, and can afford only 40 percent of the medical fee; the four workers were probationers or apprentices, so Article 82 relieved it of notice [Para 30][Para 31][Para 22][Para 23].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel allowed the workers to withdraw the percentage-wage and overtime-bonus demand, ordered a nursing room and a day-care centre for children aged 18 months to five years within 30 days, ordered reimbursement of the medical check fee (printed as 100 riel, reasoned at 10,100 riel) within 30 days, and ordered severance, damages and annual leave for the four workers [Para 65][Para 66].

On issue 1, no law requires equal wages across groups or pay by percentage, piece rates were impractical because the section worked as a group and the work required no special skill, so the demand was an interests issue; the union's letter of 4 January 2005 asking that it be left to collective bargaining led the panel to issue no award on it [Para 32][Para 35][Para 36][Para 37][Para 38][Para 39].

On issue 2, Articles 184 and 186 impose three duties — an hour's nursing break, a nursing room, and a creche for children over 18 months or payment for day care in lieu; the employer acknowledged some 20 workers had children each month and undertook to comply, so the panel ordered the room and centre within a time it fixed at 30 days [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 65].

On issue 3, Article 247(c) was held a sufficient basis by itself to oblige the employer to bear pre-employment medical checks even without a new Prakas, following award 60/04 (United Art) and Prakas 09/94, while the panel noted its earlier divergent reading of that Prakas; the workers' own payment did not discharge the duty [Para 44][Para 45][Para 47][Para 48][Para 52].

On issue 4, the four contracts were fixed-duration under Article 67, and because their two-month terms overlapped the probationary period, probation formed part of the whole contract; Article 73(5) requires notice only for contracts longer than six months, so no notice was owed, but Article 73(3) entitled each worker to damages for premature termination by the employer's will, Article 82 being confined to undetermined-duration contracts and no serious misconduct being proved [Para 52][Para 54][Para 55][Para 59][Para 60]. Termination was fixed at 24 September 2004, giving Mr H 32 days, Mr C 11 days, and Mr T and Mr S six days of wages [Para 60][Para 61][Para 62]. Article 73(6) added 5 percent severance on wages paid and Articles 166 and 167 unused annual leave [Para 63][Para 64].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A written two-month fixed-duration contract whose term coincides with a probationary period remains a fixed-duration contract; probation does not convert it into an undetermined-duration contract, so Article 82 cannot be invoked, and an employer that ends it early without proved serious misconduct owes damages under Article 73(3) equal to wages for the unexpired term, plus 5 percent severance and accrued annual leave [Para 52][Para 59][Para 60][Para 63].

**Obiter dicta:** that no law obliges an employer to pay all workers the same wage or to adopt piece rates, and that group production made individual output hard to measure — observations on a demand the panel declined to decide [Para 32][Para 35][Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The decision paragraph prints the medical reimbursement as "100 riel" while the findings and reasons repeatedly state 10,100 riel [Para 21][Para 52][Para 66]. The first dismissed worker appears as "Ho Saroi" in the contract list and "Ho Saray" in the findings and decision, and the spelling "Thau Chanry" / "Thor Channy" likewise varies [Para 26][Para 60][Para 66]. The workers pleaded termination on 20 October 2004, the company and time cards 24 September 2004, and the panel adopted the latter [Para 27][Para 28]. Conciliation is dated 30 November 2004 in the jurisdiction paragraph but 3 November 2004 in the case summary [Para 10][Para 18]. Line 19 records that the parties agreed to a non-binding award, while the closing paragraphs apply the ordinary eight-day binding rule [Para 19][Para 68]. OCR loss is heavy throughout ("themployer", "annualeave", "bargaing").
