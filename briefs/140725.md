# Award No. 77/06 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី ស៊ី អេស ហ្គាម៉ិន លី មីធិត (PCS Garment) (Arbitration Council, 19 September 2006)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · arbitrators កៅ ថា ច (employer side), លីវ សុវណ្ណា (worker side), គង់ ផល្ហៈ (chair) · Doc ID 140725

## 1. Facts
On 7 July 2006 the Khmer Youth Trade Union Federation (សហព័ន្ធ សហជីព យុវជន ខ្មែរ) complained to the Labour Dispute Department on behalf of five workers of ក្រុមហ៊ុន ភី ស៊ី អេស ហ្គាម៉ិន លី មីធិត (PCS Garment), a Phnom Penh garment factory employing about 5,000 workers, demanding better working conditions on 14 points [Para 19][Para 28]. Conciliation failed on 31 August 2006 and the file reached the Council on 1 September 2006 [Para 17][Para 20]. The five came from the warehouse (one), sewing (three) and cutting (one), and no other worker authorised the federation to act for them [Para 30][Para 31][Para 33]. The demands included repayment of 30,000 riel per worker for health checks and workbooks, a nursery, full wages during work shortages, annual leave, a $10 monthly incentive, 50% wages plus seniority during maternity leave, transport, one hour a day for breastfeeding, and conversion of probationers to full staff [Para 36][Para 2][Para 3][Para 4][Para 9][Para 10]. The panel heard both parties on 8 and 9 September 2006 and declined to consider any of the 14 demands for want of jurisdiction [Para 19][Para 22][Para 70].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Jurisdiction) Whether the 14 demands of five workers out of about 5,000 amount to a collective labour dispute within Article 302 of the Labour Law, so that the Council has jurisdiction? [Para 55][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The federation pressed the 14 demands as working-condition claims of the five workers after conciliation failed [Para 19][Para 25]. The workers sought reimbursement of the 30,000 riel health-check and workbook deductions, a nursery or its cash equivalent, and full wages during work shortages [Para 36][Para 3][Para 4].
**Employer (respondent):** The company did not attend the Labour Department conciliation [Para 20]; before the panel it argued that the five workers did not represent the 5,000 workers in the factory and that it therefore had no basis to negotiate with them [Para 23]. It also maintained that the factory's four unions were not parties to the dispute [Para 29].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to consider all 14 demands, holding that it had no jurisdiction over the dispute [Para 71].
The panel framed the question as its own jurisdiction [Para 55]. It noted that Article 313 of the Labour Law and Prakas No. 099 of 21 April 2004 govern its authority, and that it ordinarily follows the Labour Inspector's and the Minister's classification of a dispute unless there is a clear reason to depart [Para 1][Para 15][Para 57]. Reading Article 302, it identified three conditions for a collective dispute: several workers against one or more employers; a subject concerning working conditions or the rights of a recognised professional organisation; and a capacity to make the enterprise run badly or disturb social peace [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60]. Conditions one and two were met, since five workers had filed and the demands concerned working conditions [Para 61][Para 62]. Condition three failed: the five worked in different sections, some demands touched only one or two of them, they were a tiny fraction of about 5,000 employees, no other worker supported them, and they produced no evidence that the dispute could obstruct the factory or disturb social peace [Para 63][Para 64][Para 65][Para 66]. The panel followed its earlier awards in cases 10/03, 07/05, 41/04 and 02/04 on deference, and 20/05 and 57/06 on the third condition [Para 57][Para 66]. Because Chapter 12 gives it power only over collective disputes, it held it had none here and left all 14 demands undecided [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A dispute is collective only when the workers' action can make the enterprise run badly or disturb social peace; a handful of employees drawn from different sections, unsupported by the wider workforce and offering no evidence of such impact, fails that test even where the demands concern working conditions, and the Council must decline to consider their claims [Para 63][Para 66][Para 69].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that it normally defers to the Labour Inspector's and the Minister's view of whether a dispute is individual or collective, absent a clear contrary reason [Para 56][Para 57]; and that the factory's four unions were not parties to the case [Para 29].
