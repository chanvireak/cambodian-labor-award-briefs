# Award No. 159/12 — Star Night Thing & Garmen Factory LTD (Arbitration Council, 10 September 2012)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambona for the employer, Tuon Siphan for the workers, Pen Bunhea presiding) · Doc ID 140166

## 1. Facts

Star Night Thing & Garmen Factory LTD, a garment factory operating since 2004 with 296 employees, was the respondent in a collective labour dispute filed by the CCAWDU union seeking improved working conditions [Para 38][Para 39]. Conciliation at the provincial labour department failed on 3 of 12 demands on 30 July 2012, and the unresolved points reached the panel at a hearing on 24 August 2012 [Para 14][Para 12]. Because only 42 workers had thumbprinted an authorisation for the union to represent them, the panel decided the dispute for those 42 workers alone [Para 42][Para 43]. Of the three unresolved issues, Issue 1 was settled by the parties at the hearing, leaving Issues 2 and 3 for adjudication [Para 19][Para 20].

## 2. Issues in dispute

1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the 50% wage and allowances for the 90 days of maternity leave before the leave begins, rather than one week to ten days after the worker returns to work? [Para 44][Para 63]
2. (Issue 3) Whether workers whose successive fixed-term contracts have a cumulative duration exceeding two years must be converted into indefinite-term contracts? [Para 77][Para 88]

## 3. Positions of the parties

**Workers (claimant):** The employer already grants 90 days' maternity leave at 50% of wage and allowances, but pays it only after the worker returns; the workers demanded payment of the whole 90 days before the leave starts [Para 44][Para 46]. On contracts, they argued that several workers had been renewed in six-month terms for more than two years — one from 19 March 2010 and another from 13 July 2009 — and that the law converts such contracts to indefinite term [Para 58][Para 59][Para 60].

**Employer (respondent):** The employer said it could not advance maternity pay because it works on subcontracts from other companies and has repeatedly suspended operations, and asked to keep its existing practice [Para 48][Para 49]. On contracts, it maintained that it pays 5% termination compensation at the end of each six-month fixed-term contract and renews without any break, so no conversion was owed [Para 7][Para 62][Para 92].

## 4. Award and reasoning

**Outcome:** granted — the employer was ordered to pay 50% of wages and allowances for 90 days of maternity leave before the leave begins, and to conclude indefinite-term contracts with workers whose total contract duration exceeds two years, those workers repaying (or the employer offsetting) the 5% severance already paid [Para 102][Para 103][Para 101].

On Issue 2 the panel read Article 103 with Article 115 of the Labour Law: wages may not be paid on a day the worker is entitled to rest, and where the payment date falls on such a day it must be made one day earlier [Para 65][Para 66]. Since maternity pay is expressly treated as wages, it cannot be handed over on the first day of the leave [Para 67][Para 68]. The panel followed its own earlier awards (No. 57/06, No. 97/06, No. 08/08) holding that maternity pay is due before the leave commences [Para 70][Para 71], and found the employer's practice of paying one week to ten days after the return to work contrary to the Labour Law [Para 74][Para 75].

On Issue 3 the panel applied Article 67(2): a fixed-term contract may not run beyond two years, and where renewals produce a total duration over two years the contract becomes one without a fixed term [Para 77][Para 78][Para 79]. It approved the reasoning in Award No. 10/03 that the two-year cap refers to aggregate duration, not to each renewal taken alone [Para 80][Para 84][Para 88]. Although six-month renewals without any interruption of work kept every contract formally fixed-term [Para 89][Para 93], the panel held that contracts exceeding two years had already become indefinite-term by operation of law [Para 94][Para 99]. Because the employer had paid 5% severance at each maturity, the panel ordered that those sums be returned or set off when the employment relationship ends, so the workers do not receive the compensation twice [Para 100][Para 101].

## 5. Ratio decidendi

Where an employer renews successive fixed-term contracts without interruption and their cumulative duration exceeds two years, the contracts convert by operation of Article 67(2) of the Labour Law into indefinite-term contracts regardless of the form of each renewal; and maternity pay, being a component of wages under Articles 103 and 115, must be paid in full before the maternity leave begins, not after the worker returns. [Para 88][Para 68][Para 76]

**Obiter dicta:** ILO Recommendation No. 166 of 1982, paragraph 25(3), which discourages fixed-term contracts for long-term work, is not binding but is a useful context for interpreting Article 67 [Para 87]. The panel also observed that Cambodian labour law leans toward indefinite-term contracts because job security benefits both worker and employer [Para 81][Para 82].

Several workers and union officers are named in full in the body, including in the example list of long-serving employees; those have been reduced to initials here [Para 58][Para 59]. Paragraph 70 prints "៤០" (40) where the surrounding text concerns 90 days, an evident OCR or typographical slip [Para 70].
