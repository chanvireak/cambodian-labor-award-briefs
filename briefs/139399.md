# Award No. 245/16 — Kennetex International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 9 January 2017)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy, appointed by the employer; On Non, appointed by the workers; Run Saray, chair) · Doc ID 139399

## 1. Facts
Kennetex International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd cuts and sews garments at Damnak Thom, Stung Mean Chey, Mean Chey, Phnom Penh, and told the panel it employed about 700 workers, down from 1,370 at the start of 2016. [Para 1][Para 31][Para 47] For years the company had issued fixed duration contracts of eight months, but it had moved to three-month contracts, which it said reflected the loss of buyers; eight workers hold undetermined duration contracts. [Para 38][Para 39][Para 40][Para 46][Para 48] When there was no work the company paid 50% of wages to all workers. [Para 52] The Khmer Workers' Spirit Union held a registration certificate but its leadership term had expired, and the panel found the delegation letters transferring members' rights carried no names or thumbprints. [Para 32][Para 35][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Four demands were conciliated, three settled, one remained. [Para 10][Para 11]
1. Whether the employer must conclude fixed duration contracts of eight months with its workers, as it did in previous years, rather than the three-month contracts it now uses? [Para 2][Para 38][Para 85]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** three-month contracts are too short, they let the employer drop workers easily at each expiry, and the constant renewal deadline leaves workers anxious that their contract will not be renewed; the eight-month form, used for years, gave them security and let them work with peace of mind, and the first three months are not enough for a slow worker to improve. [Para 41][Para 42][Para 43][Para 44][Para 45]
**Employer (respondent):** it keeps the three-month form because it currently has few buyers, orders fell from 1,370 workers' worth of volume to 700, and an eight-month contract raises the 5% contract compensation and other benefits it must pay, which it cannot fund on time; when orders return it will again offer long contracts, and a worker who does not accept the three-month form is free to leave. [Para 46][Para 48][Para 49][Para 50][Para 53]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel refused to order the employer to use eight-month fixed duration contracts. [Para 112][Para 113]

The panel first resolved who the claimant was. Because the union's leadership mandate had expired, the union could not represent the workers, and the transfer letters it relied on bore no worker names or thumbprints and could not be considered. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 77][Para 78] Article 32 of the Labour Union Law requires staff delegates to be elected in any enterprise with at least eight workers, and Article 40 scales their number to headcount; the two elected delegates, Ms K and Mr T, were therefore the proper complainants for the workforce. [Para 79][Para 82][Para 83][Para 84]

On the merits, the panel cited Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311, 336 and 337 of the Civil Code: a contract arises from offer and acceptance, and a contract, its modification or its termination requires the consent of both parties. [Para 86][Para 92][Para 94][Para 96][Para 97] Following its reasoning in Award No. 001/16 (Strong Hold International) and Award No. 115/10 (G-Fourmous), it held that neither the workers nor the employer can force a contract type or duration on the other, and that the panel itself, as a third party, cannot impose terms the parties have not agreed. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 102][Para 103] A demand for an eight-month contract is therefore a right to make an offer, not a right to obtain the contract, even though no statute confers that right; equally, the employer's refusal is lawful because accepting or rejecting the offer is its own commercial decision. [Para 109][Para 110] The employer accordingly had no obligation to conclude eight-month contracts. [Para 111]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The type and duration of a fixed duration contract are matters of party autonomy under Article 65 of the Labour Law and Articles 311 and 336 of the Civil Code: workers may propose an eight-month contract but cannot compel it, the employer may refuse without breaching any obligation, and the arbitration panel has no power to impose a contract term on unwilling parties. [Para 99][Para 100][Para 109][Para 110]

**Obiter dicta:** where a union's leadership mandate has expired, the union cannot represent workers before the panel, and representation falls to the elected staff delegates under Article 32 of the Labour Union Law. [Para 69][Para 71][Para 84] A delegation letter without worker names and thumbprints is inadmissible as a transfer of rights. [Para 77][Para 78] The award is non-binding and becomes final if no objection is filed within eight days. [Para 17][Para 114]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company's registration is printed twice with incompatible particulars: a commercial register certificate numbered Inv. 667 E/1999 dated 21 December 1999 at [Para 30], against a certificate numbered 1675 dated 13 February 2015 and statutes deposited in 2002 at [Para 22][Para 23]. The Latin form of the employer's name appears as "Kennetex International (Cambodia) Co., Ltd" in a party document [Para 19], while the Khmer face renders it ខិន នីតិច អ៊ិនធើណេសិន ណល, sometimes reduced to ខិន នីតិច; the Latin form is used here because the award itself prints it. The hearing date is stated twice in consecutive paragraphs [Para 8][Para 9]. The party list at [Para 1]-[Para 2] is truncated, leaving the employer with a single named representative and the worker delegation running to seven numbered names of whom only two are identified as delegates. Paragraphs 55, 78 and 90 contain Latin OCR gibberish where statutory text and figures were lost. No monetary amounts are printed.
