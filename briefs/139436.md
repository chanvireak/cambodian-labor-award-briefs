# Award No. 251/15 — Wai Ful Garment (Cambodia) Ltd (Arbitration Council, 26 October 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Pen Bun Chhea presiding; Uk Ri, employer nominee; Tuon Si Phan, worker nominee) · Doc ID 139436

## 1. Facts
Wai Ful Garment (Cambodia) Ltd cuts and sews garments at Damnak Ampil, Ang Snuol, Kandal; the employer put its workforce at "more than 10" while the court injunction in the file refers to 1,040 workers clocking in voluntarily. [Para 58][Para 59][Para 91] Workers struck from 13 July 2015; the Kandal Provincial Court's injunction No. 197 DKS/15 of 16 July 2015 ordered them back to work within 48 hours, they returned on 18 July, struck again on 20 July, and on 22 July the court recorded service of the order at the factory gate. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 71][Para 72] When 135 workers had not returned within the 48 hours, the employer dismissed all of them on 24 July 2015, including the eleven union leaders and activists whose case this is. [Para 73][Para 74] Conciliation on 2 September 2015 failed on two points, which reached the Secretariat on 11 September 2015. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer must take back the eleven dismissed union leaders and activists and pay them wages and other benefits from the date of dismissal until reinstatement? [Para 2][Para 65]
2. Whether the employer must withdraw the complaints it lodged in court against the federation's officials and the factory's union leaders and activists? [Para 3][Para 205]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the eleven came to work on 23 July 2015 but the company's security guards refused them entry, and the employer let every other dismissed worker back in on 29 July under agreements with other federations while excluding these eleven — so the dismissal is union discrimination. [Para 77][Para 78][Para 85]
**Employer (respondent):** the eleven committed serious misconduct by disregarding the injunction and by inciting and obstructing others, causing losses of USD 100,000; it had asked the labour inspector for permission to dismiss the three leaders but never produced the request or any authorisation; and it regarded the other 135 as victims of the eleven's incitement. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 81][Para 82][Para 83][Para 86]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the panel ordered reinstatement with wages from the date of dismissal for four workers only (three union leaders and one member), refused to consider the demands of the seven workers who were not claimants before it, and dismissed issue 2 for want of jurisdiction. [Para 213][Para 214][Para 215]
On standing, Article 268 ties a union's rights to registration of its statutes and leadership list; the local union was registered on 29 August 2013 but its first-term leadership had expired and the second term was not yet recognised, and only four of the eleven were present at the hearing, so the panel treated those four alone as claimants. [Para 104][Para 112][Para 116][Para 117]
On the three leaders, Articles 293 and 295 protect staff delegates from dismissal without the labour inspector's prior consent, and Article 4(2) of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 extends that protection to three union leaders; no request or authorisation was ever filed, so the panel found the employer had not discharged its burden and the dismissals unlawful. [Para 121][Para 125][Para 135][Para 136][Para 138] It rejected the argument that the protection lapses with the leadership term, following Award 007/14 Alim (Cambodia). [Para 126]
On the fourth worker, his three-month fixed-duration contracts ran from 19 February 2012 to 24 July 2015, past the two-year ceiling, so they had become a contract of unlimited duration under Articles 67(2) and 73(4). [Para 152][Para 153][Para 154] The employer's case was serious misconduct in disobeying the injunction, but the record of service of 22 July listed him among those attending, and the panel accepted that he presented himself on 23 July and was turned away by security; photographs and video could not establish his identity, and no appraisal or warning was produced, so the misconduct was unproven and the dismissal lacked a valid reason under Article 74. [Para 175][Para 179][Para 180][Para 192][Para 194][Para 196][Para 197] Reinstatement with back wages was the remedy under Article 34 of Prakas 099. [Para 198][Para 202]
On issue 2, Article 302 defines a collective labour dispute by reference to working conditions, exercise of union rights and recognition of a union in the enterprise; a demand that the employer withdraw criminal complaints is not such a dispute, so the panel declined jurisdiction. [Para 206][Para 209][Para 210][Para 211][Para 212]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Terminating a union leader requires the labour inspector's prior consent, and where the employer produces neither a request nor an authorisation the dismissal is unlawful and reinstatement with wages from the dismissal date follows, whether or not the leader's term of office has since expired. [Para 125][Para 136][Para 138] Where the alleged serious misconduct is disobeying a return-to-work injunction, the employer must prove the worker did not in fact present himself for work; proof that he attended the court's service of the order and was barred by security defeats that ground. [Para 179][Para 196] A chain of fixed-duration contracts running past two years converts to unlimited duration regardless of the label used. [Para 153][Para 154]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel observed that the security guards may have mistaken the court's posted record of service — which carried the leaders' names — for a list of workers banned from the factory, an observation it did not need in order to decide the case. [Para 178] It also noted the employer's claim of USD 100,000 in losses without ruling on it. [Para 81]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing date is printed 23 September 2014 in the procedural summary but 23 September 2015 in the invitation list, and the panel is said to have been constituted on "15 October September 2015". [Para 8][Para 12][Para 13] Worker counts conflict throughout: eleven leaders and activists, four claimants present, seven refused for want of standing, 135 dismissed, and one passage referring to "all 112" claimants. [Para 73][Para 116][Para 118][Para 166] The employer's own size is unhelpfully printed as "more than 10 workers" against a 1,040-worker injunction. [Para 59][Para 91] The fourth worker's start date is 19 February 2012 in the finding but "19 December 2012" in the tabulated service list. [Para 67][Para 153] The company appears as "Wai Ful Garment" on the face of the award and as "Vai Hglel Garment" inside the quoted injunction. [Para 1][Para 89] Individual worker and union names are not reproduced here; one leader's name is printed both "Sem Kantha" and "Sam Kantha".
