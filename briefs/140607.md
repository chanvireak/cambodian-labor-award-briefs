# Award No. 44/07 — ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ីន ណឺ ណៃ ធីង ហ្វាក់ តូរី អិលធីឌី v its workers, assisted by សហជីព ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ (Arbitration Council, 4 June 2007)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Kong Phalla, president; Ung Sothy; An Norn) · Doc ID 140607

## 1. Facts
The employer, ក្រុមហ៊ុន វ៉ីន ណឺ ណៃ ធីង ហ្វាក់ តូរី អិលធីឌី, runs a garment factory at Village 4, Svay Rolum, S'ang district, Kandal, employing 4,165 workers, of whom about 300 work in the ironing section [Para 20][Para 21]. The claimant party is the company's own workforce, which had no union of its own and was assisted at the hearing by សហជីព ស៊ី ខៅ ឌូ [Para 2][Para 24]. Of the 300 ironers, 113 signed the complaint by thumbprint [Para 21][Para 22][Para 19]. Conciliation settled four of seven demands; three came to the Council: a piece-rate increase, a change to the attendance bonus regime, and a seniority increment for long service [Para 13][Para 14].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 1) Whether the company must raise ironing piece rates to US$0.55 per lot for sleeveless shirts, US$0.65 for ordinary long-sleeve shirts and US$0.75 for long-sleeve shirts with an open pocket? [Para 26]
2. (Issue 2) Whether the US$5 monthly attendance bonus must be reduced by only 5,000 riel for a single day of lawful special leave instead of being forfeited in full? [Para 42]
3. (Issue 3) Whether workers with six years' service or more are entitled to a further US$1 a year added to their seniority pay? [Para 49]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The company pays one flat lot price without distinguishing shirt types, the pay does not cover living costs as goods and rent have risen, and no agreement fixes these rates [Para 26][Para 27][Para 28]. They asked that the US$5 bonus be treated as four parts of 5,000 riel so that one lawful day of urgent leave costs only one part, and that seniority rise by US$1 a year from six years' service [Para 42][Para 49].
**Employer (respondent):** It conceded 2 cents per shirt type and offered to reopen discussion if any category still falls short of the minimum wage [Para 3][Para 39]. It refused the bonus change because the sum exists to reward attendance, so one day's absence in a month forfeits the US$5 [Para 44]; on seniority it asked only that the Labour Law be applied [Para 52].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the piece-rate and seniority demands were refused, and the company was ordered to deduct 5,000 riel from the attendance bonus when a worker takes one day of lawful special leave [Para 130][Para 131][Para 132].
On Issue 1 the panel applied Article 108, under which piece work must be priced so that a worker of average skill working normal hours earns at least the guaranteed minimum wage [Para 55], and Article 137 capping normal hours at eight a day and forty-eight a week [Para 56]. Notification 745 set the minimum at US$50 a month [Para 57][Para 58]. The panel found that about 90 per cent of ironers reach that figure on the company's lot prices and that the company tops up anyone who falls below [Para 63][Para 64], so the increase claimed exceeded what the law required [Para 69]. It added that a benefit dispute may only be brought by a union holding most-representative status, which these workers did not yet have [Para 71][Para 76].
On Issue 2 the panel relied on point 3 of Notification 745, which preserves the benefits conferred by points 3 to 6 of Notification 017 [Para 82], and on point 3 of Notification 017, which gives a worker who works the regular number of days at least US$5 a month [Para 83]. Neither instrument nor the company's internal rules define regular work or say whether paid lawful leave forfeits the bonus [Para 84][Para 85][Para 86]. Reading the rule with earlier awards [Para 88][Para 90], the panel held that the scheme was not intended to penalise lawful leave [Para 95] and that the bonus should be reduced in proportion to days taken [Para 102]. Applying its own formula, one day off leaves about US$4.80 [Para 105]; the workers asked for a deduction of only 5,000 riel, about US$1.25, leaving about US$3.75, which is less [Para 106][Para 107], and the panel granted the smaller figure they had claimed [Para 109][Para 131].
On Issue 3 the panel found point 5 of Notification 017 fixes the seniority amounts at US$2, US$3, US$4 and US$5 a month for one, two, three and four or more years [Para 116][Para 117][Para 118][Para 119][Para 121], and that the company had already paid at those rates [Para 123], so the further US$1 a year had no legal basis and was a benefit claim [Para 125][Para 126]; the absence of a most-representative union also barred it [Para 128][Para 129].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where a notification fixes a monthly attendance bonus but neither defines regular work nor states whether paid lawful leave forfeits it, the bonus is not lost in full for one day of lawful special leave: the employer may deduct only in proportion to the days actually taken [Para 84][Para 95][Para 102]. A panel will not award more than the workers themselves claim, so where their claimed deduction is smaller than the proportionate formula yields, the smaller claimed figure is the order [Para 106][Para 109]. Piece rates satisfy the law when a worker of average skill reaches the minimum wage on them and the employer tops up any shortfall; a demand for a higher rate is then a benefit claim the panel will not entertain without a most-representative union [Para 63][Para 69][Para 71].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel recorded that the workers had begun preparing a union registration application, a fact it did not rely on [Para 24]; it also observed that with four days off the proportionate bonus would fall to zero [Para 108].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The company name appears in three spellings across the header, the parties block and the findings, and the file itself is titled with yet another [Para 1][Para 20]. The employer-side arbitrator is named one way on the face and differently on the signature page. OCR losses: a stray "2015" is embedded in the restatement of the piece-rate claim [Para 67]; the demand paragraph prints the bonus share once as 50,000 riel although the order and every other reference say 5,000 riel [Para 81][Para 131]; a fragment "4p" interrupts the start of the reasons [Para 55]; and the piece-rate figures are printed with a comma in one place where the rest uses a period. Worker numbers are also inconsistent: 300 in the ironing section but 113 claimants [Para 21][Para 22].
