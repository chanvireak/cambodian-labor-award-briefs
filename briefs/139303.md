# Award No. 19/04 — Khbal Koh II factory (Arbitration Council, 5 May 2004)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Samborana (employer-chosen), Tuon Siphann (worker-chosen), Kong Phallack (chair) · Doc ID 139303

## 1. Facts
Khbal Koh II, on New Road in Russey Keo District, had no internal work rules, was not registered at the Ministry of Commerce and held no operating licence in 2004 [Para 21]. Mr S worked there from 23 January 2003 on a contract without fixed dates and acted as the workers' representative, settling grievances with the employer under an agreement of 19 September 2003 [Para 22][Para 37][Para 56]. He asked in writing for leave to attend his brother's engagement, was refused, took 11–12 March 2004 off anyway, and was dismissed on 16 March 2004 [Para 29][Para 32][Para 22]. The union's registration letter was filed on 8 February 2004 and the certificate issued on 19 March 2004 [Para 68].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the factory must reinstate Mr S, the union president at Khbal Koh II, whom it dismissed for faults? [Para 4]
2. Whether the factory must pay back the medical examination fees charged to workers? [Para 5]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** the dismissal was retaliation for his work as workers' representative and union president, was procedurally defective, and the severance offered was refused because the grounds were not legitimate [Para 24][Para 26][Para 54]. On medical fees, the factory forced workers to be examined on site at 15,000 riel when the Labour Medical Department charged 10,100 riel [Para 35].
**Employer (respondent):** Mr S was at fault — he took two days' unauthorised leave and was slow at work, taking 45 seconds to iron a suit against 108 seconds for others — and the factory did not know he was union president when it dismissed him, having received no official letter until afterwards [Para 13][Para 43][Para 61]. It said it could not decide the medical fees and was waiting to consult its owner in China [Para 6].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the factory must reinstate Mr S and pay him full salary from the date of dismissal, immediately after the award takes effect; reimburse 10,000 riel in examination fees to every worker who paid their own; and pay the examination fees of workers not yet examined [Para 86][Para 87][Para 88].

On the first issue the panel held the contract to be of undetermined duration because it fixed no commencement or termination date, so Article 74 required prior notice and a valid reason going to aptitude or behaviour, failing which Article 91 gives a right to compensation [Para 37][Para 38]. Articles 12 and 279 forbid terminating or recruiting on grounds of union membership or activity [Para 39]. The employer itself conceded in the hearing that Mr S worked well, which removed aptitude and behaviour as reasons [Para 41]. On the leave, Articles 169 and 171 entitle each worker to at least seven days' special leave a year for family events such as a sibling's engagement, so permission should have been granted, and the procedure for seeking it must be set out in internal work rules the factory did not have [Para 43][Para 45][Para 46]. Disciplining him for those two days was disproportionate under Article 27 and unregulated by any internal rules, so the dismissal on that ground was unlawful [Para 48][Para 52].

The panel went further: the real motive was union activity. Under Article 282 (discrimination against unions) and Article 293 (dismissal of union leaders and shop stewards) the employer must obtain the Labour Inspector's permission in advance, and Article 4 of Prakas 305 of 22 November 2001 extends staff-delegate protection to union founders and volunteers from the time they apply to register, lasting 30 days after the certificate's date [Para 69][Para 70]. The panel found the factory knew Mr S was union president although it had not yet received the certificate, since he had told it on the day of the announcement and union activity was underway; the dismissal therefore breached Article 293 procedure and was discriminatory [Para 66][Para 73]. Following Cheer View Textiles (17/04), Cambodiana Hotel (02/04), Standard Garment (27/03) and Ho Hing Garment (17/03 and 18/03), it ordered reinstatement with full salary from dismissal [Para 75].

On the second issue Article 247-a and Article 247-C(4) require Prakas on pre-employment examinations, and Article 7 of Joint Prakas No. 9 of 9 January 1994 places the cost on the owner of the enterprise [Para 76][Para 79]. Article 395 abrogates contrary provisions and Article 13 declares the Labour Law to be of public order, so a contract term or in-house condition shifting the fee to the worker is void and the employer cannot escape the cost [Para 79][Para 81][Para 82]. Following Chou Sing Garment (02/03) and Loyal Cambodia (21/03), the panel ordered reimbursement and future payment by the factory [Para 83][Para 84].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Union founders and volunteers acquire staff-delegate protection from the moment they apply to register the union, not from the certificate's date, so a dismissal effected between the application and the certificate without the Labour Inspector's prior approval breaches Article 293 and Prakas 305 Article 4, and reinstatement with salary from dismissal follows [Para 68][Para 69][Para 70]. Ignorance of the worker's union role is no defence where he announced it when the dismissal was declared [Para 66]. A dismissal for taking special family leave to which Articles 169 and 171 entitle the worker is disproportionate under Article 27, all the more where no internal rules govern the discipline at all [Para 45][Para 48].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that the factory held no Ministry of Commerce registration or operating licence, and that no shop stewards had yet been elected in this, the factory's first union [Para 21][Para 71]. It observed that the employer's preference for on-site examinations was driven by lost working hours, a consideration that cannot override the Joint Prakas [Para 36].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
Three different figures are printed for the examination fee: 10,100 riel at the Labour Medical Department, 15,000 riel charged at the factory, and 10,000 riel ordered to be reimbursed [Para 35][Para 83][Para 87]. The case summary states that the employer "did notify" the Department of Labor Inspection of the dismissal, which contradicts the panel's finding that Article 293 procedure was not followed [Para 27][Para 73]. The chair's name appears as "Kong Phallak" in the composition and "Kong Phallack" at signature [Para 8][Para 88]. The contract is dated 29 September 2003 while the employment is said to run from 23 January 2003 [Para 9][Para 22]. Large tracts of the panel-composition and quotation passages are reduced to OCR noise ("<unk>", repeated bracket fragments), and the Prakas 305 Article 4 sentence on protection duration survives only partially [Para 8][Para 70].
