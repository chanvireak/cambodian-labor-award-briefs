# Award No. 253/12 — Green Tech Style (Cambodia) Co Ltd (Arbitration Council, 23 January 2013)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ouk Ry, Liv Sovanna; Pen Bun Chhea presiding) · Doc ID 140234

## 1. Facts
A Phnom Penh garment factory, Green Tech Style (Cambodia) Co Ltd, with about 1,120 workers faced six collective demands from the Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions; the local Cambodian Workers' Union held both a registration certificate and a most-representative certificate valid to 14 June 2013, so its standing was not in issue. [Para 1][Para 2][Para 45][Para 46][Para 47] Three demands were withdrawn at the hearing and two more were settled in the panel's own conciliation, leaving Issue 2 (pro-rating the regular attendance bonus against authorised leave) and Issue 5 (a 2,000 riel midday meal allowance). [Para 21][Para 22][Para 124] The employer paid a USD 10 attendance bonus but cancelled it entirely in any month the worker took even authorised leave. [Para 48][Para 49][Para 69]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pro-rate the regular attendance bonus to the number of days of authorised leave instead of cancelling it in full? [Para 48][Para 70]
2. (Issue 5) Whether the employer must pay a 2,000 riel daily midday meal allowance to every worker? [Para 110][Para 118]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant), the Federation of Cambodian Workers' Unions and its local Cambodian Workers' Union at Green Tech Style (Cambodia):** A bonus is a form of wage under Article 103, and the employer withholds the whole attendance bonus whenever a worker takes leave that the employer itself authorised, even though no wage is paid for those days; many other factories, including named garment firms, already provide a midday meal allowance. [Para 50][Para 51][Para 60][Para 61]
**Employer (respondent):** Internal rule clause 6 allows two days of personal leave a month on written request and states that such leave carries no wage and forfeits the whole attendance bonus; Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 makes the USD 10 bonus conditional on no absence at all, and allowing pro-rating would encourage excessive leave and disrupt production. [Para 56][Para 57][Para 58][Para 62]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — the employer was ordered to pro-rate the attendance bonus to the days of authorised leave actually taken (Issue 2) and the midday meal allowance claim was dismissed (Issue 5). [Para 109][Para 124][Para 126]

On Issue 2 the panel classified the demand as a rights dispute, because point 2 of Notification No. 230 of 25 July 2012 imposes a minimum USD 10 attendance bonus on all textile, garment and footwear employers. [Para 67][Para 76] It found the notification ambiguous: the phrase "without absence" does not distinguish absence that the law permits from absence it does not, and neither the Ministry nor the Labour Advisory Committee had explained it, so the panel declined to construe the phrase generally and instead decided the case before it. [Para 79][Para 80][Para 83][Para 84] It then held that once the employer has authorised leave, the worker cannot be treated as absent, because authorised leave suspends the contract under Article 71(6) and Article 72(1). [Para 92][Para 105][Para 106] Since no wage is paid for those days, the employer's only entitlement is to reduce the bonus in proportion to the days of leave. [Para 99][Para 107][Para 108]

On Issue 5 the panel found no law, agreement or collective agreement creating a 2,000 riel meal allowance, so the demand was one of interest. [Para 110][Para 111] Because the union was certified most representative, the panel did have power to decide interest disputes in equity. [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117] In equity it refused the claim: the assertion that monthly wages are too low was not a sufficient basis for judgment, and the practice of other named companies did not bind this employer, who had shown other benefits above the statutory floor. [Para 118][Para 121][Para 63][Para 123] The panel added that the proper route for such an improvement is a collective agreement negotiated with the most representative union. [Para 122]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Where an employer has already authorised leave, the worker is not "absent" for the purposes of the statutory attendance bonus: the contract is suspended, no wage is owed for those days, and the employer may reduce the bonus only in proportion to the days taken, not cancel it entirely. [Para 92][Para 106][Para 108]

**Obiter dicta:** That an attendance-bonus demand framed on comparative practice alone will fail in equity, and that interest claims above the statutory floor belong in collective bargaining rather than arbitration. [Para 121][Para 122]

All worker, union and employer representatives appear under their full names; I have reduced them to initials. [Para 1][Para 2] The award is internally inconsistent on which demands survived: line 17 says five of six demands were not conciliated, line 21 says two remained after the panel's own conciliation, and line 22 records three withdrawals. [Para 17][Para 21][Para 22] The award date of 23 January 2013 is printed on the face and never contradicted. [Para 1] Line 39 cites "Article 102" among the worker exhibits where the reasoning relies on Article 103. [Para 39][Para 100] The signature block at line 128 is corrupted, the presiding arbitrator's name appearing twice with stray characters between the copies.
