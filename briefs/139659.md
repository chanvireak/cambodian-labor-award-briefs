# Award No. 197/14 — Khan Theron I Pearl (Cambodia) Co., Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 August 2014)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Seng Vouch Hun, appointed by the employer; Sin Kim Sean, appointed by the workers; Kong Phalha, chair) · Doc ID 139659

## 1. Facts
Khan Theron I Pearl (Cambodia) Co., Ltd sews footwear in Chom Chao, Phnom Penh, and employs about 1,300 workers. [Para 65][Para 66] The union federation lodged 14 demands to improve working conditions on 13 June 2014; conciliation settled one, and the remaining 13 came to the panel as a collective dispute. [Para 34][Para 36] The workers struck from 25 December 2013 to 21 January 2014, which grounds the wage claim for the strike period. [Para 131][Para 24]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the base wage must be raised to USD 160 a month, that is USD 56 more than the USD 104 paid? [Para 159]
2. Whether a lunch allowance of 5,000 riel a day must be paid in place of the USD 6 a month now paid? [Para 181]
3. Whether the USD 5 a month incentive for the finishing and cutting sections must be restored? [Para 189]
4. Whether travel and accommodation allowance must rise from USD 10 to USD 20 a month? [Para 216]
5. Whether the employer must provide the union a meeting room, table and chairs and two hours of working time a week? [Para 225]
6. Whether the attendance bonus must rise from USD 10 to USD 20 a month? [Para 234]
7. Whether chairs must be placed beside the cutting stations where workers stand? [Para 243]
8. Whether the milk allowance must rise from USD 8 to USD 20 a month? [Para 256]
9. Whether a health-care allowance must be paid where none is paid? [Para 264]
10. Whether USD 5 a month must be paid to workers who can sew on two machines or more? [Para 277]
11. Whether wages and other benefits must be paid for the strike period? [Para 285]
12. Whether monthly wages must be paid on the 5th of the following month? [Para 296]
13. Whether hot and cold water tanks must be provided for drinking? [Para 322]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** goods, rent and food have risen so that the present wage and allowances no longer cover living; neighbouring factories pay more, naming Alim, Hung Wah, JRB, Darong, FWKK and Hung Wah for the various items; the 2007–2008 incentive was a benefit already earned, and the union needs a room to inform members. [Para 72][Para 79][Para 102][Para 129][Para 227]
**Employer (respondent):** it has no capacity to grant any of the demands; it already pays above the legal minimum and above the Prakas 317 rate; it applies the old practice, has given notice of any late payment, and cannot place chairs closer to the cutting table. [Para 74][Para 88][Para 108][Para 139][Para 151]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — all 13 claims were refused, the rights-based refusals taking effect immediately and the interest-based refusals becoming final eight days after notification if no party objects in writing to the Minister. [Para 337][Para 343][Para 352]

The panel first separated rights disputes from interest disputes under Article 312 of the Labour Law and Prakas 099: a rights dispute rests on a law, collective agreement, internal rules or past practice and is decided by law, while an interest dispute rests on none of these and is decided by equity. [Para 153][Para 157][Para 158] It then held that the claimant union, though properly registered under certificate 3101 of 29 November 2013, was not the most representative union in the enterprise. [Para 67][Para 69] Under Prakas 305 article 9 and prior awards 81/04, 113/14 and 169/11, only a most-representative union, or unions together holding more than half the workforce, may bring an interest dispute. [Para 172][Para 175][Para 176] Every claim classed as an interest dispute was therefore refused without reaching its merits, and the same ground was repeated for issues 1, 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10. [Para 180][Para 188][Para 224]

On issue 1 the panel found the USD 104 paid already exceeded the USD 100 minimum set by Prakas 317 for 2014, so the extra USD 56 had no legal or practice basis — an interest claim. [Para 159][Para 163] On issue 3 it treated the incentive as a rights claim turning on past practice, and found the USD 5 had been paid in 2007 and 2008 but stopped in 2009 with no protest from the affected workers for five years, which under award 95/09 Tak Fat showed the practice had been lawfully ended and accepted. [Para 190][Para 193][Para 211][Para 214] On issue 7 it applied Prakas 053 on seats at workstations: chairs were provided and stood about 4.8 metres away, or 6 to 7 metres for the other two workers, which it held was not far. [Para 245][Para 246][Para 253] On issue 11 it applied Article 332(1) and Article 72(1) of the Labour Law — the contract is suspended during a strike and wages are not payable — following awards 49/05, 27/10, 91/10, 118/10 and 133/13, and refused the claim even though other factories had paid. [Para 287][Para 289][Para 294] On issue 12 it held Article 116 fixes only the frequency of payment, not the day, so the employer's choice of the 7th was lawful and its two- and three-day delays were explained and notified in advance. [Para 298][Para 301][Para 318] On issue 13 it held Prakas 054 requires only sufficient hygienic drinking water, not cold-water tanks. [Para 323][Para 332]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
A union that is not the most representative in the enterprise, or a group of unions without more than half the workforce, cannot put an interest dispute to the panel at all; the claim fails for want of standing however strong its merits. A benefit paid by past practice is a right only while the practice stands: where the employer withdraws it and the affected workers do not protest over a sustained period, the practice has been lawfully ended and cannot be revived by demand. [Para 175][Para 213][Para 214]

**Obiter dicta:** the panel's remarks that the employer may fix the pay day and organise production under Article 2 of the Labour Law so long as it acts lawfully and reasonably, and that some practices belong to management rather than to past practice, are general observations going beyond the orders made. [Para 204][Para 306][Para 309]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
- The decision section prints the base-wage claim as "USD 1600" [Para 344] and the lunch allowance as "50 riel" [Para 345], against USD 160 and 5,000 riel throughout the reasons; both are OCR losses of a digit.
- The panel's constitution date reads "22 July 2023 2014" [Para 37], and the pay-date order refers to "the 5th of December next" where the reasons say the 5th of the following month [Para 340].
- The company's name appears on the face of the award in at least three Khmer spellings (Khan Theron I Pearl / I Peril / I Perel), and the findings once render it "Sen Theron" [Para 66].
- A block of the workers' comparative-wage argument is unrecoverable machine noise, leaving only "100 per cent" [Para 133]; the reasoning at [Para 133] is reconstructed from the parallel passage at [Para 293].
- The union's registration certificate is cited as 3101 "Kap/Vok" at [Para 58] and [Para 67]; the party list at [Para 1] and the findings give the same number but different letter strings.
