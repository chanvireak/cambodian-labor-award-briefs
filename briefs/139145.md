# Award No. 035/15 — Pou Yuen (Cambodia) Enterprise Ltd (Arbitration Council, 19 March 2015)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Mar Sambor Na, appointed by the employer; An Vireak, appointed by the workers; Aing Eng Thong, chair) · Doc ID 139145

## 1. Facts
The company runs a shoe factory employing about 2,560 workers. [Para 49] Ms D began work on 03 September 2012 on a one-month probation contract, followed by three successive one-year fixed-duration contracts running from 03 October 2012 to 03 October 2015. [Para 52] She was elected deputy president of the Cambodia Union at the factory in a leadership election on 21 December 2014. [Para 99][Para 148] The employer dismissed her on 09 January 2015 at 09:42 for clocking in without performing her work, disobeying her team leader and refusing to acknowledge fault. [Para 55][Para 167]

## 2. Issues in dispute
Three points went to conciliation; one was settled, leaving two, which the workers then merged into a single demand for reinstatement with preserved wages and benefits plus back pay from dismissal to reinstatement. [Para 13][Para 20][Para 52] The panel framed four questions:

1. Whether Ms D held a fixed-duration or an indefinite employment contract? [Para 100]
2. Whether her dismissal was connected to union discrimination? [Para 120]
3. Whether Ms D enjoyed special protection against dismissal? [Para 136]
4. Whether the employer dismissed Ms D lawfully? [Para 167]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The dismissal was union discrimination: she had no problem at the factory while she was an activist in the former union, and problems began once she joined the Cambodia Union. [Para 57][Para 59][Para 125] The employer gave no clear reason and issued no written warning, so she should be reinstated in her original post with the same wage, benefits and back pay. [Para 2][Para 58]

**Employer (respondent):** She never performed her duties: she clocked in and then left the workplace, did not follow her team leader's instructions, and ignored the counselling she was called in for. [Para 61][Para 62] The company did not know she was a union leader, and refuses reinstatement. [Para 63]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** partly granted — reinstatement and back pay were refused; the panel ordered the employer to pay severance equal to 30 days' wages and allowances, damages in lieu of notice equal to one month's wage, compensation for unused annual leave, and final wages for the days worked in January 2015, and ordered Ms D to repay the 5% contract-end indemnity she had already received. [Para 223][Para 224][Para 233]

On issue 1 the panel applied Article 67(2) of the Labour Law, under which a fixed-duration contract may not exceed two years in total including renewals and becomes indefinite if it does, read with Article 73(5): once the aggregate exceeds two years the contract is indefinite. [Para 100][Para 103][Para 113] Following Award No. 10/03 — Zaksintek, it held that her aggregate service of about 02 years, 04 months and 05 days made her contract indefinite. [Para 104][Para 118]

On issue 2 it applied Articles 12 and 279 of the Labour Law, which forbid an employer from using union membership or activity as a ground for hiring, discipline or termination, and followed four prior awards holding the worker bears the burden of proving discrimination. [Para 120][Para 121][Para 129] It found no evidence beyond her own assertion, and noted that the union president and secretary at the same factory were not dismissed and remained at work. [Para 132][Para 134][Para 135]

On issue 3 it applied Article 293 of the Labour Law and Article 3(3) of Prakas No. 305 (22 November 2001), which protect candidates for union leadership for 45 days before and 45 days after the election, provided the union notifies the employer of the candidacies. [Para 136][Para 140] Following Award No. 40/10 — Meng Yan, it held protection lasts a reasonable period until registration, and set three conditions: the worker must be a protected category, the dismissal must fall within the protected period, and the union must have notified the employer. [Para 144][Para 147] The first two were met — she was elected on 21 December 2014 and dismissed 19 days later — but notification reached the employer only after the dismissal, so the third failed. [Para 153][Para 161][Para 164][Para 166]

On issue 4 the panel found clocking in without working to be a genuine fault, but held it is neither "serious misconduct" (កំហុសធ្ងន់) under Article 83 nor listed as such in the company's internal rules, and the employer had not shown any disciplinary procedure was followed. [Para 181][Para 182][Para 184] Article 74 nonetheless allows dismissal at will on grounds relating to the worker's aptitude or conduct, and Articles 74 and 69 made the dismissal lawful on the proven facts. [Para 185][Para 191][Para 192] Because there was no serious misconduct, Article 89 severance remained payable, computed on the average of the last 12 months' wages under Article 168; the 4 months and 05 days remainder was not rounded up. [Para 193][Para 195][Para 196] No moral damages were awarded under Article 91 since the dismissal was justified, but Article 75 and 77 damages in lieu of notice were due for her 02 years and 05 months' service. [Para 211][Para 212][Para 215]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
An employee who clocks in but does not perform work for the contractual hours commits a real fault that can justify dismissal at will under Article 74, but it is not "serious misconduct" under Article 83 unless the internal rules say so, so Article 89 severance stays payable. [Para 182][Para 184][Para 196] Union protection under Prakas No. 305 Article 3(3) is lost where the union failed to notify the employer of the candidacy before the dismissal, even if the worker was in fact elected. [Para 161][Para 164]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that the employer's own team leaders and group leaders testified they had never warned Ms D and did not know of any warning, which it called a gap in the employer's case, though this did not disturb the finding of a lawful dismissal. [Para 179][Para 180]
