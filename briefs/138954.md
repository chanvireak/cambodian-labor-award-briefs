# Award No. 050/17 — Yap Design Limited (Arbitration Council, 11 January 2018)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators — Mar Sambor Na (employer-appointed), An Non (worker-appointed), Sokh Mathoeung (chair) · Doc ID 138954

## 1. Facts
Yap Design Limited makes furniture, decorative fittings and sculptures at Trapeang Krasang village, Boek Chan commune, Ang Snuol district, Kandal province, and employed about 198 workers at the time of the dispute. [Para 28][Para 29] On 16 December 2017 the workers petitioned for October and November 2017 wages; the Kandal Provincial Department of Labour received the complaint on 18 December 2017, one point failed conciliation that day, and the file reached the Council on 19 December 2017. [Para 9][Para 10][Para 11] Both parties admitted that the employer had not paid 181 workers 50 per cent of their October 2017 wages and all of their November 2017 wages. [Para 33]

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. Whether the employer is obliged to pay the wages for October 2017, still 50 per cent short, and for November 2017, not yet paid at 100 per cent, to the workers? [Para 33][Para 57]

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** The withheld wages had left them indebted and struggling to meet daily living costs, and they asked for the October shortfall and the full November wages. [Para 36][Para 59][Para 65]

**Employer (respondent):** It had the will to pay and no plan to close the company to escape liability, but it could not state when payment would be possible: it lacked the funds, its owner was abroad, and it was waiting on proceeds from selling 25 per cent of its shares to a new Australian investor. [Para 34][Para 35][Para 58]

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** granted — the panel ordered the employer to pay immediately the 50 per cent shortfall of October 2017 wages and the unpaid 100 per cent of November 2017 wages to the workers; the order is non-binding (សេចក្តីបង្គាប់មិនជាប់កាតព្វកិច្ច) and becomes final 8 days after notification absent written objection to the Ministry of Labour. [Para 75][Para 76][Para 77]

The panel applied Article 116(1) and (2) of the 1997 Labour Law, which requires wages to be paid at least twice a month with no more than sixteen days between payments, and at least once a month for employees, and found the employer had not complied: neither the October nor the November 2017 wage had been paid by the hearing date. [Para 60][Para 61][Para 63] It held that an employer's legal duty to pay wages due in October and November 2017 cannot be postponed on any other ground, and noted that the employer could not say when the share sale would produce funds. [Para 64][Para 66] Because Article 117 of the Labour Law routes persistent non-payment through the labour inspector, the panel recorded that route as still open to the workers, but relied on Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 on the Arbitration Council — which empowers it to order immediate payment of sums due — for its own order. [Para 69][Para 73][Para 74][Para 67][Para 68]

Before reaching the merits, the panel addressed representation: neither party asserted a most-representative union at the company, so under Article 54(1) of the Law on Trade Unions and Article 1 of Prakas No. 362 it recognised the three workers named in Provincial Department of Labour letter No. 013 of 19 December 2017 as representatives, all three being present at the hearing. [Para 30][Para 43][Para 54][Para 56]

## 5. Ratio decidendi
Wages already earned are a statutory debt: an employer's financial difficulty, an absent owner, or a pending sale of shares to a foreign investor is not a lawful reason to defer them, and the panel will order immediate payment of the shortfall under Article 34 of Prakas No. 099 once Article 116 of the Labour Law is breached and the amount is admitted by both sides. [Para 63][Para 66][Para 68]

**Obiter dicta:** The panel observed that where payment is delayed without justification, the workers may separately seek the labour inspector's intervention under Article 117 of the Labour Law, including referral to the competent court at no cost and the appointment of a provisional administrator. [Para 69][Para 70][Para 73][Para 74]

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The hearing date is printed as 21 December 2017 in the procedural summary but the summons and the reasoning refer to a hearing on 28 December 2017. [Para 8][Para 13][Para 63] The claim as recorded on the face of the award is garbled, printing "៤១០០%" where 100 per cent is meant, and the October figure acquires a stray "on 25" date in the reasoning. [Para 2][Para 57] The operative paragraph prints the year as "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ២០១៧". [Para 75] Worker numbers are inconsistent — 198 employees in the factual findings against 181 workers in the admitted arrears. [Para 29][Para 33] The chair's name is lost to a bare "សុខ" in the signature block. [Para 78] No per-worker amounts or total sum are printed anywhere in the award.
