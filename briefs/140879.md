# Award No. 021/23 — Bouy Gor German Factory (Cambodia) Khmer Peni Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន បូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី (ខេមបូឌា) ខម ភេនី លីមីធីត) (Arbitration Council, 21 November 2023)
Forum: Arbitration Council of Cambodia · 3 arbitrators (Ing Sothy for the employer, Liv Sovanna for the workers, Pen Bun Chheam presiding) · Doc ID 140879

## 1. Facts
Bouy Gor German Factory (Cambodia) Khmer Peni Limited (ក្រុមហ៊ុន បូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន ហ្វេក ធើរី (ខេមបូឌា) ខម ភេនី លីមីធីត) operates a garment-cutting and sewing enterprise at Pong Tuek, Ang Snuol, Kandal, with 4,289 workers [Para 1][Para 79]. On 25 August 2023 the worker representatives filed a complaint with three demands, of which the second was that the mid-day meal allowance and the overtime meal allowance be paid in cash, week by week, directly into the workers' hands [Para 67][Para 82]. Conciliation on 10 October 2023 failed, and the non-conciliated dispute report No. 644 of 18 October 2023 reached the Council on 19 October 2023 [Para 33][Para 38]. At the hearing the parties agreed Issue 1 and the workers withdrew Issue 3, leaving only Issue 2 for decision [Para 42].

## 2. Issues in dispute
1. (Issue 2) Whether the employer must pay the mid-day meal allowance and the overtime meal allowance in cash, hand to hand, each week, rather than by transfer into the workers' ACLEDA bank accounts [Para 82][Para 99]?

## 3. Positions of the parties
**Workers (claimant):** Article 113 of the Labour Law requires wages to be paid directly into the hands of the worker concerned, and the workers only accepted bank transfer in April 2021 because of COVID-19, on the employer's promise to revert to cash once the epidemic ended [Para 86][Para 87][Para 92]. Meal allowances are small sums that are hard to withdraw, and older workers must ask others to withdraw for them [Para 90].

**Employer (respondent):** The shift to digital payment followed guidance from the Ministry and the Government, and the workers themselves asked for the payment day to be moved from Saturday to Friday, which the company granted [Para 93][Para 94]. Clause 7 of the registered collective agreement requires the company to pay the mid-day and overtime meal allowances every Friday by computer system, and transfer to each worker's own account complies with it [Para 98][Para 116][Para 117].

## 4. Award and reasoning
**Outcome:** dismissed — the panel rejected the workers' demand that the employer pay the mid-day meal allowance and the overtime meal allowance in cash, hand to hand, and ordered no payment [Para 125][Para 126].

On Issue 2 the panel first construed Article 113 of the Labour Law, which requires wages to be paid directly to the worker personally save where the worker agrees to another method [Para 101]. Following Award No. 274/13 (Sihn Plus), it held that "paid directly" does not mean literally hand to hand but payment to the worker himself, avoiding payment to a substitute, a creditor or a relative without his agreement [Para 103][Para 108]. It then applied Article 103 of the Labour Law, which lists the components of wages and excludes benefits granted specially to workers to facilitate the performance of their work [Para 109]. Following Award No. 194/14 (Bouy Gor German), it held that the overtime meal allowance and the mid-day meal allowance are not part of wages but benefits for buying food [Para 110][Para 113]. Article 113 therefore does not govern the payment of those allowances [Para 114]. The panel also relied on Award No. 042/19 (Sihn Bao (Cambodia) Factory) and Award No. 026/21 (Ken Sport Sew) for the same reading [Para 104].

Turning to the collective agreement, the panel found that clause 7 requires the company to pay the mid-day and overtime meal allowances every Friday by computer system, and that transfer into each worker's ACLEDA account is performance of that clause [Para 115][Para 116][Para 117]. It rejected the workers' convenience arguments: a transfer can equally be spent, and practice at other garment factories cannot displace the registered agreement [Para 119][Para 121][Para 122].

## 5. Ratio decidendi
The direct-payment rule in Article 113 of the Labour Law governs wages only; meal allowances are benefits within the exclusions of Article 103, so an employer may pay them by bank transfer where the registered collective agreement so provides [Para 109][Para 114][Para 116]. Reading "paid directly" as personal payment rather than hand-to-hand delivery does not breach Article 113 [Para 103][Para 108].

**Obiter dicta:** The panel noted that a transfer leaves the money available for the worker's own expenditure, so the argument that cash better serves workers' needs did not create a duty [Para 119].

## 6. Source defects disclosed
The order constituting the panel is printed with two conflicting years, "ឆ្នាំ ២០១៩ ឆ្នាំ ២០២៣", at [Para 39]. The hearing time is printed as "១៤ និង ០០៧៩ នាទី" [Para 40]. The eight named worker representatives are badly corrupted, several reduced to a single Khmer letter or a fragment [Para 80], and the employer's HR manager appears only as a bare initial [Para 1]. The company name is printed variously, including "ប៉ូវ ឃើរ ហ្គាមេន" and "ខម កេនី លីឌីត" [Para 60][Para 98]. The operative paragraph prints the allowance as "ប្រាក់ថ្លៃថ្នូរ បាយ" [Para 126]. The English masthead is misprinted "THE ARBITRATION COUNGIL" [Para 1].
